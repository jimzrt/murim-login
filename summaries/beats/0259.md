# Chapter 259

## Plot

Dharma King Hong Dao dies in Henan after entrusting Jeok Cheongang with his final words. Jeok postpones vengeance against Jongni Chu, orders the Murim leaders to gather at Shaolin, and leaves with Jin Taekyung after warning that a blood wind may already be blowing through Shaolin.

Hong Dao's final words were “Jongni Chu, Dark Heaven, Unnamed, Buddhist Staff.” Jeok and Taekyung deduce that Jongni Chu is Dark Heaven's agent or identity, that Hong Dao entrusted Unnamed with the Green Jade Buddha Staff, and that Dark Heaven's true objective is to seize Shaolin's sacred treasure and the Abbot's symbol.

As Jeok and Taekyung travel toward Shaolin, Dark Heaven operatives slaughter messengers and disguised civilians. The scholar-like old man Han Su kills a Hidden Shadow Pavilion agent and destroys his messenger eagle before joining his red-bearded old friend Flame Tiger and proceeding toward Shaolin after learning that Unnamed possesses the Green Jade Buddha Staff.

## Continuity

- Hong Dao's final words identified Jongni Chu, Dark Heaven, Unnamed, and the Buddhist Staff as connected to the impending crisis.
- Jeok Cheongang and Jin Taekyung have departed for Shaolin, while Jeok ordered the Murim leaders to gather there immediately.
- Jeok Cheongang concluded that Jongni Chu is Dark Heaven and that Dark Heaven seeks the Green Jade Buddha Staff rather than merely Hong Dao's death.
- Hong Dao had entrusted the Green Jade Buddha Staff to Unnamed; the staff is both Shaolin's sacred treasure and the symbol carried by its Abbot.
- Dark Heaven has begun operating around Shaolin and has killed messengers, martial artists, and commoners searching for or carrying information.
- Han Su and Flame Tiger are Dark Heaven operatives and longtime friends who are now walking toward Shaolin.

## Translation Decisions

- Render 불장 as Buddhist Staff when it appears as Hong Dao's cryptic final word; retain Green Jade Buddha Staff for 녹옥불장.
- Render 한수 as Han Su and 염호 as Flame Tiger.
- Keep Han Su and Flame Tiger's dialogue rough, familiar, and casually murderous despite their scholarly and old-friend presentation.
