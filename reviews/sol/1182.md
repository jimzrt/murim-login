# Review

The battle’s desperation and the Emperor’s motive are softened or shifted in three passages.

## F01 — Minor

- **Confidence:** 0.94
- **Source:** 전투는 격렬했다.
동시에 처절했다.
- **Current:** The battle was fierce.

And merciless.
- **Defect:** “Merciless” describes cruelty rather than the desperate, grueling nature of the fighting.
- **Replacement:** The battle was fierce.

And desperate.
- **Rationale:** 처절했다 characterizes the struggle as desperate or harrowing, not as showing no mercy.

## F02 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.88
- **Source:** 대의(大義)가 아닌, 인의(人義)를 위해서.

‘인의, 인의라.’
- **Current:** Not for the greater cause, but for the sake of humanity.

*Humanity. Humanity…*
- **Defect:** “For the sake of humanity” suggests a grand mission to save humankind, blurring the explicit contrast with a grand cause.
- **Replacement:** Not for some grand cause, but out of compassion for his people.

*Compassion. Compassion…*
- **Rationale:** 인의 here concerns humane obligation to people; the Emperor has moved to protect those who follow him.

## F03 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.84
- **Source:** 그가 남들과 같은, 아니 그보다 더욱 큰 두려움과 고통을 느끼고 있음에도 그것이 인의라 믿기 때문이었다.
- **Current:** He felt fear and pain just like everyone else—perhaps even more than they did. But he believed that what he was doing was an act of humanity.
- **Defect:** “An act of humanity” is vague and does not carry through the humane obligation that explains why Jin advances despite his fear.
- **Replacement:** He felt fear and pain just like everyone else—perhaps even more than they did. But he pressed on because he believed it was the compassionate thing to do.
- **Rationale:** The source makes his belief in 인의 the reason he keeps going despite fear and pain.
