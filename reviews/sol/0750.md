# Review

The draft is broadly complete, but it contains two subject/action changes, loses the Emperor/King of Japan register contrast, alters the supernatural invocation that triggers the haunting, mistranslates a generational label, and softens or misexplains several jokes and profanities.

## F01 — Critical

- **Confidence:** 0.99
- **Source:** “허어. 레비아탄을 놓치다니.”
- **Current:** “Good heavens. You let Leviathan get away.”
- **Defect:** The draft adds an explicit second-person subject and directly accuses Jin of letting Leviathan escape, whereas the Korean is a subjectless lament that Leviathan got away.
- **Replacement:** “Good heavens. To think Leviathan got away.”
- **Rationale:** Adding “you let” changes the speaker’s stated blame and sharpens the confrontation beyond what this line says.

## F02 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.99
- **Source:** 평소의 최 팀장이었다면 이쯤에서 내게 눈치를 줬을 거다.

그러니까, 평소였다면.
- **Current:** If this had been the Team Leader Choi of old, he would have given me a warning look around this point.

That is, if this had been the Team Leader Choi of old.
- **Defect:** “Of old” implies that Choi has permanently changed from a former version of himself. The source instead contrasts his usual behavior with his exceptional behavior in this particular situation.
- **Replacement:** Normally, Team Leader Choi would have given me a warning look around this point.

That is, normally.
- **Rationale:** 평소 means “normally” or “as usual,” not “in the old days.”

## F03 — Critical

- **Confidence:** 0.99
- **Source:** 그리고 그것은 항공기에서 내려 진입할 준비 중이었던 최 팀장과 이백여 명의 헌터들에게도 해당되는 문제였다.
- **Current:** The same was true for Team Leader Choi and the more than two hundred Hunters who had been preparing to descend from the aircraft.
- **Defect:** The draft reverses the Hunters’ movement and location. They had already gotten off the aircraft and were preparing to enter the operation, not preparing to descend from the aircraft.
- **Replacement:** The same problem applied to Team Leader Choi and the more than two hundred Hunters who had gotten off the aircraft and were preparing to enter.
- **Rationale:** 항공기에서 내려 is a completed action: they had disembarked before preparing to 진입, “enter” or “move in.”

## F04 — Minor

- **Confidence:** 0.97
- **Source:** ‘이걸 좆 같다고 때려치울 수도 없고.’
- **Current:** *It’s not like I can just say to hell with this and walk away.*
- **Defect:** The draft sanitizes both the explicit vulgarity of 좆 같다고 and the concrete action 때려치우다, which means quitting rather than merely walking away.
- **Replacement:** *It’s not like I can call this fucking bullshit and quit, either.*
- **Rationale:** Jin’s internal line is deliberately crude and frustrated; “say to hell with this” is substantially milder.

## F05 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.99
- **Source:** “총리. 아니면 일왕. 누구든 그쪽보단 낫겠지.”

“일왕이라니! 이자가 감히 대 일본제국의 천황께…….”
- **Current:** “The Prime Minister. Or the Emperor. Anyone has to be better than you.”

“The Emperor? How dare you speak of the Emperor of the Great Japanese Empire like that—”
- **Defect:** Using “Emperor” for both 일왕 and 천황 erases the deliberate register and hierarchy contrast: Jin uses the Korean designation “King of Japan,” and the imperialist minister indignantly insists on the exalted title “Emperor.”
- **Replacement:** “The Prime Minister. Or the King of Japan. Anyone has to be better than you.”

“The King of Japan?! How dare you refer to His Majesty the Emperor of the Great Japanese Empire that way—”
- **Rationale:** The minister’s outrage depends on Jin calling the monarch 일왕 rather than 천황; flattening both terms removes the trigger for his response.

## F06 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.99
- **Source:** “진주만의 원혼이여, 내게 임하라!”
- **Current:** “Spirits of Pearl Harbor, heed my call!”
- **Defect:** The invocation’s requested action is altered. The Skeleton King commands the vengeful spirits to descend upon or possess him, not merely to listen to him.
- **Replacement:** “Vengeful spirits of Pearl Harbor, descend upon me!”
- **Rationale:** 원혼 specifically means resentful or vengeful spirits, while 내게 임하라 asks them to come down upon him. That mechanism directly sets up the supernatural manifestation that follows.

## F08 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.98
- **Source:** 7080세대에 태어난 일본 제국주의자와 마계 출신 미국 몬스터의 치열한 논쟁이라니.
- **Current:** A heated argument between a Japanese imperialist born in the 7080s generation and an American monster from the Demon Realm.
- **Defect:** “The 7080s generation” reads as the eighth millennium and mistranslates the Korean cultural shorthand 7080세대, which denotes the generation associated with the 1970s and 1980s.
- **Replacement:** A heated argument between a Japanese imperialist from the ’70s–’80s generation and an American monster from the Demon Realm.
- **Rationale:** 7080 is Korean shorthand combining the 1970s and 1980s, not the year range 7080–7089.

## F09 — Minor

- **Current:** My entire body suddenly grew cold, and the fluorescent lights began to flicker.

## F11 — Minor

- **Confidence:** 0.97
- **Source:** “아, 한국에서는 다른 의미로 들리는 모양이군. 그럼 이름 끝 글자를 따서 경상으로 부르면 되겠소?”
- **Current:** [^1]: *Jinsang* can mean an obnoxious person, while *Gyeongsang* means a minor injury in Korean. Koizumi is making a pun based on the final syllable of Jin Taekyung’s name.
- **Defect:** The note incorrectly says Koizumi is intentionally making a pun. He first uses the ordinary Japanese address Jin-san without recognizing its Korean homophone, then proposes Gyeong-san after Jin objects.
- **Replacement:** [^1]: Japanese *Jin-san* (“Mr. Jin”) sounds like the Korean *jinsang*, meaning an obnoxious or troublesome person. Koizumi’s proposed *Gyeong-san* sounds like Korean *gyeongsang*, meaning a minor injury.
- **Rationale:** The humor comes from an accidental cross-language homophone followed by Koizumi’s attempted correction, not from a deliberate initial joke.
