# Review

Several actionable fidelity and register issues, including an altered subject, a lost speech-level pun, physicalized rage idioms, invented dialogue, and an incorrect culinary explanation.

## F01 — Minor

- **Confidence:** 0.99
- **Source:** 어지간히 간덩이가 붓지 않고서야 혈혈단신의 몸으로 고원을 가로지를 생각은 하지 못한다.
- **Current:** Unless someone had an exceptionally thick liver, no one would think of crossing the plateau alone.
- **Defect:** “Thick liver” is an untranslated Korean idiom and is not intelligible natural English.
- **Replacement:** Unless someone was exceptionally daring, they would never even consider crossing the plateau alone.
- **Rationale:** 간덩이가 붓다 describes extreme audacity or recklessness, not a physical condition of the liver.

## F02 — Critical

- **Confidence:** 0.99
- **Source:** “왜 그러시오?”
- **Current:** “Why have you stopped us?”
- **Defect:** The draft invents a plural object and says Black Sand stopped the speaker and others, although he only stopped the Human Butcher’s subordinate.
- **Replacement:** “What is it?”
- **Rationale:** The source asks generally why Black Sand is reacting that way; it does not say that he stopped “us.”

## F03 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.99
- **Source:** “어린노무 새끼가 어디서 하오체야? 하오문 출신이냐?”
- **Current:** “Where does a little bastard like you get off talking so formally? Are you from the Lower District Sect?”
- **Defect:** The draft mischaracterizes 하오체 as simply formal speech, obscures why the elder considers it disrespectful, and loses the 하오체/하오문 wordplay.
- **Replacement:** “Who told a little bastard like you that he could use *hao*-style speech with me? Are you from the Lower District Sect or something?”[^1]

[^1]: *Hao*-style speech is a semi-formal Korean speech level; its name sets up the pun on the Lower District Sect’s Korean name, *Haomun*.
- **Rationale:** The old man rebukes a younger man for addressing him with the insufficiently respectful 하오체, then jokes that its name makes him sound like a member of 하오문.

## F04 — Minor

- **Confidence:** 0.96
- **Source:** “아따, 한 상 우라지게 차려 놨네.
- **Current:** “Goodness, you’ve prepared quite a feast.
- **Defect:** “Goodness” sanitizes the coarse, emphatic regional register of 아따 and 우라지게.
- **Replacement:** “Well, damn, you’ve laid out one hell of a feast.
- **Rationale:** The source uses rough, colorful speech rather than a mild exclamation, which is part of the old man’s abrupt voice shift.

## F05 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.98
- **Source:** 다음 순간, 눈이 뒤집힌 인도의 신형이 번개처럼 솟구쳤다.
- **Current:** The next moment, the Human Butcher’s eyes rolled back, and his body shot upward like lightning.
- **Defect:** The draft physicalizes the rage idiom 눈이 뒤집히다 as his eyes literally rolling back.
- **Replacement:** The next moment, the Human Butcher, beside himself with rage, shot upward like lightning.
- **Rationale:** Here 눈이 뒤집히다 means that rage has overwhelmed his reason, not that his eyes perform a distinct physical action.

## F06 — Minor

- **Confidence:** 0.99
- **Source:** 그러자 검압에 의해 먼지구름이 흩어지며 반쯤 가루가 된 폐허가 모습을 드러냈다.
- **Current:** The resulting sword pressure scattered the dust cloud, revealing a ruin that had been reduced to half rubble.
- **Defect:** “Reduced to half rubble” is both unnatural and materially different from the ruin being half pulverized into powder.
- **Replacement:** The resulting sword pressure scattered the dust cloud, revealing a half-pulverized ruin.
- **Rationale:** 반쯤 가루가 된 describes roughly half of the wreckage having been reduced to powder.

## F07 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.98
- **Source:** 이미 눈이 뒤집힌 인도는 거리낄 것이 없었다.
- **Current:** The Human Butcher’s eyes were still rolled back. He had nothing left to restrain him.
- **Defect:** The draft again turns an idiom for being beyond reason into a literal eye position.
- **Replacement:** The Human Butcher was already beyond reason and had no reservations left.
- **Rationale:** 눈이 뒤집힌 describes his maddened state, while 거리낄 것이 없었다 means he no longer had any hesitation or compunction.

## F08 — Critical

- **Confidence:** 0.99
- **Source:** 태산압정.
- **Current:** “Mount Tai Presses Down on the Crown.”
- **Defect:** Quotation marks turn the narrative technique name into dialogue, inventing a spoken declaration by the Human Butcher.
- **Replacement:** *Mount Tai Presses Down on the Crown.*
- **Rationale:** The source presents the move name as a narration fragment and does not indicate that anyone says it aloud.

## F09 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.98
- **Source:** “닭은 버릴 곳이 없지. 맛도 좋고, 뼈는 푹 고아 먹고. 가끔은 검기도 쓰고.”
- **Current:** “There’s no part of a chicken to waste. It tastes good, you can boil the bones down and eat them, and sometimes I even use them for Sword Energy.”
- **Defect:** The draft says the boiled bones themselves are eaten, changing the culinary explanation.
- **Replacement:** “There’s no part of a chicken that goes to waste. The meat is delicious, the bones make a fine broth when simmered down, and sometimes they’re even good for wielding Sword Energy.”
- **Rationale:** 뼈는 푹 고아 먹고 refers to simmering the bones thoroughly to make and consume broth, not eating the bones themselves.
