# Review

Four actionable findings: one changes who may suffer the flood’s losses, one changes the victims’ relationship to their neighbors, and two weaken the dialogue’s intended force or clue.

## F01 — Critical

- **Confidence:** 0.97
- **Source:** 지금 눈 앞에 펼쳐진 이 광경이, 분노하는 저들의 모습이 언젠가 자신에게 찾아올 수도 있다는 것을.
- **Current:** what was happening before their eyes—that fury—could one day come for them, too.
- **Defect:** Makes the angry crowd’s fury sound like a threat directed at the onlookers. They realize they might one day be in the same position as the victims.
- **Replacement:** they too might one day be standing there, furious over their own losses.
- **Rationale:** The onlookers imagine themselves in the place of the people now expressing anger.

## F02 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.96
- **Source:** 그리고 그들 모두는 가족보다 가깝던 이웃이다.
- **Current:** And every one of them had neighbors who’d been closer than family.
- **Defect:** Changes the relationship: the people who suffered the losses are themselves neighbors close as family, not merely people who had such neighbors.
- **Replacement:** And they were all neighbors who had been closer than family.
- **Rationale:** 그들 모두 refers back to those who lost property or family.

## F03 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.94
- **Source:** 시팔, 민심이고 나발이고 알 거 없다 이건가? 제 자식 태어난 게 우선이다 이거야?
- **Current:** “Hell, does he not give a damn about public sentiment or anything else? His own child’s birthday comes first?”
- **Defect:** Softens the opening profanity and misses the dismissive force of 나발이고: the speaker accuses the Emperor of treating public sentiment as something to hell with.
- **Replacement:** “Fuck, is it to hell with what the people think? His own kid’s birthday comes first, is that it?”
- **Rationale:** 시팔 is strong profanity, while 민심이고 나발이고 contemptuously dismisses concern for public sentiment.

## F04 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.98
- **Source:** 산으로 향하시오.
- **Current:** “Go to the mountains.”
- **Defect:** The plural obscures the singular mountain that anchors the ensuing question and revelation.
- **Replacement:** “Go to the mountain.”
- **Rationale:** The speaker immediately specifies the highest mountain, and the crowd tries to identify that one place.
