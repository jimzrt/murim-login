# Review

The draft is broadly faithful, but it loses the explicit mutual-death stakes in two lines and weakens several characterization and causal cues.

## F01 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.99
- **Source:** “동귀어진(同歸於盡)이라. 하.”
- **Current:** “A fight to the death. Heh.”
- **Defect:** A fight to the death does not convey that both opponents die.
- **Replacement:** “So you’re ready to take me down with you. Heh.”
- **Rationale:** 동귀어진 means mutual destruction, which is what So Gyo recognizes in his threat.

## F02 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.98
- **Source:** “그래. 동귀어진마저 각오하는, 그 성치 않은 몸으로 굳이 내게 맞서려는 이유.”
- **Current:** “Yeah. Why insist on fighting me with that battered body of yours, even if it means a fight to the death?”
- **Defect:** The question again reduces his willingness to die alongside her to an ordinary lethal fight.
- **Replacement:** “Yeah. Why insist on fighting me with that body of yours in such poor shape, when you’re even prepared to die taking me down?”
- **Rationale:** So Gyo specifically asks why he would face her despite his condition and his willingness to accept mutual destruction.

## F03 — Major

- **Source:** “그리고 상산왕, 그 어린아이가 왜 그리 널 따르는지 조금은 알 것 같네. 응, 확실히.”
- **Current:** “And I think I understand a little why Prince Shangshan, that child, follows you around so much. Yes. I really do.”

## F04 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.93
- **Source:** 그러니 나 한 사람으로 퉁칠 수 있다면 오히려 다행이다.
- **Current:** So if one life of mine could make up for it, I’d be lucky.
- **Defect:** “Make up for it” suggests his death would compensate for those already killed, rather than stop further harm at the cost of his life.
