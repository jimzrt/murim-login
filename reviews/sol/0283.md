# Review

Two subject reversals materially alter the exchange, with additional issues involving ownership, quantity, mechanism, hierarchy, humor, and pragmatic nuance.

## F01 — Critical

- **Confidence:** 0.99
- **Source:** “이번에 제대로 배웠으니 이제 그만 놓아주게.”
- **Current:** “You learned your lesson properly this time, so let him go now.”
- **Defect:** The translation makes Taekyung the person who learned a lesson, but Park Tae Seop is referring to the beaten Park Jihoon.
- **Replacement:** “He learned his lesson properly this time, so let him go now.”
- **Rationale:** The omitted subject is resolved by 놓아주게, which asks Taekyung to release Jihoon after Jihoon has been taught a lesson.

## F02 — Critical

- **Confidence:** 0.99
- **Source:** “나도 저 친구가 썩 마음에 들지는 않지만, 그래도 내 조언을 듣는 것이 신상에 좋았을걸세.”
- **Current:** “I don’t particularly like that fellow myself, but it would have been better for his own sake if he had listened to my advice.”
- **Defect:** The translation says Jihoon should have listened to Park Tae Seop, reversing the implied subject. Park Tae Seop is warning Taekyung that Taekyung would have been better off heeding his advice.
- **Replacement:** “I don’t particularly like that fellow myself, but you would’ve been better off taking my advice.”
- **Rationale:** 신상에 좋았을걸세 is directed at 자네, Taekyung, continuing Park Tae Seop’s warning about the consequences of injuring Jihoon.

## F04 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.98
- **Source:** “혀가 맵군.”

“손맛은 더 맵습니다. 제 예전 별명이 행신동 불닭볶음손이었는데…… 어디 한번 맛 좀 보시겠어요?”
- **Current:** “You have a sharp tongue.”

“My hands are even sharper. My old nickname was the Haengsin-dong Buldak Hand.[^1] Why don’t you have a taste?”
- **Defect:** Rendering 맵다 as “sharp” breaks the repeated spicy-taste wordplay, and the nickname omits 볶음, which is necessary for the parody of buldak-bokkeum-myeon.
- **Replacement:** “You’ve got quite a spicy tongue.”

“My hands are even spicier. My old nickname was the Haengsin-dong Fire-Chicken Stir-Fried Hand.[^1]… Want a taste?”
- **Rationale:** The exchange repeatedly uses spicy flavor imagery—맵군, 더 맵습니다, 불닭볶음손, and 맛—to construct one continuous threat and food-name joke.
