# Review

Three subject or continuity errors, one obscured surname joke, and one inaccurate physical description.

## F01 — Minor

- **Confidence:** 0.95
- **Source:** 많이 쳐 줘야 이제 이립이나 되었을까 싶은 사내는 시원시원한 미남형이었고 허리춤에는 거무튀튀한 색을 띤 도갑이 매달려 있었다.
- **Current:** The man looked like a handsome, broad-featured fellow who couldn’t be much older than thirty, and a dark-colored saber scabbard hung from his waist.
- **Defect:** The source places him at thirty at most, rather than merely not much older than thirty. 시원시원한 describes an open, strikingly handsome impression, not literally broad facial features.
- **Replacement:** The man looked no more than thirty, with open, strikingly handsome features, and a blackish saber scabbard hung from his waist.
- **Rationale:** This preserves both the source’s upper age estimate and the intended favorable impression of his appearance.

## F02 — Minor

- **Confidence:** 0.99
- **Source:** “그나저나 이름 멋있네. 혹시 사마의랑 무슨 관계냐?”

“뭐?”

“뭐긴 뭐야, 사마의라고. 삼국지 나오는 그 사마의.”
- **Current:** “Your name is pretty cool, though. Are you related to Sima Yi?”

“What?”

“What do you mean, what? Sima Yi. The one from *Romance of the Three Kingdoms*.”
- **Defect:** The joke depends on Sama Pyo and Sima Yi visibly sharing the surname 司馬 in Korean, but the binding English romanizations make the names look unrelated and leave the subsequent same-surname explanation apparently contradictory.
- **Replacement:** “Your name is pretty cool, though. Are you related to Sima Yi?[^1]”

“What?”

“What do you mean, what? Sima Yi. The one from *Romance of the Three Kingdoms*.”

[^1]: “Sama” is the Korean reading and “Sima” the Mandarin reading of the same surname, 司馬.
- **Rationale:** A concise footnote preserves the established romanizations while restoring the linguistic basis of the joke.
