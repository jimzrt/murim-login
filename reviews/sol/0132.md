This kid had a lot of things he had never tried.

## F02 — Minor

- **Confidence:** 0.99
- **Source:** “배가 좀…… 많이 고프셨나 보네.”
- **Current:** “You must have been… pretty hungry.”
- **Defect:** The draft drops Taekyung’s comic self-correction from “a little” to “very,” an intentional pragmatic shift prompted by Cheongpung’s voracious eating.

## F04 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.99
- **Source:** “매구.”

“매구?”

옆 동네 섬나라 사는 메구미는 알아도 매구는 처음 들어 본다. 내 시선에 점소이가 귀찮다는 듯이 대답했다.

“매채구육(梅采拘肉)이요.”

“…….”

이제는 하다 하다 줄임말까지 쓰는구나.

내가 어이없어하는 사이 청풍은 빠르게 음식 접시를 비워 나가고 있었다.
- **Current:** “Plum Pork.”

“Plum Pork?”

I knew Megumi, who lived in the island country next door, but this was my first time hearing of Plum Pork. The waiter answered irritably, as though I had inconvenienced him.

“Preserved Vegetable Pork.”

“…”

Now he was shortening the names, too.

While I stood there dumbfounded, Cheongpung was rapidly emptying the plates.
- **Defect:** Translating 매구 as “Plum Pork” destroys the sound-based Megumi joke and no longer reads as a recognizable abbreviation of the expanded dish name. The passage also adds that seated Taekyung “stood there,” an action absent from the source.
- **Replacement:** “Maegu.”

“Maegu?”

I’d heard of Megumi from the island country next door, but Maegu was a new one to me. Catching my look, the waiter answered as if explaining it was a chore.

“Maechae Guyuk.”[^2]

“…”

Now he was even abbreviating dish names.

While I stared at him in disbelief, Cheongpung rapidly emptied the plates.

[^2]: *Maechae Guyuk* is pork belly with preserved mustard greens. The waiter shortens its Korean name to *Maegu*, which sounds like the beginning of the Japanese name Megumi.
- **Rationale:** The humor depends mechanically on 매구 being both an abbreviation of 매채구육 and phonetically close to 메구미; the localized dish names preserve neither relationship.
