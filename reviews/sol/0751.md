# Review

Six actionable findings: two altered physical/pragmatic actions, two lost register or joke mechanisms, one mischaracterized reaction, and one unnatural punchline.

## F01 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.97
- **Source:** “나를. 아니, 저를?”
- **Current:** “Me. I mean, me?”
- **Defect:** The identical English pronouns erase the Skeleton King’s self-conscious switch from the informal first-person pronoun 나 to the deferential 저.
- **Replacement:** “Me? I mean… me, sir?”
- **Rationale:** Adding “sir” makes the attempted shift into respectful speech perceptible in English while preserving his awkward correction.

- **Source:** “스톤 킹이라니까. 자. 천천히 따라해 보시라. 스. 톤. 킹.”

“스. 톤. 킹.”

“이번엔 좀 길게. 스톤. 킹.”

“스톤. 킹.”

“매우 훌륭하구나요! 이제 이어서 해 보십시오.”

“스토무-킹.”
- **Current:** “I said Stone King. Here. Repeat after me slowly. Stone. King.”

“Stone. King.”

“This time, make it a little longer. Stone. King.”

“Stone. King.”

“Very excellent! Now, say it together.”

“Stomu-King.”
- **Defect:** Both pronunciation exercises are rendered identically, so the progression from three separately pronounced units to two combined units disappears and “say it together” no longer follows coherently.
- **Replacement:** “I said Stone King. Here. Repeat after me slowly. S. Tone. King.”

“S. Tone. King.”

“This time, join the first two. Stone. King.”

“Stone. King.”

“Very excellent! Now put it all together.”

“Stomu-King.”
- **Rationale:** The source first separates 스, 톤, and 킹, then combines 스톤 before asking Koizumi to join the whole name; preserving those stages is necessary for the pronunciation joke.
