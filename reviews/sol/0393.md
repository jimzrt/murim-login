# Review

Two critical fidelity errors alter who failed to recover the bodies and who is letting the attack on General Liao pass. Additional findings concern characterization, temporal meaning, political lineage, binding terminology, and unnatural English.

## F01 — Critical

- **Confidence:** 0.99
- **Source:** 너 구하겠다고 쫓아갔던 아이들, 시신도 못 찾은 건 아니?
- **Current:** The kids who chased after you to save your ass—did you at least find their bodies?
- **Defect:** The draft makes Wu Heixing the person who may have found the bodies. The source says the bodies of those who followed him could not even be recovered and asks whether he knows that.
- **Replacement:** The kids who chased after you to save your ass—do you even know that their bodies were never found?
- **Rationale:** This restores both the omitted fact that the bodies remain unrecovered and the accusatory question directed at Wu Heixing.

## F02 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.98
- **Source:** 무섭고, 지치고, 그 와중에 전공은 세워야겠고. 그러다 보니 갈수록 조급해지지?
- **Current:** “Scared, exhausted, and still expected to earn military glory in the middle of it all. I suppose that’s why you’re getting more and more desperate.”
- **Defect:** “Expected to earn” makes military glory an external demand. Faye Chen is accusing Wu Heixing himself of still wanting to win glory despite his fear and exhaustion.
- **Replacement:** “You’re scared and exhausted, but you still want to earn military glory. That’s why you’re getting more desperate by the day, isn’t it?”
- **Rationale:** The source lists Wu Heixing’s fear, exhaustion, and personal drive for glory as the causes of his growing desperation.
