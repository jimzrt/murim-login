# Review

One critical subject error changes who performs Jeok Cheongang’s treatment. Additional findings concern sect closure, Mungyeong’s categorical confidence, omitted register and geographic nuance, the romanization of Tang Mimi, venom mechanics, and System terminology.

## F01 — Critical

- **Confidence:** 1.00
- **Source:** “……만약, 아주 만약에 만독지환이 있다면 적 대협을 치료할 수 있겠느냐?”
- **Current:** “……If, and I mean if, the Myriad-Poison Ring exists, can it treat Great Hero Jeok?”
- **Defect:** The subject is changed from the physician being asked whether he can treat Great Hero Jeok to the ring itself performing the treatment.
- **Replacement:** “……If—and I mean if—the Myriad-Poison Ring exists, can you treat Great Hero Jeok?”
- **Rationale:** Tang Sadok directly asks whether the Divine Physician can treat Jeok Cheongang if the ring exists.

## F02 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.98
- **Source:** 사천당문이 무능하다면 천하에 산재한 문파 대부분은 빗장 걸고 봉문 해야지.
- **Current:** If the Sichuan Tang Clan was incompetent, most of the sects scattered throughout the world would have to bolt their gates and enter closed seclusion.
- **Defect:** “Enter closed seclusion” describes individual cultivation and adds an action not present in 봉문, which means closing a sect to the outside world.
- **Replacement:** If the Sichuan Tang Clan was incompetent, most of the sects scattered throughout the world might as well bar their gates and shut themselves off from the martial world.
- **Rationale:** The sentence says those sects should close their gates and withdraw as organizations, not begin secluded cultivation.

## F03 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.98
- **Source:** “신의라면, 가능합니다.”
- **Current:** “If you are the Divine Physician, it is possible.”
- **Defect:** The conditional wording makes the Divine Physician’s identity sound uncertain. Mungyeong is categorically expressing faith in his Master’s ability.
- **Replacement:** “The Divine Physician can do it.”
- **Rationale:** 신의라면 means that if anyone can accomplish the treatment, the Divine Physician can; it does not question whether the addressee is truly the Divine Physician.
