# Review

The draft contains two reversed subjects/actions, several localized fidelity errors, and two culturally specific jokes whose meanings are lost without footnotes.

## F01 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.98
- **Source:** “아니, 대부분의 부족장들이 너희를 싫어하니까.”
- **Current:** “No. Because most of the tribal chiefs hate you.”
- **Defect:** The source uses the explicitly plural 너희, referring to Jin’s group, while the English “you” can be read as referring only to Jin.
- **Replacement:** “No. Because most of the tribal chiefs hate all of you.”
- **Rationale:** Making the object explicitly plural preserves who the tribal chiefs dislike and avoids changing the political situation into personal hostility toward Jin alone.

## F02 — Critical

- **Confidence:** 1.00
- **Source:** 내가 앞서 들었던 말을 부정하지 못했듯이, 녀석도 마찬가지였다.
- **Current:** Just as he couldn’t deny what I had said earlier, neither could he.
- **Defect:** The draft reverses the first clause’s subject and object. Jin says that he could not deny what he had heard earlier; it does not say Yayul Mok could not deny something Jin had said.
- **Replacement:** Just as I couldn’t deny what I had heard earlier, neither could he.
- **Rationale:** The source explicitly contrasts Jin’s inability to deny Yayul Mok’s earlier statement with Yayul Mok’s inability to deny Jin’s assessment.

## F03 — Minor

- **Confidence:** 0.96
- **Source:** ‘그렇다면 좋은 일이지. 이 넓은 땅덩어리에 우리 편이 좁쌀만큼이라는 게 문제지만.’
- **Current:** *That’s a good thing. The problem is that our allies in this enormous land number about as many as a single grain of millet.*
- **Defect:** The source compares the tiny size of Jin’s allied side to a grain of millet; it does not literally count the allies as one person.
- **Replacement:** *That’s a good thing. The problem is that in this enormous land, our side is no bigger than a grain of millet.*
- **Rationale:** This preserves the source’s size metaphor without turning it into an altered numerical claim.

## F04 — Minor

- **Confidence:** 0.99
- **Source:** “그런 게 있어요. 남 노인한테는 난생처음이 아니라, 어차피 이번 생에는 두 번 다시 못 들어볼 겁니다.”
- **Current:** “It’s a thing. And for you, Old Man Namho, it won’t be something you hear for the first time—it’ll be something you never hear again in this lifetime.”
- **Defect:** The draft produces a contradictory line and loses the punchline that this first encounter will also be Namho’s last because he will never hear the word a second time.
- **Replacement:** “It’s a thing. And for you, Old Man Namho, this will be the first and last time you ever hear it in this lifetime.”
- **Rationale:** The replacement naturally conveys 난생처음 and 두 번 다시 while preserving Jin’s deadpan dismissal.

## F05 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.97
- **Source:** “무야호(武野虎). 굳센 벌판의 호랑이라는 뜻이다.”
- **Current:** “Muyaho. It means ‘tiger of the mighty wilds.’”
- **Defect:** The draft omits the supplied characters and leaves the culturally specific Muyaho joke unexplained, so Jin’s preceding mountain-hiker association and private reaction have no recoverable punchline in English.
- **Replacement:** “Muyaho (武野虎).[^1] It means ‘tiger of the mighty wilds.’”

[^1]: “Muyaho” echoes a Korean meme catchphrase and also resembles *yaho*, a shout traditionally made in the mountains.
- **Rationale:** The characters preserve Yayul Mok’s intended martial meaning, while the concise footnote supplies the modern wordplay that Jin recognizes.

## F06 — Critical

- **Confidence:** 1.00
- **Source:** 원래 이 세상에 내 새끼 칭찬만큼 기쁜 게 없다. 야호맘 야율목이 뿌듯한 표정으로 고개를 끄덕였다.
- **Current:** There was nothing in this world more satisfying than praising one’s own child. Yayul Mok, the proud father of Yaho, nodded with a pleased expression.
- **Defect:** The first sentence reverses the action: the source describes the pleasure of hearing one’s child praised, not praising the child oneself. The draft also replaces the deliberate comic label “Yaho-mom” with “proud father.”
- **Replacement:** Nothing in this world makes you happier than hearing someone praise your child. Yaho-mom Yayul Mok nodded proudly.
- **Rationale:** Namho has just praised the White Tiger, which pleases Yayul Mok; retaining “Yaho-mom” also preserves the narrator’s gender-bending pet-parent joke.

## F07 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.99
- **Source:** “아, 그럼. 내가 평소에도 많이 좋아하지.”
- **Current:** “Of course. I’ve always liked them very much.”
- **Defect:** A literal English reading loses the sexual double meaning behind Namho’s fondness for a “White Tiger,” leaving Jin’s alarm and warning that the line is going too far unexplained.
- **Replacement:** “Of course. I’ve always been very fond of them.”[^2]

[^2]: In Korean slang, “White Tiger” can also refer to a woman with little or no pubic hair, giving Namho’s answer an unintended sexual double meaning.
- **Rationale:** The prose remains euphemistic like the source, while the rare factual footnote restores the culturally specific joke that motivates Jin’s reaction.

## F08 — Major

- **Current:** “I’ve already heard about it. I even knew Blade of Flowers Jin Baekyang personally.”

## F09 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.94
- **Source:** 훗날 서쪽에서 십만 마도라는 들불이 일어나자 그는 무림맹에 투신했소.
- **Current:** Later, when the wildfire known as the Hundred Thousand Demonic Path rose in the west, he threw himself into the Murim Alliance.
- **Defect:** The draft treats “Hundred Thousand Demonic Path” as the proper name of a wildfire, obscuring that 십만 conveys the hundred-thousand-strong scale of the Demonic Path force.
- **Replacement:** Later, when the hundred-thousand-strong Demonic Path rose like wildfire in the west, he joined the Murim Alliance.
- **Rationale:** This preserves the stated quantity, the wildfire metaphor, and the established term “Demonic Path” without inventing an event name.
