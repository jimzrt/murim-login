# Chapter 145

Hong Jin let out a quiet laugh and tipped back his cup.

“Our Young Master Jin is greedier than I thought.”

“I’m about as greedy as anyone else. And could you please just call me Young Master Jin?”

“Oh my. Pretending you don’t like it when you do.”

“Fucking hell…”

“Hmm? What did you just say?”

“I said it’s nice. Like we’re family.”

“Family, huh? I like the sound of that. I’m already looking forward to meeting Lesser Family Head Jin.”

Right. No matter how much I ran my mouth here, the final decision would come down to Jin Wikyung and Hong Jin.

They were both practically professionals at this sort of thing. The amateur should know when to step aside.

“By the way, what do you have against the Seongun Escort Bureau?”

“I wouldn’t call it a grudge… I’m just giving someone I dislike an extra slap. The chance to pick up a few crumbs along the way doesn’t hurt either.”

“Ah. Does this have something to do with the Young Bureau Head of the Seongun Escort Bureau who was supposed to come?”

This man was frighteningly perceptive.

He’d already figured out the gist of it, so there was no point explaining every little detail. I shrugged.

“Something like that. How did you know?”

“Young Master Jin, I lived in the imperial palace for over twenty years.”

“Excuse me?”

“I survived by reading the room. Reading a few children’s faces is easy.”

Hong Jin gestured with his chin toward the young prodigies of the Five Gates of Shanxi.

At the far end of a table some distance away, they had been sneaking glances at us with frozen expressions. The moment they realized they’d been noticed, they flinched and shrank back.

“They’ve been like that ever since they came in. Watching Young Master Jin’s every move.”

“Really?”

“Yes. They looked so pitiful that even I felt sorry for them. What on earth did you do?”

“Something similar to what happened with the people who left earlier.”

“The Three Hands of Zhongnan? Tsk, tsk. They failed to recognize who they were dealing with, didn’t they?”

As expected, Hong Jin caught on at once. He clicked his tongue in sympathy while Li Feng whipped his head around to stare at the young prodigies.

His gaze seemed to say that he intended to remember exactly who had provoked Martial Uncle Cheongpung.

“Gasp.”

“A-Assistant Military Commissioner! No, Great Hero Li! That isn’t what happened…”

Li Feng was both a powerful figure in the Shanxi Province military and a lay disciple of Huashan.

The government and Murim might supposedly stay out of each other’s affairs, but if they got on his bad side, the future of the Five Gates of Shanxi would become very unpleasant indeed.

Leaving them to babble excuses, Li Feng turned to me.

“Could you tell me exactly what happened?”

“It’s already over. The man involved laid into them himself. What more is there to say?”

“Laid into them?”

“I mean he beat them up. They apologized first, of course.”

“Martial Uncle did so himself? Hmm.”

Strictly speaking, Cheongpung had only dealt with one of them, but he had beaten someone.

Li Feng regarded the young prodigies with a considerably softer gaze.

“Do you understand what you did wrong?”

“Yes, sir!”

“We feel it in our bones!”

Their booming replies came at once. Li Feng’s position was intimidating enough, but now they also knew who Cheongpung was. No wonder they were sweating bullets.

Never mind that he was a Peak master. He was the Sword Saint’s disciple and the direct-line disciple of Huashan.

*They’re completely fucked.*

The Five Gates of Shanxi were ultimately just a collection of minor and mid-sized sects. If Huashan, one of the Nine Sects and One Gang, got angry, even their extended families might get beaten with a stick.

No, they would be lucky if it ended there.

“The conversations and events that took place here today…”

Li Feng’s grave voice had barely begun before answers came flying back.

“We’ll keep silent!”

“We’ll take it to our graves!”

“I’ve already forgotten everything!”

“Where am I? Who am I?”

*What a fucking performance.*

As I watched an epidemic of amnesia break out before my eyes, I added a word of my own.

“Is that enough?”

“Y-Yes?”

“What do you mean…?”

“What do you think I mean? If you want to be part of the family, put your spoon on the table too. You there. What does ‘family’ mean?”

The young prodigy I pointed at stammered out an answer.

“People who eat together… Unless that’s wrong. Then I’m sorry.”

“That’s right. So, how do you become family?”

“Ah!”

The young prodigy cried out and slapped his forehead.

At least this one had some sense.

“Then I’ll arrange somewhere nice for our next meeting. How does Honghwaru sound?”

“……”

What a complete blockhead.

With heirs like these, I didn’t even need to look to know the state of the Five Gates of Shanxi. I heaved a deep sigh and spelled it out for him.

“Forget Honghwaru. I’m telling you to do what our side asks. For example, if something comes up involving the Seongun Escort Bureau. Understand?”

“Ohhh.”

The Jin Family of Taiyuan had undoubtedly become the foremost power in Shanxi, but if the minor sects banded together in opposition, they could still make things troublesome.

We belonged to the orthodox faction, after all. If we seized whatever we wanted and punished anyone who objected like a band of mounted bandits, people would point fingers.

In Murim, wasn’t legitimacy every bit as important as martial arts?

*But there won’t be a problem if no one’s left to point fingers.*

I slowly surveyed the four men and women before me. They were the heirs of the other four sects among the Five Gates of Shanxi, excluding the Seongun Escort Bureau.

If they took the lead in supporting the Jin Family of Taiyuan, everything would become much easier.

“When it’s time to eat, pay attention to where you sit. That way, you’ll at least get a bite.”

The Seongun Escort Bureau was considered one of the foremost powers in an entire province, even if that province was on the frontier. It was a feast grand enough for everyone to share.

At my words, the quick-witted one’s eyes gleamed, while the clueless one cautiously spoke up.

“Even so, that might be a little…”

I cut the dim-looking bastard off without hesitation.

“What sect are you from?”

“M-Me?”

“Yes, you.”

After a long pause, he finally coughed up the name Gopyeong Sect.

I vaguely recalled hearing it before, but it was some obscure sect I could barely remember. That was the extent of Gopyeong Sect’s standing.

No, the same was true of every sect in the new Five Gates of Shanxi.

“Gopyeong Sect. Gopyeong Sect… Doesn’t have much of a ring to it. Want me to suggest a new name?”

“Excuse me?”

I stared into the eyes of the bastard who still hadn’t caught on.

“Starting next month, you can make a fresh start under a new name. The Gopyeong Branch of the Jin Family of Taiyuan. How does that sound?”

“……!”

“……!”

“You don’t seem to like it. I was only joking, you idiot.”

Of course, I wasn’t joking.

I patted the deathly pale Young Sect Leader of Gopyeong Sect on the shoulder, then swept my gaze across the room.

“Is anyone here sworn brothers or sisters with Woo Jintae?”

“N-No, sir.”

“Maybe you’ve been close since childhood? Known each other for over ten years? Betrothed before birth? There are plenty of possibilities.”

“Absolutely not! Never! We’ve only spent time together a few times.”

“I-I only received a few bolts of silk and some jewelry…”

“Then that settles it.”

*Clap!*

The sharp crack of my hands coming together made all four of them flinch.

“Choose. Are you going to make enemies of the Jin Family of Taiyuan, Huashan, and the government, or…”

I grinned and enunciated every word.

“Are you going to abandon someone you barely know and share the feast with us?”

*Clap, clap, clap, clap.*

This time, it wasn’t me. Hong Jin was cackling and applauding.

“Our Young Master Jin—I like you more and more every time I see you.”

“……”

*Please refrain from making such dangerous remarks, brother.*

* * *

“Are you leaving already?”

His tone was still arrogant, but his voice and eyes brimmed with disappointment.

*The more I see this kid, the cuter he gets.*

I reached out to pat my little fanboy—no, Prince Shangshan Zhu Bao—on the head, only to lower my hand when I saw Li Feng’s expression.

*Oh, right. He’s a king. And a member of the imperial family.*

“Ahem. I have urgent business to attend to.”

“Can you not put it off?”

“I’m sorry, but every moment counts.”

“I see…”

The sight of the dejected kid almost made me feel guilty—like hell it did. I wanted to hurry home and get some proper rest.

Right on cue, Hong Jin cut in with his lilting voice.

“Your Highness, you have me, so please let Young Master Jin go now. All right?”

But Zhu Bao merely stared at me, his expression stubborn.

“Then when will I see you again?”

“Hmm, I don’t know. After a thousand nights?”

“A thousand nights!”

Zhu Bao cried out in shock.

That was roughly three years. To a child of only ten, it must have sounded like an eternity.

“A-Are you truly that busy?”

“There are such things as adult circumstances, Your Highness.”

“Good heavens. Even my late father was never that busy…”

He looked utterly crushed. Zhu Bao’s head drooped, only to shoot back up at Li Feng’s next words.

“Your Highness, how about this?”

“What is it?”

“I hear the Jin Family of Taiyuan will be holding a grand banquet in fifteen days. Why not visit the Jin Family of Taiyuan in person?”

“The Jin Family of Taiyuan?”

“Yes. All the renowned masters of Shanxi will be gathering there. I’m certain you’ll enjoy yourself.”

Zhu Bao’s eyes sparkled.

“Of course! There was such a way!”

“Yes, Your Highness.”

“……”

What the hell were these people talking about?

They hadn’t even been invited, yet here they were letting their imaginations run wild. But it felt as though telling him not to come would cause an incident, so I had no choice but to nod.

“Great Hero Li is right. Come visit us then.”

“Would that truly be all right?”

A little late to ask, wasn’t it?

I put on my best customer-service smile.

“Of course. My brothers will be glad to see you too.”

“I-Is that true?”

“You know who my brothers are, don’t you? You’ve already met my second brother.”

“The Heaven Shaking Sword?”

A dark cloud passed over Zhu Bao’s bright face.

“Your second brother dislikes me. Three years ago, he ate his meal and left without saying a word. He was rude as well.”

“He didn’t say anything?”

“I do not wish to discuss that day any further.”

Hong Jin whispered under his breath.

“His Highness asked for an autograph, but he refused him flatly.”

Jin Mukyung had said he’d been invited three years ago, which meant…

Zhu Bao had been seven at the time.

Good grief. How could anyone flatly refuse a seven-year-old asking for an autograph—especially when that seven-year-old was a king?

*That man really is something else.*

In a way, it was very Jin Mukyung.

Remembering the day his innocent admiration had been mercilessly crushed, Zhu Bao silently fidgeted with his fingers.

Watching him, I felt a pang of sympathy.

“If you come this time, I’ll ask him to give you an autograph.”

“Really?”

“Pinky promise. Seal it.”

I hooked pinkies with the bewildered boy and even stamped our thumbs together.

“What is this?”

“It means I swear before all the gods of heaven and earth. Something like that.”

“Oh!”

Everyone around him made a fuss about him being royalty and a king, but a child was still a child.

Everyone smiled fondly at the sight of Zhu Bao beside himself with delight.

“It has been a long time since I saw His Highness so happy.”

“I know. After spending every day with this boring Assistant Military Commissioner, he’s finally smiling again.”

“I’ve done nothing but my best.”

“Doing your best doesn’t always produce the best results. It happens.”

“Deputy Military Commissioner!”

“What is it, Assistant Military Commissioner?”

Li Feng and Hong Jin.

As they bickered, I couldn’t tell whether they were close friends or bitter enemies.

Meanwhile, Cheongpung approached Zhu Bao with anticipation written all over his face.

“Would you like my autograph too?”

“……”

“You’ve never given anyone an autograph before, have you?”

“Gasp. How did you know? I’ve signed with a hand seal while working as a porter, but I’ve never given an autograph.”

“Wouldn’t it be stranger if I didn’t know?”

Just look at that expectant face. He was dying to give someone his first-ever autograph.

“C-Can’t I give him one?”

“Sure. Do whatever you want. His Highness will probably be pleased.”

But Zhu Bao’s response was unexpected.

“An autograph? From you?”

“Yes! I really want to give you one!”

“No.”

“W-Why not? They say my grandfather is very famous. Haven’t you heard of the Sword Saint?”

“I have. Of course I have. But…”

Zhu Bao assumed a stern expression and shook his head.

“You do not have a martial title yet.”

“Excuse me?”

“Come back once you have acquired a suitably impressive title. Then I shall be sure to ask for your autograph.”

“……”

“……”

*So autographs were only for named characters.*
