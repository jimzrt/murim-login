# Chapter 90

“A week off?”

“Yes. Exactly as you heard.”

Team Leader Choi’s call that evening came as a surprise.

A whole week off? Had something happened to the Guild?

That thought reminded me of something troubling.

“Uh, this isn’t because of some problem with Sangdong Guild, is it?”

“Sangdong Guild?”

“You know, because of the whole Im Changsoo thing…”

“Ah, you don’t need to worry about that. The Guild house renovations will be finished in a week.”

It was a relief to hear nothing had happened, but did Guilds really stop raiding because their Guild house was being renovated?

*Still, a whole week off sounds nice.*

For the past seven years, I’d done nothing but push forward without rest. Lately, I’d been traveling back and forth between Murim and reality, barely catching my breath.

Honestly, I wanted to re—no. Times like this called for working even harder. My voice rang with determination.

“Team Leader. I want to work.”

“You’ll still receive your full salary during your vacation.”

“Then I’ll see you in a week.”

“……”

“I’m hanging up. I have to eat dinner.”

“…All right.”

As soon as I ended the call, Hayeon hurried over and bowed politely.

“Oppa. Your evening meal is prepared.”

“……Could you stop talking like that? It’s giving me goose bumps.”

“Switching to casual mode requires a paid upgrade.”

“You sure found a complicated way to ask for allowance.”

I held out two fifty-thousand-won bills, and Hayeon grinned.

“Mom says dinner’s ready.”

“Oh, what’s on the menu?”

“Beef bulgogi. And bean sprout soup that I made.”

“Beef bulgogi sounds good.”

“Mom said my bean sprout soup was good. She praised me.”

“Mom’s homemade beef bulgogi never disappoints. It’s always fresh, always the best, always thrilling.”

“……”

My thoroughly pissed-off little sister and a table laden with Mom’s cooking.

Who needed a fancy vacation? I’d just eat and sleep to my heart’s content at home.

* * *

Tap.

Choi Minwoo set down his smartphone. Butler Kim, seated across the table from him, asked, “What did he say?”

“He was already worried about Sangdong Guild.”

“The more I see of him, the more interesting that young man becomes. He seems to act without thinking, yet he’s quick to grasp the situation.”

Choi Minwoo drummed his long fingers on the table.

Even now, some time after Jin Taekyung’s sudden appearance, the man remained a mystery.

When they had first met, he had seemed like nothing more or less than a diligent F-rank Hunter. Yet yesterday, he had cleared an entire B-rank Gate single-handedly.

*He’s getting stronger.*

As of yesterday, Minwoo’s suspicions had become certainty.

Jin Taekyung was growing stronger—and at an astonishing rate.

“Is there any new information on Jin Taekyung?”

“No. I’ve checked repeatedly, but nothing has turned up.”

Butler Kim was meticulous in his work, but this case was different. Choi Minwoo chose his words carefully.

“Butler Kim.”

“Yes, Young Master.”

“Could you perhaps look into cases of Hunters awakening a third time?”

“What?”

Butler Kim’s eyebrows twitched.

A third awakening? He had never heard of such a thing in all his life. If a Hunter like that had ever existed, no one would use the word *reawakening*.

It was called reawakening precisely because there was no stage beyond it.

“Young Master, that would…”

As Butler Kim prepared to explain the difficulty, a name flashed through his mind.

*Jin Taekyung. If it’s that young man, who knows?*

Butler Kim was living proof of the Great Cataclysm, having experienced it firsthand. He had distinguished himself brilliantly and fought in countless battles.

Yet even in his eyes, Jin Taekyung was exceptional.

*Those movements… They were truly remarkable.*

At times powerful, at others fluid. He shifted efficiently between offense and defense, possessing an innate combat sense for when to advance and when to retreat.

Watching Jin Taekyung fight, Butler Kim could think of only one word: overwhelming.

*A third awakening…*

The investigation would take time, but it was worth pursuing.

Butler Kim bowed his head.

“I’ll look into it.”

“Thank you. Oh, and what is Sangdong Guild up to?”

“They’ve sent out watchers. They’ll probably have most of the information within a few days.”

It had been less than half a day since they learned that Im Changsoo had been summoned to the Guild Master’s office and beaten like a dog, yet Sangdong Guild’s watchers were already on the move. Their response was faster than expected.

“Their target must be Jin Taekyung.”

“They’ve likely marked him as a key figure, though they’ll begin by thoroughly investigating the entire Guild.”

“What about the other Guild members?”

“I warned them that they might be followed. But…”

Butler Kim hesitated, then added, “Shouldn’t we warn Jin Taekyung as well?”

“It’s fine. In fact, I hope Sangdong Guild’s intelligence network is far superior to ours.”

The hardest person to understand was also the person whose life had been laid bare most thoroughly.

It was like staring into a crystal-clear spring and still being unable to see what lay within.

Even if it meant using Sangdong Guild’s power, Choi Minwoo wanted to get closer to the truth about Jin Taekyung.

“More importantly, Sangdong Guild’s Master must be furious.”

“He seems to have grown much more cautious. In the old days, he would have stormed in and caused a scene by now.”

“Wait. Could it be…?”

Butler Kim smiled at Choi Minwoo’s reaction and nodded.

“I’ve met Sangdong Guild’s Master before.”

“Was it a bad encounter?”

“Who knows?”

Butler Kim’s smile deepened.

* * *

“What can I do for you?”

The real estate agent’s forehead gleamed under the lights.

“I’d like to look at a house.”

“Are you looking to rent monthly? A jeonse lease? Or perhaps…”

“I’m buying.”

“Well, look at you, young Boss. Sorry, but give me just a minute. I need to take care of something, and it’s too urgent to hold in.”

I wondered what he meant by *something* until I noticed the toilet paper in his hand.

If that was the issue, he definitely needed to hurry. When I nodded, the real estate agent dashed into the bathroom.

“Have a snack while you wait on the sofa. There’s mocha bread on the table. Gnnngh…”

*Prrt. Prrrt.*

“……”

I’d never be able to eat mocha bread again.

Lamenting inwardly, I leaned back against the sofa. A documentary about the Great Cataclysm was playing on the television.

> “Aaaah!”
>
> “Crash! Boom!”
>
> “This is an emergency bulletin. Unidentified phenomena are currently occurring across the country. In response, the government has declared martial law effective immediately…”

People scattered, screaming. Buildings collapsed, flames soared, and news reports announcing the beginning of the Great Cataclysm flashed by one after another. Then the haggard face of the president of the United States filled the screen.

> “We have yet to determine their identity, but one thing is certain: they are our enemies. Not only the enemies of the United States, but of the entire world and all humanity. Even now, countless monsters are passing through Gates and invading Earth.”

Gate.

The word meant exactly what it sounded like: a door. The Demon King Asmodeus had opened that door from beyond another dimension and descended upon Earth with a countless horde of monsters.

*After that, things went exactly as the textbooks said.*

Humanity was helpless. Gates appeared in cities, farming villages, mountains, seas, jungles… They opened regardless of time or place, and the monsters pouring through them spread death and destruction.

By the end of the hellish period known as the Bloody Week, which began when the Gates first opened, casualties had reached the tens of millions. The property damage was too vast to calculate accurately.

> The Great Cataclysm: The Most Horrific Ten Years in Human History.

Around the time the documentary reached its midpoint, the bathroom door flew open.

“Whew. Now I feel alive.”

The real estate agent wiped his sweat-slick forehead and dropped heavily into his seat.

“Sorry to keep you waiting. You said you were looking to buy, right?”

“Yes.”

“Did you stop by anywhere else before coming here? There must’ve been plenty of real estate offices along the way.”

“No, this is my first stop.”

“Is that so?”

Judging by the way his eyes darted around, he was debating whether to take me for a sucker. Pretending not to notice, I held out the note I’d prepared.

The address was written on it.

“I’d prefer to see a property around this address, if possible.”

“This address… That’s in a safe zone.”

“Yes.”

“You definitely said you wanted to buy, right? Did I mishear you because I had to take a dump so badly?”

I nodded, and his eyes subtly traveled up and down my body.

Jeans and a white T-shirt. Twenty-thousand-won sneakers bought at a market or online. No matter how you looked at me, I wasn’t dressed like a man with money.

“What do you do for a living?”

“I raid Gates.”

“Oh, a Hunter? I thought so. You’ve done well for yourself at such a young age.”

A broad smile spread across his face. Hunters were among the best-paid professionals around. It wasn’t unusual for young, successful Hunters to buy expensive homes and cars.

His tone immediately became friendlier.

“Have you checked the market price?”

“I looked it up online on the way here.”

“Then you’re in luck. There happen to be a few listings available. Let’s see…”

Eager to close a sale, the agent began making call after call.

He spent about five minutes hanging up and dialing again before putting away his smartphone and turning to me.

“I found a listing that’s perfect for you, Boss. If you’re not busy, how about we go see it now?”

“Sure. Why not?”

I was on vacation, so I had no reason to refuse. As I rose, the man beamed.

* * *

Vroom.

Sitting in the passenger seat, I watched the scenery pass by. Detached houses stood in rows, with shops here and there, along with playgrounds and schools.

“It’s changed a lot…”

The agent glanced sideways at me.

“Did you used to live around here?”

“When I was a kid.”

I’d been sixteen, in my third year of middle school. That was exactly eleven years ago. Since I’d been born and raised here, I supposed this was my hometown.

“This area was redeveloped about ten years ago, so it must look completely different. It used to be an old apartment complex, but once they heard an Association branch was being built thirty minutes away, they tore the whole place down.”

“I see.”

I already knew. Housing prices had skyrocketed as soon as news of the redevelopment broke. The jeonse deposit had risen by hundreds of millions of won, far more than we could afford, so we decided to move.

*It wasn’t long after Dad died.*

The night before we moved, I saw Mom crying in silence.

It wasn’t until years later that I learned the apartment had been my parents’ first home as newlyweds.

“We’re here.”

The agent’s voice pulled me from my thoughts. I opened the door and stepped out to find a detached house with a yard.

“This is the address you gave me, Boss. The owner happens to be out, so let’s have a quick look and be on our way.”

“Ah, hold on a moment.”

Maybe it was nostalgia. The old apartment complex was long gone, yet something about the area still felt familiar.

*Still… I’m glad it hasn’t completely changed.*

Even after the redevelopment, traces of the old neighborhood remained. The agent clicked his tongue as he watched me look around, lost in memories.

“You must be enjoying yourself, coming back after all this time. Why don’t you take a walk around the neighborhood while you’re here?”

“Is that okay?”

“You’re going to sign the contract, aren’t you?”

“No. I mean, yes.”

I had to buy this house, no matter what.

The agent chuckled and took out a cigarette.

“Then I ought to do you a favor. A walk around the neighborhood won’t take long. I’ll stay here and have a smoke, so take your time.”

After briefly thanking him, I set off at a leisurely pace.

*Is this the right way?*

I passed through an alley and spotted the supermarket I’d often visited as a child.

“This used to be my hangout when I was a kid. Back when Oppa smoked like crazy in middle school, the old lady here was so old that…”

A gleaming foreign car was parked outside the supermarket. A man and a woman had been talking beside it, but they stopped when they saw me.

No—the man was the one who stopped.

He tilted his head, approached me, and asked, “Do you know me?”
