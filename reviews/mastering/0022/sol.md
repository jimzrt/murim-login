# Chapter 22

The smell of mold hit me the moment I stepped into the room. The place reminded me of a military barracks, and the members of the reconnaissance squad were waiting inside.

*Ten people.*

I sharpened my senses and looked them over one by one.

Each time my gaze passed over an unfamiliar face, a Level display popped up.

*Level 14. Level 15. Level 14…*

Most of them were around the same level. Then I reached the ninth man—

> **Lv. 22 Hyuk Mujin**

The number jumped sharply. The face was familiar, too.

*Hyuk Mujin?*

It was the same Hyuk Mujin who had picked a fight with me when I first arrived at the Jin Family of Taiyuan a few days ago. When our eyes met, he curled his lips into a smirk.

“What a pleasure to see you again, Third. Young. Master.”

I stared at his openly hostile smile.

“From now on, call me Squad Leader.”

“…As you wish.”

Ignoring Hyuk Mujin’s piercing stare, I turned to the tenth member of the reconnaissance squad. The young man who had been beaming ever since I walked in shot to his feet.

“Young Master—no, Squad Leader! I look forward to working with you.”

> **Lv. 13 Han Yeop**

It seemed some assignments had been changed because of the war. The White Tiger Hall clerk I had met earlier said the reconnaissance squad was made up of martial artists drawn from several different units.

*Hyuk Mujin originally belonged to the Gate Watch Office, too.*

Maybe that was why the atmosphere in the room felt so unsettled.

The squad members watched me with expressions that mixed the awkwardness of strangers with anxiety and anticipation about the war.

I spoke first.

“I’m Jin Taekyung, the newly appointed leader of White Tiger Hall’s reconnaissance squad. I look forward to working with you.”

Clap, clap, clap.

The lonely applause died after only a few seconds, and Han Yeop lowered his hands, looking embarrassed.

The atmosphere was stiffer than I had expected.

*But that isn’t necessarily a bad thing.*

We were at war. Better to keep them on edge than have everyone laughing and trying to make friends.

Of course, too much tension could do more harm than good. Easing it at the right moment was one of my responsibilities as squad leader.

*That much, I’m used to.*

Newbie Hunters froze the moment they entered a Gate for the first time. Real combat was nothing like practice. Everything they had learned at the Hunter training center flew straight out of their heads, and the primal scent of death overwhelmed them.

That was why Guild veterans helped the rookies keep their heads. I had been one of them.

*Though I only handled F-ranks.*

Their abilities were similar, too. From what I could tell, a second-rate martial artist in Murim fell somewhere between an E-rank and an F-rank.

In other words, I had become the leader of a ten-person F-rank party. I had never actually been a party leader before, but I had watched other people do the job until I was sick of it.

“Raise your hand if you have combat experience.”

At the abrupt question, every member of the reconnaissance squad raised a hand. They all looked bewildered.

“Keep it raised if you’ve fought at least five times.”

Half the hands went down. Han Yeop’s was among them.

Five people with experience in at least five battles. That wasn’t bad. In fact, it was better than expected.

But the most important question remained.

“Keep it raised if you’ve killed someone.”

Four hands slowly fell. I looked at the only member of the reconnaissance squad whose hand was still raised.

> **Lv. 22 Hyuk Mujin**

“How many?”

He snorted.

“Five. During last year’s bandit suppression campaign. One of them was a deputy stronghold chief, and he was quite a strong bastard—”

I cut him off.

“Good. You’re the deputy squad leader from now on.”

Hyuk Mujin’s mouth snapped shut mid-boast.

“Deputy squad leader?”

“Yeah. Speak up now if you don’t want the job.”

With nothing but rookies gathered here, experience mattered more than anything.

Hyuk Mujin was the only one who could swing a weapon at the enemy without hesitation.

*Black cat, white cat.*

White, black, or downright obnoxious—it didn’t matter as long as the cat caught mice.

Hyuk Mujin considered it with a complicated look before answering.

“…Hmph. Since it’s an order, I suppose it can’t be helped.”

He sure had a difficult way of saying he wanted to be deputy squad leader.

“Then Hyuk Mujin is the deputy squad leader. From now on, we’ll call you Number One.”

“Number One? What’s that supposed to mean?”

“It’s your number. From now on, everyone in the reconnaissance squad goes by a number instead of a name. Hyuk Mujin is Number One. Next, you sitting over there. Yes, you’re Number Two.”

I pointed them out one by one until I had assigned Numbers One through Ten.

Hyuk Mujin frowned.

“Why are we doing that?”

“Because it’s easier. We could end up fighting today. Do you want me to spend all day memorizing names?”

“That’s—”

“Then do as you’re told. It’s an order.”

Hyuk Mujin glared at me, his face rigid, but I didn’t look away.

Part of me even hoped he would get cocky like he had that day. A battle could break out within the next few days, and I couldn’t have him acting like this when it did.

If he challenged me, I would have to show him the difference in our strength.

Clearly.

“…I’ll follow your orders.”

“What did you say?”

“Number One. I said I’m Number One.”

Hyuk Mujin’s voice trembled. He was more perceptive than I had expected. Maybe it was because of the rumors about the Sleeping Dragon of Shanxi.

What mattered was that he had accepted my authority.

Without letting anything show on my face, I continued.

“What I’m about to say may sound strange. But bear with it. That’s better than getting stabbed to death, isn’t it?”

The other squad members didn’t show their displeasure as openly as Hyuk Mujin, but they still looked at me uneasily.

Everyone except one.

“I’ll do whatever the Squad Leader says!”

Han Yeop shouted like a fangirl. The only difference was that he was holding a spear instead of an idol light stick.

*Oh, right.*

I grinned at the reconnaissance squad.

“Now, raise your hand if you use a sword.”

The key to party hunting was assigning positions.

* * *

Raid strategies in the real world had been standardized into manuals long ago.

Three tanks. Four damage dealers. Two mages and one healer. For a ten-person party, that was the ideal combination.

*Of course, there are no mages or healers here.*

I had to balance the party as best I could using only tanks and damage dealers, but—

“…You’re telling me no one knows how to use a shield?”

My voice trembled at the shocking result. Good lord, all ten of them were damage dealers. Nine used swords, and Han Yeop was the only one with a spear.

*What kind of horrifying single-species party is this?*

A hybrid would be better. At least then there would be some variety.

Hyuk Mujin spoke as though he couldn’t understand what the problem was.

“A man ought to wield a sword.”

Seeing the others nod along left me speechless.

*They’ve had it way too easy. Way too easy.*

Let them stick their heads into a pool of blood and see if they could still say that.

Sword? Spear? There was no such distinction. You bashed people with whatever rock you happened to grab, threw dirt in their eyes, climbed on top of them, and bit them with your teeth.

When death was on the line, anything within reach became a weapon and a lifeline.

These people had only ever fought bandits at best. They still didn’t understand that.

*Do I need to start training them tomorrow?*

Not for their sake. For my survival.

Even teaching them a few tricks would make a real difference in a melee.

*I worked like a dog for seven years. I can’t die because of a bunch of rookies.*

That was when it happened.

Ding. Ding. Ding.

A great bell tolled three times.

Here, where no one had a way to know the exact time, bells were rung at set intervals throughout the day. The three tolls marked Mi-si, roughly one to three in the afternoon.[^1]

And—

“Get ready. This is our first deployment.”

They also signaled the reconnaissance squad’s first mission.

* * *

“He should be doing fine.”

Jin Wikyung looked up at Wipeng’s abrupt remark. Until a moment ago, he had been staring blankly at his cooling tea.

“What are you talking about?”

“The Third Young Master.”

A full day had passed since the reconnaissance squad led by Jin Taekyung left the Jin Family of Taiyuan. Their mission was to scout one of the county towns near the family estate.

“They left around noon yesterday, so they should arrive within two days.”

“Ah. Taekyung.”

Jin Wikyung let out a weak laugh. He looked exhausted.

“I was wondering what you meant. Never mind.”

“Never mind?”

“How long are you going to treat him like a child? He’s a grown man now. I’m sure he’ll manage on his own.”

“…I’m beginning to doubt my ears.”

“I did coddle him quite a bit. He was very young back then.”

“I agree to some extent. He’s changed considerably over the past few days.”

“Heroes grow by overcoming adversity.”

“…”

“Anyway, I can stop worrying about the youngest now. I’ll be able to focus more on the family.”

“You should rest for a while. You look tired.”

“Wipeng. Members of our family have died.”

Wipeng fell silent at the sorrow and determination in his voice, and Jin Wikyung returned to his work.

But the silence lasted only two hours.

“What is that…?”

A black speck in the sky was drawing closer. The messenger hawk spread its enormous wings and landed on the office windowsill.

It belonged to the Lower District Sect.

Jin Wikyung sprang to his feet and opened the tube fastened to the hawk’s leg. The moment he unfolded the letter, rows of tiny writing met his eyes.

> One Question, One Kill Jopil and twenty members of a special detachment have appeared in Jeongyang.

“Jeongyang…!”

Beyond Jeongyang lay Honju. Beyond Honju lay Taiyuan. Even for a special detachment, he had never expected them to cover hundreds of li in only a few days.

That wasn’t all.

There were still members of the branch families who had yet to return. What if the enemy was tracking them?

*Every moment counts.*

It didn’t take Jin Wikyung long to decide.

“Select fifty martial artists immediately and send them to Jeongyang. One Question, One Kill Jopil is a vicious Peak master. Bring our family members—”

He thought of the children.

Their small limbs. Their faces twisted in pain. Their vacant eyes.

Jin Wikyung clenched his teeth.

“Bring our family members home safely.”

“My lord.”

Wipeng’s expression had hardened. Jin Wikyung stared blankly at him as though seized by a thought, then opened his mouth.

“Taekyung. Where did you say Taekyung went?”

He forced out the words.

“…Jeongyang.”

[^1]: Mi-si is one of the traditional two-hour divisions of the day, corresponding roughly to 1–3 p.m.
