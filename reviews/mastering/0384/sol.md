# Chapter 384

Mount Qingcheng.

A sacred Taoist site steeped in countless ages of history.

Anyone confronted by its majestic, overwhelming peaks would fall silent and stare in awe.

But my surprise came from something else entirely.

*They’re clearly different… but they look alike.*

Mount Qingcheng in the Murim, and Mount Qingcheng in the modern world of the twenty-first century.

The two worlds I had experienced were similar in many ways—the terrain, the language, even the way people looked and lived.

At one point, I had even wondered whether the Murim might be the distant past of the modern world.

*But it wasn’t.*

The butterfly effect? There was no way something I’d only ever seen in movies had actually happened.

The two worlds were certainly similar, but there were subtle differences between them, and their histories were different as well.

The Murim world was also smaller than the modern one. It wasn’t divided into five oceans and six continents.

People with colored eyes lived in foreign lands thousands of miles away, but that was all. At the center of that world lay a continent ruled by a vast and powerful nation.

Perhaps the Zhonghua the Chinese never tired of proclaiming was the Murim itself.

*But why did even this have to be similar?*

I had just returned from the Three-Sect Bloodbath, which had mercilessly drenched Sichuan in blood, only to find myself at Mount Qingcheng in Sichuan Province.

I didn’t know whether it was mere coincidence or some cursed connection.

I just hoped that part wouldn’t turn out to be similar too…

“Teacher Jin?”

“Mr. Jin Taekyung.”

“Huh?”

I lifted my head like someone waking from a dream.

Wei Penghu and Team Leader Choi had already left the business jet and were watching me with strange expressions.

“Sorry. I got distracted by the scenery for a moment.”

“You can see Mount Qingcheng’s beauty even in this darkness. You must possess tremendous mana, Teacher Jin.”

That wasn’t entirely wrong, but now that I had reached the Supreme Peak realm, I could see through most darkness with my eyesight alone, without using any internal energy.

When I nodded with a complicated expression, Wei Penghu exclaimed in admiration.

“Now I understand why Korea has kept you so carefully hidden. You are indeed worthy of being called an S-rank Hunter—the face of your nation.”

“What? My license still says I’m A-rank.”

“There is no need to hide it. Our country has already discerned the truth to some extent.”

Before I could say anything, Wei Penghu continued smoothly.

“To become an S-rank Hunter, one must attain enlightenment through constant mental discipline and arduous training. Our country endured countless trials and errors in cultivating the Hunters we have today… The fact that you reached such a realm at so young an age means you must have received the Korean government’s full support.”

“…”

“…”

“Ah, of course, Teacher Choi beside you is also an excellent Hunter. Having people like the two of you with us is most reassuring.”

*What was this nonsense about being reassured? Was he serving up a hearty bowl of gukbap or something?*

Team Leader Choi and I exchanged a brief glance and reached a silent agreement.

*Keep quiet.*

*Let’s just go.*

This misunderstanding had probably arisen because I had reached this point in such an absurdly unrealistic way, but there was no need for us to peel off the label they had already slapped on me.

Besides, trying to convince Wei Penghu here and now sounded like a pain.

“Well, I think there’s been a slight misunderstanding. I’ll explain it gradually when the time comes.”

“What misunderstanding? We both know how these things work.”

“…”

“…”

“The Chairman is already aware of the matter, so when you meet him, do not bother denying it. Simply accept it.”

*What the hell does he know?*

I gave a quiet laugh.

*Speaking of the Chairman…*

He was like a king, holding the population, economy, and military of the People’s Republic of China—a nation of more than a billion people—in one hand.

Even now that I had reached the Supreme Peak realm, to someone like me, with modern common sense rooted deep in my bones, he felt both close and infinitely distant.

*I wonder if I’ll get to see him once this is all over.*

Well, what did it matter? That was something to think about much later.

Even without my raid pay, my weekly salary was a whopping ten billion won. To me, he was just a generous employer.

I could save people and make money at the same time. Two birds with one stone.

“That’s what I’ll do. If I meet him.”

“Good. Then let us go meet him.”

“What?”

“Did I not tell you? He is waiting in the underground bunker right now.”

*What the hell was going on?*

I stared blankly at Wei Penghu’s retreating back, then hurried over to Team Leader Choi and whispered,

“D-Did you hear that?”

“Yes, I did. But even I find it rather unexpected. For the Chairman of China to leave the safety of Beijing and come all the way here… It seems the public’s assessment of him is at least somewhat accurate.”

“To hell with the public’s assessment. China’s Jongseok—Jongseok!”

“Not Jongseok! General Secretary! The Chairman of China!”

“Hmm? What did you just say?”

“Nothing, Minister of Defense.”

Wei Penghu had suddenly turned around, but Team Leader Choi politely covered for us. Then he looked at me with an earnest, dead-serious expression unlike anything I had ever seen from him before.

“Mr. Jin Taekyung. You cannot make a slip like that in front of the Chairman. Understood? Especially the Jongseok thing. Don’t even mention it. You make it sound like he’s an old high school classmate.”

“Huh? How did you know?”

“…”

I could have sworn Team Leader Choi had just muttered *fuck*.

Probably my imagination.

I took a deep breath and repeated the words in my head.

*Not Jongseok. General Secretary. China’s Chairman.*

Unlike Team Leader Choi, who had been born into the highest ranks of the aristocracy, I was a commoner down to my bones.

Whatever feelings I normally had toward China, the prospect of meeting the leader of one of the world’s ten greatest powers made my heart pound.

*Just don’t make a mistake. Especially not with Jongseok.*

Ten minutes later, under the gazes of the important figures gathered deep inside the underground bunker, I shook hands with the leader of the People’s Republic of China.

“It is a pleasure to meet you, Teacher Jin. This old man is Xiao Yang, Chairman of the People’s Republic of China.”

*Good. I won’t even have to utter the first syllable of Jongseok.*

Having cleared the first hurdle, I relaxed and opened my mouth.

“Welcome.”

“…”

“…”

*Oh, fuck.*

* * *

Chairman of the Chinese Communist Party’s Central Military Commission. General Secretary. And Chairman of a nation of more than a billion people.

Xiao Yang.

His voice was gentle as he addressed the people gathered before him, but there was strength in his eyes.

“As you all know, regrettably, I am neither a military expert nor a brilliant general. I am merely a schemer who entered politics at an early age and did not achieve his modest ambition until the age of seventy.”

It was a startling way for him to describe himself—so startling that it was difficult to believe those words had come from the leader of the nation with the fourth-largest territory and largest population on Earth.

*Was this what Team Leader Choi meant by the public’s assessment of him?*

I felt as though I had a rough idea of what kind of person he was.

Perhaps it was merely a mask he wore in front of others, or simple hypocrisy.

But at least from the old man speaking before all of us—including me—Chairman Xiao Yang of China, I sensed a kind of qi entirely different from either of those things.

“Please do your utmost. Save as many of our people as possible, and bring this terrible disaster to an end as quickly as you can. If you do, I will express fitting gratitude to both you and your countries, and I will long remember the aid you have given us.”

I knew nothing about the life that old man had led or the policies he had pursued.

But I wanted to give him considerable credit for reaching out to countries across the world in order to save his own people.

“That is all this old man has to say. I earnestly ask that you leave complicated matters such as politics aside and find the best way to end this crisis with the fewest possible casualties.”

The old politician, who had devoted his entire life to politics, turned toward one man.

“Minister of Defense Wei Penghu. My old friend.”

“Yes, Comrade Chairman.”

“Do you want full authority over the Central Military Commission?”

Wei Penghu hesitated briefly, then nodded gravely.

“Yes.”

“You would use that power well. But I refuse.”

“…Comrade Chairman?”

“Bring me the written order once this meeting ends. I will assume full authority over everything—and bear all the responsibility myself.”

For a moment, I wondered what had gotten into China’s Jongseok.

Then I understood. He was declaring his intention to shoulder everything himself.

Watching the scene, Team Leader Choi murmured beside me,

“He’s a good leader.”

I shook my head slightly.

“No. To me, Team Leader Choi, you’re the best.”

“Mr. Jin Taekyung…”

“So please raise my share of the Guild settlements.”

“Mr. Jin Taekyung…”

Same words. Completely different feeling.

Team Leader Choi looked at me as if to say, *Of course you’d say that, you bastard,* and shook his head.

That was when it happened.

“The Chairman is leaving.”

At the secretary’s announcement, everyone seated in the room rose.

It was the minimum courtesy owed to a head of state.

“I wish you all good fortune.”

The Chairman met each person’s eyes and addressed them in turn.

Naturally, I was no exception.

And as luck would have it, I was last.

“Teacher Jin.”

“…Yes.”

A faint smile touched the Chairman’s lips as he looked at me.

“I have very high hopes for you, Teacher Jin. Though this is a contract through which we each receive what we need, I hope you will put human life first under all circumstances.”

Was it my imagination, or was his farewell noticeably longer than everyone else’s?

Feeling every eye in the room on me, I nodded.

“I understand.”

“Please lend us your considerable strength.”

The Chairman began to turn away, only to stop midstep.

Then he tossed out one last remark.

“Welcome.”

“…”

“Then I shall take my leave.”

Once the high-ranking Chinese officials accompanying the Chairman had disappeared, I collapsed into my chair.

*Fuck.*

If I died, the cause would be humiliation.

Even if a monster killed me, I would make sure the official cause of death was listed as humiliation.

*No! Nooooooo!*

As I screamed inwardly in every direction, something pressed firmly down on my foot.

Obviously, it was Team Leader Choi, seated beside me.

“What?”

Team Leader Choi coughed quietly.

“Ahem.”

“What?”

“Ahem. People. People.”

“Oh.”

I looked around and finally realized that more than a dozen people of different genders and races were watching me inside the underground bunker.

Four of them stood out in particular.

*Those people are…*

A Chinese man and woman.

And two Western men, one wreathed in green light and the other in blue.

I could feel it merely by meeting their eyes—the immense power coiled within their bodies.

Rather than surprise, my first thought was that it made perfect sense. Anyone who knew who those four were would have felt the same.

*S-rank Hunters.*

People whose very existence made headlines. The pinnacle of the countless Hunters across the world.

Faces I had seen until I was sick of them on television and in advertisements were now right in front of me.

And now, one of them rose and extended a hand.

“Nice to meet you. I’m… Ah, do you perhaps not speak English? I can cast translation magic if you’d like.”

I hadn’t expected him to approach me first. Still dazed, I took his hand and replied,

“No, I’m fine.”

“Oh, listen to this man’s pronunciation. I’d believe you if you told me you were American.”

The middle-aged Black man was a giant well over two meters tall. His blue eyes gleamed as he asked,

“You seem to know who I am. Don’t you?”

How could I not?

I answered with even more excitement than I had felt when facing Chairman Xiao Yang.

“Of course, Magic Johnson.”

Only three people in the entire world had been granted the Title of Archmage.

And the Black man before me, Magic Johnson, was a War Mage—the most combat-oriented of those three Archmages.

*I’m actually talking to Magic Johnson. I guess you really do live to see all kinds of things.*

As I congratulated myself for coming here, the world’s greatest War Mage gave me a broad smile.

“Haha. I’m glad you recognized me. Actually, I’ve known about you for quite a while too.”

“M-Me?”

“Of course. Even my youngest daughter, who started elementary school this year, knows Sibeol-jwa.”

“…”

*Just how far has that damn nickname spread?*

What did people in the English-speaking world call Sibeol-jwa?

*Fuck Guy? Fuck Man?*

The thought of Magic Johnson’s youngest daughter knowing me by a name like that didn’t make me happy in the slightest.

And apparently, I wasn’t the only one who disliked it.

“What an utterly vulgar nickname. Though I suppose it suits a mere A-rank Hunter like you.”

A relatively young Chinese man, perhaps around thirty, stared at me with his arms folded.

“Wouldn’t you agree, peninsula bangzi?”[^1]

Team Leader Choi didn’t even have time to stop me.

My voice had already come out like an automatic response.

“What the fuck are you talking about, you mainland chink bastard?”

[^1]: *Bangzi* is a derogatory Chinese slur for Koreans.
