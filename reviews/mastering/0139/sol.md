# Chapter 139

Less than a shichen after the six-horse carriage carrying Jin Taekyung and his group departed, the streets of Taiyuan erupted into excitement once more.

This time, it was because of the fifty mounted soldiers surrounding a four-horse carriage and the martial artists whose eyes gleamed with intensity.

“That’s…”

“It’s the Jin Family of Taiyuan!”

“Woooooah!”

“First the Sleeping Dragon of Shanxi, and now the Jin Family of Taiyuan? What a feast for the eyes!”

Inside the carriage, Jin Wikyung’s ears pricked up at the cheers pouring in from every direction.

“Mukyung, did you hear that?”

Jin Mukyung yawned and nodded.

“Yes.”

“Wipeng, you too?”

Wipeng answered with an exasperated look.

“You don’t care whether I heard it or not. Just say what you want to say.”

“Why do you always speak like that? Does it make you feel better?”

“Feel better? I’m already about to die from pent-up frustration. So what did you want to say?”

“Those people just said Taekyung…”

“Wow. This is driving me insane.”

Jin Wikyung pretended not to hear Wipeng’s muttering and continued.

“It seems he’s on his way to attend a luncheon with the City Lord. He might even have arrived by now.”

“He probably has. It’s just a stone’s throw away.”

“You don’t think anything will happen, do you?”

“The Third Young Master isn’t some child left alone by the water. He’s probably being treated to such a lavish meal that the table legs are buckling. Stop worrying.”

“I don’t know. He’s such a free spirit.”

Wipeng’s eyes widened as if to ask what the hell that was supposed to mean.

“A free spirit? Isn’t he more reckless and out of control?”

“Ahem.”

“You could just say you’re worried he’ll cause trouble. Why dress it up like that…?”

“Shut your mouth.”

“Yes, my lord. I won’t say another word. If you’re really that worried, ask the Second Young Master over there.”

At Wipeng’s indifferent reply, Jin Wikyung’s gaze shifted slightly to the side.

In truth, Wipeng’s advice was sound. Jin Mukyung was the only one among them who had ever met the current City Lord of Shanxi.

*The problem is that he won’t tell us anything.*

Jin Mukyung had summed up his luncheon with the City Lord in three words—*It was awful*—and refused to say anything more.

“Mukyung, by any chance…”

The answer came before he could finish.

“There shouldn’t be any problems.”

Jin Wikyung let out a sigh of relief at the decisive answer, but Jin Mukyung added one more thing.

“As long as he has a strong stomach.”

“…A strong stomach? What do you mean?”

This was a banquet hosted by a member of the imperial family and the City Lord. Was he saying they served food crawling with maggots?

As Jin Wikyung stared at him in confusion, Jin Mukyung’s face slowly twisted.

“There’s someone there as revolting as a bug.”

* * *

The Shanxi Provincial Office, where the City Lord resided, had long since ceased to resemble an ordinary estate.

The Jin Family of Taiyuan and the Mount Heng Sword Sect, which I had visited not long ago, were both enormous, but they looked laughably small compared to this place.

*What am I supposed to call this? A fortress? Or a castle?*

I already knew everything on the continent was built on a massive scale, but this was beyond anything I had imagined.

Cheongpung, the other young prodigies, and I all gaped as we looked around. The official smiled faintly.

“What do you think?”

“It’s huge. Ridiculously huge.”

“It can easily accommodate several thousand people, so naturally it is. If we ever had to withstand a siege, we have enough grain to last ten years.”

The official pointed out several enormous warehouses in turn, adding that they were all filled with food.

“All of them?”

“Of course.”

He continued with the proud smile of a Gangnam apartment owner.

“Provincial offices are generally built large and sturdy in preparation for wartime, but they aren’t normally this large.”

“Then…?”

“Have you already forgotten who lives here?”

“Oh.”

Right. He wasn’t just the City Lord. He was a member of the imperial family and even held a formal royal title—a proper prince in his own right.

“Then is this a royal palace?”

“In a manner of speaking.”

We continued on, gawking like country bumpkins.

Everywhere we looked, servants and maidservants hurried about their work, while highly disciplined elite soldiers stood guard or sparred on the training grounds.

*They’re pretty good.*

The most surprising thing was how skilled the soldiers were.

Most of those standing guard were around Level 20, while the commanders clad in impressive armor were comfortably above First Rate.

*Well, it’s not as if there’s any reason soldiers shouldn’t learn martial arts when they’re readily available.*

If anything, it would make the army stronger. It was something they ought to encourage.

As we walked, I kept stealing glances at the training grounds.

*Boom! Boom! Boom!*

“Thrust!”

“Argh!”

It was noon, and the sun was already high overhead.

Hundreds of spearheads flashed in time with the drums as the soldiers gathered and scattered in perfect unison, moving as one.

Watching them train in formations, tactics, and strategy brought one phrase to mind.

*Raid team.*

Uniform weapons and coordination honed through systematic training. In a large-scale battle, that kind of teamwork would be terrifyingly effective.

*Their individual skills might be inferior to those of Murim martial artists.*

A fight between a few dozen on each side might be another matter, but if hundreds fought hundreds, or thousands fought thousands, an average Peak master couldn’t turn the tide alone.

That was the terrifying strength of a trained force.

*Make up for inferior quality with numbers and training. Is that it?*

It was exactly what one would expect from a unified empire that had ruled a vast territory for centuries.

Perhaps this was one reason those fiercely proud Murim martial artists acknowledged themselves as subjects of a Great Nation.

And yet…

*What the hell are they saving all this for? Are they going to trade it for candy?*

There were already several hundred soldiers in sight. If they had sent even a fraction of them to northern Shanxi, the Red Wind Band would have turned tail and fled long ago.

*Fuck. Some of us nearly died dealing with that mess.*

The sight of such useless public authority left me feeling suddenly empty.

What good was the finest sword under heaven if you never drew it? In its scabbard, it was no better than a club.

As I cursed inwardly, the official spoke.

“We’re nearly there.”

He was right. A massive iron gate came into view at the end of a long corridor lined with dozens of pillars, drawing anxious sighs from both sides.

“Phew…”

“Whew. What should I do, Young Hero Jeong? I’m so nervous.”

“Don’t worry. You have me.”

“…What a fucking load of bullshit.”

People who were meant to find love really did find it anywhere. I had no idea how those two had managed to hit it off in the middle of all this.

*Did getting beaten together make them fall for each other?*

*What kind of country is this?*

As a two-star general of the Singles Brigade, I couldn’t hide my displeasure. Beside me, Cheongpung walked with a spring in his step and asked brightly,

“Is the king in there?”

King.

At that single word, the young prodigies of the Five Gates of Shanxi gaped, while the official leading us leaped as though he had been stung by a bee.

“K-king? How dare you!”

“Huh? Isn’t he a king?”

“Of course he’s a king!”

“Then I was right, wasn’t I?”

“No, that’s not what I mean…!”

At this rate, one of two things would happen. Either the official would collapse from high blood pressure, or he would start shouting about treason and summon the hundreds of soldiers training nearby.

I wanted neither, so I stepped in.

“First, calm down. And Young Master Cheongpung.”

“Yes, Benefactor.”

“You can’t call him the king. You have to say ‘His Highness.’ Right?”

I directed the last question at the official. He glared at Cheongpung and nodded furiously.

“Absolutely! You must!”

“Oh, really?”

“Phew. Yes.”

Cheongpung blinked his clear eyes.

“Why?”

“…Young Master Jin. Do we really have to take this bastard—I mean, this person—with us?”

“I don’t mind leaving him behind. But are you sure you’ll be all right?”

The official fell silent.

If he brought only five people without Cheongpung, his superiors would reprimand him and he would lose his job. But if Cheongpung let his tongue slip even once, he might lose his head.

When he finally spoke again, he looked ten years older.

“…Let’s just go.”

Cheongpung beamed.

“Thank you. If there’s time, I’ll put in a good word with the king—I mean, His Highness.”

“Please, just keep that man’s mouth shut.”

While the official earnestly rattled off every precaution we needed to take, we finally reached the iron gate.

The gate was enormous and incredibly thick. Quiet voices drifted from behind it.

*Assistant Military Commissioner, Huashan, insult?*

Those fragments alone gave me no clue what they were discussing. Still…

*The atmosphere doesn’t sound too good.*

I had a feeling this wasn’t the best place to sit across from someone over a meal.

Just then, the man standing at attention before the gate shouted,

“The young prodigies of Shanxi Murim request an audience!”

*Groooan.*

The iron gate began to open.

The official gave me one final warning, glancing worriedly at Cheongpung.

“Just keep that man’s mouth shut.”

“…Ah. Yes.”

He must have been seriously worried.

* * *

The moment we entered, my eyes lit up.

The lavish grand hall was filled with all kinds of food and a long table like the ones I had only ever seen in movies set at magic schools.

The instant I saw the five people who had arrived ahead of us, one thought crossed my mind.

*We’re screwed.*

When it came to reading the room, I was second to none. After scraping by as an F-rank Hunter for so long, always watching everyone’s mood, I could size up an atmosphere in 0.1 seconds.

Like right now.

*What a wonderful atmosphere.*

The air around the five people was pulled taut. I had sensed something was wrong from outside, but it was even worse than I had expected.

It was fortunate everyone was empty-handed given the occasion. If they’d had anything hanging from their waists, swords would already have been drawn.

“Well… Now that our guests have arrived, shall we end this reunion here?”

A man’s delicate voice broke the tension.

Though was he really a man? He was slender enough to be mistaken for a woman, with a pale face and lips as red as though they had been painted.

He smiled at me.

“What a strikingly handsome young man, just as one would expect of a young prodigy of the martial world. I heard a young hero from the Jin Family of Taiyuan would be joining us today. Might that be you…?”

This was my cue to introduce myself. I gave the five men a fist-and-palm salute.

“My name is Jin Taekyung of the Jin Family of Taiyuan.”

The unpleasant atmosphere immediately eased. Surprise spread across the other four faces, covering the lingering traces of displeasure and ridicule.

“If you’re Jin Taekyung of the Jin Family of Taiyuan…”

A huge man in black martial robes muttered.

He had radiated tough-guy energy from the moment I saw him. His name was Li Feng, and the number 68 hovered above his head.

*Is he in the military?*

I couldn’t know everything from a first impression, but that was the feeling he gave me. A proud, upright soldier.

*Li Feng… Level 68 means he’s probably at the very top of First Rate.*

As I committed his name and Level to memory, the other three men reacted.

“Hm. That’s the Sleeping Dragon of Shanxi?”

“He’s young. No, he’s a child.”

“He doesn’t look as impressive as the rumors claim…”

Their curious gazes held surprise, a hint of jealousy, and a subtle sense of superiority.

In situations like this, there was usually no need to ask who the other person was. People like them were always desperate to brag about themselves.

“Apologies for the late introduction, Junior.”

A sharp-eyed man grinned at me. The other two stood with their arms folded, looking at me like I was a cute little chick.

*This is strangely irritating.*

Who the hell were they to start acting like my Seniors?

The answer came soon enough.

“Ah, you don’t know yet, do you? We’re from Shaanxi. The Zhongnan Sect of Shaanxi—have you heard of it?”

My mouth fell open before I knew it.

“Th-the Zhongnan Sect? *That* Zhongnan Sect?”

All three men broke into broad smiles.

“Haha! Look how surprised he is.”

“Exactly.”

“Do you know our sect well?”

I shouted, suddenly brimming with excitement.

“Of course! I know it very well! I had so much fun reading about it!”

“We’re glad to hear you know us… Wait. What do you mean, ‘reading about it’?”

“What else? Obviously, *The Reign…*”

I stopped halfway through my answer.

*Ah. This wasn’t a novel.*
