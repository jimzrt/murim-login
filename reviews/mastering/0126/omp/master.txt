# Chapter 126

Four days earlier, after completing his mission and returning to the Jin Family of Taiyuan, Wipeng bitterly regretted how efficiently he had handled everything.

*I should’ve come back a day later.*

But it was already too late. Jin Wikyung had received a messenger eagle from the Lower District Sect and was flying into a rage, his eyes practically rolling back in his head.

“Those goddamn mounted bandits dare!”

“What is it this time?”

“Pung Yang! The Red Wind Band! The Mount Heng Sword Sect! My brothers are in danger! Grave danger! We leave at once!”

“…I’ll assemble the guards.”

Now that every obstacle had been swept aside, Jin Wikyung’s authority was absolute. Before even half a shichen had passed, the two left the family grounds with fifty elite guards and raced onward without rest.

Two days later, they stopped at a Lower District Sect branch to change horses and received new information.

“What? Pung Yang is dead, and the Red Wind Band has been annihilated?”

“Yes! According to our sect’s intelligence, most of the enemy force—some three hundred men—was wiped out, and Young Master Jin Taekyung defeated Pung Yang himself.”

“Oh. Ohhh! Taekyung!”

Jin Wikyung’s triumphant laughter vanished without a trace at the informant’s next words.

“Tell me again. What happened to Mukyung?”

“W-well… he suffered considerable injuries during his life-and-death duel with Pung Yang. But his life isn’t in danger, and he’s recovering quickly, so there should be no need to worry.”

It was already too late. Jin Wikyung had probably heard nothing beyond *considerable injuries*.

When his brothers were children, even a splinter in one of their fingers had been enough to send him into a panic as if the finger had been severed.

*And these aren’t minor injuries, but considerable ones. This is going to be a disaster.*

Experience told Wipeng exactly what would happen next.

Sure enough, he was right.

“Mukyung is hovering between life and death?!”

The Lower District Sect member blinked at Jin Wikyung’s roar.

“P-pardon?”

“Pung Yang! You dare kill my little brother!”

First a considerable injury had become a brush with death, and now Mukyung was dead outright. Finally realizing what was happening, the informant hurriedly interjected.

“Lesser Family Head, I believe there has been a terrible misunderstanding…”

“I’ll tear your limbs to shreds and scatter them across the Nine Provinces!”

“…”

“…”

Jin Wikyung’s fury did not subside until another day had passed.

“Mukyung and Taekyung left the Mount Heng Sword Sect yesterday?”

“Yes. So please calm down already.”

“Are they both safe?”

“If they weren’t, they’d be carried here on a cart. Why would they be riding in a carriage?”

“Then…”

“You should see them around noon tomorrow.”

The thought that he could finally rest filled Wipeng with relief.

Who was he, after all? He was a Peak master known by the epithet Ghost Sword. He had the skill to secure an important position wherever he went, yet choosing the wrong lord had condemned him to this grueling labor.

*When was the last time I had a drink?*

At last, he could enjoy some roast duck and a warm cup of liquor.

A contented smile had just spread across Wipeng’s face when Jin Wikyung spoke.

“Good. Then let’s hurry and get ready.”

“Pardon? Get ready for what?”

“My brothers overcame countless hardships and successfully completed their mission. We ought to hold a welcoming ceremony.”

“…And what about me? Did I spend more than half a month touring the martial world for pleasure?”

“Hm? Who? Oh, you?”

Jin Wikyung blinked at Wipeng, then burst into hearty laughter.

“Of course that includes you! Surely you didn’t think I’d forgotten?”

*I was afraid of that. He definitely forgot.*

As Wipeng gaped at him in disbelief, Jin Wikyung continued.

“Ah, have your men buy some cloth from a nearby fabric shop. The largest piece they can find.”

“Cloth? Why do we suddenly need that?”

“I have something in mind.”

* * *

“…And that’s what happened.”

I glanced around as I listened to Wipeng, who looked ten years older than the last time I’d seen him.

We had arrived in Sakju two days after leaving the Mount Heng Sword Sect. The city was packed with an unexpected crowd, and an enormous white banner with black lettering fluttered above its entrance.

Congratulations on the safe return of Jin Mukyung, Jin Taekyung, and Hyuk Mujin!

—Everyone in the Jin Family of Taiyuan—

A groan escaped me.

“Oh, fuck me. What the hell is that…?”

I had never seen anything like it in my life.

The banner alone was more than twenty jang—over sixty meters—wide.[^1] Strung between the roofs of two pavilions facing each other across the broad avenue, it looked large enough to be visible from the Mount Heng Sword Sect.

*And look at that needlessly dramatic calligraphy.*

Even overbearing parents celebrating their child’s acceptance into a prestigious university wouldn’t go this far.

Jin Mukyung, Hyuk Mujin, and I stared up at the banner with our mouths hanging open as if we had planned it.

“Why is my name so small?”

I looked again to see what he meant. Sure enough, Hyuk Mujin’s name had been squeezed in at the end in tiny letters.

“Are you upset because the letters are small? I’d be thrilled if I were you.”

“It looks strange. You can barely see my name from down here.”

“Want to take my name out and put yours there instead? I mean it.”

Hyuk Mujin considered the offer for a moment.

“Now that I think about it, it seems fine as it is.”

“Then shut up.”

“Yes, sir.”

That was as far as our conversation got. The overbearing parent—no, Jin Wikyung—came charging toward us with the brightest smile in the world.

“You rascals!”

Was he a man or a brown bear?

The giant, who stood well over two meters tall, pulled Jin Mukyung and me against him with hands the size of pot lids. His grip was so powerful that I wouldn’t have been surprised if he crushed us on the spot.

“I’m so glad you’re safe. Truly!”

We had been safe.

Right up until Jin Wikyung hugged us with all his strength.

*Crack.*

“Guh!”

“Mukyung!”

…He didn’t look particularly safe anymore.

The assailant clutched his trembling victim and shouted, “Doctor! Get a doctor!”

“I think we really should. He looks like he’s in serious pain.”

Wipeng answered me wearily.

“Do you think this is the first time he’s done something like this? I knew it would happen, so I summoned one in advance.”

“Ohhh.”

For the first time, Wipeng seemed truly magnificent.

* * *

The three Jin brothers—including me—and Wipeng gathered together shortly after sunset.

When Jin Mukyung appeared wrapped in even thicker bandages, Jin Wikyung watched him apprehensively.

“Are you all right?”

“Would you be all right in his place, my lord? How could you treat someone who was already injured so roughly?”

“I thought I was being gentle…”

Jin Wikyung might not have been Shanxi’s greatest martial artist, but in terms of raw physical strength, he was Shanxi’s Number One.

I quietly slid my chair farther away as Jin Mukyung answered with a haggard expression.

“I’m fine.”

“…”

He looked anything but fine.

It was fortunate that Jin Mukyung was a Peak master. An ordinary civilian who had never learned a single martial art wouldn’t have been able to walk after that.

“I heard the Second Young Master had been injured, but I didn’t realize it was this severe. Even his Internal Injury hasn’t fully healed…”

“Did that Pung Yang bastard really do this to you?”

Jin Mukyung calmly nodded in response to their questions.

“He was strong. Stronger than I expected.”

And who was Jin Mukyung? A promising young prodigy who had drawn the attention of the entire realm. Through dazzling talent and relentless effort, he had reached the Peak realm at an early age—yet he had been defeated by a mere mounted-bandit leader.

“Was he truly that powerful?”

“Pung Yang… I’ve heard there are some formidable masters among the mounted bandits of Gaoyuan. But still…”

Their gazes suddenly turned toward me.

The silent pressure told me to stop stuffing myself with roast duck and say something.

I swallowed my mouthful of food.

“It’s true. You’ve heard what happened to the Great Hero known as the Tiger of Mount Heng, right? His arms and legs got wrecked too. He’s been getting around in a wheelchair lately.”

“What is a wheelchair?”

“Ah, a cart. A cart.”

Jin Wikyung tapped one thick finger against the table.

“A master of that caliber should have become famous long ago. Mukyung, could you have let your guard down?”

Jin Mukyung shook his head without hesitation.

“He caught me with a move I hadn’t anticipated, but that’s no excuse. Even if we fought again, the result would be the same.”

“…He was that strong?”

“He used Body-Protecting Qi. It was overwhelming.”

Jin Wikyung’s and Wipeng’s eyes widened simultaneously.

“Body-Protecting Qi!”

“Second Young Master, is that true?”

There was no need for him to answer. Jin Mukyung had no reason to tell such an obvious lie. Facing their shocked expressions, he continued.

“He was the strongest opponent I’ve ever fought. No—to be precise, I should say he *became* that strong.”

“Became?”

“What do you mean…?”

“The instant he swallowed a blood-red pill, he became terrifyingly powerful.”

At last, the conversation had reached the Temporary Strength Pill.

I tried to act as naturally as possible.

*I can’t let them find out I have it.*

The Temporary Strength Pill was a poisoned chalice. It was undeniably ominous and suspicious, but its tremendous effects couldn’t be ignored.

I had already decided to use it as the lesser evil in the worst possible situation—as a last resort when facing death.

“It was only for a moment, but when he went to take the pill, I clearly saw another one remaining in the wooden case…”

Jin Mukyung trailed off and looked at me.

“Did you happen to find anything on Pung Yang’s body afterward?”

“Hm? Like what?”

“A wooden case, perhaps. Or the red pill I mentioned.”

I deliberately furrowed my brow.

“I’m not sure. I searched him afterward to see if he had anything, but a bunch of wooden splinters spilled out. Maybe those were pieces of the case?”

“Then the pill? What happened to the pill?”

“No idea. I was half-dead myself. How was I supposed to search through everything?”

It was a fairly convincing excuse.

It wasn’t as though only one or two people had died, and the battle had been brutally fierce. It was only natural that I’d been too exhausted to search properly. What more could he say?

“Is that so?”

“Someone from the Mount Heng Sword Sect might have found it. Or it could’ve dissolved in one of the pools of blood lying everywhere.”

“Hmm.”

Jin Mukyung regarded me with faint suspicion, but I merely shrugged.

*You won’t find it no matter how hard you search, idiot.*

I had safely tucked it into a corner of the greatest vault in existence—my Inventory, which only I could open and close. Neither Jin Mukyung nor the greatest thief under heaven could touch so much as a hair on the Temporary Strength Pill.

*It really is convenient.*

While I marveled once more at the convenience of the System, Jin Wikyung and Wipeng began speculating about the pill’s origins.

“It must be a relic of demonic, heterodox arts. I recall hearing that many pills with similar effects were used during the Great Faction War.”

“Northern Shanxi, including Gaoyuan, once fell into the hands of the Demonic Cult. If Pung Yang discovered some remnant they left behind, it would make sense.”

I had been listening intently when I suddenly froze.

*Wait. The Demonic Cult?*

The Demonic Cult was a fixture no Murim novel could do without—the licorice in every medicine shop, the honorary firefighter to Geum Jandi.[^2]

Of course, it wasn’t some religious organization devoted to world peace and helping the poor. It was more like ISIS.

In short, they were a bunch of fanatics you had absolutely nothing to gain from getting involved with.

*What if the Demonic Cult made the Temporary Strength Pill?*

Pung Yang’s eyes had turned as red as those of a demon fresh from hell. The pill had granted him unimaginable power, if only temporarily.

*I’m starting to see the picture.*

This felt wrong. Really fucking wrong!

Still, nothing came without a price. Surely the side effects would be manageable enough—

“The best-known pill used by the Demonic Cult at the time was the Blood-Exploding Pill, if memory serves.”

“I’ve only heard stories about it. Don’t all the blood vessels in the user’s body burst after two shichen, killing them?”

“That was the price of gaining power through dark arts.”

“If the Blood-Exploding Pill was that terrible, just how severe would the side effects of Pung Yang’s pill be?”

“Who knows? But they must be beyond imagination. It would damage his innate qi, for one, and once the time limit expired, the strain on his body would be immense. Ultimately, such a pill burns the body itself as kindling to blaze brightly for a brief time.”

I swallowed hard. The question escaped me before I realized it.

“And after that?”

“It was made by the Demonic Cult. What do you expect? If demonic qi seeped all the way into the marrow… the user would become a murderous fiend who knew nothing but blood.”

“…A murderous fiend? Demonic qi in the marrow?”

“If an item like that fell into the hands of a villain, it would be a true disaster… Taekyung, what’s wrong?”

Jin Wikyung looked at me with concern. I wiped my forehead and found beads of sweat covering it.

“Nothing. I’m just a little hot.”

“What are you talking about? It’s snowing outside.”

“What would a Soeumin know? I’m a Taeyangin. That’s why…”[^3]

Damn it. I didn’t even know what I was saying anymore.

I gave the three of them an awkward smile.

“There’s something I forgot earlier.”

“…?”

“…?”

“…?”

“That pill. Now that I think about it, I have it. Heh. Heh-heh-heh.”

“…!”

“…!”

“…!”

[^1]: A jang is a traditional Korean unit of length measuring roughly three meters.

[^2]: Geum Jandi is the heroine of the Korean drama *Boys Over Flowers*.

[^3]: Soeumin and Taeyangin are two of the four constitutional types in traditional Korean Sasang medicine.
