# Chapter 72

“You died once today.”

His cold voice continued.

“Your limbs were cut off. You were subjected to Tendon-Splitting and Bone-Twisting. Your throat was cut. The intensity and nature of the pain may have differed, but you died. No question about it.”

As the relief of being alive faded, anger rushed in to fill its place.

I rose on trembling legs, swallowed the blood pooled in my mouth, and glared at Jin Mukyung.

*This fucking lunatic.*

I wanted to drive my fist into that smug face of his, but I held myself back. Not because I was weaker than Jin Mukyung, but because I knew he was right.

“…So? What are you trying to say?”

His answer came at once, as though he had been waiting for me to ask.

“That your days are numbered.”

“What?”

“You’re only half-finished. Neither a martial artist nor a wandering martial artist. Just an incomplete mess. A sloppy bastard like you is begging to die the moment he ventures into the Murim.”

Half-finished.

That might have been the most accurate description of my current state. I was both a Hunter and a martial artist.

“Sleeping Dragon of Shanxi? First Rate master? Even a passing dog would laugh. You’re nothing but a brawler. You’re sloppy for a martial artist, but you don’t fight as pragmatically as a wandering martial artist either. Don’t mistake surviving through sheer luck for skill.”

I barely managed to speak.

“Then what about Jopil? According to you, was that just luck too?”

“Jopil, One Question, One Kill? He was obviously an idiot who let his guard down in front of an enemy. You just happened to have one last move that could turn the tables.”

“…!”

“What? Think I guessed too accurately for someone who wasn’t there?”

Jin Mukyung clicked his tongue.

“Even a deaf old man knows the Third Young Master of the Jin Family of Taiyuan is a wastrel. Do you think Jopil didn’t? The moment he let his guard down, he was finished.”

*What is this bastard, a stalker?*

I felt like Sun Wukong trapped in the Buddha’s palm. His guess was that accurate.

“Let me make this clear.”

Jin Mukyung fixed me with a somber gaze.

“That luck of yours ends here.”

“…”

“The Murim is crawling with all kinds of monsters, and they won’t let their guard down like Jopil did. You’re no longer the Jin Family’s wastrel of a Third Young Master. You’re the Sleeping Dragon of Shanxi.”

Every word struck deep. Everything Jin Mukyung said was true, and it was time to face reality.

“Damn it.”

He was right. I was half-finished.

Thanks to the greatest stroke of luck in my life—the System—I had somehow survived this long. But it seemed that luck had brought me as far as it could.

*Still… I really am lucky.*

Not only had I discovered the problem early, but I had an excellent problem-solver standing right in front of me, ready to help fix it.

“Help me.”

Jin Mukyung.

A fully realized Peak martial artist and a genius of martial arts.

And—

“…Hyung.”

My older brother.

Ding.

* * *

Martial artists were a proud lot. Even a Third Rate wanderer with a rusty sword at his waist and cheap baijiu in his hand had his pride, so the arrogance of scions from prestigious sects could reach the heavens.

“Help me, hyung.”

In that sense, this guy had finally grown up. Three years ago, he would have run crying to his eldest brother… but he had matured. Far more than expected.

*He’s a strange one.*

Neither his personality nor his martial arts were easy to pin down. That was both a strength and a weakness. But one thing was certain: none of it would work against a *true master*.

*Still, his talent is real.*

Over the past three years at Heaven’s Gate Temple, Jin Mukyung had encountered countless prodigies, but none could match Jin Taekyung’s rate of growth.

*And he has strengths they don’t.*

He had the eye to grasp a martial art at a glance, excellent instincts in real combat, and the good sense to listen to other people’s advice.

*Even if he’s still a half-finished mess with everything jumbled together.*

Given time, his weaknesses would be filled in and his excesses smoothed out. When that happened, Jin Taekyung’s martial arts would be complete.

Like taiji brought into harmony.

*Taiji? Is that a little too grandiose?*

This was starting to feel like a lot of pressure.

But Jin Mukyung could not contain his curiosity. How would that bastard grow? How far would he climb?

*I’m going to be busy.*

He had to leave for Heaven’s Gate Temple within fifteen days at the latest. Jin Mukyung finally spoke.

“What are you doing? Pick up your spear.”

“Hyung!”

Seeing Jin Taekyung’s face light up, Jin Mukyung suddenly had a thought.

*When did this bastard start speaking informally to me?*

That was the moment the intensity of the training rose another level.

* * *

Bang.

Jin Wikyung stamped his seal on the final document, his eyes heavily bloodshot.

Nearly twenty hours of backbreaking labor had finally come to an end, but he felt no joy. By tomorrow morning, another mountain of work would be waiting for him.

*Are these things breeding behind my back?*

At least the end was finally coming into sight. That was some consolation.

After finishing his final review, Jin Wikyung rang a small bell. Before its clear chime had faded, two burly servants appeared.

“Did you call, Lesser Family Head?”

“Take these away.”

“Yes, sir.”

The servants skillfully stacked the documents on a cart. Just as they were about to leave, Jin Wikyung spoke.

“Ah. You stay.”

The servant he had singled out blinked in surprise.

“Me, sir?”

“That’s right. You.”

Once the two of them were alone, Jin Wikyung began in a dignified voice.

“So, how have you found the work lately?”

“Very agreeable, sir. All thanks to your generosity, Lesser Family Head.”

“Nothing causing you any inconvenience?”

“Oh, goodness. Of course not.”

The servant, Childeuk, nodded over and over. He was illiterate and had never even made it through the Thousand Character Classic, but he still had ears to hear and eyes to see.

After its victory in the recent war, the Jin Family of Taiyuan had risen to become the foremost family in Shanxi. Its Lesser Family Head, Jin Wikyung, had begun to be called the Junzi Sword[^1] for his swift recovery efforts and fair handling of the aftermath.

*Why would such a great man want me?*

His heart pounded with nerves. Had he made some mistake? Or had Jin Wikyung somehow noticed his talent for martial arts?

The former would be disastrous. The latter would be a chance to turn his life around.

*My bones and muscles are sturdy, at least. I never even had a stomachache as a child.*

He could already see himself wearing a sword at his waist, a hero’s headband fluttering in the wind.

But when Jin Wikyung saw Childeuk’s eyes glaze over, he flinched.

*What’s with this guy?*

His eyes burned with desperate longing, as though he were prepared to offer up his very soul for what he wanted.

It was not the sort of look one man should give another.

*Don’t tell me…*

Male love, the sort of thing Jin Wikyung had only ever heard about…

No. That could not be it. He must not jump to conclusions. Childeuk was a member of the Jin Family household, someone he ought to trust and cherish.

Jin Wikyung forced his suspicions aside and spoke.

“I’ve heard a great deal about you.”

“Y-You have, sir?”

“Of course. I’ve had my eye on you for a long time.”

More precisely, not for a long time, but for the past three days.

Jin Wikyung had been searching for a trustworthy servant to entrust with a very important task, and Childeuk was the ideal candidate he had personally selected.

“A talented man possessing all four virtues—benevolence, righteousness, propriety, and wisdom. That was you.”

“How can this be…!”

Childeuk, an exceptional servant possessing all four virtues, trembled with emotion. Being completely uneducated, he had no idea what those four virtues meant, but he understood the word *talented* perfectly.

*I’m talented?*

He had been praised for being strong and diligent, but no one had ever called him talented. And now he was hearing it from none other than the Lesser Family Head himself, a man as lofty as the heavens.

Was this a dream or reality? Childeuk was overcome with excitement—so much that his tongue became tangled.

“I’ve always been watching you too, Lesser Family Head!”

Jin Wikyung flinched.

*What did I just hear?*

“…What do you mean?”

“I’ve dreamed of this moment for years. The day I would finally stand behind you, Lesser Family Head!”

“Wait. That sounds strange. Why would you stand behind me?”

“Ah!”

Childeuk sucked in a breath. Jin Wikyung was telling him not to stand behind him. In other words, he wanted Childeuk to take the lead and win glory.

“Then I’ll stand in front!”

“No! That sounds strange too!”

But Childeuk’s wild-stallion charge could no longer be stopped.

“I will gladly offer you this body of mine!”

Jin Wikyung’s vision went dark.

“No. Don’t! Don’t offer it!”

“Lesser Family Head!”

Huff, huff.

Childeuk panted heavily, and Jin Wikyung gathered his internal energy.

*I never imagined something like this would happen.*

Open-minded though he was, this was too much.

Personal sexual preferences were one thing, but he had no desire to become their object. Jin Wikyung swallowed hard.

“Then… are you really into men?”[^2]

Childeuk’s eyes flashed. His heart pounded at the thought of wearing the navy-blue uniform of the Jin Family’s martial artists.

“Yes! Just give the order!”

“How dare you set your sights on me? Not a chance, you bastard!”

Smack!

A slap from a Peak master carried tremendous force. Childeuk crumpled like a puppet with its strings cut. Jin Wikyung stared down at him, breathing hard, then hurriedly rang the bell.

Ding. Ding.

“Lesser Family Head, did you call—? Gasp! Childeuk!”

Jin Wikyung spoke to the horrified servant.

“Take him out of here immediately!”

“W-What happened?”

“That bastard tried to… No. Never mind.”

He could not possibly say such a thing to a member of his household. For the first time in his life, a mixture of rage and wounded sorrow threatened to bring tears to his eyes.

“I-I’ll take care of it.”

Just as the quick-witted servant hoisted Childeuk onto his back, Jin Wikyung remembered the most important part.

“And that man.”

“Yes?”

“Remove him from his post.”

“Ah.”

The servant suddenly remembered Childeuk’s assignment.

*Delivering meals.*

The most important duty entrusted to Childeuk, exceptional servant and possessor of all four virtues, was to bring every meal to Jin Mukyung and Jin Taekyung.

“Don’t let him anywhere near my younger brothers. Understood?”

“Yes, sir!”

Compressed air erupted from the tip of the wooden spear. Jin Mukyung skidded backward, then clicked his tongue as he looked down at his broken sword.

“You barely managed it.”

I did not answer. I stood there in a daze, gripping my spear.

*So this is what it was.*

I had thought I knew the martial arts I’d learned inside and out. But I was wrong. I had merely reached the middle of the mountain and mistaken it for the summit.

Every time my martial arts rose to a new level, a new landscape came into view.

*Just like now.*
