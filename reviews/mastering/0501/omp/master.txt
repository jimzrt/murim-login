# Chapter 501

“When is it?”

On the way back after parting with Zhuge Feng, I asked the question out of the blue. Jin Wikyung answered quietly.

“In a month. Mount Song, Henan Province.”

“Mount Song…”

The very place where Shaolin Temple—the Mount Tai and Northern Dipper of the Murim—stood.

Only three months ago, the Star-Array Grand Banquet, a grand gathering of the Murim under Heaven, had begun there. Dark Heaven had unleashed a storm of blood on that same ground. Now the New Murim Alliance would be born there.

Given the symbolism of Mount Song and Shaolin Temple, it was a fitting place for the Murim Alliance to begin again after more than fifty years.

But if there was only a month left…

“It had already been decided when you came to Sichuan, hadn’t it?”

Contrary to my expectations, Jin Wikyung gave a small shake of his head.

“It hadn’t?”

“Why do you think Wipeng happened to be in Henan at just the right time?”

“Then Great Hero Wipeng came because…”

“The Jin Dragon Squad is our family’s finest fighting force. As the Lesser Family Head, I would never summon half its members merely to serve as porters, even if they were carrying the Son of Heaven’s jade seal.”

“So the date was moved up.”

“Dark Heaven moved more aggressively, and faster, than we expected. The Sichuan Blood Tragedy was decisive.”

To catch an enemy who was ahead of you, you had to run.

The storm of blood that had swept through Shaolin Temple had spread to Sichuan and now reached Hubei. The leaders in Henan had to make a choice.

“Bringing the Murim under Heaven together is no easy task. But it seems they have finally made up their minds.”

Jin Wikyung took a sealed dispatch from inside his robes and held it out.

“What’s this?”

“A letter Wipeng brought from Henan.”

“From Henan? Did the Murim Alliance send it?”

“You guessed.”

“I thought so. I saw you give one to Sir Zhuge earlier.”

Even at a glance, it looked nothing like an ordinary letter.

But what made it special were the three characters written across its face in a bold, soaring hand:

**Murim Alliance.**

“Read it yourself, youngest.”

“I’m already reading it, so stop talking. I can’t concentrate.”

“…Oh. Right.”

Leaving Jin Wikyung looking dejected, I unfolded the letter and skimmed it.

It began, *To the Murim under Heaven,* and was short and forceful. That made it easy to sum up in three lines.

1. Hey, let me tell you about Dark Heaven. These guys are seriously fucking crazy, so listen up.

2. You remember the Great Faction War PTSD? Let’s stop this by any means necessary.

3. Any dumbasses who don’t know where Mount Song is? Be there by the stated date. Anyone who doesn’t show up is Dark Heaven.

“…”

Put that way, it sounded pretty pathetic.

Of course, that was not what the letter actually said.

Some unknown calligrapher’s bold strokes flowed like clouds. Every sentence was solemn, charged with feeling, and deeply moving.

Personally, I almost wanted to show it to that bastard, the Lord of Heaven.

*If he saw this, even the Lord of Heaven might join the Murim Alliance.*

Then everyone could have a happy ending. Unfortunately, the chance of that happening was smaller than the amount of earwax in Hyuk Mujin’s ear.

“In any case, the Murim Alliance will be born soon.”

“In a month, the Murim under Heaven will gather beneath its banner.”

After a moment’s thought, Jin Wikyung added, “Not the Murim under Heaven. I should say the orthodox Murim of the Central Plains.”

I knew what he meant.

*Right… It’s a stretch to call the orthodox faction the whole Murim under Heaven.*

The orthodox faction, led by the Nine Sects and One Gang and the Five Great Families, was unquestionably the dominant force in the Murim today.

But it had not been the only side to survive the Great Faction War and emerge victorious.

“The unorthodox faction.”

Jin Wikyung nodded at my mutter.

“Yes. We cannot leave them out.”

The fishbowl called the world held more than one color of fish.

The unorthodox faction was weak compared to the orthodox faction, but it had carved out territories of its own. It also included the dark-path figures who ruled the streets at night.

*At first, I wondered why the orthodox faction kept shouting about wiping out demonic, heterodox arts but left those people alive.*

There was a reason for everything.

The unorthodox forces still around today had survived because they sided with the orthodox faction during the Great Faction War.

The trouble was, they had not done it out of goodwill.

“Do you know why the unorthodox forces now active in the Central Plains sided with the orthodox Murim?”

“You haven’t forgotten who I’ve spent the past year with, have you?”

“Great Hero Jeok told you.”

“I picked up quite a bit.”

As the old saying went, green went with green, and crayfish took the crab’s side.

When the Demonic Cult crossed Qinghai and surged toward the Central Plains, the unorthodox faction had quickly joined them, shouting, “Long live the Cult Leader!”

The Demonic Cult’s momentum was overwhelming, but the unorthodox faction also wanted people they saw as their own kind to rule the Murim under Heaven.

For unorthodox martial artists already living in the Central Plains, though, things were different.

*If they didn’t side with the orthodox faction, they’d be hunted down before they even got out of the Central Plains. You think those bastards could afford not to join us?*

Jeok Cheongang had once said that with a snort. For the unorthodox martial artists of the Central Plains, it had been the plain truth.

“I heard they were practically eating dumplings through their tears.”

“I did not live through that era either, but what I found confirms it.”

“What you found?”

“After you left for Sichuan, I stayed in Henan and searched the old archives. There were countless records, including ones about the unorthodox faction.”

The Murim Alliance had disbanded when peace returned, but the memories of those who had lived through the Great Faction War—and records of what had happened—remained.

That made sense. In the long history of the Murim, fifty years was *only* half a century.

“What did the records say?”

“What do you think?”

“I can guess. I’ve heard a few stories.”

“I do not know exactly what Great Hero Jeok told you, but most of it was probably true.”

“…Then it’s pretty damn bad.”

From the start of the Great Faction War well into its middle and later stages, the unorthodox martial artists who joined the orthodox faction betrayed them again and again.

Jeok Cheongang had told me the Murim Alliance’s leaders had even discussed rooting out every unorthodox martial artist in their ranks. Things must have been serious.

“But I heard they fought hard later on.”

“Unlike at the beginning, neither side’s victory was certain. The Hidden Shadow Pavilion, led by Sir Song, the Thousand-Faced Fox, also kept them under close watch. And according to the records, the unorthodox martial artists sensed the leadership’s mood changing and began cutting off their own tails.”

Not everyone in the orthodox faction was righteous, and not everyone in the unorthodox faction was a monster.

But the unorthodox faction had the will and the cruelty to cut off its own tail without hesitation.

The moment they suspected someone of being a Demonic Cult spy, they slit his throat.

“In the end, the body survived without its tail. By cooperating with the orthodox faction, it shared in the victory.”

The surviving unorthodox forces carried on in the shade of the great tree that was the orthodox faction. Naturally, they could no longer wield the influence they once had.

Their reputation had been bad enough to begin with, and there had been countless instances of them siding with the Demonic Cult.

Most people agreed that if the Murim Alliance’s leaders had not acknowledged at least some of their contributions and rights, hardline heroes calling for the extermination of demonic and heterodox elements would have dragged them out and torn them limb from limb.

The contempt directed at the unorthodox faction after the war had endured like an ancient tradition.

That alone was not a serious problem. What came next was.

“We need them to fight alongside us.”

“Yes. If they do not join the Murim Alliance, they will side with Dark Heaven.”

“I don’t like the thought of bringing them into the fold.”

“They are not people I would want to trust with my back on a battlefield. There is the precedent, and they must have spent decades resenting how honors and rewards were divided after the war.”

“What if we tell them to stay neutral?”

“Suppose the Murim Alliance looks certain to lose. What would you do if you were an unorthodox martial artist, youngest?”

“…Hmm.”

Put that bluntly, I had no answer. I smacked my lips before speaking.

“I hate to say it, but I’d side with Dark Heaven.”

“Why?”

“If the Murim Alliance wins, they’re no better off than before. But in your scenario, the scales have already tipped. Dark Heaven is about to rule the Murim of the Central Plains.”

“Exactly.”

“And what kind of people are Dark Heaven? They wouldn’t leave the unorthodox faction alone. They wouldn’t praise them for staying neutral—they’d be furious they hadn’t helped when it mattered and kill every last one of them.”

“Precisely. That is why the unorthodox faction is a chicken rib.”

A chicken rib. That was a good way to put it.

There was not much to gain from it, but throwing it away would be a waste. Fighting beside them felt risky, but if we cast them off, Dark Heaven would pick them up and throw them straight back at us.

However soft a chicken bone might be, it was still a bone. Getting hit with one would hurt.

Which meant…

“Henan has already sent them a letter too, hasn’t it?”

Jin Wikyung gave a small nod.

“Without a doubt. If they must choose one side, we have to bring the unorthodox Murim over to ours.”

For a chicken rib, the unorthodox faction had plenty of meat on it.

Even the dark-path swordsmen who wandered the back alleys after sunset belonged to it. Right now, we could use even one Third Rate swordsman.

Besides, it was better to keep them close enough to yank their leash than to let them join Dark Heaven. That would cost us far too much—

*Wait. Dark-path figures?*

Something came back to me, and I frowned.

“What is it?”

“I just remembered something I heard recently. Over the Water God Dragon incident, didn’t we blame the dark-path figures in Hubei Province and wipe them out?”

“We did.”

“…Then the unorthodox faction might not be happy. We accepted the government’s proposal and went after their own people.”

It was a bad look. They might even reject the Murim Alliance’s offer of membership over it.

Jin Wikyung, however, looked perfectly calm.

“Two things are wrong with that.”

“What?”

“First, the unorthodox Murim has been fractured since the war ended. Those people were rivals, not family. We used this opportunity to uproot the unusually numerous dark-path figures in Hubei. Other unorthodox forces will move into the space they left. For them, it is a new opportunity.”

“…!”

“We have given them enough to feed on. They will be satisfied. And when they hear what happened in Hubei, they will understand that it was also a warning.”

We had thrown them food and fastened a leash of fear around their necks.

That was how the Murim Alliance tamed the hunting dog known as the unorthodox Murim.

“And second, the government did not make the proposal for us to accept.”

Jin Wikyung’s voice went dry.

“We did. No—I made the proposal first.”

“Oh.”

“The government merely accepted it. That distinction matters a great deal.”

“…The government agreed that easily?”

“The Provincial Administration Commissioner of Hubei holds a post that can sway an entire province. But people know only that the new commissioner formerly served as an Assistant Provincial Administrator in Shanxi Province. They do not know he is a secret loyal retainer of Prince Shangshan.”

I remembered our arrival in Hubei.

The hostile looks from the people. Jin Wikyung speaking to the official who had surrounded the ferry landing with government soldiers.

*“By the way, do you know a man surnamed Yi whose given name is Hongcheon?”*

*“H-he was recently appointed Provincial Administration Commissioner. May I ask what your relationship with him is…?”*

*“We’ve met a few times and shared a drink or two. I’ve helped him when he needed it.”*

Jin Wikyung looked straight at me. His gaze was gentle, but a cold blade lay behind it.

For an instant, I saw the strategist and politician in him. Then that look melted away.

“Do not worry. Everything will work out.”

Tap, tap.

There was strength in the hand patting my shoulder. I studied him for a moment, then asked without warning,

“What was the Family Head… No, what was Father like?”

The faint smile on Jin Wikyung’s lips vanished.

“We were having such a nice moment. Why bring up that man?”

“As you know, I hurt my head back then. I don’t remember him very well.”

“You don’t need to! Erase him from your mind! I’m your father!”

“…”

I knew he had grievances, but apparently there were more than I had realized.

Jin Wikyung’s fist trembled, as if he were remembering the paperwork hell of those days.

“But why do you ask?”

“No reason. I was just thinking how lucky it is that you took charge of the family after Father disappeared.”

“…Youngest. Please don’t cross the line.”

I shrugged and turned away.

Behind me, Jin Wikyung demanded to know where I was going while my hyung was still talking, and whether I loved him any less now. I answered without looking back.

“You said it’s in a month. I’m going to get ready to leave.”

It was time to leave Hubei behind.

With the people who were by my side, and whom I wanted beside me in the days to come.

“I think I’ve given you enough time by now… Don’t you?”

My mutter disappeared beneath the surrounding noise.

I scratched my chin and looked up at the dizzyingly high cliff.
