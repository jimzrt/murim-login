# Chapter 374

Tang Horyong, Master of the Poison Dragon Pavilion, rose to greet his guest.

The imposing man who had just entered through the door radiated an indefinable pressure.

“You’ve come a long way. It must have been an arduous journey.”

“Compared to what the Sichuan Tang Clan has endured, it was nothing. Once again, please accept my deepest condolences.”

Despite his rugged features, the man’s voice was low and gentle, yet it carried a force that compelled anyone listening to pay attention.

Perhaps it was the aura unique to those who led others.

As Tang Horyong met the man’s mild yet deeply composed gaze, someone suddenly came to mind.

*It feels as though I’m facing another version of my older cousin.*

The thought left Tang Horyong more than a little unsettled.

From what he had heard, the man before him was not even forty yet.

His older cousin, by contrast, was far older and held an eminent position in Murim.

He had distinguished himself countless times during the Great Faction War and, as an iron-blooded Family Head, had rebuilt his fallen family on an even stronger foundation.

The Myriad-Poison Asura, Tang Sadok.

Tang Horyong, acting Family Head of the Sichuan Tang Clan, sighed inwardly as he thought of his older cousin, who had yet to regain consciousness.

*Please wake soon and lead us again, Family Head.*

Tang Horyong was a martial artist to his very bones.

He prided himself on having attained mastery in poisons and hidden weapons, but leading a family was an entirely different matter.

Especially of late, problems had come pouring in from every direction, leaving him no time even to dwell on the grief and fury of losing his own kin.

*If only a man like this were in my position.*

In that regard, Tang Horyong could not help envying the man before him.

It was not a judgment based solely on first impressions. He had already heard plenty of rumors about the man.

Even if only a quarter of them were true, the man was more than capable of leading a family.

“Sir Tang, is there something on my face?”

“Ah, no. With everything going on, I lost myself in thought and behaved rudely. My apologies.”

At Tang Horyong’s hurried apology, the man nodded gravely.

“Not at all. It’s understandable after what you’ve suffered.”

In truth, even *devastating* was too mild a word for the damage the Sichuan Tang Clan had sustained.

Nearly nine-tenths of its household had perished, and most of the clan grounds had been destroyed.

Their bloodline had survived, at least, but it would take a very long time to restore the prosperity they had once enjoyed.

“Our fellow martial artists in Sichuan have gone out of their way to help us, but… I fear for what lies ahead.”

Tang Sadok would never have shown weakness, no matter the circumstances. Tang Horyong was different.

At his candid admission, the man ran his long fingers over his teacup.

“I believe you already know why I came here, Sir Tang.”

“I understand you came to investigate the Three-Sect Bloodbath.”

“To be precise, when I first left Henan, my purpose was to track down those responsible for murdering the late Poison King Tang Sadok and the Heaven-Shaking Divine Nun. But the situation changed dramatically along the way.”

“Indeed. Dark Heaven—those accursed bastards—has finally shown its hand.”

“There was already a precedent in Henan, but for them to attack three renowned great sects so brazenly and simultaneously proves that war is nearly upon us.”

Small disturbances bred greater chaos.

But Dark Heaven’s existence could no longer be concealed, and there was no reason to conceal it.

Seven days and nights had passed since the horrific Three-Sect Bloodbath.

By now, word without feet would have traveled a thousand li, while countless carrier pigeons flew to every corner of the land.

And war, unlike mere chaos, brought people together.

“I heard the orthodox faction has been gathering in Henan since the Shaolin Bloodbath. Then, perhaps…”

“They are still making preparations. But it is already a foregone conclusion.”

The man continued in a weighty voice.

“That is why I wish to make a suggestion. Why not go to Henan?”

“Henan…”

“Yes. What you’re thinking of will happen soon.”

After a brief silence, Tang Horyong spoke.

“I appreciate being invited to such an honored occasion, but I cannot leave now. As you know, I am the acting Family Head, and the members of our family…”

“Sir Tang.”

“Go on.”

“As you just said, you are the acting Family Head. Naturally, you cannot abandon the Tang Clan’s household and leave on your own.”

“…!”

Only then did Tang Horyong grasp the meaning behind the man’s proposal. His mouth fell open.

It was something he had never dared to consider.

“Are you suggesting… that we move the entire family?”

“Well…”

The man fixed Tang Horyong with clear eyes that seemed to see straight through him.

“Great Hero Mae Jonghak once told me that a war even greater than the Great Faction War would soon erupt.”

“The Sword Saint…”

Tang Horyong swallowed a groan.

That could not be the Sword Saint’s opinion alone.

The Shaolin Bloodbath had roused countless orthodox martial artists to anger and vigilance, and the new Murim Alliance was already poised to emerge.

The entire orthodox Murim was preparing for the coming war.

*Can our family survive under these circumstances?*

The question flashed through his mind, and his heart sank.

The Sichuan Tang Clan had already suffered a blow unprecedented in its history.

Worse, the Qingcheng and Emei Sects had also lost a considerable portion of their strength.

If the enemy attacked again, there would be little hope of stopping them.

“Haah…”

As Tang Horyong stared blankly and sighed, the man’s quiet voice reached him.

“Sir Tang, why do you think the Sichuan Tang Clan has endured for hundreds of years?”

“That is…”

“Because it is the Tang Clan. Remove the first two characters from its name, and that fact remains unchanged.”

“…!”

“If you make the decision, the orthodox faction will help the Tang Clan.”

Tang Horyong trembled, unable to speak. At last, he forced out the words.

“We have made countless enemies over the years. Even among the Nine Sects and One Gang and the Five Great Families, there are those who regard our family with hostility. Will it truly be all right?”

“Everything will be handled fairly and aboveboard. Set your mind at ease and establish a new home. Henan, Shaanxi, or perhaps…”

A gentle smile touched the man’s lips.

“Shanxi would be suitable as well.”

“Shanxi?”

“Just say the word. I have plenty of land to spare.”

Tang Horyong suddenly remembered something he had forgotten.

The man before him was the leader who had unified Shanxi’s Murim, the greatest landowner in Shanxi Province, and a man of immense wealth.

“Thank you. Truly, thank you, Sir Jin!”

“Think nothing of it.”

The man—Jin Wikyung, Lesser Family Head of the Jin Family of Taiyuan—was respectfully yet majestically returning the acting Family Head of the Sichuan Tang Clan’s fist-and-palm salute when—

Clunk.

“Um, I heard you called for me.”

A young man cautiously poked his head through the gap in the pavilion door.

The instant Jin Wikyung spotted him, he unleashed a lion’s roar.

“Youngest—!”

Thud-thud-thud-thud—wham!

He charged like an enraged bull.

Watching the two brothers reunite in what might have been an embrace or a collision, Tang Horyong recalled one of the rumors about Jin Wikyung.

*They say he’s an absolute fool when it comes to his younger brothers.*

The leader who had unified Shanxi’s Murim and the greatest landowner in Shanxi Province had vanished without a trace.

All that remained was a hopelessly doting fool.

*He is still young, after all. He lacks experience. He cannot compare to my older cousin, who remains cool-headed at all times. Yes, of course.*

Tang Horyong had no way of knowing that Mimi-chan existed.

* * *

“Youngest!”

Jin Wikyung charged at me, spraying tears from his damp eyes, and swept me into a crushing embrace.

I had two years of Murim experience under my belt. After enduring this sort of thing time and again, I’d expected a reaction about this dramatic.

Crack!

…What I hadn’t expected was that.

Seriously, why could I hear my bones shifting out of place?

I sighed as his arms constricted around my entire body like an anaconda in the Amazon.

Still, I was happy to see him, so I let him hug me. His reaction had just never been this violent before.

“Hold on, hyungnim. Let go so we can talk. Let go.”

“Hyungnim?! Have I not told you to drop that stiff formality and simply call me hyung? You’ve changed. You’ve changed!”

“Fine, I get it. Now let me go.”

“You spoke casually to me when you were little, so why are you using polite speech now? You’ve changed. You’ve changed!”

“Let go, fucker.”

“Gasp! Even during your rebellious phase, you never swore! You’ve chang—!”

“Wow. That running commentary is driving me insane.”

Was he a Murim martial artist by trade and a Dementor on the side?

Feeling as if he were sucking out my soul, I shuddered and reached out.

Whirl—crash!

Jin Wikyung’s massive body slammed headfirst into the ground, his legs sticking into the air. The impact rang through the pavilion.

The middle-aged man from the Sichuan Tang Clan who had been watching let out a startled gasp.

“It’s fine, really. We’re just playing around.”

“B-but still…”

“Look. He’s getting right back up.”

Just as I’d said, Jin Wikyung sprang to his feet as though nothing had happened, tears of emotion glimmering in his eyes.

“You’ve grown even stronger while we were apart. That’s our youngest.”

His complete lack of change made me snort.

“You’re exactly the same.”

“The same? Do you know how worried I’ve been these past two months? I couldn’t sleep at night or stomach any food. I’ve been reduced to skin and bones.”

I looked over his massive, muscular frame and muttered, “Your bones look pretty well padded.”

Still, two months.

Had that much time already passed?

It struck me anew that it was almost time to return.

Given that spending ten days in one world meant only about an hour passed in the other…

*Around six hours must have passed. The plane might be landing soon.*

Perfect timing. I’d been exhausted after overcoming the massive obstacle known as the Western Heaven Demon Lord.

Rather than drag out the conversation, I got straight to the point.

“When are we leaving?”

“Our youngest, you must have suffered so much… Hm?”

Jin Wikyung, who had been anxiously looking me over, froze.

“What did you say?”

“You came to take me with you, didn’t you? Oh, and Samgoe too.”

Jin Wikyung’s eyes widened.

“How did you know?”

The middle-aged man, who seemed to hold an important position in the Sichuan Tang Clan, also looked surprised.

“Sir Jin, did you not come to investigate the full circumstances of the incident?”

“I did. However, several members of the investigative party, myself included, will be returning to Henan. Just before we reached Sichuan, we received orders to escort Samgoe, one of the principal culprits behind the Three-Sect Bloodbath.”

“Then what about your proposal concerning our family…?”

“It still stands, of course. It would simply be difficult for the Tang Clan to act on it immediately.”

“Indeed. The Family Head is not yet capable of traveling such a long distance, and I must seek the consent of the rest of the household.”

“Yes. And…”

After quietly conversing with the middle-aged man for a moment, Jin Wikyung sent me a discreet Sound Transmission.

*But how did you know?*

*Come on, it was obvious. I just pieced it together from the circumstances.*

*Ah, our youngest. What terrible ordeal did you overcome that even made you clever?*

*…*

Why did that piss me off?

The truth was, I hadn’t pieced together anything. I knew because a Quest about escorting Samgoe had appeared.

“So when are we leaving?”

Having just finished his conversation, Jin Wikyung answered me.

“If you already know, that makes things simpler. The sooner, the better. Are you ready?”

“The only things I brought are the two balls between my legs. I’m ready to go whenever.”

Well, those and two bundles of luggage wrapped tightly in bandages.

Jin Wikyung nodded.

“Sir Tang, where is Samgoe?”

The middle-aged man he had addressed as Sir Tang replied, “We bound all four of his limbs and imprisoned him under strict guard. Ever since his balls were crushed, he has tried to kill himself whenever he gets the chance. You’ll need to be careful.”

“Oh dear…”

*I’d want to die too if I were him.*

In any case, everything was in place for a swift departure.

Jin Wikyung considered it for a moment, then gave a decisive answer.

“Half a shichen, then.[^1] We’ll leave within half a shichen. Is that acceptable?”

“No problem.”

The moment I answered without hesitation, hurried footsteps sounded outside the pavilion, followed by someone shouting.

“The Family Head! The Family Head has awakened!”

Jin Wikyung corrected himself in a lukewarm voice.

“One shichen. Let’s make it one shichen.”

“…Yes. That would probably be better.”

Damn. I could’ve grabbed the Myriad Poison Ring and bolted.

[^1]: A shichen is a traditional two-hour period.
