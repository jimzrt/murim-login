# Chapter 33

“Please kill me.”

A young man with disheveled hair, covered in blood and dust, dropped to his knees. His name was Lee Seogwang—the elder brother of the late Lee Seogeun and the Young Sect Leader of the Mount Heng Sword Sect.

“Report it. Tell me yourself.”

The cold voice froze everyone inside the tent. Every last one of them was a senior figure in the Mount Heng Sword Sect, a master who had spent long years roaming the martial world.

Yet even they had to bow their heads before one man.

“Father…”

“That isn’t the answer I want.”

The Blood Wolf Sword, Lee Cheonbaek, did not even turn around. Lee Seogwang forced the words out in a trembling voice.

“There are only twenty-three survivors, including me. We don’t know whether the rest are alive or dead.”

A heavy silence settled over the gathering.

Some two hundred troops had been killed or captured, and they had lost three Peak masters. That alone would have made punishment unavoidable, but there was an even bigger problem.

“What orders were you given?”

“…To wipe out the Jin Family of Taiyuan’s branch and advance as far as Jeongyang.”

“And after that?”

“To blockade the road and wait for the main force to join us.”

“Why did you push as far as Honju?”

“Intelligence—I obtained intelligence.”

“Intelligence?”

“Yes. I was told that Jopil, One Question, One Kill, had chased Jin Taekyung all the way to Honju and captured him. But…”

“He said the Jin Family of Taiyuan’s pursuit party was on his heels and asked you to help him.”

Lee Seogwang lowered his head, and Lee Cheonbaek let out a hollow laugh.

“Who gave you that information?”

“A wandering martial artist under Jopil.”

“Do you still believe it?”

“…No.”

*Smack!*

Lee Seogwang’s head snapped to the side. Lee Cheonbaek glared down at him, his gaze blazing.

“Be grateful you were born my son.”

In the end, Lee Seogwang was ordered to return to the Mount Heng Sword Sect. Once he withdrew, the matter was settled for the time being.

Now it was time to assess the situation with a cool head.

“What is our current position?”

“We lost two hundred men, but more than half were wandering martial artists we scraped together in a hurry. The damage isn’t as severe as it sounds. The problem is…”

“We lost three Peak masters.”

The thought left a bitter taste in Lee Cheonbaek’s mouth. The Mount Heng Twin Devils had stood beside him for twenty years, and Jopil, whom he had paid a fortune to hire, was dead as well.

A Peak master could change the course of a battle. Somehow, they had to fill the void.

“Gather more. Buy wandering martial artists from the black market, hire mounted bandits—use whatever means necessary.”

“The expense is already too high. By now, word of what happened in Honju will have spread, so they’ll try to drive up their prices.”

“They aren’t trustworthy, either. Especially the mounted bandits. Aren’t they human butchers? If we hire men like that, our sect’s reputation afterward…”

Lee Cheonbaek did not even blink.

“All the better. Hire them all.”

“Sect Leader!”

“There are no fewer than five hundred men gathered here. We outnumber them by more than two to one, and we aren’t behind in the number of Peak masters either.”

“My son thought the same thing. Then he got utterly crushed in Honju.”

“That was…”

“Forget whether they’re human butchers or anything else. Throw everything we have into this. If we lose this war, it won’t be our reputation we lose—it’ll be our lives!”

At Lee Cheonbaek’s thunderous command, the seniors realized that trying to persuade him was pointless. But that did not mean the discussion was over.

“The martial artists’ morale is in shambles.”

“The defeat at Honju is one thing, but they’re also shaken by the news that Jin Taekyung killed Jopil, One Question, One Kill.”

Jin Taekyung.

Hearing the name of his enemy made Lee Cheonbaek’s stomach churn.

*Are there really fools stupid enough to believe such an absurd rumor?*

As if it weren’t enough that the pathetic fool Jin Taekyung had poisoned his son, now he had supposedly killed a Peak master too. The scheme was obvious. The Jin Family of Taiyuan’s despicable tactics made Lee Cheonbaek grind his teeth.

“Find everyone spreading that nonsense. Don’t miss a single one!”

Taking a few heads as examples might hurt morale further, but it would keep the army from collapsing. Now was the time to clamp down and push forward.

“Three days. In three days, we march on Taiyuan!”

That was how the debts and grudges of Murim worked. Their chain would not break until one of the two sides fell.

*I’ll take everything from you. Just as you did.*

* * *

“What an incredible recovery.”

The physician clicked his tongue in amazement. It was the second day since I’d regained consciousness, and overnight, the scabs had fallen away to reveal smooth new skin.

“In ten years as a physician, I’ve never seen anything like this.”

Even to me, the speed of my recovery was astonishing. The flesh melted by the heat of Flame Divine Palm, the fractured bones, the deep sword wounds—there was no trace of them left.

Oh, and the internal injuries too.

*Must be the effect of leveling up.*

There was only one method in the real world that could produce similar results.

Healing magic, used by the tiny handful of Hunters known as healers.

*Was a full recovery too much to hope for?*

I was a little disappointed, but it was probably for the best. If a single level-up had healed every wound on my body, avoiding suspicion would have been difficult.

Even now, the physician kept stealing glances at me as though I were a monster.

“Heavens. How bizarre. This is far too much to attribute to the effects of hundred-year snow ginseng…”

“Hundred-year snow ginseng?”

I remembered seeing something like it in martial arts novels. Ginseng that had grown for a hundred years, or whatever.

*Why is that suddenly coming up?*

Seeing my confusion, the physician explained. He was one of the few NPCs who knew I had lost my memory.

“Around this time last year, the Medicine King Hall’s storeroom was robbed.”

Ah. The moment he said it, I understood.

The stolen medicinals must have included hundred-year snow ginseng, and the culprit was obviously…

“The Medicine King Hall Leader was absolutely furious.”

The physician gave me an awkward smile.

“It isn’t something you can obtain simply by having enough money, after all. You stole and consumed an elixir capable of granting twenty years of internal energy in a single dose.”

“Twenty years of internal energy?”

Something clicked. The third internal energy that had refused to obey me.

*So that was it.*

At the same time, a pang of regret washed over me. At the last moment, when I used the Skill, most of the twenty years of internal energy from the hundred-year snow ginseng had vanished.

The whole disaster had happened because it ran wild instead of obeying my control.

*I blew it all on one use.*

The physician misread the look on my face and hurriedly added, “Of course, Young Master, you must have made that decision for some grand purpose.”

*Grand purpose, my ass. I probably ate it as a tonic.*

The snow ginseng’s internal energy had kept me alive, but it was still such a waste.

Damn, what a waste.

“You’ve almost completely recovered, so you shouldn’t have any trouble moving around. Then I’ll be on my way.”

The moment the physician hurried out, I opened the System window.

The moment I saw it, pride welled up inside me.

*I’ve grown a lot.*

It felt like only yesterday I’d been trembling in front of a few two-bit bandits. Now I was something of a master in my own right. My Level had shot up after I took down Jopil, and I’d gained a massive amount of Fame.

That wasn’t all. I’d absorbed some of the hundred-year snow ginseng’s remaining energy and gained another four years of internal energy. On top of that…

I had a new martial art—or rather, a new Skill.

But it was very different from my Skills in the real world.

*I can adjust how much it consumes and how much power it puts out.*

Thrust with All My Might. This Skill, now named One Flash, couldn’t be used recklessly. It could unleash destructive power several stages above my usual level for an instant, but that single blow would exhaust all my strength.

*No. Maybe it was always a Skill I could adjust.*

In the real world, I was an F-rank Hunter. My physical abilities and the mana in my body were pathetic. But in this game—in Murim—I was different.

Here, I was a martial artist with fifteen years of internal energy and physical abilities superior to NPCs twenty or thirty Levels above me. Changing the vessel that held the power had revealed how versatile the Skill had always been.

*I’ve gotten stronger again.*

It felt like only yesterday Hyuk Mujin had been wiping the floor with me while I struggled to learn martial arts. Now I’d taken down a Peak master.

Outside the window, people chanted my name several times a day.

Sleeping Dragon of Shanxi. Hero of the family. That sort of thing.

*A hero.*

I never thought I’d hear a word like that in my life. For a lowly F-rank Hunter whose motto was safety first, it was a word that had never had anything to do with me.

I lay still and wiggled my fingers. The palms that had once belonged to a pampered young master—white and soft—were now covered in calluses.

*With these hands, I took down Jopil.*

All told, the people I’d defeated numbered more than a few dozen. Bandits, wandering martial artists, even people rated as First Rate—and I had survived. I’d even taken down a Peak master I thought I could never beat: Jopil, One Question, One Kill.

I suddenly remembered something Wipeng had said.

*The one who survives is strong.*

If he was right, I was definitely strong. I’d survived every enemy I’d faced so far, and people were calling me a hero.

Yeah. If I was being honest…

*It didn’t feel half bad.*

The real-world me was pitiful.

I ate and slept in a one-room goshiwon barely ten square meters in size,[^1] a breadwinner responsible for supporting my family. I couldn’t become a hero, and I didn’t want to.

I was just Jin Taekyung, a bottom-rung Hunter who fought every day while praying he would survive.

That was me.

*But in this game, I’m different.*

I’d done many things that F-rank Hunter Jin Taekyung could never have done. At the very least… I could protect the people who trusted and followed me from their enemies. People acknowledged me. They called me a hero.

Even if everything here was nothing but virtual, even if the people before me were NPCs, that fact didn’t change.

The thought made me laugh.

*This is why games are scary.*

Was this game addiction?

Before I knew it, I’d started enjoying life as Jin Taekyung, a martial artist of Murim. Because this was a game. Because it could turn every impossibility into a possibility.

But now it was time to leave.

Back to that place packed with impossibilities—the real world. My family was there. The real me was there.

The real problem lay elsewhere.

“First Rate.”

What did I even need to become First Rate?

If it wasn’t Level, stats, or Fame, then…

*Internal energy? Or do I need to raise the realm of my martial arts further?*

Just then, a polite voice came from outside the door.

“Young Master. The Lesser Family Head is looking for you.”

“Ah.”

Right. When you don’t know something, the best thing to do is ask.

And for that, Peak master Jin Wikyung was the best private tutor I could get.
