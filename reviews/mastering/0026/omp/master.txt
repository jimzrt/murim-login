# Chapter 26

“So…”

Gong Yacheong gave me a strange look.

“So you’re that Young Master Jin Taekyung.”

The moment had finally come. I let go of Hyuk Mujin’s collar—he’d passed out for the third time—and smiled awkwardly.

“Yes. I’m Jin Taekyung.”

The reconnaissance squad members preparing camp perked up their ears. We were on our way back to the main family.

We had ridden for more than five hours without a break since leaving the cabin where the fight had taken place. Gong Yacheong had kept his mouth shut the entire way. Only now did he finally speak.

*If it weren’t for that bastard.*

I glared at Hyuk Mujin as one of the quicker-witted squad members dragged him away. I should have hit him one more time before letting go.

While I was still regretting that, Gong Yacheong continued.

“I’ve heard plenty about you, Young Master.”

“…I see.”

“Even a three-year-old knows who you are. You must be the main family’s biggest celebrity.”

At this point, it would have been faster to find someone who didn’t know me.

“Lazy by nature and arrogant beyond belief. Lost in wine and women, with no interest in learning martial arts… But I see the rumors of the martial world truly can’t be trusted. The martial prowess you displayed today was remarkable.”

“…”

If there were something like a *Martial World Daily* that collected every rumor in the martial world, I’d subscribe immediately. It sounded pretty reliable to me.

*More importantly…*

Did Gong Yacheong really not know?

Whatever bullshit the Mount Heng Sword Sect had pulled, I was at the center of this entire mess.

There was no denying that countless people had been sacrificed along the way.

It wouldn’t have been strange if Gong Yacheong and Socheon held a grudge against me. That was why I had hidden my name.

*If he really doesn’t know, that’s a relief.*

We were being chased by a ruthless Peak master. I had no interest in stirring up trouble we didn’t need.

“How long until we reach the main family?”

Gong Yacheong’s question snapped me back to my senses.

“We should arrive in two or three days.”

“Of all times, it had to snow now…”

Gong Yacheong sighed, glaring resentfully at the sky as it continued dumping snow.

“Young Master, we should prepare for the worst.”

“The worst?”

The blizzard had slowed us down, but it had slowed our enemies down too. There was even a chance they had given up the chase.

Gong Yacheong shook his head.

“Don’t underestimate wandering martial artists. The purity of their internal energy and the quality of the martial arts they’ve learned may be lacking, but their greatest weapon lies elsewhere.”

I gave him the answer.

“Experience.”

“Exactly. The experience gained from fighting a hundred battles. What’s chasing us isn’t merely a group of martial artists. They’re wandering martial artists. The same, yet entirely different.”

The same, yet different. I thought I understood what he meant.

In short, they were well-trained hunting dogs. And the man holding their leash was Jopil, One Question, One Kill.

“The reason Jopil is truly frightening isn’t simply that he’s a Peak master. It’s his persistence. His viciousness. He has never once failed a commission or allowed a target to escape.”

I answered with a sigh.

“It’ll be the same this time, then.”

“Probably.”

Gong Yacheong stared into the darkness beyond, as though something might leap out and swallow us whole.

“We’re being chased by a man like that.”

Whoosh.

The snow-laden wind vanished into the darkness.

* * *

We finished making camp. We had dug several small holes and blocked their entrances with branches, fallen leaves, and whatever else we could find. It was crude, but this was no time to be choosy.

“Have a restful night.”

Gong Yacheong disappeared into one of them with the sleeping siblings in his arms. I couldn’t stop thinking about the weary smile on his lips.

*He really is an adult.*

If all he cared about was surviving, he could have run away long ago. Gong Yacheong was a skilled martial artist and a seasoned man of the martial world.

But he hadn’t.

He had risked his life fighting for two children who didn’t share a drop of his blood.

That was what made Gong Yacheong a good adult.

*Could I have done the same?*

I shook off the question as soon as it arose. This was a game. It wasn’t good to think too deeply about an illusion that would soon disappear.

That was right. An illusion that would soon disappear.

*Open Quest Window.*

Ding.

> **System**
>
> **Logout**
>
> You must now make your way through this harsh Murim.
>
> Become stronger. Become famous.
>
> For the day that will come someday…
>
> **Grade:** Main Quest
>
> **Limit:** Jin Taekyung
>
> **Task:** Reach the First Rate realm — Incomplete  
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Reach Lv. 30 — 24/30  
> &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Reach Fame 500 — 250/500
>
> **Reward:** **Logout**

The EXP and Fame I had earned from dealing with the enemies, plus the Quest reward.

At last, the summit of this treacherous mountain was coming into view.

I opened my Status Window and distributed all my remaining points. Vitality flowed back into my exhausted body.

*I’m almost there.*

I only had to get past this hurdle. If I could avoid that bastard Jopil, One Question, One Kill—the man whose face I didn’t even know—I could finally put an end to this miserable journey.

But there was another problem.

*Let’s say I can somehow fill up my Level and Fame. How am I supposed to raise this damn realm?*

Back when I first started clearing the tutorial Quests, I had raised my realm from Third Rate to Second Rate by distributing my stats. Since then, nothing.

*What the hell am I missing?*

I was an absurd existence by Murim standards. I could learn martial arts and raise my stats faster than anyone else. The System had helped me grow stronger in all kinds of ways.

That was why I could literally wipe the floor with most First Rate martial artists despite being a dime-a-dozen Second Rate.

And yet I wasn’t First Rate!

*I should’ve asked Jin Wikyung.*

At first, I thought I just needed to keep raising my stats or internal energy. Lately, though, I had begun to suspect I was barking up the wrong tree.

*Level? Stats? Internal energy?*

What was it? The final piece needed to complete the puzzle?

The answer came quickly.

*If I don’t know, I’ll have to find out.*

Just as I had until now. If I kept walking, eventually I would find an exit.

I sat cross-legged and regulated my breathing. The internal energy coiled in my dantian answered my call.

* * *

“We lost them.”

Jopil muttered as his black, viper-like eyes swept the surroundings.

He had lost them. But traces remained. Those faint traces would become new signposts.

“About twenty people. They left roughly two shichen ago.”[^1]

Jopil nodded at his subordinate’s report. The man’s martial arts were only Third Rate, but his tracking skills were at the Peak level. Jopil had countless hunting dogs like him under his command.

*Two shichen.*

The gap had already narrowed to two shichen. How long would it take to catch the fugitives?

Half a day at most.

*They should have run with everything they had. Without looking back. They should have cast off every burden, abandoned any stragglers, and kept running for their lives.*

Had they done that, the gap would not have narrowed. But they had let their guard down. They must have assumed the blizzard would stop him too, and that mistake had led to the current situation.

*But I’m a little different.*

The previous night had been long. Even among his pursuit-hardened subordinates, some had fallen behind.

In the mountains during a blizzard, falling behind was the same as dying.

The only difference from dying on a battlefield was that it took a little longer.

That was why Jopil had given the order.

*Kill them.*

They said that when a tiger died, it left its hide, and when a person died, they left their name.

But Jopil thought differently. What name could a Third Rate nobody who had spent his whole life drifting through back alleys possibly leave behind?

He should at least leave a hide.

Leaving twenty naked corpses behind, they continued onward. When the long night finally ended, they found the fugitives’ tracks around daybreak.

*We’ll be meeting soon, friend.*

The corners of Jopil’s mouth lifted. This mysterious master was the first person in a long while to pique his interest.

His long-stilled heart began to pound.

* * *

I shuddered.

“Ugh. What the hell?”

A sudden chill ran through me. I raised my arm and found it covered in goose bumps.

If this were a scene in a novel, the protagonist would have muttered, *Something feels off,* and continued on his way.

But I was different. I acted on firm suspicion.

“Hey. Get over here, quick.”

I couldn’t see him, but I could feel it. Someone trudging along behind me faltered.

“W-why?”

“I’m counting to three. One, two. Three.”

The instant I reached three, Hyuk Mujin rushed over and pressed himself against my side.

“It was you, wasn’t it?”

“What?”

“You know what I mean. Tell me the truth and I’ll let it slide.”

“What are you talking about? It wasn’t me!”

I stared at his face, swollen like a steamed bun.

“You were cursing me behind my back just now, weren’t you?”

“Gasp.”

“You were, weren’t you?”

“I-I was…”

I knew it was this bastard. When I raised my hand, Hyuk Mujin squeezed his eyes shut.

After getting beaten three times, he had lost all will to resist. He had also learned a valuable life lesson:

Dodging only meant getting hit more.

“Fine. Since you told the truth, I’ll let it go this once.”

Hyuk Mujin’s head shot up.

“Really?”

I gave him a warm smile.

“Of course. But don’t even think about deceiving me again. I’ll be watching you with my mind-reading technique.”

Hyuk Mujin gave me a glare full of murderous intent, then darted back to his place. If I’d had more time, I would have crushed his skull with a mace.

Swallowing my regret, I kept walking.

“What’s a mind-reading technique?”

The chirping voice tickled my ears. It belonged to Soyul, Socheon’s little sister. Had he said she was five?

She was small enough to fit inside my backpack.

“Is it martial arts?”

“Something like that.”

“Is mind-reading strong?”

“Really strong.”

“Wow! Soyul wants to learn mind-reading too!”

“But you have to be one-eyed.”

“Gasp!”

I glanced back and saw Soyul staring at me in shock, her eyes huge. She was ridiculously cute.

*Hayeon used to be like that too.*

These days she was an obnoxious little sister, but when she was young, she had been a baby angel. She had even gotten offers to work as a child model…

Carrying Soyul on my back felt exactly like carrying Hayeon around at that age.

“Want me to teach you?”

“…Soyul doesn’t like martial arts. I’m going to be a proper young lady.”

“That’s fine too.”

“Mm-hmm. Mister, do you like martial arts?”

“Me?”

“My brother says you’re really strong. Dad said only people who work hard can become strong.”

“Your dad said that?”

“Yes. My dad is really strong too. Because…”

She chattered excitedly for a while before sticking out her lower lip.

“Soyul wants to see Dad. But I guess Dad doesn’t want to see us. Oppa says he went off to have fun with Mom and left me and Oppa behind.”

My heart dropped.

An old memory filled my vision. In a funeral hall draped in black and white, little Hayeon had searched for our father, and I had no choice but to tell her an obvious lie.

Just as Socheon had done for Soyul.

It was the only thing he could have said.

“…I see.”

What else could I say?

I looked at Socheon, following along in the middle of the formation. He was panting hard, drenched in sweat.

*He must be exhausted.*

His willpower was far beyond his years. The saying that pain made people mature was fucking bullshit, but it was true.

Socheon hadn’t fallen behind even once, and the other reconnaissance squad members marveled at him for it.

*But the real problem lies elsewhere.*

Gong Yacheong.

His face was deathly pale; he still hadn’t recovered from his injuries. If not for the remarkable effects of the fasting pills I had given him and the qi circulation he had performed the previous night, he might have collapsed long ago.

*At this rate, they’ll catch us.*

Even after sunrise, the accumulated snow showed no sign of melting. I was out front, forcing a path through it, but everyone was exhausted, and our pace had slowed.

On top of that, we had an injured man and two children.

*Should I run away by myself?*

The thought came to me in an instant.

It was a game. So what?

Gong Yacheong, those two kids, the entire reconnaissance squad—every one of them was an artificial intelligence created for the game. Nothing more than NPCs.

But me?

I was alive. Out of all of them, I was the only one who was real. The only one who truly existed.

But…

*Fuck. It’s a simple problem… So what’s wrong with me?*

An inexplicable aversion surged through me. It was strong enough to startle me, even leave me shaken.

*Why?*

I didn’t know.

I spent the next several hours searching for the answer without finding it.

And then night fell.

[^1]: A shichen is a traditional time period of roughly two hours.
