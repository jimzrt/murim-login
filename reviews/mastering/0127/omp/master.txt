# Chapter 127

It didn’t take long for the question marks to turn into exclamation points, and the exclamation points into bewilderment and rage.

Jin Mukyung was the first to break the silence.

“You…”

His face was flushed red, his breathing ragged. His fist twitched as if he wanted to plant one right in my mouth.

*Yep, he’s seriously pissed.*

A chill ran through me. It felt like a dagger had lodged in my chest.

But there was no need to worry. I had a sturdy shield.

“Now, now, Mukyung.”

At the quiet voice, Jin Mukyung’s face twisted.

“Eldest brother!”

“Taekyung must have had his reasons. Isn’t that right?”

I lowered my eyes with deliberate solemnity.

“No, eldest brother. I was shortsighted.”

“Hm?”

“I let my curiosity get the better of me… But after hearing what Eldest Brother said, I realized the truth. That object must never be kept—or hidden.”

I even made a show of trembling my fist, as though the mere thought made me shake with rage.

“The Demonic Cult! Just hearing the name of those vile bastards makes my blood boil!”

That part was sincere. If they were going to make something, they should’ve made it properly. Turning the user into a deranged murderer was one hell of a design flaw.

“Good heavens.”

Jin Wikyung gazed at me with boundless affection.

“I remember a sweet little boy who said he would grow up to become a chivalrous hero. You were six years old then. Do you remember?”

Of course I didn’t.

Even the year before last was already hazy. How was I supposed to know what the original owner of this body had done at age six?

Still, I nodded solemnly.

“I remember it clearly. It was my one and only dream.”

Chivalrous hero, governor of Gyeonggi Province—whatever. As of this moment, that had been my dream at age six.

“Ha-ha. To think that little boy would grow into such a fine man.”

Jin Wikyung smiled with satisfaction, then turned to the other two.

“You were there too, Wipeng. Do you remember?”

Wipeng answered without missing a beat.

“I don’t remember that, but I do remember him starting to chase women exactly ten years later. When I asked what he wanted to be when he grew up, he said he’d become the greatest ladies’ man under heaven.”

“A hero ought to appreciate romance.”

“He didn’t know squat about martial arts, so what good was romance? Is this hero you’re talking about a hero of the night? A hero to courtesans?”

“Be quiet. Our youngest showed unusual promise from an early age.”

“That promise of his… Ah, forget it. I should just keep my mouth shut.”

Glug, glug.

Jin Wikyung neatly ignored Wipeng as he poured liquor straight from the bottle and turned to the next man in line.

“Mukyung. Now that you understand your little brother’s sincerity, let go of your anger.”

Jin Mukyung, whose face was twisted into a fearsome scowl, finally spoke.

“Can’t I hit that bastard just once?”

“Now, now.”

“Just once. Please.”

At the icy voice, I quickly bowed my head.

“Please forgive this foolish little brother, Second Brother.”

“You’ve been talking down to me this whole time, and now you’re calling me ‘brother’?”

“Pardon? Me?”

“That’s enough. This is your final warning.”

“No. Hit me instead. If that will ease your anger, this little brother will gladly endure it.”

“You little shit!”

Jin Mukyung shot to his feet, only to sink back down with a gasp. The bandages wrapped around his chest were turning red. His wound must have reopened.

“Oh, no! Second Brother, are you all right?”

“This bastard, again… Guh!”

“Physician! Get the physician!”

The drinking party descended into chaos in an instant.

Wipeng silently picked up his second bottle and muttered,

“This family is really something…”

And how something it was—the foremost family in Shanxi.

* * *

The atmosphere finally settled down after the physician had come and gone.

Ignoring Jin Mukyung’s murderous glare, I took the Temporary Strength Pill from inside my robes.

“This is it.”

The pill was bloodred, as though blood itself had been condensed into a tiny sphere.

Jin Wikyung and Wipeng examined it closely.

“Wipeng, what do you think?”

“Just looking at it, I can almost smell blood. It’s a sinister object.”

“Could it really have been made by the Demonic Cult?”

“I couldn’t say. If it had, we should be able to sense demonic qi… but I can’t be certain.”

“Right? Something about it is different.”

Both men looked deathly serious. I was just as curious about where the Temporary Strength Pill had come from and who had made it, so I decided to give them a hint.

“Pung Yang called it a Temporary Strength Pill.”

“A Temporary Strength Pill?”

“Yes. I heard it from his own mouth.”

Jin Mukyung abruptly cut in.

“Pung Yang? When?”

“While you were unconscious.”

“…Hoo. Hoo…”

No matter what anyone said, I was the sole eyewitness and witness. While Jin Mukyung steadied his breathing to calm himself after getting nowhere, the furrows between the other two men’s brows only deepened.

“A Temporary Strength Pill, Wipeng?”

“I’ve never heard the name either. If something this potent belonged to the Demonic Cult, it must have been used during the Great Faction War…”

“What if the Demonic Cult didn’t make it?”

Their gazes turned toward me.

“Not the Demonic Cult?”

“What makes you think that?”

“I’m just saying there’s no need to jump to conclusions.”

In truth, the idea had sprung from my faint hope that I might get the pill back if it wasn’t a Demonic Cult artifact.

But something else had occurred to me as well.

*The Head Elder.*

On the surface, our enemy in the last battle had clearly been the Mount Heng Sword Sect. But the true enemy had been the Head Elder himself.

I believed it was always better to consider the possibility of a third faction than to limit ourselves to a simple either-or assumption.

“Well, the thought just came to me.”

After hearing me out, the other two men’s expressions turned grave. Then a single word slipped from Wipeng’s lips, quiet as an unconscious groan.

“Dark Heaven…”

“Wipeng.”

Jin Wikyung’s sharp gaze cut him off.

“Ah, my apologies. I misspoke.”

Wipeng hurriedly tried to cover it up.

But it was already too late.

The words *Dark Heaven* had etched themselves deep into my mind.

*Dark Heaven? What’s that?*

Then something unexpected happened.

> **System**
>
> You have obtained a small amount of information about **Dark Heaven**.
>
> The item description for the **Temporary Strength Pill** will be updated.

The System notification had come out of nowhere. I held out my hand toward Wipeng, who was holding the Temporary Strength Pill.

“May I take a quick look?”

“Of course.”

*Check Item.*

The moment I muttered the command in my head, the information on the Temporary Strength Pill appeared.

The change was easy to spot.

> **System**
>
> **Item Window**
>
> **Temporary Strength Pill**
>
> **Type:** Elixir  
> **Grade:** ???  
> **Restriction:** Peak martial artist or higher  
> **Description:** A pill manufactured by **Dark Heaven**. It greatly raises the user’s latent power for approximately one shichen, but a price must be paid in return. Do not take it except in the worst-case scenario.  
> **Effect:** Combat-related stats +100
>
> **Internal energy:** +15 years
>
> **Body-Protecting Qi:** Available

*An unknown person* had disappeared, replaced by the unfamiliar name *Dark Heaven*.

*Judging by the context, it’s definitely some kind of organization…*

Well, anyone who made things like the Temporary Strength Pill was bound to be the same rotten lot as the Demonic Cult.

*Dark Heaven.*

Whoever had named them had one hell of a gift. Those two words alone announced that they were shady bastards.

*These fuckers definitely have something to hide. I’m ninety-nine percent sure.*

*Jin Wikyung and Wipeng seem to know something.*

The problem was that, judging by their reactions, they were extremely reluctant to reveal anything about Dark Heaven.

*Should I try prodding them?*

But before I could open my mouth, Jin Mukyung beat me to it.

“Dark Heaven? What is that?”

“Well…”

A troubled look crossed Jin Wikyung’s face.

“I’m sorry. I can’t tell you yet.”

Those words had come from a man who cherished his younger brothers more than anything.

If even Jin Wikyung refused to speak, there was no point asking Wipeng.

“My apologies, Young Masters, but I cannot tell you until we know more.”

His attitude was firmer than I had ever seen it. Jin Mukyung and I both realized that we would have to back down for now.

But the more they tried to hide it, the more curious I became about Dark Heaven.

*So it’s a secret they have to keep even from us.*

Setting aside the fact that Mukyung and I were direct descendants, we were both core masters of the Jin Family of Taiyuan. If Wipeng was Jin Wikyung’s right arm, either of us could more than qualify as his left arm—or at least one of his legs.

*Then it must be top-secret information known only to those two, even within the family.*

I was only human. Of course I was curious. The fact that the System had reacted now, despite remaining silent throughout the Mount Heng Sword Sect incident, only made it worse.

*Dark Heaven. The Temporary Strength Pill. A top-secret matter known only to Jin Wikyung and Wipeng.*

The keywords raced through my mind.

All right. I’d made up my mind.

*I’ll stay the hell out of it.*

Too much curiosity had a way of shortening your life. It had only been a few days since I’d gone to deliver some mail to the Mount Heng Sword Sect and nearly died.

A mysterious organization with an ominous name? Getting involved with them obviously wouldn’t end well.

“Come now, let’s stop talking about this and have another drink.”

Jin Wikyung forced the mood back to normal.

Wipeng had already demolished two bottles by himself, and even the injured Mukyung was filling his glass. How could I be the only one to sit out? I accepted the liquor Jin Wikyung poured and downed it.

Gulp, gulp.

The notoriously potent fire liquor burned down my throat in a rush of heat.

“Guhhh.”

Wow. This was no joke.

I’d known it was strong, but it was even more potent than I’d expected. At this strength, soju and beer couldn’t even compete.

While I shuddered from head to toe, the other three immediately refilled their empty glasses to the brim.

“Drink!”

“Pour it down!”

“Keep going till we drop!”

“….”

It wasn’t until we had drunk through the entire night that I learned Shanxi Province was part of North China—and that every man from North China could drink like a fish.

* * *

At noon the next day, Hyuk Mujin watched me mount my horse feeling perfectly refreshed and stared as though I were a monster.

“Is your stomach all right?”

“Yeah. It’s fine.”

“Don’t tell me you were the only one who didn’t drink last night. Or did you fall asleep halfway through?”

“No. All four of us kept drinking.”

“…You drank all of that?”

His mouth fell open.

“How is that possible? Are you even human?”

“It all went down.”

“Good heavens. How many bottles did you drink last night?”

He had the wrong unit.

Not bottles. Barrels.

We had kept draining enormous casks of liquor—the kind I’d only ever seen in pirate movies—one after another.

“I think we went through close to twenty barrels. I stopped counting after ten, so I’m not sure.”

“Wow. That’s incredible.”

Hyuk Mujin raised a thumb in admiration. Just then, the inn door swung open.

Three zombies—or rather, three Peak masters—emerged.

“Uuugh.”

“Urk.”

“Huff, huff.”

Pale faces, parched lips, and hollow eyes.

Every last one of them dragged his feet straight into the carriage. The martial artists of the escort force stared wide-eyed.

“Why are they suddenly taking the carriage…?”

“They look awfully unwell.”

“That can’t be right. Haven’t you ever drunk with our Commander? Wipeng, the God of Drinking? Never heard of him?”

“Wasn’t the Commander’s epithet Ghost Sword?”

“Whatever else you might say about him, when it comes to drinking, he could probably beat even the Martial God. They must be like this because all their accumulated fatigue finally caught up with them.”

As the martial artists whispered among themselves, the carriage door flew open. Someone darted out and doubled over.

“Urrp, buuurrgh!”

Splash!

Wipeng spent a good while spewing nothing but pale liquid, then staggered back into the carriage. One of the martial artists who had been talking so confidently muttered in a daze,

“…This can’t be happening.”

“Of course it can. Anyone can see that’s a hangover. They drank like that all night without sleeping. No wonder he’s sick.”

“Then why is the Third Young Master perfectly fine?”

Every eye in the escort force turned toward me.

The reek of alcohol pouring off my entire body was almost frightening. In complete contrast, my face looked impossibly refreshed and my breathing was calm.

“No way…”

“The Third Young Master beat the Commander? The God of Drinking?”

The courtyard erupted into murmurs.

Hyuk Mujin’s admiration had gone beyond awe and become outright reverence.

“Ah, of course! That’s our Captain—the man who used to drink with courtesans every damn day!”

“….”

“I remember what the Chief Steward said. If Captain had kept drinking for another three years, he would’ve drunk away the very pillars of our family. So that’s why you always had to steal from the family coffers!”

“…Hey, you punk.”

We weren’t alone. What did he think would happen to my image if he talked like that?

The stares raining down from every direction were already making my face burn.

“Ahem. Ahem!”

I cleared my throat and glanced around. And what do you know? The eyes of all those burly men were sparkling brighter than stars in the night sky.

“The true God of Drinking. That’s him.”

“He’s famous in Taiyuan’s red-light district. Haven’t you heard of the Night King?”

“The Night King? The epithet says it all. So he was already renowned for his drinking.”

“No, not that… You know. That.”

“Gasp. Is it true?”

“How would I know? I’ve never seen it.”

“Turns out he’s a true man among men.”

> **System**
>
> Those gathered here are impressed by your drinking capacity and prowess!
>
> **Fame** rises by 20!
>
> **Fame** rises by 22!
>
> **Fame** rises by 25!
>
> If a certain rumor spreads, you may obtain a related **Title**.

“….”

Why the fuck was my Fame shooting up?

And what was this about a related Title? No, thanks. Put it away. Please, just let me keep the Sleeping Dragon of Shanxi.

*Stop it, you lunatics…*

I turned away in inexplicable shame—only to find Hyuk Mujin staring intently at a certain part of my body.

“…What are you doing?”

“Oh, I was just measuring it by eye.”

He answered so matter-of-factly that I was almost lost for words. Hyuk Mujin held out his forearm with an innocent smile.

“Wow. As expected, you’re incredible. Hehe.”

In return for his forearm, I offered him my fist.

Thwack!
