# Chapter 1183

It was a nameless ruin.

Judging by its size and the number of buildings, it had once been a small city where several thousand households lived. But as everyone had expected, there was not a person to be found.

Only the mountain range stretching endlessly along the distant horizon remained to watch over the ghost town.

The Tianshan Mountains: a giant of nature wearing a bamboo hat of snow that never melted, no matter the season.

*The mountains of heaven, huh.*

I gazed through the window at the distant peaks.

Overwhelming. Almost awe-inspiring.

It was a sight that could only be called Tianshan. The peaks rose through the clouds, high enough that I could almost imagine immortals living somewhere up there.

No, that wasn’t such an absurd fantasy.

Replace *immortals* with *Demon King*, and fantasy became reality.

A horrifying reality.

“Captain?”

A voice suddenly broke into my thoughts.

Plenty of people called me their benefactor. Only one called me Captain.

I kept my eyes on the view outside. “Yeah.”

Maybe it was because my thoughts were tangled like a ball of string. My voice came out subdued, and Hyuk Mujin hesitated before speaking again.

“Um, have you eaten?”

“Sort of. Why?”

“Slau—no, Great Hero Mungyeong told me to stop by when I had a chance. He wanted to make sure you weren’t skipping meals.”

“He did? That’s unusual.”

It surprised me a little. We’d grown close during our time together, but I hadn’t thought he was the sort to worry about whether I’d eaten.

“He said these are special fasting pills he made before we set out, just in case. We’re running low on food already, and he said he’d kill you if you left any.”

“…Yeah, that sounds more like him.”

I shook my head and took out the fasting pill I’d tucked inside my clothes. Hyuk Mujin’s expression went flat.

“You said you ate.”

“I said *sort of*. I didn’t say I ate.”

“Honestly. I knew it.”

Maybe he was acting up because he hadn’t been hit enough lately.

Ignoring his grumbling, I popped the pill into my mouth. I chewed and swallowed through a bitterness I couldn’t begin to identify. Then, along with a surprising feeling of fullness, a System notification rang out.

*Ding.*

> **System**
>
> You have consumed Mungyeong’s Special Fasting Pill.
>
> Fullness will be maintained for one day.
>
> A small amount of fatigue has been recovered.
>
> All stats increase by +3 for one hour.

It tasted like shit, but it certainly worked.

Though for something made by the Divine Physician, its effects were a little disappointing.

I grimaced at the lingering taste, then caught Hyuk Mujin staring at me so intently it was uncomfortable.

“What?”

“Y-yes?”

“Why are you staring? Is there something on my face?”

“Uh, no. You just look really… handsome today. Hehe.”

His ingratiating flattery almost made me smile despite myself.

It was obvious what he was doing. My mood had been low lately, and he was trying to cheer me up.

I didn’t let on. I clicked my tongue instead.

“Enough nonsense. If you’re done here, get out. Don’t bother me just to make sure I eat.”

“Hey, what do you mean, *just*? We’re doing all this so we can keep living. What’s more important than food?”

“You sound like someone I know.”

“Are you seriously comparing me to Taishan, that A-Gwi of a bastard—”

His words trailed off.

For a while, Hyuk Mujin merely moved his lips, as though he’d said something he shouldn’t have. I turned back to the window.

After a brief silence, his voice sounded through the dusty room, quieter than before.

“Captain.”

“Go on.”

“Taishan… he’s doing all right, isn’t he?”

“He should be.”

“He eats so much, he might’ve cleaned out their food supply already. Sneaking food whenever he gets the chance, taking a few whacks from Old Man Nam, getting scolded by Sama Pyo…”

“Could be.”

“I’m sure that’s exactly what’s happening. Want to bet?”

“No.”

“Why not?”

“Because we’d both bet on the same thing.”

“Oh. Right.”

“Yeah.”

Silence returned, longer and much heavier than before.

Once again, Hyuk Mujin was the first to break it.

“Captain.”

“Yeah.”

“Everyone’s… doing okay, right?”

I didn’t answer.

Instead, I looked out at the landscape I’d watched for the past two days, a view that hadn’t changed for even a moment. It was overwhelming and awe-inspiring, yet it felt utterly empty.

I wondered why a landscape filled to the last detail looked to me like a blank sheet of paper.

No.

I thought about the people.

Jin Wikyung, who would run toward me shouting “Youngest!” without caring who saw him.

Jin Mukyung, watching his eldest brother rush ahead with a sullen expression.

Sama Pyo, now leading hundreds of subordinates like a proper Sect Leader, and Namho and Taishan, who had somehow become inseparable.

The Thousand Captain of the Embroidered Uniform Guard who’d thanked me with a face as stiff as wood. The ruler of the continent, whose body was dead, yet who seemed more full of life than ever.

I thought of everyone we’d parted from with smiles in Qinghai.

I thought of the past, already gone, and the future soon to come.

But why?

We’d left the desert behind. Two days had passed, and now the sun was sinking. Still, there was no sign of them.

No Murim Alliance banners rising like mountains in the west. No golden armor surging like a tide from the east.

That was it.

I hadn’t been looking at the landscape. I’d been looking for the people coming from somewhere far away.

Yet as the last day we’d agreed upon drew to a close, I still couldn’t find them.

*…Maybe.*

I forced down the three syllables that had surfaced unbidden.

Then, as though a wave could smooth away sand scattered across a beach, I told myself:

*No. That can’t be.*

They were strong.

Their blades and spears were sharp, and their resolve burned bright.

They were Murim. They were the Nine Provinces and all under Heaven.

So I held on tight to my belief.

This wait would be rewarded. However exhausted and dust-covered they were after their endless forced march, we would see each other again soon, smiling.

I believed it.

Believing was all I could do.

“Let’s do it.”

Hyuk Mujin had come to stand beside me without a word. Now he looked over from the window.

“Do what?”

“That bet from earlier. Let’s do it after all.”

I watched the world slowly turn red.

“They’ll come before midnight. I’m sure of it.”

Hyuk Mujin stared at me with wide eyes, then let out a quiet laugh.

“That can’t be a bet.”

“Why not?”

“Because we’d both bet on the same thing. That’s not a bet.”

“Damn. You’re right.”

“See?”

“So you’re not doing it?”

“No.”

He added firmly, “Of course I’m doing it.”

His voice drifted out the window on the wind.

Then the sunset weighed down on the world, and darkness came.

A darkness so still it was suffocating.

* * *

“We leave in fifteen minutes.”

A draft slipped in from somewhere, making the candle flicker precariously. Jeok Cheongang’s voice remained calm.

“Murim Alliance or Imperial Guards, those fools have forgotten the date we agreed on. Wait another day for them and I’ll start growing relics inside my own body. We’d be a hundred times better off going ahead as scouts and vanguard.”

On the surface, it made sense.

Perhaps Jeok Cheongang’s composure made it seem even more reasonable. He spoke as though he’d expected all this from the start.

The Slaughter Saint and Bow Saint nodded on either side of him. The others listened in silence.

But Jin Taekyung was different.

“You want us to go ahead?”

“Is there another way?”

“It’s not that I have another way. It’s that we can’t take this one.”

“Go on.”

“If we went back west and east—even a day’s travel, no, just half a day’s—we might run into our scouts…”

“Midnight has passed. The date we agreed on is behind us. We discussed what to do afterward in advance.”

“But…”

“Enough!”

*Whoosh!*

The air flared hot. His master fixed his Disciple with a level gaze after that sharp rebuke.

“I know what you fear, but moving armies of that size brings countless difficulties. We’re only going one step ahead according to the plan already made. No more arguing.”

Normally, Jin Taekyung would have obeyed Jeok Cheongang. Their relationship might have been more informal than most between master and Disciple, but Taekyung trusted and followed him more than anyone.

Normally.

Not today. Not at this moment.

An inexplicable sense of déjà vu came over him.

*What is it?*

Something was wrong. No, it was more than that.

Xinjiang had long been treated as a deathtrap under the Demonic Cult’s rule, but it wasn’t unknown territory.

Where else could the cult have drawn the immense resources to become practically a kingdom of its own, or the strength to wage war against the Murim of the Central Plains?

Xinjiang’s vast lands held substantial manpower and resources. It had once been one of the most important trade routes under Heaven.

The Hidden Shadow Pavilion had long since obtained information about the region, including its geography. Only a few months ago, it had sent in dozens of spies, though just one had returned alive.

But Tianshan was different.

If Xinjiang lay beneath the Demonic Cult’s shadow, Tianshan was its Sacred Land: the place where the cult had first risen and put down roots.

A truly unknown land within the deathtrap of Xinjiang.

*And no one had been able to reach those roots.*

Yet Jeok Cheongang had just said they were merely going one step ahead according to a “plan already made.”

A plan Taekyung had never heard of.

*That can’t be. Once we enter Tianshan, joining up with our allies will be next to impossible.*

His thoughts whirled. He felt his heart pounding and heat rising through him.

And all at once, he realized a truth he could hardly believe.

“You were lying. Just to deceive me.”

Taekyung’s voice trembled. Jeok Cheongang answered.

“From the beginning, only one thing was decided. If we failed to meet on the agreed date, we would advance without hesitation.”

His master’s heavy voice struck him like a war hammer.

“We… No. *You* are the main assault.”

His master’s heavy voice came down upon his Disciple like a war hammer.
