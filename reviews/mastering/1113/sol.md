# Chapter 1113

Nothing can be gained without sacrifice.

That was especially true of those who sought victory through war—the most terrible form of violence—in a savage age that had thrown law and humanity to the dogs.

But even knowing that cruel reality, there were some things one could never get used to.

Like realizing that someone who had been laughing and talking with you not long ago was gone.

“……So, that’s how it ended.”

After hearing what had happened while he was unconscious, Jin Taekyung muttered to himself and quietly closed his eyes.

Faces came to mind. So many people who, though he had known them only a short time, had looked at him with trust.

And among them, the stern face and voice of someone who had become familiar before he knew it.

*“It doesn’t matter who you are. The Embroidered Uniform Guard obeys only His Majesty the Emperor’s commands. If you stand in our way in defiance of an imperial decree, I’ll kill you.”*

*“Perhaps it’s because you’re a martial artist without even an identity tag, but your manners are atrocious.”*

*“Thank you. For protecting us—for protecting the imperial family.”*

Fleeting moments brushed faintly past his eyes and ears.

At the end of those memories was always the figure of a man who had pressed forward without wavering.

*“Jeong Hogun, Thousand Captain of the Embroidered Uniform Guard, answers the command of the Marquis of Shangshan!”*

Remembering the voice he would never hear again, Taekyung opened his eyes without a word and forced down something rising from deep in his chest.

The officer had been steadfast to a foolish degree, always calm and unafraid to stand tall.

Taekyung had lost consciousness before witnessing Jeong Hogun’s final moments, but he was certain the man had faced them the same way.

Because that was who he was.

The Embroidered Uniform Guards who had served under Taekyung over the past three months must have been the same. So must the defenders at the West Gate, who had fought with their lives on the line.

They had all fought to protect others and died valiantly.

Taekyung would never forget that their sacrifice had allowed so many people, himself included, to survive.

Neither would the person beside him, head bowed and shedding tears.

“……I’m sorry, Benefactor. I wasn’t good enough.”

Cheongpung’s eyes glistened, and his voice trembled.

Taekyung looked at him blaming himself and spoke calmly.

“You’re right. We weren’t good enough. Neither you nor I.”

“No. You did everything you could. If only I’d been a little stronger……”

“Young Hero Cheongpung.”

“Yes?”

“If only the Blood Lord—no, the Lord of Heaven—had never been born. That would’ve been nice, wouldn’t it?”

“……!”

“If, maybe, what if…… Get those stupid thoughts out of your head. Even if you’re sorry to the dead, even if you hate yourself like crazy for surviving, bear it.”

*Grit.*

Taekyung began to shift away from the Inner City wall he had been leaning against. Without realizing it, he clenched his teeth.

The pain was terrible, as if it reached every cell in his body.

So were the fury and self-reproach he had been struggling to hold back since regaining consciousness. No matter how hard he suppressed them, they kept surging up from deep in his chest.

His legs suddenly went slack like a puppet with its strings cut.

He caught the wall but staggered, and Cheongpung hurriedly reached out to support him.

Or tried to.

Before Cheongpung’s hand could touch him, Taekyung firmly shook his head.

“B-Benefactor.”

Cheongpung watched, his eyes wavering, as Taekyung forced himself upright. Beads of sweat ran down Taekyung’s forehead, and his face was deathly pale.

But what showed on the outside was not the whole story.

Cheongpung had poured his own internal energy into Taekyung to lend him strength. He knew better than anyone that the damage inside his body was even worse than it looked.

Some of the tears Cheongpung had shed earlier had come from the terrible feeling that he would not be able to keep Taekyung from dying.

But—

*Clack.*

Taekyung did not stop. He did not give up.

Bearing the pain with everything he had and suppressing the emotions boiling like lava in his chest, he gripped the wall and stood again.

Slowly. Stubbornly.

Each second felt like ten years as he forced the words out.

“Think about why we survived. Why they had to give their lives to save us.”

They could not fall apart. They must not.

If they gave up now, it would all have been for nothing.

The sacrifice of those who had died opening a path of retreat.

The sacrifice of others who might be dying even now.

“Apologize to the dead and cry your eyes out after we’ve finished everything we have to do.”

He wasn’t speaking only to Cheongpung.

Taekyung addressed everyone around him, those who stood with their heads bowed and their faces marked by desperation or defeat. He reminded them of the duty they had briefly forgotten in their exhaustion.

“Only then…… will we have shown them the respect they deserve and fulfilled our duty.”

*Step.*

Taekyung finally took a step after forcing out those last words.

“……!”

“……!”

The air around them thrummed. An unseen surge rose together with a wave of heat.

One step.

He had taken only one step.

He was a pitiful sight, a young man so covered in blood that no one would have been surprised if he collapsed on the spot.

Yet simply watching him made their hearts pound.

The heavy sense of defeat that had gripped them began to lift, and something hot filled the space it left behind.

*Thump. Thump. Thump.*

A pulse began to spread like ripples across a still pond. No one knew who had started it, or where.

Outside the Inner City, the war drums beat faster and faster, announcing the approach of death. But that did not matter.

Not now. Not while they stood with him—a martial artist who had made his way through a mountain of sabers and a forest of swords, a marquis of the Great Nation, and, beyond that, a fellow subject living in this vast realm.

For a little while, they could forget their fear.

And remember what they had forgotten.

*KABOOM!*

A thunderous crash sounded in the distance. The war drums, which had seemed as if they would beat forever, fell silent.

*Rumble-rumble-rumble!*

Countless enemies came sweeping down the avenue toward the Inner City, their footsteps shaking the ground.

*Clang-clang-clang!*

Steel rang out, and desperate screams filled the air.

*Thump-thump-thump-thump!*

Still, the defenders stamped their feet with all their strength and struck their weapons against the ground, coaxing the embers of their battle cry back into flame.

They thought of the countless people huddled deep inside the Inner City, trembling.

Their eyes, alight with renewed fire, fixed on one man.

“Remember this, all of you.”

His voice had grown clear. They watched the silver-white spearhead rise slowly.

“Remember what we’ve stayed alive for.”

*Shing.*

He spat out the words, and the spearhead gave off a frost-sharp aura.

*Whoosh.*

Behind Taekyung, who stood tall as an iron tower, strands of violet light rose one by one, shining brighter than torches against the darkness.

The energy of the Zaha Divine Technique spread like the glow of sunset. The eyes of the man wreathed in it were no longer wet.

His voice was steadier than ever.

“I’ll do it. No matter what.”

The battle was not over yet.

* * *

Red. Everything was red.

At least, everything one man could see was awash in corpses and blood.

*Slice!*

How many lives vanished in the space of a single deep breath?

Heads flew here and there. Limbs were severed. Some people died slowly in agony.

They had once been Daoists in fluttering white robes, as refined as immortals; soldiers fed by the nation they served; martial artists and common people. Now it was impossible to tell them apart.

Covered in blood from head to toe, they had become part of the hellscape painted across Xining’s main road.

The man who had drawn its first stroke—and would put the final mark on it—watched the slaughter unfold before him.

*What fools.*

He could not understand them.

What were they fighting for?

An Emperor whose bloodline alone had qualified him to rule?

Or that embarrassing notion they called “justice,” which did not even have a form?

*Ridiculous.*

Most of them had probably never seen the Emperor’s face. And the justice the martial artists of the Central Plains would die for was nothing more than something they had made up within their own little circle.

But the Lord of Heaven was different. He alone was different.

His power and overwhelming presence made the Blood Lord tremble simply by being near him.

In a world where might made right, what master could be more fitting?

*This loyal servant will fulfill your wish.*

The Blood Lord shot forward with a vow he could not tell whether he was making to the Lord of Heaven or to himself.

*Whoosh!*

A chillingly low whistle cut through the air. A moment later, the front line of defenders blocking the road to the Inner City crumpled, though they had not given up the fight.

A hundred bodies fell like puppets with their strings cut. Fountains of dark blood shot high above them.

*Splatter!*

Blood rained in every direction.

Among the defenders who witnessed the unbelievable sight, a trembling voice spoke.

“A-a monster……”

“Yes. I suppose that’s what I look like to you.”

The Blood Lord smiled and reached out.

*BOOM!*

A flash of blood-red light swelled and exploded. The blast swallowed the screams, scattering torn flesh and bone.

“But once he has taken the world, who will dare call me a monster?”

In that world, might would make right.

The day was not far off when a blood-mad monster would be hailed as a divine general sent by Heaven.

Though he had failed to take the life of the one man who had to die for that great undertaking—

“Open the way, you worthless moths.”

*Slice! Stab-stab-stab!*

To the Blood Lord, it was all only a matter of time.

The Inner City was close enough to see now. As long as fierce fighting continued at the other three walls, no one could stand in his way.

*Grand Mage, you bitch, stay out of this. Even I don’t want to kill one of the Lord of Heaven’s favored servant girls with my own hands.*

The Blood Lord was about to resume the slaughter when—

*Whoosh! BOOM!*

A sharp, dazzling flash blocked the steps of the monster who had seemed impossible to stop.
