# Chapter 147

Jin Wikyung let out a deep sigh.

“I have to work the moment I get back home.”

“Haven’t you been away for several days?”

“That was due to circumstances beyond my control!”

“Getting angry won’t make the work disappear.”

“Wipeng, have mercy. At this rate, I really am going to die of overwork.”

Despite his desperate, pitiful plea, Wipeng answered coldly.

“Finish your work before you die. I’ll give you a grand funeral.”

“……You’re a demon. Are you even human?”

Jin Wikyung had returned to the Jin Family of Taiyuan half a day earlier only to find a mountain of work waiting for him.

After nearly ten days away, hundreds of bamboo slips covered not only his writing desk but the floor as well.

“How am I supposed to do all this alone?”

“You can manage. You’ve done fine until now, so why start complaining?”

“Does our family really have so few people? No—are there really so few capable people in all of Shanxi Province?”

“Have you considered lowering your standards?”

“Are you saying this is my fault?”

“More than ten people have already come and gone. They all seemed like decent scholars to me, but you made the mistake of hiring only two.”

“Decent scholars? Please. Do you have no eye for people?”

They couldn’t hire just anyone simply because he had studied.

The Jin Family of Taiyuan was a Murim sect, after all. How much of the Four Books and Three Classics someone had memorized, or which renowned scholar he had studied under, didn’t matter.

Jin Wikyung wanted practical thinkers with flexible minds, not rigid scholars who spent their lives quoting Confucius and Mencius.

“Those two were the only useful ones among them.”

He still didn’t regret his choice. At his firm answer, Wipeng picked at his ear.

“Oh, really? Then where are those two now? What are they doing?”

“……Well, that…”

Jin Wikyung faltered, and Wipeng answered for him.

“They worked four straight nights without sleep, then ran away.”

“R-ran away? Who says they ran away? One of them had a gravely ill mother…”

“I looked into him after he disappeared. His mother died ten years ago.”

“……Really?”

“The other said he was going to the latrine, then slipped away and never came back. Am I wrong?”

“Ahem! Ahem!”

“My lord, you’ve trained in martial arts, so you can go without sleep for several nights and remain perfectly fine. Those men can’t. They’re ordinary commoners who have never read a single line of a cultivation technique formula in their lives.”

“I know that. That’s why I pay them so generously.”

“Another fifteen days and that silver would have gone to their surviving families.”

“……”

“Anything else to say?”

“……No.”

“Then start working. From now on, if the scholars who come looking for work seem useful, hire them all.”

Jin Wikyung nodded gloomily and reached for a bamboo slip.

Just then, one of the martial artists from the guard detail waiting outside the office cautiously entered with an unexpected report.

“Who did you say?”

Wipeng answered Jin Wikyung’s question.

“A messenger sent by the Deputy Military Commissioner of Shanxi Province.”

“I heard that much. But the Deputy Military Commissioner is… the man said to be Prince Shangshan’s closest aide and the true power behind him?”

“Yes. There was plenty of talk about a mere palace attendant securing such a high-ranking military post.”

“Right. I remember.”

Jin Wikyung had heard the rumors about the palace attendant who sat near the top of the military hierarchy and controlled the young prince single-handedly.

“We’ve never even met. What could he want all of a sudden?”

“What do you think?”

“Could this be because of the Third Young Master?”

“Given the circumstances, that seems highly likely. Bring the messenger in first.”

“Yes.”

“Ah, and call Mukyung too, just in case.”

“Understood.”

Not long after Wipeng nodded to one of his subordinates, a man entered the office.

His movements were precise and disciplined, and he wore light armor.

One glance was enough to tell that he belonged to the military. This was the messenger Hong Jin had sent.

“Are you the Lesser Family Head of the Jin Family of Taiyuan?”

Jin Wikyung nodded.

“I am Jin Wikyung. Let’s dispense with the tedious formalities. What brings you here?”

“I have come to deliver a message from the Deputy Military Commissioner.”

“Does this concern my younger brother?”

“Yes. He is currently on his way here with the Deputy Military Commissioner.”

“With him?”

“Yes. They should arrive in half a shichen.”

Jin Wikyung silently stroked his chin.

Hong Jin was effectively the second-most powerful man in Shanxi Province. He wasn’t someone who would move without good reason.

A letter alone would have been unexpected from a man with whom they had never had any dealings. The fact that he was coming in person made it all the more bewildering.

“I don’t recall making an appointment.”

Wipeng added coldly, his expression frosty.

“No matter how high-ranking an official the Deputy Military Commissioner may be, this is a clear insult to our family. Are you aware of that?”

“Th-that…”

Beads of sweat formed on the messenger’s forehead.

He had received some minor training in military martial arts. Faced with the gaze of the Peak master known as the Ghost Sword, he felt his heart drop.

“What business brings him here?”

“I-I was only ordered to deliver his message…”

“He acts as though he owns the place.”

Jin Wikyung came to the rescue of the increasingly anxious messenger.

“Wipeng, that’s enough. What exactly did the Deputy Military Commissioner say?”

“He asked me to convey his apologies in advance for the discourtesy of arriving so suddenly. And…”

The messenger took a small cylinder from inside his robes and handed it to Jin Wikyung. Inside the palm-sized cylinder was a rolled sheet of white paper.

“What is this?”

“This humble one was not told. He merely said you would understand once you received it.”

“Hmm.”

Jin Wikyung furrowed his brow and unfolded the paper.

At that moment, the office door opened and a second guest entered.

“Did you call for me?”

“……”

“Older brother?”

Jin Wikyung stared silently at the paper in his hand for a while before raising his head.

“You’re here.”

“Yes. I was training when I heard you were looking for me… Why did you call me?”

“We have an honored guest coming. We need to prepare to greet him.”

“An honored guest?”

“My lord, what do you mean, an honored guest? Do we really need to go out and greet him?”

Wipeng frowned in protest.

“He’s coming without so much as a warning. What kind of honored guest is that? He’s an uninvited guest.”

He didn’t restrain himself even with the messenger standing right there. Considering the Jin Family of Taiyuan’s current standing in Shanxi, he wasn’t entirely wrong.

Jin Wikyung quietly handed him the paper.

“Look at this, then say that again.”

“What is it?”

“Seeing is believing.”

“You could simply tell me instead of making such a fuss…”

Wipeng’s voice dwindled before cutting off completely. His trembling eyes darted back and forth across the paper.

A moment later, his lips parted.

“An honored guest is coming.”

“Right?”

“Yes. Absolutely.”

The two men turned toward Jin Mukyung, who was still standing there without a clue what was happening.

“Mukyung.”

“Second Young Master.”

“Yes?”

“You said you came straight from training.”

“You smell of sweat.”

“It’s only natural to sweat while training.”

“Go wash.”

“Wash immediately.”

Baffled by their reaction, Jin Mukyung asked in frustration,

“Who on earth is coming to make you act like this?”

Jin Wikyung and Wipeng answered simultaneously.

“A big spender.”

“An exceptionally big one.”

Wipeng waved the paper in his hand. It was a bank draft for a thousand nyang, issued by the Golden Star Exchange and valid anywhere under heaven.

“This isn’t just a thousand nyang. It’s a thousand silver nyang.”

A thousand silver nyang was worth one hundred thousand nyang in iron coins. To the Jin Family of Taiyuan, which was already pouring money out in every direction, it was a fortune as welcome as rain during a drought.

For the first time in a long while, Jin Wikyung gave his second brother a stern look.

“Mukyung, it’s time to wash.”

“……”

* * *

The moment I stepped out of the carriage, the words slipped out on their own.

“Wow, fuck…”

There was no way not to swear. High atop the towering wall, an enormous banner fluttered in the wind, emblazoned with huge letters:

**The Day the Deputy Military Commissioner Came to the Jin Family of Taiyuan**

*It’s not Buddha’s Birthday. What the hell is this?*

I was too embarrassed to raise my head. Hong Jin climbed down behind me, clutched his stomach, and burst out laughing.

“Wow. This is even more than I expected.”

“Are you and my eldest brother old ball buddies or something? How else did you get such an enthusiastic welcome…?”

“Young Master Jin, I don’t have balls.”

“Ah—oh. I’m sorry. I’m so sorry.”

That was a tremendous blunder. Without the stick, there was no way any fertilized eggs could have survived.

As I writhed beneath the weight of my guilt, Cheongpung approached to console me.

“Benefactor, my grandfather said people who can’t read the room never have any friends. But don’t worry. I’ll be your ball buddy.”

“……”

*I don’t need one, you bastard.*

As I struggled to swallow the curse, Hong Jin spoke.

“Young Master Jin, do you know what binds people together? Wealth. They say gold and silver can make even ghosts work for you. Imagine what they can do to the living. Don’t you agree?”

“So?”

“I told you I’d give him a present. It’s basically a bribe.”

*Money can make even ghosts work for you.*

I agreed to some extent, but I didn’t like that he was talking about Jin Wikyung.

Deep down, I already thought of him as my brother. Hearing Hong Jin paint him as a mercenary opportunist who could be bought with a bribe rubbed me the wrong way.

My displeasure must have shown on my face, because Hong Jin smiled.

“Was that too harsh? But it’s only natural. Who doesn’t like wealth?”

“He’s still my eldest brother. Don’t think you’ve won over the Lesser Family Head of the Jin Family of Taiyuan with a measly few silver nyang.”

“Young Master Jin…”

Hong Jin’s eyes widened at my low voice.

“A few silver nyang? I gave him a thousand.”

“A measly… How much?”

“A thousand silver nyang. One hundred thousand nyang in iron coins.”

By now, I had a rough grasp of Murim prices and currency.

A private suite at the Phoenix Inn—the equivalent of a luxury hotel—cost fifty silver nyang a night. Apparently, that was nearly twice the annual living expenses of an ordinary family of four.

*In modern money, that would be tens of millions of won.*

A thousand silver nyang was twenty times that. In other words, Hong Jin had casually tossed over several hundred million won at them in one go.

“That’s… a lot.”

“It is. Even I dug deep this time.”

“Still, that’s far too much.”

“It’s an investment in our future relationship. Besides, the Jin Family of Taiyuan is probably spending money faster than it can bring it in right now. Winning a war and occupying your enemy’s territory isn’t the end of it.”

“Ah, right.”

“Help means the most at times like this. I learned that after giving and taking so many bribes myself. Ah, of course…”

Hong Jin winked.

“I also spent a little extra because I’ve taken quite a liking to you, Young Master Jin. You know how I feel, don’t you?”

The instant he finished speaking, something hard pressed against my butt.

The repeated poking sensation jolted me wide awake.

*No way. Is this bastard seriously…?*

I swear, it was the most spine-chilling moment of my entire life.

*Fine. You die, I die. Let’s have another war!*

I spun around at lightning speed.

There, I found a lump of silver about half the size of my palm. What was that called again? A silver yuanbao?

“Here. Some pocket money from me.”

*Oh, right. He’s a eunuch.*

I calmed my pounding heart and answered.

“Th-thank you.”

“Sure. Go buy yourself some candied hawthorn skewers.[^1]”

“Benefactor, could you take me with you when you buy them?”

Cheongpung smacked his lips as he joined in.

Just then, a familiar voice rang out behind us.

“Heh heh. I’ll tell the cooks, so ask for as many as you like. Isn’t that right, Wipeng?”

“We’ll build you a mountain of candied hawthorn skewers.”

“Who is that fellow? Candied hawthorn skewers? He isn’t a child.”

I didn’t have to look to know who it was.

I turned around with a delighted smile, only to find myself speechless.

*Flutter. Flutter.*

Jin Wikyung and Wipeng stood there grinning broadly. Jin Mukyung’s face was bright red.

All three held small scraps of cloth that fluttered in the wind. I had no idea when they had found the time to make them.

> **Long live the Great Nation! Long live His Imperial Majesty the Emperor!**
>
> **His Highness Prince Shangshan, may you become a sage king!**

“……”

“……”

*What had I said to Hong Jin earlier?*

*Had I told him not to think he’d won over the Lesser Family Head of the mighty Jin Family of Taiyuan with a measly few silver nyang?*

*Fuck, forget winning him over.*

He was completely cooked.

[^1]: Candied hawthorn skewers are a traditional snack made by coating fruit in hardened sugar.
