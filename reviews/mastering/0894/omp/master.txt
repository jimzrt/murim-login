# Chapter 894

“Benefactor. No, Pavilion Master.”

At the sight of Jin Taekyung rising with a hardened expression, Ju Hwaran hurried to stop him.

Or tried to.

“Let him go.”

A quiet voice reached her ear.

Her hand froze halfway to Jin Taekyung. Ju Hwaran stared at the door, shut tight again after a gust of wind and rain, then turned around.

“Was it really all right to let him go like that…?”

Jeok Cheongang, who had stopped her at the last moment, answered calmly.

“Who knows? How should this old man know?”

“Old Master!”

Jeok Cheongang smiled at her reproachful cry.

For a century and several decades, he had roamed Murim. He had seen all kinds of people over those years, and the enemies he had brought down were too many to count.

The name Fire King Jeok Cheongang belonged to history, past and present.

Even the leaders of the Nine Sects and One Gang and the Five Great Families wouldn’t dare raise their voices in front of him.

So what had made this child so angry in front of the Fire King himself?

Jeok Cheongang knew the answer.

He also knew why his one and only Disciple had stormed out.

“I knew a monk for quite a long time. Night after night, he’d sneak off somewhere secluded to drink liquor and eat meat. A sham monk if ever there was one.”

Ju Hwaran paused at the abrupt story, then spoke carefully.

“Could you be talking about…?”

“That’s right. That sham monk was Hong Dao, the Dharma King.”

Their friendship was widely known.

Fire King Jeok Cheongang had a temper as fiery as his title, and hundreds—no, thousands—more enemies than friends. Of the few friends he did have, Hong Dao was the only one who could calm him with a few words.

Jeok Cheongang didn’t know it, but apart from his martial arts, Hong Dao’s ability to keep his foul temper in check had played a large part in earning him the title Dharma King.

Of course, the chief reasons Hong Dao commanded the respect of martial artists everywhere were his gentle nature, worthy of a great and virtuous monk, and his mysterious prophetic gift.

“Sometimes he’d read the heavenly patterns and learn the will of Heaven. It was a wondrous thing to watch.”

By now, everyone, Ju Hwaran included, was listening quietly.

The Dharma King was that important a figure in the Central Plains Murim. Even if he hadn’t been the Abbot of Shaolin Temple—the Mount Tai and Northern Dipper of the Murim—that wouldn’t have changed.

“But one year before that sham monk entered Nirvana, he told me something.”

Jeok Cheongang drew out a memory etched deep in his mind.

It was the memory of a night he could never return to, one that came back whenever he thought of the friend who had died before him.

“He said the heavenly patterns were going awry. They had been for decades already. Before long, storm clouds of an unknown calamity would gather and cover the sun, the moon, and even the stars.”

“Dark Heaven…!”

The words escaped someone’s lips.

Jeok Cheongang nodded and continued in a low voice.

“That was when I began to take the existence of Dark Heaven seriously.”

But that wasn’t all.

Four months before that conversation, Hong Dao had looked up at the sky and seen something shining amid the turbulent darkness.

“The Morning Star.”

Jeok Cheongang let out the breath he’d been holding.

A morning star was supposed to shine brilliantly for a moment, then fade.

But the one that had begun to shine clearly in the north never faded.

If anything, its light grew stronger by the day.

It kept shining as an old man and a young man, brought together by what seemed like chance but felt like fate, left Shanxi and reached Henan.

It kept shining through the year they spent together on Mount Jiuhua, until the venerable monk who had first discovered it became a handful of relics.

And perhaps it was still shining now.

“Do you know what the Dharma King said when he told me about the Morning Star?”

No one answered, and Jeok Cheongang didn’t wait for them to.

“He said an insolent brat I’d taken under my wing was the master of that Morning Star.”

“…!”

“He said that even if the whole world were plunged into darkness one day, that Morning Star would never lose its light.”

Everyone stared, mouths agape.

The subject of this astonishing secret from the past, unknown until now, was someone they all knew well.

*Jin Taekyung.*

The same name struck every one of them.

The shock was greater than any of them could have imagined.

Hong Dao himself, the Dharma King, had named him the master of the Morning Star that would light the world. A star that would never lose its light, even at the end.

But the Morning Star pointed neither to the Martial God, now a legend of the past, nor to the Sword Saint, who was writing a legend of his own as the new Murim Alliance Leader.

It pointed to Jin Taekyung.

A young man barely past twenty who had nevertheless shone brighter than anyone amid the calamity sweeping the land.

Clunk. Whoosh.

In the silence that had settled over the pavilion, a fierce gust of wind and rain flung open the firmly shut door.

Rain and wind swept among them, no weaker than before, but no one spoke.

As if by agreement, they turned their heads and gazed into the darkness outside, their eyes still wide with shock.

Toward the place where a star had been moments ago.

Toward the master of the Morning Star, somewhere out there.

“I once heard someone say this.”

Jeok Cheongang spoke quietly.

“Where a star shines, darkness gathers. But the star never disappears.”

At first, Jeok Cheongang himself had been skeptical.

A calamity that would dwarf the Great Faction War?

Jin Taekyung, so young he was practically an infant, the light amid that calamity?

But the more time passed, the longer he watched Jin Taekyung at his side, the more he understood.

*This brat is the light. A new star to guide us through the darkness that will one day cover the land.*

And…

*The only light Heaven has granted this old man.*

Just as stars shone brighter in the dark, darkness was always waiting wherever Jin Taekyung went.

Yet none of it had dared touch him. None had extinguished the light he gave off.

And even if such a day came, it would not be this time.

Complete darkness had not yet fallen over the land. Only then would the Morning Star that was Jin Taekyung shine its biggest and brightest.

*Until then… find your own path. Shine on whatever you choose.*

Jeok Cheongang smiled faintly.

He had long since passed the age of weighing the rights and wrongs of the world.

There was no justice in the Murim he had known—or in this world at all.

Only monsters consumed by their own causes, pointing spears and swords at one another.

Just like the bewildering events unfolding here in the imperial palace.

*This old man is no different.*

Growing old meant growing numb.

Death had become cheap, and the people he had struggled to let into his heart had begun to leave, one by one.

His first Disciple, Jangcheon. The Dharma King.

The justice Jeok Cheongang had chosen, his great cause, was to protect what he had left.

He only hoped to make even a slightly better choice along the way, as the one Disciple who had appeared so unexpectedly late in his life was trying to do.

To Jeok Cheongang, that was the only difference between the orthodox faction and practitioners of demonic, heterodox arts.

*If the strokes are crooked and clumsy, does that change what the word means?*

However poor the handwriting, the meaning of the character for *upright* did not change. It was merely unsteady, like a child’s writing.

And his Disciple, Jin Taekyung, despised assassins who didn’t even try to make that effort. He suspected Ma Sanbao’s motives for suddenly bringing martial artists into their cause.

*Whatever you decide, it doesn’t matter. This old man will always be at your side.*

Jeok Cheongang gazed into the darkness through the door the wind had thrown wide open.

The imperial palace was unknown territory, full of secrets.

Nothing that happened next would surprise him.

No—a storm was already raging, so vast it was impossible to tell friend from foe.

*If we can’t avoid it, we’ll have to face it.*

Jeok Cheongang smacked Taishan across the back of the head. Taishan had been licking an empty plate clean.

Smack!

“Quit doing the dishes and close the door, you unorthodox bastard.”

* * *

It didn’t take long for me to come face-to-face with Ma Sanbao.

He found me before I found him.

“He’s waiting for you.”

I was heading toward the pavilion assigned to the East Depot, picturing the layout I’d already learned, when a eunuch in a close-fitting martial uniform approached and spoke. My eyes narrowed.

*How did he know?*

I’d used the darkness and the surroundings to keep myself thoroughly concealed, moving as little as possible.

However skilled the East Depot’s eunuchs were at concealment techniques, it was hard to believe they could spot someone so far above them with such ease.

But…

*Damn it.*

Right now, I had to set the question aside and keep going. I stepped quietly out of the darkness, and the eunuch led me to a small pavilion.

“He’s waiting inside.”

I quickly surveyed the area.

The imperial palace was divided into countless pavilions and sections for its various departments.

But this wasn’t the East Depot’s section, according to the palace map I’d obtained from Ma Sanbao beforehand.

“Wait. This place…”

“Isn’t a building assigned to the East Depot. Officially, that is.”

“…”

“Then I’ll take my leave.”

A secret refuge inside the palace, somewhere they could meet away from prying eyes. Something like that?

The eunuch vanished before I could ask anything else. I went inside.

Creak.

For once, the foul weather was useful.

The old wooden door’s groan was swallowed by the pounding rain.

*Ma Sanbao is… upstairs.*

I could sense just one person on the floor above. I climbed the stairs, which creaked almost as badly as the door, and saw a lone figure standing in the pitch-black room.

“You came?”

Teeth far whiter than those of most people in this era flashed faintly in the darkness.

Ma Sanbao glanced around the room, empty of so much as a candle or a piece of furniture, and shrugged.

“Forgive the humble setting. Too many eyes are watching.”

“This is fine. I can put up with this much.”

“This much? Sounds like there’s something else you can’t put up with.”

“Since you already know, we should be able to finish this conversation quickly.”

The voice coming out of my mouth sounded like a stranger’s. My expression was probably just as stiff.

“As expected. You’re astonishingly honest.”

“I hear that a lot.”

How often did anyone speak that way to Ma Sanbao, the East Depot’s de facto leader?

But he didn’t react as I’d expected.

“So, what do you want to hear about?”

His face was calm, as if he’d known this was coming. Then he spoke again, his voice just as composed.

“The assassins I hired? Or… that woman, So Gyo?”

“…!”
