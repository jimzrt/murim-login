I stared at the Quest window floating in midair.

The name on the first line told me that the shriveled corpse before us belonged to one of only three Grand Mages in the world.

*Siegfried Wassmann.*

Siegfried, also known as the “Hero.” The greatest Hunter ever produced by Switzerland, a permanently neutral country, and a Grand Mage with exceptional talent for summoning and barrier magic.

If Magic Johnson was a War Mage who specialized in combat and killing, Siegfried was one of the few scholars who had devoted himself to the study of magic itself.

At least until today, when we found him dead in his own hideout.

“Was he like this when you arrived?”

Magic Johnson nodded, his expression grim.

“Yes. I could hardly believe it myself. I checked several times before I accepted that the body was Siegfried’s.”

“How often did you keep in touch?”

“Once or twice a year after he went into hiding. Even then, I’d have to contact him ten times or so before he answered, and it took him ages. This is my first time here. He was intensely private and hated visits like this.”

“Then the last time you heard from him was…”

“Hmm. About three years ago, I think.”

“Three years. I heard he went into hiding nearly ten years ago.”

“The last time we met, just before he retired, he gave me a communication artifact he’d made himself. Told me to use it if anything was truly urgent.”

I took the artifact Magic Johnson pulled from inside his clothes. It was a crystal ball about the size of an adult’s fist, and it held a considerable amount of mana—as if to prove a Grand Mage had made it.

*Of course. Ordinary signals wouldn’t get through all the barriers around this place. He probably wanted it that way.*

I’d never met Siegfried Wassmann, but by all accounts he was a shut-in. He avoided attention of any kind, and once a subject caught his interest, he’d dig into it until he’d dissected every last part of it.

“I contacted him every Thanksgiving and Christmas, but he stopped answering three years ago. I knew what he was like, so I let it go. Being called a hero made him uncomfortable, and he hated attention from the press. Even after the Great Cataclysm ended, he’d sometimes disappear into his research for long stretches.”

I couldn’t make sense of it.

A Grand Mage who had gone into hiding to escape the public eye had died like this in his own refuge.

One thing, though, was certain.

“This wasn’t an ordinary natural death. Whatever killed him, a body couldn’t end up like this in just three years.”

Maybe not even in three hundred.

Would a shut-in Grand Mage whose hobby was magic research and whose specialty was staying home run his hideout on gas and electricity?

The enormous cavern was accessible only through layers of barriers and filled with every kind of magic. Everything, including the temperature and humidity, was perfectly controlled.

And Siegfried’s body showed none of the rot or stench of ordinary decay.

*It’s just… dried out. As if something sucked every bit of life from it.*

I’d never seen a death this strange, even in Murim.

Which left me with more questions. How had he died in his own hideout? And how deeply was his death connected to my enemies?

“Michael Silbert. And The Prophet.”

The two likeliest suspects.

Magic Johnson understood what I meant. “If I had to choose one of them, I’d pick the first without hesitation.”

“Why?”

“Because we know there’s a connection between Michael and Siegfried. From everything we’ve seen, Michael Silbert must know Sky is unconscious. Otherwise, he’d never have joined forces with terrorists to pull off something this insane.”

It was a plausible theory. Siegfried had built A Area inside Ares Guild’s headquarters, and he could well have learned the truth about Cheon Taemin’s condition while doing it.

But…

“I honestly don’t know the answer to this. Was Siegfried close to Michael Silbert?”

“That…”

“Then how likely is it that he told Michael a secret that big?”

Magic Johnson paused and let out a low groan.

“Unlikely. No, you might as well call it impossible. Very few people had any relationship with Siegfried, myself included. And there was only one person he ever said he truly respected.”

I had a feeling I knew the answer.

“Cheon Taemin.”

Magic Johnson nodded. “That’s right. Siegfried genuinely respected Sky. From what he told me, it was practically worship. He even spoke about Sky in an interview once.”

“Oh. I think I remember that.”

I had a vague memory of seeing the interview. There were online profiles full of information about well-known Hunters, Siegfried Wassmann among them. I’d heard the interview caused a stir at the time: it was unusual enough for him to speak to the press, let alone express such strong feelings about anyone.

“Perhaps… that’s why Lee Jungryong commissioned Siegfried to build A Area.”

Team Leader Choi had spoken up. He continued slowly.

“Siegfried Wassmann was a master of his field, and he had few personal ties. He also deeply respected my maternal grandfather. Lee Jungryong could trust him to keep the secret.”

Magic Johnson gave a small nod.

“That’s true. Even I knew nothing about A Area, and I was one of the people closest to him. Hell, I never thought he’d accept a commission from anyone.”

“At least Lee Jungryong chose well when it came to secrecy,” Team Leader Choi said. “But if Siegfried didn’t tell anyone, that leaves a question…”

“How did Michael Silbert find out?”

I muttered the question as I examined the body again, searching every inch for signs of torture. Even with every sense sharpened, I found nothing new.

*If this wasn’t just a lizard cutting off its tail to escape, Michael must have had a reason to kill him.*

I couldn’t prove it yet, but Michael Silbert was still our likeliest suspect. Finding out what had happened here could be the first step toward bringing him down.

*There’s something. There has to be…*

I kept searching the body, repeating those words to myself. At last, frustrated, I looked up and saw someone standing nearby.

Or rather, a monster shaped like a person.

“Why are you standing there trying to look cool? You’ve been quiet this whole time.”

The Skeleton King stood with his arms crossed, deep in thought. He frowned.

“Silence, foolish human. This body was engaged in profound contemplation.”

“What kind of bullshit were you contemplating now?”

“Can you not feel it?”

I stopped. “Feel what?”

“This space. The concentration of magical power is higher here than elsewhere.”

If he’d said *mana*, I would have argued immediately. My Qi Sense was better than anyone else’s here, and I hadn’t noticed anything unusual.

But magical power was another matter. The Skeleton King was an S-rank monster. When it came to sensing magical power, even I couldn’t match him.

“Large human. Did you not feel it?”

Magic Johnson scratched the back of his head.

“I did, though not as clearly as you. I just didn’t think it was strange.”

“You are infinitely more foolish than this body. Why not?”

“This isn’t just a house. It’s a laboratory stocked with everything Siegfried could want. Search one storeroom and you’d probably find mountains of monster corpses and Magic Gems.”

“…Huh?”

Oh. He had a point.

Filter filthy water all you like and it still won’t be perfectly clear. Magic Gems and monster corpses were much the same: even after purification, some magical power remained.

Siegfried’s hideout was full of things that held magical power. No wonder the concentration was high.

“I see what you’re thinking,” Johnson went on, “but even if monsters are the reason there’s so much magical power here, that doesn’t tell us anything. If a monster killed Siegfried, there should at least be signs of a fight.”

“…H-huh?”

I was an idiot for getting my hopes up.

The Skeleton King could only blink at Johnson’s perfectly reasonable objection. I muttered, “Fucking moron.”

“How dare this treacherous human insult—”

“Shut up before I pull your jawbone off. I’ll let it slide since you actually tried thinking for once.”

“…”

“Don’t go confusing the investigation. My head’s about to explode as it is.”

The Demon Realm was far away. My fist was close.

The Skeleton King closed his mouth, looking thoroughly displeased. Team Leader Choi, who had continued examining the body, clicked his tongue.

“If this was an assassination, there should be at least a small wound. But there isn’t one. The body is clean.”

“Then…”

“Magic is the only explanation I can think of.”

Yet even Magic Johnson, a Grand Mage himself, looked troubled.

“I specialize in offensive magic, but I’ve never seen or heard of anything like this. It’s far more complex and sinister than the magic we use. Closer to that fellow’s work, if anything.”

“That fellow,” who had been keeping his mouth firmly shut, frowned.

“This body merely places vengeful spirits into dead flesh. This body knows nothing of such wicked magic.”

“…”

“…”

“…”

*Isn’t that already fucking wicked magic?*

But if he said he didn’t know, I couldn’t make him know. Besides, he was nothing like an ordinary monster. He had no reason to lie.

*Damn it. It has to be him.*

Michael Silbert.

That name kept circling through my mind.
