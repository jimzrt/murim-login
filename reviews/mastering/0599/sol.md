# Chapter 599

All the way back to the estate after my visit, I sensed a subtle change in the air.

Their glances and movements looked natural enough, no doubt thanks to rigorous training. But they couldn’t escape my senses.

*They definitely suspect something.*

The A-rank Hunters of the President’s Security Service.

They’d said they were there to provide temporary protection for my family and me, but I wasn’t naive enough to take their word for it.

*If they notice even the slightest thing out of place, they’ll report it straight up the chain.*

They weren’t Peace Guild members or ordinary Hunters. They were government employees whose direct superior was one person: the President.

President Baek Hanseong might already have received a report about today’s visit—my private meeting with Go Se-won, and the minute or two when no one had been able to hear what we said.

*What should I do?*

I stared through the specially treated window that kept anyone from seeing inside the vehicle.

Go Se-won had told me there was another secret area. Now I had to decide when to reveal that information, and to whom.

I didn’t have to think for long.

*I can’t do anything right now anyway.*

Only two days had passed since that day. The world was still in turmoil, and people had yet to recover from their grief and shock.

Even Team Leader Choi, who needed to know more than anyone, was no exception.

So I had to keep my mouth shut at least until Kim Hwajong’s funeral was over.

It wasn’t only for Team Leader Choi’s sake. I needed time to sort things out myself.

“Hah.”

At my quiet sigh, the security officer in the driver’s seat glanced at me in the rearview mirror.

“Is something bothering you?”

“No. It’s nothing.”

I sank deeper into the seat as I lied.

The truth was, I was uncomfortable. Everything around me made me uncomfortable.

I kept that thought to myself and looked out the window.

The scenery slipped past, and time passed just as quickly.

January 19.

The flames that had erupted that day had not died down. No one could contain them; they only kept burning higher.

The media in Korea and around the world threw firewood and oil onto the flames, while public opinion fanned the growing blaze.

The prosecutors’ office issued countless search and arrest warrants. Several overseas branch directors sensed what was coming and even tried to flee.

The world remained chaotic, and people could barely keep up with each day’s news.

In a public apology, President Baek Hanseong promised that nothing like this would ever happen again.

At the same time, under the slogan of cleansing society, he set his sights on the massive Guilds that had grown too powerful to touch.

Prominent figures and heads of state around the world continued to defend the Peace Guild.

Go Jun had committed crimes too horrifying to imagine, and Ares Guild was riddled with corruption. Even its former Vice Guild Master, Lee Jungryong, could not escape condemnation.

Yet among all the truths coming to light, one question remained unanswered.

Why? Why, even now, had *he* not appeared?

Why had Cheon Taemin not shown himself?

Everyone wondered, but no one could find the answer.

The whereabouts of Cheon Taemin, the immortal hero who had vanished without warning one day, remained shrouded in mystery.

He had not appeared when an Arc Lich emerged in China several months earlier and caused astronomical damage. Nor had he appeared at the national funeral of Lee Jungryong, who had been practically his sworn brother.

Not even when Jin Taekyung reduced the headquarters of Ares Guild—the Guild Cheon Taemin himself led—to ruins…

Cheon Taemin still did not appear. Questions about his inexplicable seclusion grew and grew.

And while the chaos and uncertainty had yet to subside, a massive national funeral was held for the victims of the January 19 Incident.

* * *

Korea, usually so noisy, was quiet that day.

The government declared a temporary public holiday. Roads that should have been packed with cars were empty, and the news reported in solemn tones that nearly thirty million people had gathered at locations across the country to mourn.

And… I was here.

At the place where those who had served with honor and devotion were laid to rest.

The National Cemetery.

*Step.*

Perhaps spring was slowly approaching. The grass at the end of January was green and full of life.

A breeze brushed my face, and the sunlight was warm.

It was a beautiful day. A day to send someone off, and to remember them again.

> **January 19. Remembering them.**

I looked up from the short phrase engraved at the bottom.

Dazzling sunlight stung my eyes above the tall memorial. Among the names of more than two thousand victims carved into its surface, I found one I knew.

> **The late Kim Hwajong**

I was suddenly glad the weather was so fine.

He had shivered in the cold until the moment he died. On a warm day like this, with countless people there to pay their respects, I thought he could finally rest.

*It must be warm now. Isn’t that right, Butler Kim?*

His ashes would be buried somewhere other than this memorial, but I believed part of his spirit remained here too.

I had just asked Kim Hwajong a question that could never reach him when a low voice spoke beside me.

“Butler Kim will rest peacefully.”

Team Leader Choi gazed at the memorial with sorrow in his eyes, then added, “But we’ll be even busier from now on.”

I nodded.

It was time to tell him what I had kept hidden.
