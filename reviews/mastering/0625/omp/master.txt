# Chapter 625

People have something called an aura.

Some people’s auras are so faint and hazy they can barely be felt. Others are powerful and rough as waves, overwhelming anyone who faces them.

The Beast Miao King, Yayul Cheok, belonged to the latter sort.

*Rustle.*

Yayul Cheok slowly rose to his feet, like a mountain beginning to move.

Though he was well over eighty, the muscles covering his body were as solid as armor. He walked without hesitation, his half-gray beard billowing like a lion’s mane.

*Thud. Thud.*

Everyone could see him now as he came down the vine-covered steps, trampling them beneath his bare feet.

Yayul Mok and his guards immediately dismounted and dropped to one knee. It was their way of showing respect to the lord of the Nanman Beast Palace.

“Greetings, Palace Lord.”

The Beast Miao King gazed at Yayul Mok.

“You ran off again without a word.”

“There was a fire in the northeastern pasture. I happened to be nearby…”

“Did you put it out?”

“Yes.”

“And the culprit?”

The question was clearly for Yayul Mok, but the Beast Miao King was looking at me.

I met the fierce light in his tiger-like eyes and stepped forward.

“‘Culprit’ is putting it a bit strongly. There was an accident.”

“It has been a long time since I’ve seen Han Chinese. Are you the culprit who started the fire?”

Before I could answer, he went on in a growling voice.

“Think carefully before you answer. Nanman’s laws are not merciful.”

I thought for a moment. “What’s the punishment for something like this?”

“That depends on the damage. In serious cases, the offender is fed to wild beasts. Or thrown into a pit full of venomous beasts.”

“What about minor cases?”

“Hanged from a vine.”

“That’s minor?”

“Of course. The body remains intact.”

“Ah.”

I nodded, stepped casually aside, and introduced someone.

“Ta-da. He’s the culprit, actually.”

An ordinary person might have panicked, but a Hidden Shadow Pavilion agent with fifty years of experience was made of sterner stuff. Namho spoke calmly.

“Are you fucking insane?”

“I’m not wrong, am I?”

“You fucking bastard…”

Namho was about to launch into a full stream of abuse when the Beast Miao King cut in, his voice rough.

“So you mean to abandon your companion to save your own skin.”

“Hmm. I think you’ve misunderstood. I never said I was abandoning him.”

“You didn’t?”

“I’m just getting the facts—no, setting the record straight. We haven’t known each other long, but he’s a good man. And there’s the Murim Alliance’s reputation to consider.”

I had brought up the Murim Alliance deliberately. He could have let the matter drop there, but the fierce light in his eyes did not dim.

“Once you enter Nanman, you follow Nanman’s laws. What if one of you must take responsibility?”

“If you insist…”

I scratched the back of my head.

“We’d have to stop you somehow, wouldn’t we?”

At that moment—

*Whoom!*

The air around me seemed to vanish. A fist shot toward me before I even heard its short, heavy rush through the air. It carried the force of a battering ram. I crossed my arms to meet it.

*Boom!*

A roar like the sky splitting open rang out, and the shock wave swept in every direction.

The tremendous force drove me back a full ten feet. I sighed.

“Will that do?”

The Beast Miao King’s eyes flashed coldly. “Will what do?”

“Our first greeting.”

“What?”

“Before I came to Nanman, someone told me you might look ignorant and reckless, but you’re actually warmhearted and fond of pranks. He said to play along and get along with you.”

“……!”

“……!”

The Fire Dragon Pavilion members stood with their mouths open. That was nothing compared to Yayul Mok and his guards. They stared at me in horror, then reached for their weapons at lightning speed.

Before they could act, the Beast Miao King’s twitching lips gave way to a great burst of laughter.

“Ha ha ha! Old Master Jeok hasn’t changed a bit.”

I rubbed my stinging forearms. “He’s as hale as ever.”

“I heard he’d taken a Disciple, but I didn’t expect to meet you so soon.”

He looked me over with fresh interest, then grinned.

“Good to meet you. This old man is Yayul Cheok.”

With anyone else, things might have been different. But before he was lord of the Nanman Beast Palace, he was someone Jeok Cheongang knew.

I clasped my hands respectfully toward the Beast Miao King.

“Jin Taekyung, nineteenth successor of the Fire Gate Clan, pays his respects to Great Hero Yayul Cheok, the Beast Miao King.”

“Good! Ha ha ha!”

He laughed again, rough and hearty, then spoke with unmistakable warmth.

“We have much to discuss. Follow me.”

Everyone stared after him as he strode away, then turned to me. Their faces all asked the same question.

I shrugged. “He’s pretty close with my master.”

It really was all about school ties, hometown ties, and family ties.

* * *

The Beast Miao King looked happy. No, he definitely was happy.

Jeok Cheongang had told me he was usually cheerful, but as he sat in the Nanman Beast Palace’s main hall telling old stories, he looked like an oversized child.

“I remember the day I first met Old Master Jeok. He took one look at me and hit me with a Flame Divine Palm.”

“…The Old Master—no, my master—said he didn’t hit you. He said he almost did.”

“No, he definitely hit me. It knocked the breath out of me and scared the shit out of me.”

The Sect Leaders and Family Heads of the great factions I’d met so far generally maintained some dignity. The Beast Miao King walked around in nothing but tiger-skin pants and didn’t give a damn about dignity.

He showed me the mark the Flame Divine Palm had left on his side, then added as if it were nothing.

“Quite a first meeting. I shit blood twice, went back to see him, and he poured me a drink to disinfect the wound. What a hearty man.”

“…Ah. Right.”

I wondered whether *hearty* meant what I thought it did. But if the man who’d been hit was fine with it, what could I say?

Besides, criticizing Jeok Cheongang would be like spitting in my own face.

“I regretted not seeing Old Master Jeok after the Great Faction War ended. Meeting his Disciple like this is a pleasure. Do you drink?”

“Of course I do, but I can’t right now.”

“Why not?”

“Something’s more urgent than a drink.”

If the world had been at peace, I’d gladly have thrown a drinking party. I’d have toasted the Beast Miao King, shouted, “Yayul Cheok’s favorite—random games!” and blown the non-Han people away with every drinking game I knew.

But not now.

“This time, I’m not here as my master’s Disciple. I’m here as a Pavilion Master of the Murim Alliance.”

The smile on the Beast Miao King’s lips faded a little.

“Because of Dark Heaven?”

“So you know about them.”

“I received a messenger eagle concerning them. We’re tens of thousands of li from the Central Plains, so word reached us late. But I’m not entirely ignorant of what’s happening there.”

“If you don’t mind my asking, when did you receive it?”

“Let me think. More than a month ago now, I believe.”

Given the distance between Henan and Nanman, the letter must have been sent before the Murim Alliance formally gathered at the Mount Song Resolution.

They had needed as many sects as possible to attend. The Thousand-Faced Fox, Chief of the Hidden Shadow Pavilion, would not have sent a letter without accounting for the travel time.

Which meant…

“The Nanman Beast Palace refused to join the alliance.”

There had been time to reply, and time to bring some of Nanman’s people to Mount Song. Sending no answer at all could only be taken as a polite refusal.

The Beast Miao King was silent for a moment.

“It was not an outright refusal. We simply haven’t reached a decision.”

“But surely, Palace Lord, that decision is yours to—”

He raised a hand, cutting me off. Stroking his mane-like beard, he spoke again.

“Old Master Jeok’s Disciple. No, Jin Taekyung, was it?”

“Yes.”

“I have a question for those you brought with you. Do you know who I am?”

The group hesitated. Hyuk Mujin was the first to meet his gaze, and he swallowed hard before answering.

“You’re Great Hero Yayul Cheok, the Beast Miao King. A Supreme Peak master and one of the Ten Kings.”

“Correct. The woman beside you. Yes, you.”

Ju Hwaran answered at once. “As everyone knows, you’re the Palace Lord of the Nanman Beast Palace.”

“Also correct, but not the answer I want. You beside her—tell me.”

Before anyone could stop him, Taishan blinked his enormous eyes.

“Taishan. Hungry.”

*You bastard, Taishan. You answering-machine bastard. You fucking bastard…*

The answer was so far from anything I’d expected that even the Beast Miao King looked taken aback.

“Who is he?”

I thought for a moment, then answered honestly. “I’m not really sure anymore.”

“What a strange fellow. Then let the old man beside him answer.”

Namho, who had been cursing Taishan under his breath at breakneck speed, looked up.

“You are the Great Chieftain who leads the Miao people.”

“Yes.”

The Beast Miao King nodded.

“I am the Great Chieftain who represents the Miao people. I brought them together under one banner and became Palace Lord of the Nanman Beast Palace with the support of the other tribal chieftains. Mok, do you know how many tribes there are in our land?”

Yayul Mok, who had been standing quietly at attention, answered.

“Across all of Nanman, there are thirty-two tribes, and four great tribes, including the Miao people.”

“Yes. That is the Nanman Beast Palace.”

I understood what the Beast Miao King was getting at. So did everyone else in the hall.

Except Taishan, of course.

“Are you saying…”

“Before I am Palace Lord, I am the Great Chieftain of the Miao people. And the Palace Lord of the Nanman Beast Palace is not a king.”

He couldn’t make every decision alone.

The Nanman Beast Palace might be like a separate kingdom, far removed from the Central Plains, but that kingdom was an alliance of tribes large and small.

Why tell us so openly, though?

*Are the other tribes opposed to joining the alliance, while the Beast Miao King himself is willing?*

I was about to ask when a quiet voice came from beyond the firmly shut doors of the main hall.

“Palace Lord, may I speak with you for a moment?”
