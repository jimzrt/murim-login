# Chapter 114

The Tiger of Mount Heng, Cheol Mubaek, stood like an iron tower.

The entrance gaped wide enough for two carriages to pass through side by side, yet not one of the fifty-odd mounted bandits of the Red Wind Band dared step forward.

They had seen exactly how the men before them had died.

Some had died with their heads crushed. Others had been punched through the abdomen or killed with their limbs broken. No one had clearly seen when or how Cheol Mubaek’s fist had moved.

More than twenty men had died that way.

“Monster…”

The mounted bandits stood paralyzed with fear until a deep voice came from behind them.

“You may leave now. I’ll handle this.”

At the appearance of Pung Yang, the Red Wind Band Leader, the mounted bandits withdrew like an ebbing tide. Only then did the two Peak masters face each other.

“Good to see you again, Senior Cheol.”

“I don’t recall ever taking a bandit as my junior.”

“You’re still as prickly as ever. They say even brushing sleeves creates a bond, and you and I have traded blows, haven’t we?”

“We have. Then you ran away without looking back.”

“Let’s call it a strategic retreat. I didn’t expect you to show up there either, Senior Cheol.”

“Have your internal injuries healed?”

“My insides burned for several days, so I had a rough time of it. But it wasn’t enough to kill me, which is why I’ve shamelessly returned.”

“Today, it won’t end with a little heat.”

“Oh, dear. Why don’t we settle this with words? A man of your age shouldn’t have such a fiery temper…”

Cheol Mubaek ground his teeth at Pung Yang’s glib response.

“Words? If you hadn’t betrayed us, my friend Cheonbaek might still be alive.”

“I’m not stupid enough to join a fight with no chance of victory.”

“And that wasn’t enough for you? You had to kill Seogwang too?”

“I’m not softhearted enough to spare a brat who attacked me without knowing his place.”

Pung Yang smiled gently and continued.

“If I’d known the brat would become my brother-in-law, I might have spared him.”

“You bastard!”

An aura like molten lava boiled from Cheol Mubaek’s entire body. Before the formidable Scorching Yang Qi of a Peak master, the snow covering the ground melted and the vegetation yellowed.

Pung Yang exclaimed in admiration at the sight.

“Your internal energy is remarkable, as expected. If you’d put your mind to it, Senior Cheol, I suppose I’d be facing the Mount Heng Fist Sect today.”

“I’ll rip off your limbs.”

“I wouldn’t be so confident.”

“Don’t count on the same stroke of luck as last time. You have no one to use as a shield today.”

Pung Yang smiled faintly.

“Why do you think I sent my men away?”

“That…”

Cheol Mubaek hesitated. Pung Yang’s relaxed demeanor had been bothering him for some time.

*What is he plotting?*

Last time, Pung Yang had retreated with internal injuries after barely a hundred exchanges. The man had used his subordinates as shields while he fled, yet now he had dismissed everyone and come forward alone.

That could only mean he was confident.

“What dirty trick are you planning?”

“A dirty trick? I merely stepped forward because one doesn’t use a knife meant for chickens to kill a tiger.”

“You? Alone?”

“Why not?”

“No reason. I’m grateful, if anything.”

Cheol Mubaek glared at Pung Yang suspiciously and clenched his fists.

“Thanks to you, this will be over quickly.”

Whoooosh!

The instant he finished speaking, a scorching gale hurtled forward. Pung Yang caught his breath and swung his curved saber at the red fist energy flying toward his chest.

Boom! Boom-boom!

The two Peak masters collided. Thunderous explosions rang out one after another, and the resulting gusts swept across the snow-covered ground.

Beneath the plume of snow thrown high into the air, one man staggered backward.

“Ugh.”

Pung Yang swallowed a groan and looked down at his torn palm. He had avoided the humiliation of dropping his weapon, but the difference in strength was undeniable.

“You’re every bit as strong as I expected.”

Cheol Mubaek advanced toward him.

“It’s too late for regrets.”

“My thoughts exactly.”

“I’ll start by tearing that mouth apart.”

Fwoooooosh!

Cheol Mubaek lunged like a tiger.

The ferocious forms of the Shura Annihilating Fist, a martial art thought to have been lost long ago, poured down upon Pung Yang.

Kwararabang!

* * *

A fierce battle raged atop the fortress walls. Despite being outnumbered four to one, the martial artists of the Mount Heng Sword Sect refused to retreat.

“Retreat, and all that awaits us is death!”

“Are you going to let those mounted-bandit bastards steal our home?”

“Avenge the martial brothers they killed!”

Slash! Thrust!

“Aaargh!”

“D-don’t push!”

The mounted bandits of the Red Wind Band were in disarray. The earlier fire attack had shaken them, and the fear of another trap held them back.

The martial artists of the Mount Heng Sword Sect, meanwhile, charged with bloodshot eyes. Before their unstoppable onslaught, the mounted bandits were helplessly cut down one after another.

“Don’t run!”

“Anyone who retreats dies by my hand!”

The mounted-bandit captains shouted themselves hoarse, but it wasn’t enough to restore order. Instead, they too gave up their lives to arrows that came from nowhere.

Thwack! Thwack!

“Ghk. Gaaah…”

“C-Captain!”

Lee Seowol stood atop the highest watchtower, loosing arrow after arrow.

Beside her were the five finest archers among the Mount Heng Sword Sect’s martial artists.

Twung! Thud!

Every time a bowstring sang, a mounted bandit fell. Captains and those who appeared to hold even higher positions were their first targets.

*One more. Just one more.*

But the battle was going worse than expected.

The Mount Heng Sword Sect had begun the battle with too few fighters. Its martial artists tired quickly, and before long, they began falling one by one to stray blades.

The mounted bandits, meanwhile, were gradually recovering from their confusion.

“Get ahold of yourselves! There are barely any of these Mount Heng Sword Sect bastards!”

“Kill them, and victory is ours!”

The martial artists of the Mount Heng Sword Sect had nothing left to lose. They fought desperately, heedless of the odds against them.

“Kill them!”

Slash! Slash! Slash!

But whenever they cut down one mounted bandit, two more filled the gap. Whenever they cut down two, three took their place.

“Gasp, gasp!”

One Mount Heng martial artist swung his sword frantically, only for five or six weapons to strike from every direction.

Slash! Thud-thud-thud!

Neck, chest, abdomen… All across the wall, martial artists were hacked and pierced from head to toe, dying before they could even scream.

Their momentum rising, the mounted bandits of the Red Wind Band pressed their attack without pause. Before long, enemies crowded half the fortress wall.

“Haa, haa.”

Twung! Twung! Twung!

Lee Seowol drew on every last ounce of strength to pull her bowstring. Blood ran from her once-delicate fingers and seeped between her clenched teeth. Her parched mouth tasted sickly sweet.

“There she is!”

After her relentless barrage of arrows, it wasn’t long before they discovered her position. Some twenty mounted bandits raised their shields and charged toward the watchtower, prompting desperate shouts.

“Sect Leader!”

“You must get away! They’re coming!”

Three shichen had passed since the siege began. Lee Seowol had only practiced archery as a hobby. She was no martial artist, and her Stamina had reached its limit long ago.

Yet she did not stop. She forced strength into her slender, trembling arms and searched for another target.

*Get away? Where would I go?*

She had lived here all her life. To Lee Seowol, the Mount Heng Sword Sect was both her home and something she had to protect until her final breath.

The same was true of the martial artists still making their last stand.

“Stop them!”

“Don’t let them reach the Sect Leader!”

Their desperate cries were in vain. The fortress walls had already been overrun.

The surviving martial artists of the Mount Heng Sword Sect retreated toward the watchtower, but well over a hundred mounted bandits were closing in from every direction.

Between the torches in their hands gleamed eyes filled with killing intent and desire.

“How dare these damned bastards…”

“I’ll tear every last one of you apart and throw the pieces to the dogs.”

The killing intent of the mounted bandits encircling the watchtower prickled against their skin.

Just as everyone sank into despair, Lee Seowol suddenly aimed her bow at the sky.

Whoooosh.

Trailing a tail of flame, a single fire arrow descended toward the gate shrouded in darkness.

It was a light meant to find one person.

*Uncle Cheol.*

The Tiger of Mount Heng, Cheol Mubaek.

He was the Mount Heng Sword Sect’s final hope.

Then a man stepped into the light cast by the fire arrow.

Step. Step.

“I suppose it’s rather late to say this…”

Lee Seowol had heard that voice only once, but she could never forget it, not even in her dreams.

Unable to bring herself to look, she closed her eyes.

Pung Yang beamed at her.

“You’ll have to marry me.”

* * *

The hunter Cheol Mubaek had become the Tiger of Mount Heng because of a fortuitous encounter.

While tracking a wolf on a mountainside in the vast Mount Heng range, he fell into a hidden cave between the cliffs. There, he discovered a martial arts manual and an elixir left behind by a reclusive master.

*I’m going back. I’ll make it back alive, no matter what!*

Cheol Mubaek learned martial arts to survive. When the fasting pills in the hidden cave ran out, he pulled up grass growing between the cliffs and caught bats to eat as he trained.

After three long years, he climbed the cliff with his bare hands and returned to his village.

What awaited him was his home in ruins and his wife and child dead.

*Had it been two months or so since we last heard from you? That bastard Hwang, who’d always had his eye on your wife…*

By the time Cheol Mubaek came to his senses, he had already beaten the village’s great landowner and all his servants to death.

After avenging his family, he returned to the hidden cave and resumed his martial arts training. It was both punishment for himself and atonement to his family.

How much time had passed like that?

Before he knew it, Cheol Mubaek was being called the Tiger of Mount Heng.

But…

“Huff… Even a tiger would weep.”

Cheol Mubaek breathed raggedly. His once-brilliant eyes were clouded as if by storm clouds, and his beard was drenched in blood.

*I have to hurry. I have to stop him…*

But willpower was all he had left. With all four limbs broken, his body was already beyond his control. He had unleashed martial arts worthy of the name Tiger of Mount Heng, yet he had still failed to defeat Pung Yang.

*How did that bastard…?*

The outcome should have been obvious. Pung Yang had only just entered the Peak realm and could barely produce blade qi, while Cheol Mubaek was a seasoned Peak master.

Pung Yang had been flickering like a candle in the wind—until he pulled an unidentified wooden case from inside his robes.

*That red pill. Yes, that was definitely it.*

Mistaking it for a hidden weapon, Cheol Mubaek had backed away. That had been his mistake. The moment Pung Yang swallowed the pill, he ceased to be the mere leader of a mounted-bandit group Cheol Mubaek had known.

*How can any human become that strong?*

Cheol Mubaek’s eyes trembled as he recalled Pung Yang’s movements.

The gap between them had been so vast that he could not imagine winning even if they fought ten or a hundred more times. Pung Yang had overturned the battle in an instant, broken all four of his limbs, inflicted grievous internal injuries, and left.

*I’ll let you live for now. I’ve decided I want the formula for your martial art as a wedding gift.*

Cheol Mubaek’s eyes reddened as he recalled Pung Yang’s parting words.

The Shura Annihilating Fist was a martial art passed down to a single successor and never taught to outsiders.

He would sooner take his own life than hand it over to Pung Yang, but he couldn’t stop thinking about Lee Seowol, whom he cherished like a daughter and a granddaughter.

*What am I supposed to do?*

Cheol Mubaek was staring at the sky with a heavy heart when it happened.

Thud-thud-thud-thud!

The distant thunder of hooves drew closer and closer before stopping abruptly at his feet.

Under the brilliant moonlight, four pairs of eyes looked down at him.

“Doesn’t the Red Wind Band have a retirement age? Why is an old geezer still out here playing bandit?”

“Young Master Jin, that’s Great Hero Cheol Mubaek, the Tiger of Mount Heng.”

“Gah! I’m sorry. Hey, Mujin. Hurry up and apologize.”

“You’re the one who made the mistake, Captain. Why should I…?”

Smack!

“Grandpa—no, Sir. Are you all right?”

Instead of answering, Cheol Mubaek stared intently at the young man’s chest.

A single character was embroidered on his navy martial robe.

進.

“Taiyuan… the Jin Family?”

“Oh, you recognize it?”

The young man, Jin Taekyung, grinned.
