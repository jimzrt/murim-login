# Chapter 62

The world seemed to stop.

My heartbeat thundered in my ears, and I could see every last grain of dirt drifting through the air.

And then…

Whoooosh.

A flash of light.

The glow pouring from the Sword Force was beautiful—and precise. I could feel its destructive power, enough to split not only the spearhead but my body itself in two.

*It’s over.*

I had done my best. It would be a lie to say I had no regrets, but they would not change the outcome.

All I could do was meet it with everything I had left.

Shwaaaak!

The spearhead tore through the wind. The Sword Force erased it.

Death was nearly upon me when—

Shreeeek! Thunk!

The Head Elder’s eyes flew wide.

An unknown figure had sprung up at lightning speed and driven a dagger into his dantian.

“You…”

“You should’ve struck a fatal acupoint.”

It was an ambush no one could have expected.

Until a moment ago, he had appeared to be nothing more than one of the countless corpses scattered around us.

But he wasn’t.

The man had simply waited with incredible patience for his chance to avenge his child.

The Head Elder screamed his name.

“Lee Cheonbaek!”

“Kahahaha!”

The instant Lee Cheonbaek burst into wild laughter, my spearhead punched into his back.

It tore through flesh and bone, driving forward without resistance.

> **System**
>
> - You have defeated Lv. 75 Lee Cheonbaek!
> - Level up!
> - Level up!
> - Level up!
> - …
> - All status ailments have been recovered through the stacked effect of consecutive level-ups!

The change came at once.

My aching muscles, my leaden feet, my empty dantian—all of them swelled with new strength.

At the same time, I knew exactly what I had to do.

*One Annihilation.*

Once more, a white vortex erupted.

Kraaaack!

* * *

A narrow escape.

Never in my life had those words hit so close to home.

I had seriously come back from the dead. I’d even seen a vision of myself clearing immigration into hell, sharing a big hug with King Yama, and snapping a commemorative photo with him.

If it hadn’t been for Lee Cheonbaek, that vision would have become reality.

*Good thing I saved him.*

I wanted to close Lee Cheonbaek’s eyes so he could rest in peace, but I still had work to do.

“You should’ve been a better person. Seriously.”

The Head Elder let out a quiet laugh. He looked horrific.

One Annihilation had swallowed his remaining arm, then gone on to punch a fist-sized hole through his chest.

“What a spiteful brat. Have you no respect for a dying old man?”

“The old folks I know spend their twilight years enjoying their grandchildren’s antics. They don’t run around trying to kill their own grandchildren like you, you old bastard.”

“Why, you brat! Try acting like a grandson before you say something like that.”

He laughed heartily, looking both hollow and strangely relieved, as though he had finally let everything go.

“Taekyung is a good kid. If you had opened your heart first, the two of you might have made a fine grandfather and grandson.”

The Head Elder turned his head.

Jin Wikyung stood there, sword in hand.

“Are you planning to stab me with that sword?”

“I’m considering it.”

“You’d better decide quickly. I don’t have much time left.”

He was right.

Even as he stood there talking as if nothing were wrong, blood poured like waterfalls from the severed stumps of both arms and his lower abdomen.

Then there was the backlash from drawing up his innate qi.

It was a miracle he was still alive.

“You look like you’re suffering.”

“No. I’m finally starting to feel at ease.”

The answer was firm.

“I was barely thirty when the Great Faction War began. From that day onward, I never enjoyed a single moment of peace. No…”

The Head Elder continued in a strained voice.

“Perhaps I’ve been tired for a very long time.”

I muttered in disbelief.

“You did everything you wanted, and now you come out with that?”

“Taekyung!”

Jin Wikyung gave me a mildly reproachful look, but the Head Elder did not seem offended. A breathy laugh escaped his closed lips.

“Puh-huh. Yes, you’re right. Think of it as the bullshit of a senile old man.”

“Guess it really is time for you to die.”

“Why, you little brat!”

“What? It’s not like I’m wrong.”

The Head Elder watched Jin Wikyung and me bicker through fading eyes.

“We had days like this too. Yes. There truly was a time when we were like you.”

But the Head Elder no longer had even enough time to linger over those memories.

“Cough! Guuaack!”

He staggered after vomiting what had to be a bowlful of blood.

He was at death’s door. Every capillary in his eyes had burst, and the blood pouring from his body had long since formed a pool at his feet.

Anyone could see there was no hope left for him.

*He’s really dying? The Head Elder?*

Everyone died.

Hundreds of lives had been lost on this battlefield alone—perhaps more than a thousand.

But the Head Elder’s death was something I had never even been able to imagine.

That was how overwhelming his martial prowess had been. It made his current state seem all the more wretched.

And that only made me more curious.

“Why are you holding on this hard?”

The Head Elder answered.

“Because I want to tell those who went before me… that I did my best.”

“Any regrets?”

“None.”

He smiled broadly and thrust out his chest.

“Finish it. With your own hands.”

I raised my spear.

Jin Wikyung looked conflicted, but he did not try to stop me.

Shhk!

A gust of wind passed, and the Head Elder’s knees—which had seemed as though they would never buckle—touched the ground.

A peaceful smile settled across his wrinkled face.

At the final moment, his lips moved, but no sound emerged.

That was all.

> **System**
>
> - You have defeated Lv. 95 Jin Baekyang!
> - Quest **Traitor** completed!
> - Your Level has increased greatly!
> - Your Fame has increased greatly!

For one brief moment, silence fell.

Then a colossal roar unlike anything I had ever heard erupted.

“The Sleeping Dragon of Shanxi, Jin Taekyung, has cut down the Blade of Flowers, Jin Baekyang!”

> **System**
>
> - You have acquired the Title **Sleeping Dragon of Shanxi**!

Dozens.

Maybe hundreds.

Every survivor was shouting my name.

*The Sleeping Dragon of Shanxi.*

I rather liked my new name.

* * *

“Third Young Master Jin Taekyung of the Jin Family of Taiyuan cut down the Head Elder!”

“The Sleeping Dragon of Shanxi defeated the Blade of Flowers!”

“The Sleeping Dragon of Shanxi…”

Wipeng chuckled quietly.

He had been the wastrel Third Young Master who had never even been called an earth dragon.

But now, there was no denying it.

He was a sleeping dragon—one that could soar through the heavens once it claimed the dragon pearl.

“What do you make of it?”

The First Elder answered.

“Do you believe them?”

“Everyone is shouting that the Head Elder is dead.”

“That’s because my lord wanted them to. A mere Third Young Master killing him? Don’t make me laugh.”

“You can tell that from here? You have sharp eyes.”

“This is what comes of letting your attention wander. Hahaha.”

He was slumped against a heap of corpses.

A diagonal sword wound ran from his shoulder to his waist, pouring blood in torrents.

“Surrender. If we treat you now, you can still live.”

“No. This old man’s end was decided long ago. It will be a very painful death.”

Wipeng shook his head.

“I won’t allow it.”

“My death requires no one’s permission. Neither you nor even I can do anything about it.”

“What do you mean?”

Wipeng furrowed his brow, unable to follow.

“Beware Dark Heaven… Grrk.”

It happened in an instant.

Blood poured from all seven of the First Elder’s orifices. His eyes rolled back, and his entire body convulsed.

“First Elder!”

By the time Wipeng rushed over, the First Elder had already stopped breathing.

Just as he had said, he had met a painful death. His face was twisted in agony.

*This is…*

*Poison? Or some kind of restriction?*

There was no way to know yet.

Wipeng carved the single phrase that had become the First Elder’s final words deep into his mind.

*Dark Heaven. He definitely said Dark Heaven.*

It was the only clue the First Elder had left behind.

Wipeng stared at the dead man’s face with complicated emotions, then turned away.

“I, Wipeng, have cut down the First Elder!”

Despair settled over the faces of the black-clad men.

Faced with two paths—death or surrender—they chose the latter.

Clang. Clatter-clatter.

Weapons fell limply to the ground.

The war was over.

* * *

Where there were dead, there were also survivors.

Jin Chung, the Sect Leader of Gunggwimun,[^1] was one of them. He had climbed to the top of the cliff before the battle began.

“How futile.”

It was a grand scheme to which he had devoted half his life.

The result was catastrophic.

The Head Elder he had served as his lord, the Elders he had called brothers, and the Sect Leaders of the Five Gates of Shanxi had all died.

The survivors’ last desperate struggle was over as well.

Now, only he remained.

*So this is how it ends.*

Jin Chung turned around.

Fifty martial artists of Gunggwimun waited for his orders.

“Leave.”

A silent ripple of unease passed through them.

One of the nearest martial artists spoke cautiously.

“Sect Leader, what do you mean…?”

“The fight is already over. I will not force you to sacrifice yourselves. Leave this way. Scatter as widely as you can and flee Shanxi. If you do, you may at least save your lives.”

The martial artist nodded resolutely.

“I will follow you until the day I die.”

“I… am staying here.”

“What?”

The confusion lasted only a moment before the martial artist’s voice began to tremble with emotion.

“Is it because of us?”

“Of course not.”

Jin Chung answered firmly, but his thoughts said otherwise.

*If I go with them, the Jin Family of Taiyuan will hunt them relentlessly.*

The Five Gates of Shanxi were many, yet one.

One, yet many.

They had been created for the same purpose, but each sect had trained its martial artists differently.

Jin Chung had not raised his men as weapons.

He had accepted them as disciples.

“Sect Leader!”

“Please lead us!”

Every one of them had been an orphan with nowhere else to go.

For at least ten years—more than twenty in some cases—he had fed them, sheltered them, and taught them martial arts.

Had the grand scheme succeeded, they would have become the backbone of Shanxi’s Murim.

Now that it had failed, they were nothing more than traitors.

“Do you not understand what is happening?”

“Even if we must die, we will die with you, Sect Leader.”

“You fool!”

“Please allow us.”

The martial artist who had stepped forward first slammed his forehead against the stone.

One by one, the other disciples began to kneel.

Jin Chung looked up at the sky and lamented.

“If only the grand scheme had not been delayed. If only they had stepped forward!”

The existence of *them* was a secret known only to the eight leaders.

Speaking of them aloud was no different from declaring that Jin Chung had chosen to meet his end alongside his disciples.

*This too must be heaven’s will.*

Jin Chung tore his gaze from the dark night sky and helped the prostrate martial artist to his feet.

He felt immeasurably sorry, yet deeply moved by the loyalty the man had shown.

“That’s enough. Get up.”

At the warmth in his voice, the martial artist raised his head.

He flicked his tongue over the trickle of blood running down his forehead, then grinned.

“Yes.”

Thuck!

Jin Chung stared blankly at the martial artist.

The shock was so great he could not even feel the pain.

*What in the world…?*

Shwaaak!

The martial artist pulled his hand from Jin Chung’s chest.

A glow brighter than moonlight clung to it—a blood-red Force so ominous that the mere sight of it inspired dread.

“You…”

“You already know, so why ask? Oh, and about what you just said—I’ll give you a simple answer.”

The martial artist’s smile deepened.

“Why would we step forward? Your role ends here.”

Jin Chung’s eyes flew wide.

*Them.*

The unknown beings who had refused to reveal themselves until the very end.

Dark Heaven!

“You bastards!”

“Don’t look at me like you’ve been used. Have you forgotten who got you out of that hell alive?”

Jin Chung remembered the nightmare from forty years ago.

The corpses of his allies covering the ground around him.

The endless forces of the Demonic Cult surging toward them.

They had rallied around the Head Elder and prepared to die.

Then Dark Heaven had appeared.

“We saved your lives and gave you a chance at revenge. What more did you want?”

He was right.

Dark Heaven had annihilated the Demonic Cult’s forces, then offered them a deal.

They had accepted.

It meant having a gu planted inside their heads, but they would have done anything for revenge.[^2]

But…

“Wasn’t your plan to use us to rule Shanxi?”

“Well, perhaps it was at first.”

“Then what was all this for?”

The martial artist grinned.

“A bigger picture.”

At the same time, his bloodstained hand pressed against Jin Chung’s chest.

Boom.

A small explosion erupted inside Jin Chung’s body.

The energy burst his eardrums, severed his blood vessels one by one, and reached his heart.

*To die like this…*

The thought went no further.

Already dead, Jin Chung’s body flew like a bird and plunged from the cliff.

Shiiiiik! Crash!

The martial artist glanced down and winced.

“Ouch. That must’ve hurt.”

When he turned around, screams and blood awaited him.

Ten black-clad men who had appeared out of nowhere were massacring Gunggwimun’s disciples.

“Let’s finish this quickly and go.”

“As you command.”

Shreeeeek! Thud!

The martial artist turned his gaze toward the foot of the cliff.

Screams erupted all around him, but below, the air rang with cheers and shouts.

“The Sleeping Dragon of Shanxi!”

“Jin Taekyung! Jin Taekyung!”

“The Sleeping Dragon of Shanxi…”

The plan had succeeded.

But Jin Taekyung’s appearance had been a variable even he had failed to anticipate.

He did not like that.

*Should I take him out or not?*

If he chose to, he could tear him out by the roots.

His gaze darkened as it settled on Jin Taekyung, surrounded by cheering warriors.

“Our youngest! My little brother!”

“Let go! Let go, you bastard!”

A quiet laugh escaped him.

*I’ll let you live. Today.*

The martial artist turned away.

Some fifty corpses lay spread like a carpet in his wake.

[^1]: 弓鬼門, literally “Bow Ghost Gate.”
[^2]: A *gu* is a traditional poison associated with venomous creatures; in Murim fiction, it may be implanted in a person’s body.
