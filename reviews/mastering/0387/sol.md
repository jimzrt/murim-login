# Chapter 387

By the time we emerged from the underground bunker, it was already deep into the night.

I drew a deep breath, letting the cold air seep into my lungs. As I gazed up at the star-studded sky and thought of Murim’s Sichuan, somewhere out there, a small hand tapped me on the shoulder.

“Want to come along? I was thinking of having a quick drink.”

“Now?”

“Yeah. Just us.”

It was Pai Chen. Behind her stood the other two people included in that “us.”

One of them spoke with overflowing dignity.

“O noblewoman of the East, might there be a bottle of Romanée-Conti among the wine we shall drink together?”

“I’m no noblewoman, but of course there is. Do you like wine, Prince?”

Prince Felix answered with a displeased expression.

“I told you to call me Your Highness Felix, not Prince. And I enjoy wine in moderation—especially 1945 Romanée-Conti.”

“We don’t have the 1945 vintage.”

“Then I must decline. Farewell.”

*Is he actually insane?*

Prince Felix disappeared with his attendants, and Pai Chen turned to the one person left.

“You’re coming, right, Johnson?”

Magic Johnson smiled, baring his gleaming white teeth.

“My apologies. Unfortunately, I think I’ll have to take a rain check.”

“Why?”

“I might be deployed to the front as early as tomorrow. I’d feel better if I at least prepared *Memorize* beforehand.”

As expected of an Archmage. You didn’t become a hero of the Great Cataclysm for nothing.

Deeply impressed by Magic Johnson’s attitude, I spoke up.

“I don’t think I can go right now either.”

“No.”

“Why not?”

“You’re neither a prince nor an Archmage.”

“…”

“What kind of logic is that?”

Unbelievable. Her ridiculous stubbornness was one thing, but this middle-aged woman was really something, suggesting drinks at a time like this.

“You just insulted me in your head, didn’t you? Called me a thoughtless middle-aged woman for wanting a drink in a situation like this.”

“…How did you know?”

“Oh my. Look how pointlessly honest you are.”

Pai Chen poked the tip of my nose with one long finger. She looked like a beautiful woman in her early twenties, but she was actually older than my mother. It felt strange.

“How about finding someone else? Mr. Lee, maybe…”

“Lee?”

Pai Chen tilted her head slightly. Lee Jungryong had long since disappeared with the Ares Guild members he had brought along.

“Hmm. He’s difficult to deal with. He wasn’t that bad during the Great Cataclysm, but he’s turned into a slippery old fox. I can never tell what he’s thinking.”

*She’s a pretty good judge of character.*

Pai Chen continued, casting a sidelong glance at another candidate disappearing in the distance.

“Wu Heixing. He’s too rude. Drinking with him would ruin the wine.”

I watched Wu Heixing’s back as he headed into a deserted forest and answered.

“Nothing you can do, then. You’ll have to drink some other time.”

“No. This is exactly the sort of night you drink on.”

“…?”

“It might be our last chance.”

“Ah.”

I thought I understood, at least to some extent, why Pai Chen wanted a drink.

War exhausted people. It also brought unexpected farewells in the form of death.

This was her own eve-of-battle ritual after losing countless companions during the Great Cataclysm.

By the time this crisis was over, one of us might have crossed the river of no return.

“Well, now that things have turned out this way, I have no choice but to change targets. Don’t you agree, handsome young man over there?”

“I told you I’m not going.”

Pai Chen stared at me in disbelief.

“Have you no shame? I meant that young man, not you.”

Contrary to my expectations, the handsome young man Pai Chen had singled out—Team Leader Choi—nodded readily.

“If you’ll have me, I’d be honored.”

“Wait, Team Leader. You’re really going?”

“Of course. When will I ever get another chance to have a drink with Pai Chen? Besides, I have plenty of questions.”

Pai Chen burst into laughter.

“I thought you were only handsome, but you have a way with words too. All right, then. What are you so curious about?”

“The Equipment you’re wearing right now. Where did you buy it?”

“…That’s your question?”

“Yes.”

“You’re quite a handful too.”

Pai Chen let out a deep sigh, and Magic Johnson burst into hearty laughter.

“Haha! Miss Chen, shall we all go have that drink now?”

“All of us? Johnson, didn’t you say you weren’t coming?”

“I did. I have no interest in drinking alone with a woman. But if a charming man like Mr. Choi will be joining us, that changes things.”

“…”

“I like men. Especially East Asian men.”

“R-right. Let’s go.”

No wonder *Time* had named him the world’s most influential LGBTQ person.

I sent Team Leader Choi a Sound Transmission as he was dragged away, stiff as a board.

—Contact me if anything happens.

Team Leader Choi looked back at me with the mournful eyes of an ox being led to slaughter, then disappeared with the two S-rank Hunters. After glancing around, I set off.

*This was the direction.*

I walked along the dark path, keeping out of sight. After some time, I stopped in the pitch-black forest and spoke.

“Come out.”

After a brief silence, someone’s voice drifted from the darkness.

“…Not bad.”

Rustle.

A figure emerged with the sound. Wu Heixing looked me up and down.

“How did you know?”

*What kind of question was that?*

I answered flatly.

“You might as well ask me what one plus one is. That’d be harder.”

“Hmm. You’re more capable than I expected.”

“Don’t pretend to compliment me now. It’s obvious you’re trying to pull some cheap trick.”

“…!”

Bull’s-eye. Wu Heixing’s face flushed red.

In a way, he was incredibly easy to handle. He had to be well into his mid-thirties, yet it was hard to imagine anyone being this simple.

“N-no, that’s not it. I sincerely—”

“You probably do sincerely think I’m a *bangzi*.[^1] Some random Korean who isn’t even officially an S-rank Hunter yet was getting all the attention instead of you, so naturally you got pissed off. You picked a fight in your usual one-dimensional way, but no one reacted the way you wanted, and that *bangzi* bastard turned out to be tougher than expected. That about right?”

Without giving him a chance to answer, I continued at machine-gun speed.

“So now you’re throwing out a few compliments you don’t mean and acting friendly while you look for an angle… Did I get any of that wrong?”

“…”

“Yeah, I’ve seen plenty of guys like you. Take enough hits to the back of the head and even a slow brain like mine learns to work fast.”

I gave him a contemptuous look. His face already bright red, Wu Heixing stammered out a reply.

“I wasn’t trying to pull another trick.”

“Then what? If you were thinking of becoming friends with me, fold that idea up neatly and put it away. Stand next to shit and the stink rubs off on you.”

“…!”

“By the way, what are you doing?”

At my quiet question, the hand moving toward his sword hilt stopped dead.

“Don’t draw it. You’ll get hurt.”

Wu Heixing stared at me, visibly conflicted, before blurting out,

“You knew all along… so why did you follow me so readily?”

“Because I had something to ask.”

“What?”

I looked straight into Wu Heixing’s eyes.

“That was Sound Transmission, wasn’t it?”

“…!”

His face stiffened. That was answer enough. I’d had my suspicions, but I still scratched the back of my head and muttered,

“So it was. Well, this is the mainland. I suppose all kinds of martial arts might have survived here. An internal-energy cultivation technique, for example.”

“W-what are you talking about? That was a Message Spell…”

“Oh, please.”

A dry laugh escaped me at the sight of his frantic excuses.

Someone who had never encountered martial arts might not be able to tell the difference, but he couldn’t fool me.

*Come see me for a moment.*

What had reached my ear near the end of the meeting had unmistakably been Sound Transmission.

The reason I had accepted an invitation I could easily have ignored was that Wu Heixing himself had sent it.

“H-how do you know about that?”

“What, was it some secret no one else knew?”

I stared at the flustered Wu Heixing and continued.

“I knew it was possible, but it’s still fascinating. Didn’t you wipe out all your martial artists during the Cultural Revolution and whatnot? I’m impressed any martial arts survived.”

“Shut your mouth!”

“Ah, that’s right. You said your family was powerful. Did someone at the highest levels of the Communist Party use their position to quietly spirit them away?”

“…”

His expression instantly went cold.

*Looks like I was right.*

As a foreigner, I didn’t know exactly how serious an issue this was. But I did know the value of an internal-energy cultivation technique—the thing known in the modern era as a mana cultivation method.

*If this were Murim, it would have sparked a bloody storm.*

He had been caught with the golden calf his family had secretly spirited away. No wonder he wasn’t taking it well.

“You would do well never to repeat what you just said.”

“I wasn’t planning to, but that tone of yours is pretty damn irritating.”

Wu Heixing glared at me viciously.

“Do you think you’ll still be able to act this way once you learn who my father is?”

“I don’t know who your father is, but I have a feeling he used to be a Red Guard.”

“…!”

“Was your father the one who went around smashing Confucius’s tomb with a sledgehammer in his youth?”

“You fucking *bangzi* bastard—!”

Fwish!

With a furious roar, he shot forward.

A straight sword flashed from its scabbard. The Aura Blade rising from it—no, the Sword Force—slashed toward my neck.

Shiiiiing!

The rush of wind scattered my hair. I bent backward until my waist nearly touched the ground, letting the Sword Force pass overhead, then sprang upright and drove my knee into his chin.

Crack!

Teeth and blood sprayed into the air. As he staggered, I seized both his arms and whispered into his ear.

“I told you not to draw that sword.”

Hiss! Crack!

“Gaaaaaaaaah!”

The powerful Scorching Yang Qi surging through my hands shattered his armor and seared his flesh.

The scream that tore from Wu Heixing’s lips struck the Qi Curtain I had spread and went no farther.

“You bastard!”

Whooom!

This bastard had learned unarmed combat too. Unlike his shoddy sword technique, this was reasonably sharp.

Of course…

*Compared with Murim, the quality of his martial arts was far lower.*

Slightly disappointed, I reached out.

Boom!

Internal energy collided with internal energy.

The leg he had whipped toward my waist like a lash stopped dead.

Wu Heixing’s eyes trembled with shock and disbelief.

“H-how?”

“I’m good.”

I seized his ankle and slammed him into the ground with all my strength.

Whoom! Boom!

Once more.

Whoooom! Crash!

Again. Again and again.

Crash! Crash! KRA-KOOM!

The ground churned, and rocks and trees were ripped from the earth.

By the time I stopped using him as a living pickaxe, Wu Heixing lay spread-eagled in a massive crater, looking as though his soul had left his body.

“You’re pretty sturdy, at least. You barely got hurt.”

“Hh… hhaa…”

“Hey, are you crying?”

“Hhaaa…”

He was completely out of it.

Clicking my tongue, I bent over and searched his pockets.

After rummaging through the pouch enchanted with spatial expansion magic for some time, I finally found what I wanted.

“Ah, here it is. An Advanced Potion.”

Ding.

> **System**
>
> **Acquired:** Supreme Potion!

“…Wait. Supreme Potion? What the hell, you bastard?”

I stared in shock at the sprawled-out Wu Heixing. Even for an S-rank Hunter, who carried something like this around?

Advanced Potions were rare enough, but only one or two Supreme Potions appeared in an entire year, if that.

Never mind the price, which was so high it hardly felt real. They were so scarce that even having the money wasn’t enough to get one.

*I’d only ever seen these online. And now I find one here.*

After a moment’s thought, I quietly slipped the Supreme Potion into my inventory. Then I searched his pouch again, found an Advanced Potion, and poured it over him.

“I’ve taken my settlement payment, so I’ll let you off here. You’ve got plenty to hide yourself, so if you go around blabbing about what happened today… you know what’ll happen, right?”

“Hh… hhh…”

“Okay. We’ve reached a settlement.”

Just as I neatly wrapped things up, the phone in my pocket shuddered.

A short text message had arrived from Team Leader Choi.

> 〈 Team Leader Choi
>
> Team Leader Choi
>
> Ji nTaekyung pleas ecomequick ly

“…”

*No, Johnson.*

[^1]: *Bangzi* is a derogatory Chinese slur for Koreans.
