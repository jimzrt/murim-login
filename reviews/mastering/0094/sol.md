# Chapter 94

“Three hundred fifty million won.”

The employee’s voice trembled as she processed the payment, and my hand shook as I passed her the card.

*Good lord. Three hundred fifty million won?* In barely an hour, I had blown through three years’ worth of salary from back when I worked like a dog.

*No. Look on the bright side.*

I was spending this money to protect my family from whoever was watching us.

I could always earn more, and the things I bought today would last me a long time.

Beep.

> Payment approved.

“Your payment has gone through.”

Thanks to me, this young lady had hit the jackpot. I took my card back from Assistant Manager Kim Seonhee, whose grin stretched from ear to ear.

“So that’s everything, right?”

“Ah, if you give us your address, we can have everything delivered.”

“That’s all right. I have somewhere to be.”

Delivery, my ass. The moment I was out of sight, I could just store everything in my Inventory.

*The System sure is convenient at times like this.*

With that thought, I accepted enough shopping bags to fill both hands. Of course, these weren’t ordinary paper bags. They were tough, durable leather shopping bags supposedly made from ogre hide. They had thrown them in for free, but instead of making me happy, they only made the expense sting more.

“Take care.”

“Please be sure to visit us again!”

“…Ah, sure.”

She bowed at a perfect right angle as she held out her business card, and I accepted it.

*One obsessed with sales figures. Their name: salesperson.*

* * *

I stopped by a nearby restroom, stored everything I had bought at the Store in my Inventory, and hailed a taxi. These days, I was really regretting never getting my driver’s license.

“Apartment A in Ilsan, please.”

The moment I sat down in the passenger seat, I pulled out my smartphone and searched for information.

The search term was…

*Familiar.*

The instant I tapped the search button, related results flooded the screen.

A considerable amount of it was restricted information that ordinary people couldn’t access without Hunter certification. One title in particular caught my eye.

No Joke: Don’t Use Familiar Magic. It Made My Hair Fall Out.

Now there was a title that spoke to the soul. How could I not click it?

*Ah, so this is where it was posted.*

The post was on a famous Korean Hunter forum.

It had made the monthly best list and racked up over a hundred thousand views and more than two thousand comments.

*Let’s see what it says.*

Click!

No Joke: Don’t Use Familiar Magic. It Made My Hair Fall Out.

I’m an active B-rank mage.

I’ve been around for a while and make pretty good money as a freelance Hunter. Please understand that I can’t reveal my exact stats.

Anyway, I’m writing this for exactly the reason in the title.

Familiar magic hit me with the hair-loss beam…

I’m still in my twenties, but the top of my head looks like it went to school with a two-thousand-year-old mummy. Fuck me.

Before any assholes start arguing, let me make one thing clear: My family has always had thick hair. I’ve even seen a picture of my great-grandfather from the Japanese occupation, and the man looked like the Rapunzel of Joseon.

Anyway, Familiar magic. This shit is a real double-edged sword.

The other mages here will know that mental magic isn’t common. Even civilians can make a living if they master a single trade, right?

Mental magic is like that for mages. Learn it, and you’ll always make a good living.

But fuck, your hair falls out. And it keeps falling out.

Anyone here ever washed their hair with a Superior Potion? I have.

You crazy bastards, I spent tens of millions of won on a single shampoo. I tried every goddamn thing under the sun, and even that only worked for a little while.

When nothing worked, I finally went to a regular doctor. The bastard let out a long sigh and asked me:

“You’re a mental-magic mage, aren’t you?”

That’s exactly what he said, word for word. I nearly jumped out of my skin and asked how he knew. Then he told me something truly horrifying.

More than ninety percent of mental-magic mages suffer from hair loss. Among the ones who use the more difficult Familiar magic, it’s one hundred percent.

Apparently, we work our brains so hard that sooner or later, even people with thick hair end up bald.

At first, I thought he was talking out of his ass, but I looked into it and found out it was true.

I rushed to join a café for mental-magic mages and searched for people like me. Ninety-eight of its hundred members were bald.

When they heard about my situation, they said it was already too late. Potions only restore cells temporarily, so using them too often ends up killing the hair-follicle cells instead.

The café offered to upgrade my membership if I posted a review of my medical consultation. I ignored them, quit the café, and am now searching for my own cure…

Three-line summary:

1. Mental magic is great, but it makes your hair fall out.

2. Potions are useless. Use hair-loss shampoo and go to a regular hospital instead. Hair must be treated with heart and desperation, not magic.

3. I will not give up.

That’s all.

“…”

By the time I finished reading, my vision had blurred. So even the mages I had always envied—the people who seemed to have everything—had hardships of their own.

“Are you all right, sir?”

“I’m fine… Hngh.”

“What’s wrong?”

“Ah, nothing. I’m fine.”

Of all people, the taxi driver happened to be bald. Feeling like a sinner, I bowed my head over my smartphone.

I was scrolling through the comments when I suddenly stopped.

Had I just stumbled across something important?

*The connection to a Familiar breaks beyond five hundred meters?*

That was the absolute maximum, and the safe working distance was three hundred meters.

In other words, if the author’s comments were true, the people controlling yesterday’s Familiars had been somewhere close to my house.

*And they’d be losing their hair. Maybe they’re completely bald.*

There was a good chance they wore wigs, but it couldn’t hurt to know.

I kept searching for information about Familiar magic and managed to put together a few facts.

*For a B-rank mage, the maximum Familiar connection range is five hundred meters. The safe range is three hundred. If a Familiar dies, the Link is forcibly severed, and the caster suffers a minor backlash.*

I also realized why someone had gone to the trouble of hiring an expensive Familiar mage to watch me.

*What the hell? Tiny Familiars can slip past most detection magic?*

It worked like this: detection magic was a net, and tiny Familiars like flies and rice weevils were small enough to pass through its holes.

Of course, there were products with top-of-the-line detection magic capable of catching even those. But when I looked them up, they cost five hundred fifty million won—and that was the summer special price.

“…”

What part of that was supposed to be special? I already had plenty of expenses coming my way. Five hundred million won, my ass.

*One more reason to catch them myself.*

Then I thought of the other Guild members. Had they only planted Familiars around my family?

I still didn’t know exactly who had commissioned the surveillance. Sangdong Guild was only the prime suspect.

“That’ll be 8,400 won.”

“Ah, yes. Here you go.”

I paid the fare and got out of the taxi. The entrance to the apartment complex had always looked the same, but today it felt strangely unfamiliar.

The watcher’s presence bothered me like a thorn caught in my throat.

*Should I contact Team Leader Choi?*

I was staring at his number on my phone when—

“Oppa!”

A familiar swan[^1] in a green tracksuit and round glasses waved at me, her greasy hair tied tightly back.

“Oh. Uh, hey.”

“What’s with that reaction?”

“I don’t know. Something about seeing you outside makes me want to pretend we don’t know each other. It’s embarrassing…”

“You’re embarrassed by your one and only little sister? Huh?”

“Go buy some clothes. What happened to the clothes you bought at the department store? Why are you wearing that tracksuit again?”

“You should worry about yourself. You wear the same kind of clothes every day.”

Hayeon’s jab left me speechless. Barely an hour or two ago, I had spent three hundred million won, yet I was still dressed in jeans and a T-shirt. Apparently, twenty-seven years of ingrained penny-pinching hadn’t quite worn off yet.

“A-Anyway. Where are you going?”

“Where do you think? The convenience store!”

Hayeon had been sulking a moment ago, but now she started grinning.

*What’s gotten into her?*

I asked suspiciously, “Why are you so excited? Is your heart already pounding at the thought of cleaning out the convenience store?”

“Cleaning it out, my ass. I’m just buying canned tuna.”

“Canned tuna? Are we having tuna kimchi stew for lunch?”

“No! We’re not!”

“…Seriously, what’s wrong with you today? Have you been drinking?”

“You’ll understand when you see this little one.”

Instead of snapping back like usual, Hayeon kept grinning. She unzipped her tracksuit and pulled something out from inside it.

A tiny ball of white-and-yellow fur wriggled in her palm.

“Meow.”

“…A kitten?”

“Cute, right? I found it when I went out to sort the recycling. Someone had abandoned it in a box with a note saying anyone who wanted it could take it home.”

“…”

“I don’t know what kind of bastard left it there, but it’s so pitiful. What did this little thing ever do wrong? Right, Oppa?”

“…Yeah. It’s not the animal’s fault.”

“Mom said I could foster it, even if it’s only for a while. You’re okay with that too, right?”

“Me?”

“Yeah. You know how Mrs. Kim melts for her eldest son. She told me to get your permission too, so put in a good word for me when we go inside.”

“We’ll see.”

“What? You loved animals when you were little.”

I did. I still did, in fact. But the little thing sitting quietly in Hayeon’s palm was…
