# Chapter 149

Xi’an had a long history. Until the last change of dynasty, it had been called the center of all under heaven for hundreds of years.

Its enormous population, the abundant resources produced by its plains and mines, and the scenic and historic sites left behind by three fallen unified dynasties still drew countless visitors to the city.

Among them were two Confucian scholars in thick fur coats, sitting in one of Xi’an’s bustling inns.

“Come on, hurry up. If we want to make it back to the inn before sunset, we don’t have much time.”

Unlike his enthusiastic friend, the other scholar shook his head with a thoroughly fed-up expression.

“Again?”

“What do you mean, again?”

“Can’t we just rest for today? We’ve been walking around for days. My legs feel like they’re about to break.”

“Don’t be such a baby. We walked a thousand li to get here—how can we just turn around and go home? At our age, do you really think we’ll ever have another chance to visit Xi’an?”

“Good grief. I came to see Xi’an, and now it looks like I’m going to see Mount Beimang.[^1] Leave me alone.”

[^1]: Mount Beimang is traditionally associated with burial grounds and death.

“Come now. We can skip the other places, but we absolutely must visit Western Peak. You’ll regret it until the day you die if you miss such a magnificent sight.”

“Western Peak…”

The five most renowned mountains under heaven were known as the Five Great Mountains.

Western Peak referred to Huashan, which lay near Xi’an.

“Imagine climbing Huashan and looking down upon all under heaven. Doesn’t your lofty spirit surge at the mere thought?”

“Well… I suppose it does.”

Worn down by his friend’s fervent persuasion, the scholar reluctantly nodded.

He knew perfectly well that Huashan was as famous for its breathtaking scenery as it was for its rugged terrain.

“A famous mountain doesn’t become famous for nothing. We need to soak up plenty of its sacred energy this time if we want good news at the next civil service examination. How many times have you failed already?”

“Why are you bringing that up all of a sudden?”

“Don’t get angry. I’m just saying we should visit Huashan and take in some lofty spirit and sacred energy.”

When the scholar still looked hesitant, his friend added coaxingly,

“I won’t ask you to go anywhere else. We’ll visit Lotus Peak and come straight back down.”

At that, even the scholar who had resisted so stubbornly began to waver. Who knew? Perhaps he really would pass the civil service examination with flying colors next year.

But one rumor still bothered him.

“I heard Huashan is crawling with martial artists…”

“If you mean Huashan’s Daoists, there’s nothing to worry about. An acquaintance of mine went there years ago and said he didn’t have any trouble.”

“Ahem. Then shall we give it a try?”

The scholar made a show of giving in as he rose, only to lose his balance and stagger. The strength drained from his legs, which had been trembling for some time.

He was about to crack the back of his head against one of the many table corners around him.

“Wh-whoa!”

Just as he began to fall with a startled cry, a rough, sturdy hand caught him by the back.

“Ugh. That was close.”

After barely righting himself, the scholar sighed in relief and looked at the owner of the hand.

The young man appeared to be around thirty. He wore a white robe and had an unremarkable face, but his smile was gentle.

“Are you all right?”

“Th-thank you, Young Master.”

“It was nothing.”

Having narrowly escaped disaster, the scholar regarded the young man with curiosity.

*He looks ordinary.*

He was of middling height and had a slender build. Yet he had supported the scholar, who outweighed him by several dozen pounds, with one hand.

Wondering whether he might be a martial artist, the scholar glanced at his waist. It was bare, so apparently not.

*He’s much stronger than he looks.*

Regardless, the young man had saved him. The sages of old had said that repaying kindness was a person’s duty.

“Thank you again. Thanks to you, Young Master, I avoided a terrible mishap.”

“I only did what anyone should have done. Please don’t concern yourself with it.”

“You did me a great service. How could I leave it at a few words? Why don’t you join us? The meal is on me.”

His fellow scholar cut in with an incredulous look.

“What are you talking about? What about Huashan? What about Lotus Peak?”

“Didn’t you just see me nearly drop dead? This is a sign that I should rest at the inn. Besides, this Young Master saved me, so I ought to repay him. Isn’t that right?”

The young man smiled and waved him off.

“I’m really fine. I’m waiting for my companions.”

“Companions? I’ve been watching you, and you’ve been sitting here alone for more than a shichen.”

“Ha-ha. Something must have delayed them. I have no choice but to wait.”

He had already been there for more than a shichen, yet he still intended to wait? The man was as good-natured as he looked.

Still, if he was expecting companions, the scholar could hardly insist that he join them. He smacked his lips regretfully.

“Then it can’t be helped. May peace reign throughout your household, and may you enjoy good health and a long life.”

“Huashan! Lotus Peak!”

“I’m going, so shut that obnoxious mouth of yours.”

“I knew I could count on you.”

“Maybe I should throw you off the summit of Lotus Peak.”

The scholar glared at his companion and was just about to take a step when—

Bang!

A deafening crash made everyone’s hair stand on end. The inn’s door shattered, and a man and a woman appeared with thunderous shouts.

“We’re here!”

“Eunhyang’s here too!”

Every patron in the inn stared at them with their mouths hanging open.

The man’s enormous physique was startling enough, as was the beautiful girl beside him.

*What kind of pair is that?*

*Good heavens. I’ve never seen anyone so huge in my life.*

The inn fell silent. Only one person showed no surprise—the young man who had helped the scholar moments before.

“You’re late.”

The man, who stood several heads taller than everyone else, scratched his head vigorously.

“I’m sorry. We got caught up in a small dispute on the way.”

“What sort of dispute delayed you by more than a shichen?”

“Well, you see…”

When the man began to mumble, the girl who had introduced herself as Eunhyang cut in with a broad grin.

“Big Brother, have you ever heard of the Black Serpent Sect?”

“The Black Serpent Sect? I can’t say I have. Judging by the name, though, they don’t sound as if they do much good.”

“Exactly. They’re a dark-path gang that runs a gambling den just up the road. Their boss took one look at Brother Chulwoo and asked if he wanted to work with them… Mmph! Mmph-mmph!”

“No, he didn’t! I swear! Look how innocent I am!”

The man named Chulwoo clapped a hand over Eunhyang’s mouth and protested, but no one in the inn believed him.

*Look at that face. With a face like that, he already belongs to the dark path.*

*If I were the Black Serpent Sect’s boss, I’d have tried to recruit him too.*

*He’d be my first pick. No question.*

Everyone kept those thoughts to themselves because Chulwoo was glaring around the room with his eyes wide. Faced with the gaze of an enraged beast, the patrons could only swallow nervously.

Of course, one person remained an exception.

“So what happened?”

At the young man’s question, Chulwoo answered at once.

“I just told them I wasn’t interested and sent them away.”

“Is that true?”

Chulwoo’s gaze slid away.

“Y-yes, it’s true.”

“There’s blood on your fist.”

“Gasp! Really? I was sure I wiped it off!”

“…”

“…”

“Mmph. Mmph!”

The young man let out a deep sigh.

“Let go of Eunhyang first.”

“…Yes, Senior Brother.”

“Mmph—phew!”

Finally freed, Eunhyang scrunched up her face and spat several times.

“Ugh, salty. When did you last wash your hands, Brother?”

“Yesterday.”

“What? I’ve seen you visit the privy at least five times since yesterday. Then…”

“Ahh!”

“It’s fine. I still smell wonderful even if I only bathe once every fifteen days.”

“Are you insane? No wonder women hate you.”

“What did you say?”

As the two growled at each other, the young man rubbed the corners of his eyes wearily.

These two were notorious troublemakers even within their sect. He had known that something like this would happen eventually, but he hadn’t expected them to cause trouble before they had even left Xi’an.

*I couldn’t refuse when it was the Sect Leader’s order.*

What could he do? He could only consider it his karma.

Already feeling half drained, he spoke.

“Do you both want to go back? Should I ask the Sect Leader to put you through wall-facing meditation until you come to your senses?”

“Gasp! No, Senior Brother.”

“I’m fine too, Big Brother.”

The young man deliberately hardened his expression.

“Come now. Not Big Brother. Senior Brother.”

“Yes, Big Brother.”

“Eunhyang, you… Phew. Never mind.”

“Hehe.”

Such was the power of a beautiful woman’s smile.

When Eunhyang smiled sweetly, the chilly atmosphere inside the inn warmed at once.

That was when the innkeeper, who had been watching them nervously, approached.

“Um, sirs…”

Noticing him, the young man spoke apologetically.

“Ah, I’m sorry for causing such a commotion. We’ll leave at once.”

“No, that’s not it…”

The innkeeper glanced fearfully at Chulwoo before forcing out the words.

“Compensation, please.”

“Ah.”

Only then did they notice the shattered door.

As the young man sighed again, Chulwoo swiftly pulled a silver nyang from a heavy pouch.

“This should be enough.”

“Where did you get that silver nyang?”

Eunhyang answered with a sunny smile.

“The Black Serpent Sect.”

Chulwoo cried out in alarm.

“Hey!”

“What? I didn’t do anything wrong.”

“You took a jade hairpin too!”

“Oops. How did you know?”

“…”

Apparently, smashing the Black Serpent Sect hadn’t been enough. They had stripped the place of its valuables as well. The young man’s forehead began to throb.

“Return it immediately.”

“Senior Brother, even if those men kept this money, they’d only use it for evil.”

“That’s right. Since we already have it, we could eat something delicious on the way to our destination…”

The young man cut them off sternly.

“Since when has running a gambling den been an evil deed? Have you personally witnessed them committing any evil?”

“We don’t need to see it. It’s obvious. They’re dark-path figures.”

“How could this vast Murim contain only one color? And if the Black Serpent Sect were truly a band of villains, the main sect would have dealt with them long ago.”

“But…”

“Enough. We’re in a hurry, so we’ll leave the valuables here. Would that be acceptable, Innkeeper?”

By now, everyone in the inn knew that these people were martial artists—and that they had made enemies of one of Xi’an’s dark-path factions.

The innkeeper, who wanted nothing more than to avoid becoming entangled with martial artists, looked as though he had stepped in filth.

“G-Great Hero, forgive me, but this is more than an old man like me can handle.”

Chulwoo, about to lose the fruits of his hard work, spoke gruffly.

“Don’t worry. Nothing will happen.”

“Perhaps not right away, but once you leave, I’ll be in terrible trouble.”

“Come now, I told you that won’t happen. Even after we leave, they won’t dare touch a hair on your head.”

“No, that’s not something you can say so easily…”

*Is his brain made of muscle too? He’s damn short-sighted.*

The innkeeper couldn’t bring himself to say it aloud and could only stew in silence. At that moment, the young man smiled calmly.

“When they come, simply return this pouch and give them a message.”

“Great Heroes, I don’t think you understand what I’m saying…”

The innkeeper’s words stopped dead at what the young man said next.

“Tell them that Baek Museong, a first-generation disciple of Huashan, apologizes for his junior disciples’ mistake.”

The inn fell silent.

The name *Huashan* was intimidating enough, but the young man’s name also sounded vaguely familiar.

“Baek Museong of Huashan?”

“Baek Museong… Baek Museong… Wait. Could it be?”

Xi’an was practically Huashan’s front yard. It didn’t take long for several martial arts aficionados to realize the young man’s identity.

“Baek Museong, Huashan’s Lone Crane!”

The epithet came from his lofty bearing, said to resemble that of a solitary crane.

Renowned as an outstanding prodigy from the moment he entered Huashan, he also held another title.

“If he’s Huashan’s Lone Crane, isn’t he the first of the Three Plum Blossom Elites?”

The current Sect Leader of Huashan had three disciples, every one of whom had grown into an outstanding master.

Naturally, they had been appointed Plum Blossom Swordsmen—the pride of Huashan—and soon distinguished themselves.

“I heard one of them was a woman… Then those two must be—”

“Why even ask? Didn’t you hear her call Huashan’s Lone Crane her Senior Brother?”

“Good heavens. I never thought I’d live to see all three of the Plum Blossom Elites in a place like this.”

Ignoring the exclamations erupting throughout the inn, Baek Museong spoke.

“Would it still be impossible?”

The innkeeper answered with a solemn expression.

“I shall return these valuables to the Black Serpent Sect even if it costs me my life. At your command!”

“…”

* * *

“Ah, that’s right.”

At Baek Museong’s mutter, his two junior disciples turned toward him.

“What is it, Senior Brother?”

“Did you leave something behind?”

Baek Museong shook his head.

“I forgot to tell him that Huashan has been sealed off.”

“Tell who?”

“I don’t know his name. That man is going to drag his aching legs all the way there for nothing.”

Eunhyang clicked her tongue sympathetically.

“What a shame. He won’t have a chance for the next few months.”

“Indeed.”

They recalled the incident that had thrown all of Huashan into an uproar several days earlier.

While the Sect Leader—their Master—was asleep, an intruder had slipped in and out without anyone noticing.

The intruder had even committed the astonishingly brazen act of leaving a dagger and a handwritten letter beside the Sect Leader’s head.

> I’m going out to get some air. Don’t slack off just because you’ve become Sect Leader. Spend less time sleeping and more time training your martial arts.

Under ordinary circumstances, they would have immediately cast a dragnet across the entire mountain. But when the intruder was Sword Saint Mae Jonghak, that changed everything.

The Sect Leader had immediately sealed Huashan tight and ordered a search for the Sword Saint’s place of seclusion. The search had continued ever since.

“Grandmaster really is something. What an incredible man.”

“I’d only heard stories. I didn’t realize he was quite this extraordinary either.”

“If that messenger pigeon hadn’t arrived, we’d still be stuck searching Huashan.”

Amid all that chaos, the messenger pigeon from Shanxi Province had been a ray of salvation.

After much deliberation, Huashan’s leaders had decided to dispatch the exceptional talents known as the Three Plum Blossom Elites.

“But this man named Cheongpung—have you ever met him, Senior Brother?”

“Yes. Once, ten years ago.”

The disciple Sword Saint Mae Jonghak had raised as both a son and a grandson.

Baek Museong had been there that day ten years ago. Huashan’s Lone Crane’s eyes gleamed.

“I’m looking forward to seeing how he’s grown.”
