# Chapter 606

*It wasn’t a Mutated Gate? What the hell was that supposed to mean?*

My confusion lasted only a moment. Then Magic Johnson dropped a single word, and my eyes widened.

“Terrorism?”

“Yeah, terrorism. An Arab man was caught trying to enter a Gate with a forged Hunter license. Turns out he was a B-rank Hunter with a terrorist group in the Middle East, and he was carrying an A-grade Magic Gem. One that hadn’t been purified, of course.”

“Wait. That sounds…”

“Familiar, doesn’t it, Jin?”

I pressed my lips together instead of answering. Recent memories flashed through my mind.

Go Jun. Song Cheonwoo. And two monster waves.

Something that should never have happened again kept happening.

“He was trying to cause a monster wave.”

“Probably. No, almost certainly.”

*Damn it.*

“What about the casualties?” I asked urgently. “Was anyone hurt? Did the news deliberately hide the damage because…”

I couldn’t bring myself to finish.

The recent incidents had been one disaster after another. The man might not have been comparable to Go Jun, but if a B-rank Hunter from a terrorist group had absorbed an unpurified A-grade Magic Gem, he could have done considerable damage on his own.

Magic Johnson answered calmly.

“Whoa. Easy, Jin. The news reports were true. Thank God, there wasn’t any significant damage.”

“Are you sure?”

“Why would I lie to you?”

“But how?”

“After what happened, the whole world’s on high alert. Identity checks and Magic Gem inspections have gotten several times stricter. That’s how they caught him. He tried to absorb the Magic Gem as a last resort. What do you think happened?”

Magic Johnson took a sip of the instant coffee in his paper cup.

“Simple. He couldn’t withstand the power in it. By the way, this coffee’s pretty good.”

“Forget the coffee. If he couldn’t withstand it, shouldn’t he have mutated like Go Jun?”

Mana and magical power were like oil and water. They could never mix.

Go Jun was proof. Even as a certified S-rank Hunter trained in the modern mana cultivation method, he had never managed to absorb magical power completely.

The real problem was the temporary surge of power—and the mutation waiting afterward.

*So how was the result simple?*

The lack of significant damage made even less sense now.

Magic Johnson’s answer took me completely by surprise.

“No. He exploded.”

“What?”

“I mean he blew apart. Into pieces. Boom!”

Bang, bang!

Magic Johnson tapped his spoon against the earthenware bowl and shrugged.

“And that was the end of it. All I had to do at the scene was protect people from the blast.”

“…!”

“In a way, you could certainly call it a suicide attack. But that was all it amounted to.”

Something clicked.

*It wasn’t that this should have gone like it did with Go Jun. What happened with Go Jun was only possible because it was Go Jun.*

I’d had it backward from the start.

It came down to the size of the vessel. Not someone’s character or generosity, but how much power they could hold without letting it overflow.

By that measure, Go Jun had been a large vessel. He could absorb some magical power and hold on to it, if only briefly.

*And even he eventually mutated.*

After absorbing two S-grade Magic Gems at once, Go Jun had lost that balance and mutated. Even if I hadn’t dealt with him myself, there was a good chance he would have died before long.

*An ordinary Hunter wouldn’t stand a chance.*

The terrorist in today’s news had been a B-rank Hunter, but the power had still blown him apart on the spot. He hadn’t been able to control even a fraction of the magical power in that Magic Gem.

“Hey, Jin. Looks like you’re getting it.”

Magic Johnson was grinning. I let out a short, disbelieving laugh.

“That’s a huge relief.”

“It is. Only a complete idiot would try the same thing after today.”

He had put his finger on the important part. It was fortunate that no one had been hurt, but more reassuring still was the prospect that this would prevent another attempt.

“The terrorist groups will have learned their lesson too. Cold as it sounds, this wasn’t worth the cost to them.”

A trained Hunter was a valuable asset, and a Magic Gem was an energy source of immense value. I didn’t know how the group had gotten hold of an unpurified A-grade Magic Gem, but today they’d failed spectacularly.

They had lost both a valuable operative and a Magic Gem easily worth billions.

“They’ll change tactics,” Magic Johnson said. “Probably go back to targeting defenseless civilians with that damn magic wand of Allah. But it’s too early to let our guard down.”

He sighed quietly.

“Terrorist organizations have been making some worrying moves lately. I hear they’ve been experimenting with Gates and Magic Gems, especially in Africa and the Middle East. The information came from the Pentagon, so it’s reliable.”

Beside us, the Skeleton King had spent the entire conversation fiddling with his smartphone. Now his ears perked up.

“The Pentagon? Are we going there right now? Is much rub-rub possible?”

“Just so you know, it’s not a club.”

“Damn it. I thought I was finally going to meet some hot girls.”

*Don’t even dream about it, you bastard…*

The Pentagon was the headquarters of the United States Department of Defense. If the Skeleton King’s identity came to light, its researchers would party all night.

In a heavily secured laboratory, of course, where they’d try all kinds of rub-rub with every experimental tool they had.

“Hmm. Everyone would certainly be happy if we took him to the Pentagon,” Magic Johnson said.

“Not so good for you or me, though.”

“True. We’d probably stand trial for violating international law. Though if a terrorist group caused trouble while the trial was going on, the whole thing might fizzle out.”

Life was hard. The moment one problem was solved, another popped up.

*Terrorist groups, huh.*

Once Go Jun’s crimes became public, countries around the world had been shocked into high alert. But terrorist groups had gained a new weapon.

If the Pentagon’s intelligence was right, they would do whatever it took to find a way to use it.

“…Of all times,” I muttered.

Magic Johnson looked puzzled.

“Of all times? What do you mean?”

“Hmm.”

I hesitated, then shook my head.

“It’s nothing. Don’t worry about it.”

“It sounds like something, and now I’m very worried about it.”

“It’s just… there’s something I’ve been preparing to do. But after hearing that, I’m hesitating for now.”

“Something you’ve been preparing?”

I gave a small nod.

I hadn’t told anyone yet. It was one of the things I’d decided to do when I returned to the modern era this time.

For my own sake, and for everyone else’s. After a long hesitation, I’d finally made up my mind and begun preparing.

*But this changes things.*

Humanity had regained peace after the Great Cataclysm. That didn’t mean the whole world was peaceful.

Mana had appeared, along with Awakened people. Those who survived and came out strong had remade the world by their own rules, and conflicts everyone thought had ended were still going on.

*Some people say they’ve gotten worse.*

Magic Gems were more valuable and useful than oil. Gates were mines that kept spitting out those diamonds. Conflict over them was inevitable, whether the people involved called themselves entrepreneurs, Guilds, or rebels.

The terrorist groups in the Middle East were more dangerous still.

They called awakening a blessing from God and held it up as proof that God existed. You might ask what the hell they were talking about when even atheists could Awaken, but looking for logic in people like that was a mistake to begin with.

*Warriors chosen by God! Advance!*

*God wills it!*

I had no idea which god or doctrine had told them to do such idiotic things.

But singers sang, blacksmiths worked iron, and idiots kept doing idiotic things. They fought across the world, dying or ending up crippled.

And they were still at it right now.

*Bastards.*

The Skeleton King was a lot better than them. He might have started out as a monster, but by now he’d shown as much sacrifice and dedication as any Hunter.

*Good lad.*

I looked at him warmly. He glared back.

“You just cursed this body in your thoughts.”

“…What the hell? No, I didn’t.”

“Tell me the truth. I’ll let it slide.”

“Johnson, can’t we just take this bastard to the Pentagon?”

Magic Johnson grinned.

“Shall we?”

“You hear that? You’re finished, you experimental-subject bastard.”

“Now that you mention it, we might as well go right away.”

“Tell them to get a cauldron ready. We can make bone broth out of him.”

“I don’t know about a cauldron, but the laboratory will be ready whenever we are.”

For some reason, Magic Johnson and I were on exactly the same wavelength today.

He played along without missing a beat, then drew a staff from inside his clothes.

“Well, now that we’ve eaten, shall we get going?”

“Sounds good… Wait.”

I’d answered on reflex. Then I felt mana circulating through his staff and gave him an uneasy look.

“Uh, there’s no need to go that far.”

“Don’t you have a traditional Korean proverb? Once a gay draws his magic wand, he must pierce even a radish.”

“…?”

That sounded like a strange and impressive twist on a proverb I’d never heard in my life. I swallowed.

“Wait. Seriously?”

“Yeah. Seriously.”

“You’re not joking?”

“The Pentagon sent an official request two days ago. They want your advice and your help, Jin. As soon as possible.”

“What?”

I blinked, then laughed weakly.

“Oh, I get it. This is an American joke.”

Magic Johnson fixed me with the profound gaze of a gay man.

“How did you know? I don’t remember showing you that memory.”

*What the fuck is he talking about?*

I stared at him for a moment, then hurriedly waved my hands.

“…No, you heard me wrong. Joke. A joke. A gag.”

“I know. That was my joke.”

He flashed a white-toothed grin.

“But the part about the Pentagon wasn’t.”

“…!”

“Come on. It looks like someone else is ready already. Isn’t that right?”

I followed his gaze and turned my head. I sensed a familiar presence, and a figure came into view.

Team Leader Choi answered in a subdued voice.

“Of course, Mr. Johnson.”
