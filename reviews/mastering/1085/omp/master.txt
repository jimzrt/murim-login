# Chapter 1085

The rumors spread in an instant.

From mouth to mouth.

On the beating wings of messenger pigeons.

On the hooves of horses racing across mountains and fields under the crack of whips, and in the smoke of beacon fires staining the sky gray.

Before the people shaken by the news could even determine whether it was true, they saw it with their own eyes.

Countless ships cutting swiftly through the river, their flags billowing proudly in the favorable wind.

The Yangtze River Channel League.

The moment they made out the five characters on those wind-swollen flags, they knew that denying it any longer would mean hiding from reality.

Everything they had seen and heard was no baseless rumor.

It was true.

A mere band of river pirates, who had lived off the Yangtze with the world’s tacit consent, had at last become its true rulers.

And they had done it against the Great Nation itself—the master of the world.

“…Gods of heaven and earth.”

The groan that slipped from someone’s lips sounded hollow.

The vast empire founded under the Mandate of Heaven was collapsing.

No—their heaven was.

“If this isn’t defying heaven, then what is?”

More than a hundred years had passed since the age of warring heroes ended and a unified dynasty was born.

The country folk, who had revered the Great Nation ruled by the Son of Heaven as heaven itself, trembled with unease. Confucian scholars groaned as they realized that the order they had taken for granted had crumbled. Betrayed martial artists seethed with anger and shock.

“Those bandits finally…!”

“I knew something was off. We should never have trusted dark-path figures in the first place!”

But the Yangtze River Channel League’s betrayal wasn’t the only news to shock everyone.

“Two days ago, the Green Forest bandits rebelled in Hubei and Shaanxi!”

The Green Forest Alliance, which divided the world’s dark-path forces with the Yangtze River Channel League, had risen up.

And people belatedly realized that storm clouds were gathering beyond the distant Great Wall as well.

“An urgent report from Sichuan! Potala Palace in Tibet is on the move!”

Potala Palace.

The news that its esoteric monks—counted among the Three Outer Powers alongside the North Sea Ice Palace and Nanman Beast Palace—had finally broken their long silence surprised no one in the Murim.

To those firmly rooted in the Central Plains, Potala Palace had long been nothing more than a false sect following evil doctrines.

Since its founding, the new Murim Alliance had sent envoys several times to seek an alliance, but Potala Palace had never answered. For it to act now could mean only one thing.

“…Dark Heaven. It’s them again.”

Fortunately, Sichuan had powerful reinforcements.

Unlike the North Sea Ice Palace, which remained neutral as always, Nanman Beast Palace had overcome the threat of civil war thanks to the astonishing exploits of a young man and resolved to ally itself with the Murim Alliance.

So attention turned to the Yangtze River Channel League and the Green Forest Alliance.

The initial shock soon passed, and quite a few people began to scoff at the dark-path forces’ uprising.

“Even if they’ve won a great victory, dark-path figures are still dark-path figures. Nothing’s changed.”

It wasn’t entirely wrong.

The Yangtze River Channel League and the Green Forest Alliance together had nearly twenty thousand men, but if numbers alone were enough to take everything, the Murim world would belong to the Beggars’ Sect.

Everyone was confident that the orthodox factions, now united around the Murim Alliance, wouldn’t be shaken by a force of this size.

“Shaanxi has Huashan and Zhongnan, and Hubei has Wudang and the Zhuge Clan. If the Son of Heaven’s Imperial Army moves out too, those bastards will regret joining hands with Dark Heaven.”

But that prediction had several fatal flaws.

First, the Zhongnan Sect had very few forces left at its headquarters.

Second, most of the hundreds of thousands of Imperial Army troops were concentrated in Jiangsu, where the Imperial Capital and the Son of Heaven were located.

Third, the Green Forest Alliance and the Yangtze River Channel League were advancing west in broad daylight, making no effort to hide, and their forces were far larger than anyone had expected.

Like a snowball growing as it rolled down a snowy slope.

“The Green Forest bandits gathered in Hubei crossed Tongsan half a *shichen* ago! Their numbers are estimated at around five thousand!”

“Five thousand? What in the—”

“Weren’t there only around three thousand half a day ago?”

“W-Well, it seems men from the other strongholds have joined them.”

“That’s absurd!”

But the men who so fiercely rejected the messenger’s report soon learned that another force had joined the two groups of outlaws secretly gathering around Hubei and Shaanxi since before the great battle in Gansu.

And it had reached them by the very means the Central Plains Murim feared most.

“…Damn it. They must have used that accursed dark art.”

“Dark art? You don’t mean…”

“I do. A Moving Formation.”

The existence of the Moving Formation had once been guarded with the strictest secrecy. Now it was no longer known to only a tiny handful of people.

As long as people had eyes and ears, secrets would eventually get out.

The Murim Alliance leadership and the Hidden Shadow Pavilion, led by the Thousand-Faced Fox Song Ho, had taken the lead. Yet somehow, word of the Moving Formation had still spread. Now that the Sect Leaders and Family Heads had confirmation it was real, a chill ran through them.

*We have to strike. Right now.*

The Yangtze River Channel League and the Green Forest Alliance were, after all, coalitions of separate groups.

Their Stronghold Lords commanded anywhere from a few dozen to several hundred—even a thousand—men each. They pledged loyalty to their respective alliance leaders, gathering and dispersing at their command.

Now was the best time to strike.

Before the enemy had fully assembled. Before they could gather in one place and become a powerful force.

But the complication everyone had secretly feared had come to pass.

No one even knew where the Moving Formations were, and Dark Heaven’s powerful forces were using them to join the dark-path figures.

*What if we fight them now?*

It was dangerous.

The Moving Formations alone posed an enormous threat, and there was no guarantee of victory.

Shaolin, the Mount Tai and Northern Dipper of the Murim, had suffered terrible losses. So had the Sichuan Tang Clan, which held its place among the Five Great Families with deadly poisons and hidden weapons. Even Nanman Beast Palace and the Hebei Peng Family had been badly hit.

And that wasn’t all. According to reports, Kongtong and Zhongnan had also suffered devastating losses in Gansu.

The damage rivaled that of the Great Faction War, a time no one wanted to remember. It might even be worse.

The Nine Sects and One Gang. The Five Great Families.

Even those fifteen great trees supporting the Murim world were trembling at their roots in the storm stirred up by the dark clouds of Dark Heaven. Risking everything to face that danger took courage few could easily summon.

Least of all Sect Leaders and Family Heads, who were responsible for their dependents’ lives as well as their own.

“I… I’m withdrawing.”

“Sir Seok! What are you saying?”

“Let’s be realistic. Do you think the forces gathered here can stand against them?”

“But—”

“You know it too, don’t you? The outcome is already decided. We have to conserve what strength we can. Fortunately, those traitors who joined hands with Dark Heaven are heading only west. We can avoid a clash for now.”

“Fortunately? Did you just say ‘fortunately’? Do you really not know what they’re after?”

“I know. They want to pin down the reinforcements from the Central Plains and take Qinghai completely into their grasp. That must be their goal.”

“And you know that, yet…!”

“But this time, I have no choice. I’m no Supreme Peak master, nor am I a Sect Leader of one of the Nine Sects and One Gang. I’m sorry.”

“Sir Seok!”

“Please don’t call me that. I’m not worthy of it. I’m just a cowardly Sect Leader who wants to keep his dependents safe.”

He muttered the words bitterly and left. Several other Sect Leaders, their faces downcast, got up and followed him.

Then the refined-looking middle-aged man who had been watching quietly from the seat of honor rose too.

*Scrape.*

The others had watched the departing Sect Leaders with anger and a sense of betrayal, though part of them understood. Now their eyes widened.

They had been certain that even if everyone else left, this man wouldn’t.

“F-Family Head!”

“What is this?”

But contrary to their fears, the middle-aged man merely shrugged, his expression calm.

“Ah, don’t misunderstand. I’m just going to get some air.”

The others relaxed, then sighed.

A third of Hubei Murim’s leading figures had just left, and he was going to get some air?

It was just like him. That didn’t make it any less ridiculous.

“Why now, of all times?”

“Yes. Right now.”

“There’s so much to discuss. Even the Murim Alliance hasn’t made a proper decision yet…”

“If it’s that urgent, I’ll just piss right here.”

“Pardon?”

As the others stared at him, the middle-aged man gripped the waistband of his trousers, looking grave.

“My bladder’s about to burst.”

“…!”

“I don’t know whether it’s age or all this time I’ve spent away from our family, living out in the world, but my bladder seems to have gotten weak. And even when I’m sitting still, a certain private spot on my backside starts tingling…”

He rambled on until he noticed their expressions, then smacked his lips.

“Well, I shouldn’t have brought it up. Let’s get back to our discussion.”

“…Just go.”

“No, no. I’ll wet myself right here. I’m sure even Zhuge Wuhou did when he was busy.”

“Please go. We’re begging you.”

“Oh? Then you don’t mind?”

“Yes. For our sake, please.”

“You’ve begged me so earnestly three times. I suppose I’ll have to follow the story of the Three Visits to the Thatched Cottage.”

Smiling at the half-resigned group, the middle-aged man—Zhuge Feng, the Crouching Dragon Guest and Family Head of the Zhuge Clan—hurried out.

He didn’t head for the privy. Instead, he wound through maze-like paths for some time before reaching the garden in the Inner Hall, where only the Family Head was permitted to enter. There he finally stopped.

“It’s all well and good to stay out of sight, but you should at least tell me where to find you.”

In a corner of the spacious garden, someone stood with their back to him, gazing at a mulberry grove said to have been planted by Zhuge Kongming himself.

Zhuge Feng added quietly, “Don’t you agree, Alliance Leader?”
