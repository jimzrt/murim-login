# Chapter 27

“Fifteen-minute break.”

The reconnaissance squad members dropped to the ground like the air had gone out of them.

They looked exhausted, but I wasn’t particularly worried about those guys. From what I’d seen, they had solid basic stamina, and they’d done nothing but stand around and watch during the battle.

The other two were the problem…

“Are you all right?”

“Huff. I’m fine. I’m fine.”

Socheon answered between ragged breaths. He didn’t look like he was about to collapse just yet, but if he kept moving at this pace, he would hit his limit before long.

*Stubborn little brat.*

Earlier, I had offered to carry him along with his sister. He had flatly refused.

“If it gets too hard, tell me. I can handle carrying both of you.”

“Huff. I’m fine like this. This is enough.”

*Doesn’t look like it.*

“I’m saying this for everyone’s sake. Don’t answer without thinking.”

“Understood.”

His eyes hardened with determination. I placed the peacefully sleeping Soyul in his arms and turned away.

“Great Hero Gong.”

The pale-faced man raised his head.

“…Young Master Jin.”

His voice sounded ready to give out at any moment. His condition was worse than I’d thought. *Are you all right?* rose to the tip of my tongue, then died there.

“How long can you hold out?”

“I don’t know.”

An honest—and grave—answer.

“What about the fasting pills?”

I had given him several of my remaining fasting pills, but Gong Yacheong shook his head.

“They weren’t much help. Some quack must have made them. All they did was ruin my appetite. Hahaha.”

“…Was that supposed to make me laugh?”

“Wasn’t it funny?”

“No. Not at all.”

“That’s a shame… Cough!”

He suddenly broke into a coughing fit.

Drops of blood spattered the white snow.

*Damn it.*

Worried someone might see, I hurriedly stepped in front of Gong Yacheong.

“What’s going on? You weren’t this bad before.”

His condition had deteriorated drastically in half a day. Gong Yacheong no longer looked merely exhausted. He looked unmistakably ill.

“It was inevitable.”

The calm look in his eyes only made it more ominous. I brushed aside the hand trying to stop me and pulled open the front of his robe.

“Ah.”

His body was covered in wounds, but what shocked me were the blue veins spreading outward from his lower abdomen.

“What is this…? Don’t tell me…”

Gong Yacheong weakly closed his robe again. He seemed worried that someone else—especially Socheon or Soyul—might see.

“Those wolf-like bastards coated their weapons with poison.”

Now I understood why Gong Yacheong hadn’t recovered after taking the fasting pills. They could stave off hunger and replenish his strength, but they had no detoxifying effect whatsoever.

“You should’ve told me sooner!”

“Those bastards must have been short on money. They used cheap poison. Its potency was so weak that I didn’t notice until last night. By then, it was too late.”

Gong Yacheong’s stamina had already hit rock bottom by the time the poison took effect. Then he had continued forcing himself through this weather…

“Is there anything we can do?”

“There is.”

“Tell me.”

“But time won’t allow it. We cannot waste such precious time on one person.”

He was right.

But still…

“We have to try.”

“Young Master.”

“Everyone is exhausted. If we rest for one shichen[^1]—no, just half a shichen—and try the method, that should be enough.”

“Hahaha.”

“Don’t laugh. I was planning to stop and rest around here anyway…”

I didn’t even know what I was saying anymore. As Gong Yacheong watched me ramble, a faint smile touched his lips.

“Go.”

“…”

“You know it too, don’t you? If we delay here, they’ll catch us.”

I fell silent.

He was right. Ever since I’d seen his deathly complexion and the blood he coughed up, I’d known this might happen.

Maybe I’d known since last night.

*So this is how it ends?*

I had to leave Gong Yacheong behind. If I took him with us, he might survive for now, but everyone’s pace would slow.

What if I carried him myself?

Even with the System’s help, I was still human. I had spent two days at the front, clearing a path as we marched, and the fatigue had piled up.

*Two fasting pills left.*

Even the thirty fasting pills I’d started with were almost gone. Could I escape their clutches while carrying Gong Yacheong with only two pills remaining?

And if they still caught up to us, would I be able to fight them while exhausted? Could I face Jopil, One Question, One Kill—a Peak master?

The answer had been clear for a long time.

We both knew it.

“Please take care of the children.”

The moment Gong Yacheong spoke, a System notification rang out.

Ding.

> **System**
>
> **Quest**
>
> **Gong Yacheong’s Last Request**
>
> He wants only one thing now. See that the surviving children make it back safely.
>
> **Grade:** None
>
> **Limit:** Jin Taekyung
>
> **Task:** Socheon and Soyul’s safe return (Incomplete)
>
> **Reward:** None
>
> - Would you like to accept the Quest?

No reward.

It was the most shameless Quest I’d ever received.

*I’m busy trying to stay alive myself, old man.*

But I nodded.

If accepting it could ease even a little of the guilt in the corner of my heart, I would gladly do it.

“All right.”

Gong Yacheong smiled in satisfaction.

* * *

“You’re saying we’re leaving Great Hero Gong behind?”

Han Yeop stared at me in shock. Hyuk Mujin said nothing, as though lost in thought, while the other reconnaissance squad members exchanged uneasy glances.

“Yes.”

“That makes no sense!”

“Keep your voice down.”

Nothing good would come of Socheon finding out. He was exactly the kind of kid who would insist on staying behind with Gong Yacheong.

“B-but this…”

“It was Great Hero Gong’s decision. I agree with him.”

That was when Hyuk Mujin abruptly spoke up.

“Why?”

*Has this bastard not been beaten enough?*

I glared at him, but Hyuk Mujin neither flinched nor backed down. My clenched fist slowly relaxed.

“His condition is serious. If we keep taking him with us, he’ll put all of us in danger.”

“Is that all?”

“Yes.”

Han Yeop cut in, his face flushed.

“No. We can’t.”

“It’s an order.”

“Then I’ll disobey.”

Everyone stared at Han Yeop in surprise.

Even I hadn’t expected the boy who had declared himself my most ardent follower from the moment we met to openly defy me.

“That won’t change anything.”

“We can’t leave him behind like this.”

“And if we can’t?”

A wave of exhaustion suddenly washed over me. I rubbed my gritty eyes.

“Are you going to carry him?”

“Yes. I’ll carry him.”

“And you’ll be exhausted in no time.”

Once Han Yeop got tired, someone else would step forward to help. Then they would grow exhausted one by one, our pace would slow, and the enemy would catch us.

“Our opponents are battle-hardened wandering martial artists led by a Peak master. Do you think we’ll survive?”

Han Yeop lowered his head without answering. The other reconnaissance squad members avoided my gaze as well.

Only one person kept looking me in the eye.

“Number One. Do you still have something to say?”

Hyuk Mujin remained silent for a long moment before bowing his head.

“I’ll follow your orders, Squad Leader.”

* * *

We started moving again.

Just before we left, Gong Yacheong gently stroked Socheon and Soyul’s heads, his expression peaceful.

“I’ll see you soon.”

Socheon nodded bravely. Still half-asleep, Soyul fussed as she settled into my arms.

Every time I heard her soft breathing, discomfort tightened in a corner of my chest.

*Has he left by now?*

Gong Yacheong hadn’t wanted the children to notice his absence. That was why he had told me he would quietly slip away along the way.

Socheon was in the middle of the formation, so the reconnaissance squad members would block his view. He wouldn’t see Gong Yacheong leave.

*How long has it been since we started moving?*

A sikyeong? Half a shichen?

I couldn’t tell. In the dead of night, with darkness swallowing everything around us, even the passage of time was impossible to feel.

With every step, one thought refused to leave my mind.

*He must have left by now.*

It was only natural. Gong Yacheong and I both knew it, and every member of the reconnaissance squad except Han Yeop had accepted it.

Most importantly…

I had a family waiting for me. A real world I had to return to.

*Then why does this feel so damn awful?*

My feet felt heavy.

Not just because the snow reached my calves. Not because grass and branches blocked the way ahead.

A mere NPC named Gong Yacheong kept weighing on my mind.

I thought of his final smile. The cheap Quest without a single reward.

I thought of Han Yeop defying my order, and Hyuk Mujin’s calm gaze pricked at my chest like a thorn.

*It’s the obvious choice. So why?*

Because it was a game.

Because it was only a game.

*If I didn’t leave him behind, everyone would die. I’d die too, you fucking bastards!*

“Fuuuck…”

The curse lodged in my throat finally slipped out.

Soyul stirred from her light sleep, mumbled something, and wrapped her arms around my neck.

Her tiny hands were cold. So vivid that they raised goose bumps across my skin. Too real to believe this was a game.

Real enough to make me feel guilty for abandoning a single NPC.

“…What a fucked-up game.”

I turned around.

“Where are you going?”

I strode back the way we had come. I didn’t look at Socheon or any of the reconnaissance squad members.

That was why I didn’t notice the fleeting smile on Hyuk Mujin’s face as he asked where I was going.

Or that Han Yeop, who should have been at the very rear, was nowhere to be seen.

“Huff. Huuuff.”

I raced across the snow like the wind.

Then I found him.

Below the hill, Han Yeop was gritting his teeth and forcing one foot in front of the other even as he gasped for breath.

Gong Yacheong lay unconscious on his back.

“You…”

I didn’t know what to say. With a sigh, I grabbed Han Yeop’s hand and hauled him up.

“Th-thank you.”

*Shit…*

*I don’t know anymore.*

* * *

This was a secluded retreat meant for one person alone.

Since becoming its owner thirty years ago, he had forbidden anyone else from entering. Over time, that prohibition had hardened into an unbreakable rule, and everyone else had come to accept it as such.

“How are things going?”

The air trembled faintly as the two shadows conversed through Sound Transmission.

“Smoothly. And on your end?”

“Do you even need to ask?”

“I suppose not.”

“The Blood Wolf Sword. For a man with such an epithet, he’s surprisingly fond of his family.”

“A wolf still cares for its own blood. So?”

“The vanguard alone numbers two hundred. A madman named Jopil is running wild as he pleases, but… it should be fine.”

“Jopil, One Question, One Kill? The Blood Wolf Sword chose well.”

“The wastrel young master is being chased all over the place. It wasn’t part of the plan, but this isn’t bad either.”

“Hahahaha.”

“Could it be…?”

“That’s right. I sent him. Once the Lesser Family Head sees the severed head of the youngest brother he cherishes so dearly, he’ll change his mind.”

“Now that is a heart as cold as poison. No blood, no tears. You have my respect.”

“Are you one to talk?”

“I merely took one hopeless life.”

“And thanks to that, a storm of blood will sweep across Shanxi.”

“Isn’t that what you wanted?”

“I can’t deny it. Yes. I’ve waited far too long.”

“The fruit will taste all the sweeter.”

“I hope so.”

“Ah, one more thing. The Lower District Sect has gotten involved.”

“The Lower District Sect? How?”

“The new Branch Leader has a sharp nose. Once this matter is settled, I plan to drive them out.”

“Be careful. No matter how formidable Heaven may be, you must never let your guard down…”

At that moment, the wind stopped.

The air quivered.

“…I misspoke.”

A long while passed before another Sound Transmission arrived.

“You would do well to watch your words and actions.”

The voice was as soft as a cat’s paw.

But the listener could feel the keen edge of a razor beneath it.

“Allow me to apologize again.”

“Let’s end here for today. If any problems arise, I’ll visit you again in a few days.”

The conversation ended there.

The other person vanished without the slightest sound or trace.

*They’re like ghosts.*

Sometimes, he wondered who they truly were. How powerful were they? Who numbered among their ranks?

But he quickly shook his head.

*Such curiosity would only shorten my life.*

He had endured long years of hardship to reap the fruit. He couldn’t allow idle curiosity to ruin his great undertaking.

*It truly has been a long time.*

The shadow reached toward the moon. Faint moonlight slipped between his fingers and illuminated a silver beard.

*Soon… everything will return to its proper place.*

The Head Elder smiled with pleasure.

[^1]: A shichen is a traditional time period of roughly two hours; a sikyeong is a shorter traditional interval.
