# Chapter 677

*Click. Rumble.*

The stone gate swung open, spilling light into the darkness.

Heugung and Yohi flinched and groaned, their eyes long since accustomed to the dark. Then a voice reached them.

“Oh-ho. Now that’s a sight worth seeing.”

“……!”

“……!”

“Of course. This is how the barbarians of Nanman ought to look.”

The voice slurred and swayed as though its owner were drunk. Both of them trembled.

Was it the mockery in his words that made them shake?

No. It was fear.

*That voice…*

Yohi recognized it, and her blood seemed to turn cold.

How could she forget the voice of the Fiend who had appeared like a ghost, slaughtered her warriors, and left Heugung with one arm after a single move?

Her mind went blank. Beside her, Heugung whispered as quietly as he could.

“Remember. Whatever happens, don’t open your eyes—”

*Boom!*

A sharp blast of displaced air sounded, followed by a scream.

Yohi squeezed her eyes shut as he had told her. She could not see him, but it was easy enough to imagine Heugung coughing up dark-red blood from his internal injuries.

“Stop! Please, stop!”

“Cough, cough. Yohi, stay still—”

*Boom! Thud.*

Another blast rang out. Yohi could bear it no longer and opened her eyes.

Heugung lay on the floor, his whole body bound. He had vomited what looked like a full doe[^1] of blood. The Fiend stood over him.

“You…”

“I heard you were a clever woman. Seems that was just a rumor.”

The Fiend bared his yellow teeth in a grin.

His white hair hung in wild disarray, and his eyes were narrow slits. He had worn a mask at the Western Yao Estate, but there was no mistaking him.

“Why didn’t you listen to him? If you hadn’t seen this old man’s face, you might have lived at least another fifteen minutes.”

“……!”

“Well, too late now. I’ll just have to send you both to the afterlife together.”

He shrugged and reached toward her. His arm was unnaturally long for such a small man.

His palm was covered in spots Yohi could not identify. As it drew closer, so did the smell of death.

Yohi knew this was the end. She was about to struggle when—

“That’s enough, Black Hand.”

The words came through the gap in the half-open stone gate. The old man called Black Hand withdrew his hand and sighed.

“Now I can’t even have a little fun without watching myself.”

Someone beyond the gate answered.

“Was that directed at me?”

The voice was as cold as eternal snow. Black Hand smacked his lips.

“Of course not. Just talking to myself.”

“Enough of this reckless behavior. Surely you haven’t forgotten the Demon Empress’s order.”

Black Hand’s face twisted.

“Reckless? I never thought I’d hear that at my age.”

“Then act your age. If you do anything rash with the great undertaking so close at hand, I will not forgive you.”

“……”

“I’ll take your silence as an answer.”

Yohi had sensed no one outside the gate, nor could she sense anyone leaving. But when Black Hand muttered a moment later, she knew the speaker was gone.

“Damn old monster.”

To Black Hand, it might have been a passing complaint. Yohi had to fight to keep her shock and fear off her face.

*There’s someone else.*

She did not know who had spoken from beyond the gate. Their exchange had told her enough: he was old and powerful enough to make even the Fiend before her obey.

And there was something more.

*The Demon Empress. He said the Demon Empress.*

Yohi knew whom he meant.

*The Southern Heaven Demon Empress.*

One of Dark Heaven’s core figures, said to be unnaturally beautiful and more dangerous still.

Information about her was kept strictly secret, but Yohi was one of Nanman’s four Great Chieftains. She was an exception.

More importantly, she had always believed she could rely on Baeksang.

> “Stand by my side, and I’ll give you what you want.”

He had said it when they first met, shortly after Yohi became a Great Chieftain. She had asked him:

> “What do you intend to give me?”

> “Vast wealth and influence. And the restoration of the Yao people.”

> “Interesting. But I’ll refuse.”

> “Why?”

> “Even if I accept your offer, Great Chieftain Baeksang, I won’t get as much as I want. I’m more ambitious than I look. And I’m not naive enough to make Palace Lord Yayul my enemy over a promise you might not keep.”

> “You inherited the Western Yao Estate when you became Great Chieftain. Do you have people you trust there?”

> “Of course. Why do you ask…?”

> “I thought I might send a belated gift to congratulate you. Five carts of gold should be enough to start with.”

> “……!”

> “Go home for now. If you wish to accept my offer, meet me here at this time tomorrow.”

Five carts of gold.

At first, Yohi had hardly believed him. She had never imagined a sum like that. Then she saw the gleaming piles of gold waiting for her, and doubt became impossible.

Baeksang kept his promises. If she stood by him, she might receive more than she had asked for.

Yohi spent a sleepless night and went straight to Baeksang the next day. She thought her judgment had been sound.

No. She had believed it was sound.

Until the Fiend came for her.

“Oh-ho. Look at this little bitch.”

As if his irritation had vanished, Black Hand gave her a hazy, drunken smile.

“Those eyes have gone so wide. You’re starting to understand, aren’t you?”

“I—I…”

“I’ve heard the gist of it. They said things went smoothly for us thanks to you. I’ll give Baeksang that much—for a barbarian, he handled his part rather well.”

Yohi’s eyes shook.

She had been a pawn of Dark Heaven, and now she might die for it. She could scarcely believe it.

“That can’t be. It can’t. I didn’t know…”

“Didn’t know? Or pretended not to?”

“……!”

“I needn’t ask. You followed Baeksang for more than ten years. A woman capable of becoming Great Chieftain must have had her doubts.”

Black Hand chuckled. Yohi could only stare at him.

Everything he said was true.

It had started small. At the annual Tribal Grand Council, she supported Baeksang’s proposals. She followed a few minor instructions from him. In return, gold and influence came pouring in.

As the years passed, her doubts deepened. A few months ago, she began hearing the name Dark Heaven more and more often, and unease took hold.

But she did nothing. She pushed down her suspicions and refused to look at them.

She had gained too much to expose everything and try to put it right.

The Yao people looked up to the woman who had restored their weakening tribe. Chieftains who had once treated her like a little girl now bowed their heads first.

Even the mighty Beast Miao King and Baeksang would one day return to dust. When they did, she could set her sights on the vacant seat of power.

She would become Palace Lord—the first woman to do so—and rewrite the history of the Nanman Beast Palace.

That was the ambition Yohi had dwelt on every night. Now it was collapsing around her.

“You ought to thank this old man. If the previous Great Chieftain of the Yao people hadn’t died suddenly, how would a stupid little bitch like you have spent all these years living in luxury?”

“Th-that can’t mean…”

“He was troublesome. His martial arts were pathetic, but he noticed things. A plague happened to strike the Yao people at just the right time, so he was easy to deal with.”

Black Hand grinned.

“Strange, isn’t it? The Great Chieftain was old, so his death was one thing. But then his three sons, the ones meant to succeed him, died too. The position passed all the way down to a daughter born to him by a concubine in his old age.”

“……!”

“You should have seen Baeksang’s face. He dragged it out to the end, insisting he’d find a way to persuade the old man… But he never came after me. I suppose that’s why the bastard has managed to stay alive so long. A pity.”

Yohi stared at him, hollow-eyed. She thought nothing could surprise her now. She thought she had no strength left for it.

She was wrong.

“You did rather well for a woman, though. Setting out a tracking scent with your life at stake—that caution must come from your old father.”

*Tracking scent.*

Yohi went rigid at the words.

Black Hand saw the last of her hope break, and his delight spilled over.

“What? Did you think this old man wouldn’t know?”

He chuckled at the despair in her trembling eyes. He had seen it countless times, but the thrill of such moments had never faded.

“Oh, you poor, green little girl. You foolish child.”

A palm mottled with black spots, true to his name, stroked her pale cheek.

“You really are beautiful. No wonder the Demon Empress wants you.”

He opened his mouth like a predator over its prey. The stench from his teeth was foul; every one of them was rotten or grotesquely twisted.

“I’d like to kill you both with one palm strike, but… I’ll restrain myself this time. I have something to do first.”

He left Yohi frozen like an insect caught in a web and stood. After a glance at Heugung, who lay unconscious on the floor, he approached the stone gate.

Then—

*Rumble-rumble-rumble!*

A tremendous crash shook the area. From far away came a shout.

—Come out, you fucking bastards!

*GRAAAAAWR!*

It was time to welcome the guest who had come such a long way.

* * *

My method of calling out enemies was simple.

Break things. Wreck things. Smash them to pieces.

Poison Mist? Venomous beasts? Enemies hiding somewhere, waiting for their chance?

I didn’t care. I rampaged like an eight-ton truck with a broken steering wheel, smashing everything in the Poisonblood Grounds.

They’d have no choice but to come out.

Before long, it worked.

“What a vigorous young man.”

“I expected the Beast Miao King to come looking for us… Are you Jin Taekyung?”

I followed the voices and slowly turned around.

One old man was short and squat, swaying as if he were on something. The other was tall and gave off so much cold I wondered if he’d grown up at King Sejong Station in Antarctica.

They could hardly have looked less alike. But they had two things in common.

First, they were both Supreme Peak masters.

Second, both were giving off enough killing intent to make it clear they had no intention of letting me leave alive.

I considered the two of them for a moment, then spoke sternly.

“One of you step aside. By the customs of the martial world, I’ll take you on one at a time.”

The answer came immediately.

*Whoosh—KWA-BOOM!*

Yeah, fuck. I knew that wouldn’t work.

[^1]: A *doe* is a traditional Korean unit of volume, roughly 1.8 liters.
