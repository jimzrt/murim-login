# Chapter 539

The Slaughter Saint.

He had entered the world as an assassin, despised by all, and risen at last to become a star that lit the heavens.

He had torn down the fence that admitted only a chosen few, becoming living proof of the four words *Might Makes Right*.

Yet most people knew neither his age nor his face—not even his name. Assassins worked in the deepest secrecy. They did not reveal themselves.

Decades passed, and the veil over the Slaughter Saint never lifted.

He vanished soon after the war ended and did not show himself again. Meanwhile, rumors traveled a thousand li, ten thousand li, without a foot to carry them.

The Slaughter Saint was dead!

That was half true and half false.

On the day the Martial God defeated the Heavenly Demon and a hundred thousand demon soldiers fell, an old man in a bamboo hat buried something on an unnamed hill before walking away.

It was not only his beloved weapon, stained with blood. He buried the name Slaughter Saint with it.

The Slaughter Saint died, and Mungyeong emerged into the world. In time, the world began calling him by another name.

The Divine Physician.

The Yangtze has many tributaries, but all their waters join the same river.

Mungyeong was a river like that, bearing two names: the greatest assassin in history and the greatest physician under heaven.

To put it simply…

*My number-one recruitment target.*

That more than qualified him for a place aboard my Going Murim ship.

Sure, spending time with him meant the occasional attempt on my life. But I’d rather shit blood a few times than die because we ran into a monster like the Western Heaven Demon Lord.

Black cat, white cat. As long as it caught mice, what did the color matter?

It was a shame my first recruitment target wasn’t a cute little deer with horns, but a former assassin who wanted to kill me roughly three times a day. I didn’t have much choice.

*Who cares about his personality? He’s got top-tier damage output and healing abilities.*

Right. That was enough.

In this harsh Murim, where I couldn’t even get my hands on a cheap potion, I needed Mungyeong. Even if he never saved me, he might save someone traveling with us when their life was in danger.

The problem was that, in my confidence, I’d overlooked one person.

“Hehe.”

“……?”

“Hello, Benefactor.”

“……!”

Hello, my ass. What was going on?

*No way.*

I stared at Cheongpung, my eyes trembling, and finally managed to speak.

“……Why are you coming out of there?”

“Oh, well. I was passing by earlier.”

“No, I know why you came here. I mean—”

Fuck. I couldn’t even get the words out.

Mungyeong watched me stammer without a trace of expression and answered for him.

“I told you. You were a step too late.”

Cheongpung smiled brightly.

“Hehe. That’s how it happened, Benefactor.”

There went any doubt I’d had. I looked from one to the other in silence before forcing my mouth open.

“Is this for real?”

Mungyeong nodded.

“It’s hardly a legend.”

“So you really did?”

“Yes. We did.”

“Why?”

“I have no reason to tell you.”

“Fair enough. But I think I could give up gracefully if I heard it from you.”

Mungyeong clicked his tongue.

“You’re making an ugly business of it.”

“What if I still want to know?”

“Then I’ll have to find a cleaner way to settle it.”

Shing.

Something silver glinted at the end of his long sleeve. I rubbed my eyes at the sight of the short sword that had popped out like a jack-in-the-box.

“Maybe I’m tired and my eyes are going bad. Why is *that* coming out now?”

“It would settle things cleanly.”

“Maybe, but wouldn’t it make a mess around here?”

“That doesn’t matter. I know a number of methods.”

“……”

He must have picked up a few tricks in all his years as an assassin. I had no desire whatsoever to see any of them firsthand.

“Whew. I’ll be going.”

“Go. Forever.”

“See you later, Benefactor!”

I turned away after those two very different farewells.

*This is driving me crazy.*

Cheongpung had beaten me to it. I hadn’t seen that coming at all.

The Cheongpung I knew should have had a meat dumpling in one hand and a vegetable dumpling in the other, trying to decide which to eat first.

*Did he put points into Intelligence instead of me…?*

Nothing for it. I’d have to move fast and recruit other people first.

Hyuk Mujin was loitering by the clinic entrance. He hurried over when he spotted me.

“Did it go well—… From your face, I’m guessing not.”

He blinked, having changed course the instant he saw my expression.

“I don’t see Mungyeong. Does that mean…?”

“Can’t you tell?”

“Huh. He turned down the Captain’s offer? He’s better at judging a situation than he looks— I mean, I’m joking. Please, I’m begging you. Put your fist down.”

“One more joke and it’ll be your last words.”

Mujin backed away to a safe distance and adopted a grave tone.

“There is one way.”

“A way?”

“Yes.”

He sounded so sure of himself that I couldn’t help listening. I was grasping at a rotten rope, but I asked anyway.

“What is it?”

“Should I have a word with him? Mungyeong hangs on everything I say.”

“……”

Hangs on your words, my ass. He’d kill you in the blink of an eye.

If Mungyeong wanted to, he could turn Mujin into a corpse on an inhale and reduce him to dust on the exhale.

“Mujin…”

“Don’t worry. We’ve been through a lot together. If Young Hero Gung and I go talk to him, he won’t turn us down cold.”

“That’s not it, you bastard…”

Now he was trying to drag someone else into his suicide attempt.

I looked at him with a pang of pity.

“You really hate Gibang, don’t you?”

“What?”

“Never mind. Anyway, don’t get married—no, don’t try to persuade him. Ever.”

“Why?”

“Just don’t, you bastard.”

Mujin didn’t know who Mungyeong really was. There was no talking sense into him about this.

I sighed at his aggrieved expression.

“Can you write?”

“Write? I’d make Wang Xizhi weep.”

“Give me a straight answer before I make *you* weep.”

“……I write well enough. Believe it or not, people called me a prodigy when I was little.”

“Then write out a few big notices and put one up.”

“A notice?”

“Yeah. If it’s come to this, we might as well look for some real talent.”

The world was vast, and masters were plentiful.

Henan, right now, was a prince’s residence teeming with dragons and tigers.

*What’s a public audition, anyway?*

The imperial examinations were the oldest audition program around, if you thought about it. With my reputation and my eye for talent, it was worth a shot.

I pictured applicants flooding in.

*Let’s do it. Fuck it.*

* * *

Shik, sssshk!

The movements were quick and supple, unlike anything the onlookers had ever seen. Each time the hands danced, the tip of a long needle flashed and its thread drew the wound shut.

In mere moments, a swordsman who had been slowly dying from a deep slash across his abdomen had a chance to live.

“How can someone so young have such skill…?”

“I’ve never even heard of a medical apprentice like him.”

“Good heavens. That’s nearly divine!”

The gathered physicians, from heavily bearded middle-aged men to white-haired elders, stared in astonishment. A voice snapped them back to their senses.

“Finished.”

Tap.

The treatment was over in a flash. More astonishing than its speed was the fact that it had been done perfectly.

Mouths agape, the physicians turned to the speaker as he rose from his seat.

“H-How did you…?”

“Wait!”

He looked too young to call a young man, yet something about him made *boy* seem wrong as well.

Mungyeong gathered his belongings.

“Are there any patients left?”

The physicians exchanged glances, then shook their heads all at once.

“N-No, but…”

“That’s right. Thanks to you, they’ve all been treated.”

This was hardly the first time the young medical apprentice had astonished them.

About one shichen earlier, he had arrived with a patient whose injuries were minor. He’d taken one look at the people lying there and remarked:

*This won’t take long.*

Then he’d begun treating them one by one, so quickly and so thoroughly that the physicians who’d come over to scold him and throw him out couldn’t tear themselves away.

*Can medicine like this truly exist?*

*Who is he?*

They had waited to ask him exactly that. Mungyeong’s answer, however, was perfectly calm.

“Then I’ll be going.”

“W-Wait! I have a mountain of questions for you!”

“What is your master’s name? Could it be…?”

Questions came at him from every side. Mungyeong inclined his head slightly.

“If I have time, I’ll stop by once more before I leave and check on the patients.”

“You may have come in whenever you pleased, but you’re not leaving!”

“Grab him!”

“Forgive me! We can’t let you go like this!”

Whoosh! Crash!

The physicians threw themselves at him, but their hands closed on empty air. They didn’t so much as brush his collar before tumbling across the floor.

As they stared about in confusion, Mungyeong’s voice rang out.

“I left a prescription for each patient where I was sitting. Read them and carry out the treatments as written.”

“W-What? Prescriptions?”

“Where? Where are they?”

To a physician, prescriptions left by a master doctor were like a peerless martial artist’s martial arts manual.

Their eyes all but rolling back, the physicians launched into a fierce struggle over the dozens of prescriptions. Mungyeong slipped outside and walked on, then spoke without warning.

“Come out.”

Swish.

With a faint rustle, a figure emerged from behind a large pillar.

“Oh! How did you know?”

“I just did.”

“You really are amazing. I thought I’d fool you this time. Hehe.”

Mungyeong studied Cheongpung’s cheerful face.

“What martial art was that?”

“A footwork technique.”

“I can see that.”

“Oh. I named it Mimi Step.”

“Mimi Step?”

“Yes. After our Mimi. Isn’t it great?”

Cheongpung’s eyes shone. Mungyeong’s reply was cold.

“……It’s a ridiculous name.”

“Oh. Ah…”

Cheongpung wilted. Mungyeong watched him, his gaze deepening.

*What is with this boy?*

The name Mimi Step might be ridiculous, but there was nothing ridiculous about the principles behind those movements.

*He created a martial art from the way a snake moves.*

Most martial artists spent their entire lives trying to understand a single martial art well enough to make it their own. The boy before him had done that as though it were nothing.

Then, having made it his own, he had recreated it entirely.

*He has the makings of a Grandmaster.*

Jin Taekyung wasn’t the only genius. In some ways, this boy’s genius far surpassed his.

*Sword Saint, you’ve raised a monster.*

Mungyeong turned to Cheongpung, who still looked dejected.

“So why did you offer me your hand?”

“Hmm. Because Benefactor is so amazing.”

“Jin Taekyung?”

“Yes. I used to hope Benefactor would catch up with me soon, but… now I feel like he’s getting farther away, little by little.”

“I see.”

Mungyeong understood at once. A genius who had never had anyone to measure himself against was burning with competitive spirit for the first time.

“Are you hoping to learn martial arts from me?”

“No. You don’t have to teach me.”

“What?”

“I’ll watch and learn from you. That’s enough. Hehe.”

“……!”

Mungyeong paused, then gave a quiet laugh.

What an absurd boy. He meant to learn just by watching—and the martial arts he intended to watch were *Mungyeong’s*.

Yet Mungyeong didn’t mind.

“Oh! You smiled.”

“……I did not.”

“You did. I saw you.”

“You impudent little brat.”

“Oh. Now you’re angry.”

“……Brat.”

“Would you like a dumpling?”

Mungyeong hesitated, wondering whether to draw his short sword. Instead, he took the dumpling Cheongpung offered and bit into it.

It tasted rather good.
