# Chapter 727

Should I tear it up or not?

Choi Minwoo watched the thought play out across Jin Taekyung’s face and let out an incredulous little laugh.

*I really can’t figure him out.*

Most of the time, his words, actions, and thoughts were as simple as could be. But every so often, Jin Taekyung showed a side of himself that went beyond surprising.

*You’d think I’d be used to it by now. But this…*

Choi Minwoo looked down at the table.

**[The Smiling Mana Cultivation Method]**

The crooked handwriting looked like a child’s, and the title was ridiculous. But there was nothing ridiculous about what filled those ten or so sheets of A4 paper.

*He created an entirely new mana cultivation method.*

There was a simple reason Choi Minwoo had teased him about his handwriting earlier. It was a small act of revenge for springing something this extraordinary on him without a word.

The shock he had felt a few hours ago had been that great.

*How is this possible?*

He had never seen explanations or instructions so simple and clear. The method was exceptionally stable, with a firm grounding in the fundamentals.

Choi Minwoo had grown skilled enough to hold his own against a Peak master of Murim. He had recognized the value of this new method immediately. He had also understood what the recent events that had drawn the world’s attention meant to Jin Taekyung.

“The Week of Judgment.”

Choi Minwoo broke the brief silence and looked at Jin Taekyung.

“‘Just as God created heaven and earth over the course of a week, terrorists around the world endured a Week of Judgment.’ President Doramp Jr. said that at a White House press conference a few days ago.”

The President of the United States had said it in public. Naturally, his provocative remarks had spread around the world in less than an hour. By now, everyone in Korea had heard about them.

Everyone but one person, perhaps.

“He said that?”

Jin Taekyung had been holed up in the training room the whole time. Choi Minwoo nodded.

“It was a fairly aggressive thing for any president to say, especially the American president. Some people criticized him for deliberately provoking the terrorists.”

“Hm. Why bring up something as grand as the creation of heaven and earth? Were there any retaliatory attacks?”

“None. Not a single one.”

“Not one?”

“No. If anything, they’ve been keeping their mouths shut.”

The sullen look on Jin Taekyung’s face eased a little.

“Guess beating the crap out of them paid off.”

Choi Minwoo studied him for a moment, then spoke.

“So that was why.”

“Why what?”

“I’ve been thinking about two things. Why you suddenly went after the terrorists, and why you gave me this new mana cultivation method.”

Now he had his answer.

**[The Smiling Mana Cultivation Method]**

Choi Minwoo lifted the papers, held together with a single careless staple.

“You’re planning to make this mana cultivation method available to everyone.”

Jin Taekyung grinned.

“Our Team Leader Choi really is sharp.”

* * *

The look Team Leader Choi gave me was hard to read. Part of it seemed like admiration. The rest looked like he had a lot to think about.

“Why? Is there a problem?”

“Well…”

He ran a hand through his disheveled hair.

“There are so many problems, I don’t know where to start.”

“There’s nothing wrong with the mana cultivation method itself, so you mean practical problems.”

“…Yes. Unfortunately.”

“What’s the biggest one?”

He thought for a moment.

“For starters, making the method public is a problem in its own right.”

“Go on.”

“You know that only a tiny fraction of high-ranking Hunters possess mana cultivation methods. They’re closely guarded knowledge—secret arts, essentially. You also know how closed-off those Hunters are, and how different their world is from the one other Hunters live in.”

“I know. I’ve been in this business for years. Though back when I was an F-rank Hunter, I’d never even heard of a mana cultivation method.”

No profession divided its members more sharply than the Hunter business.

Ordinary people judged one another by education, family background, and looks. Hunters were sorted into high and low by rank alone.

How much mana you’d been granted. How agile you were. How strong. Those crude measures determined your place, and you were graded like cattle headed for slaughter.

*There’s a reason they call F-rank Hunters slaves.*

High-ranking Hunters, meanwhile, practically lived above the clouds. They earned more from a single raid than most Hunters could make in a lifetime and spent their days under spotlights and envious stares.

Maybe that was why they’d built walls around themselves, like nobles of old living inside their castles. No one but another high-ranking Hunter could cross those walls. Even if someone was lucky enough to receive a high rank, the gate opened only with their permission.

Those high-ranking Hunters were connected by invisible ties across the world, each protected by the giant Guild they belonged to.

“This isn’t limited to Korea,” Team Leader Choi said. “If you make this method public, the backlash will be greater than you expect.”

“Because they’ll think releasing a mana cultivation method makes what they have less valuable?”

“That’s part of it. There’s another reason.”

“What?”

“The problem is that it’s *us*.”

“…!”

That brief answer was enough.

*Have we stood out too much?*

I remembered what Jeok Cheongang had told me just before I Logged Out: whether I’d wanted it or not, I’d drawn far more attention than I should have.

The spotlight was bright. But everything around it was dark.

I’d read that line in a novel I liked back in school. It was true. The brighter I became, the deeper the darkness around me grew.

In Murim and in the modern world.

“Team Leader Choi, are you saying…?”

He understood the question and shook his head.

“There’s been no major problem yet, but we’re facing pressure behind the scenes. We’ve put plans for more overseas branches on hold for now.”

“It’s that serious?”

“Every major Guild in the world is watching us. They don’t need to make a show of force to cause us trouble. If they decide to act, things won’t be easy for us.”

“Why didn’t you tell me?”

He gave me a flat look.

“Did you give me a chance?”

“…Fair.”

This place was a fucking mess too.

People with plenty to lose didn’t like the world changing. The handful of Hunters with mana cultivation methods of their own were no exception.

*At this point, calling them power brokers would be more accurate than calling them Hunters.*

Some of them might have enough influence to bend an entire country to their will. With their wealth, fame, and overwhelming strength, they could turn common sense on its head. They didn’t want change, and they certainly didn’t want anyone new rising to their level.

Anyone like me or Team Leader Choi.

*Even so, these bastards might be worse than Murim sects.*

Leak a sect’s unique martial arts in Murim, and they’d hunt you down with murder in their eyes and smash your skull. But at least they wouldn’t throw a fit over a merchant selling the Three Calamities Sword Technique at a market.

These guys already had more than enough, and they were ready to raise hell because we wanted to share one little mana cultivation method?

*Huh. Now I’m pissed.*

“If we release it, they’ll go nuts, right?”

“Yes. And we’ll have a mess on our hands.”

“What if I go after them?”

“What?”

“Now that I think about it, this is fucking ridiculous. We release the method without overthinking it, then I go after whichever assholes start trouble. That ought to shut them—”

“No! Don’t! Absolutely not!”

For Team Leader Choi’s normally low, composed voice to reach that volume, he might as well have said, *Over my dead body, you lunatic.*

This time, though, I couldn’t just give in.

“I know what you’re saying, Team Leader Choi, but we have to make it public. I didn’t create it to keep it to myself like a diary.”

Magical power had already increased throughout the world. No—it was still increasing, even now. Mutation Gates were appearing one after another, and monster waves had begun.

“Monsters are getting stronger as magical power increases. You know that. But if people learn this mana cultivation method now…”

“Hunters’ abilities will improve across the board. It’s stable enough for even the lowest-ranked Hunters to learn without difficulty.”

Team Leader Choi’s expression hardened.

“I know what you’re thinking, Mr. Jin. That’s why you went after those rebel groups and terrorists in the first place, isn’t it? To reduce the chance of this method being misused.”

“Yes. Before I worried about how sharp the weapon I’d made was, I had to think about whose hands it might end up in.”

About two weeks ago in modern-world time, an incident in Texas had driven that point home: an attempted terrorist attack using a Magic Gem.

Fortunately, the attempt had failed without any casualties. But it had made me realize something.

“When I said I wanted to share it with *everyone*, that included every kind of criminal and lunatic.”

Afterward, I followed Magic Johnson to the Pentagon. With the U.S. President’s tacit approval, we got intelligence on those people. Then my companions and I went into the desert, spilled a lot of blood, and cut down the threats we could find.

Rebel groups that trained kidnapped children to shoot. Fanatical terrorists willing to carry out suicide attacks…

I killed the ones who stood in our way. The dead joined the Skeleton King’s undead army and pursued those who fled.

For the ones farther away, we used captured leaders to send false orders: rebel groups were told to attack terrorist organizations, and terrorist organizations were told to attack rebel groups.

It was a war between bastards and motherfuckers. Tricked into launching all-out attacks on one another, both sides suffered massive losses.

“As you know, we couldn’t wipe them out completely, and we won’t be able to in the future. But we’ve achieved what we needed to for now. Even if those bastards learn this method, they won’t be as dangerous as they were before.”

They wouldn’t be the only ones growing stronger from it. Hunters everywhere would improve enough to fight monsters strengthened by the rise in magical power. That should help keep the balance.

*And if things get much worse…*

Then I’d release the Jin Family’s Cultivation Technique, too. So far, I’d taught it to only a handful of people, including Team Leader Choi.

Low-ranking Hunters who knew nothing about mana cultivation methods couldn’t make use of it if I handed it to them now. But once they’d learned the basics, they could practice it on their own.

I met Team Leader Choi’s eyes.

“We have to make it public. One way or another.”

“…”

“Luckily, it isn’t too late. The public still thinks pretty highly of both of us.”

“Pretty highly” was an understatement. People were going wild over us.

I was being hailed as a young hero of a new age. After Butler Kim’s death, Team Leader Choi had become Guild Master of the Peace Guild—and before the paint on his new nameplate had dried, he’d taken over the Ares Guild too.

Then there was the glow of his family name. His maternal grandfather was Cheon Taemin, the savior who had saved humanity from the Demon King Asmodeus. Team Leader Choi was his only blood relative and heir.

I grabbed his hand.

“Team Leader Choi.”

I gripped it tight. When he met my fervent gaze, he flinched as though he’d been burned.

“W-Why are you doing this all of a sudden?”

He looked as though he’d run into Magic Johnson in a dark alley at night. I held on and pressed ahead.

“If you’re that worried, let’s try another way. Hm?”

“What way…?”

“Let us use your grandfather’s name just this once.”

“What?”

“Come on. Let’s cash in on the savior’s name.”

“…!”
