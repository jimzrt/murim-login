# Chapter 111

*Thudthudthudthud!*

Four fine horses thundered down the main road. Exhausted from too little rest, they were already gasping for breath, but we couldn’t afford to slow down.

> **System**
>
> **Time Limit:** 16:25:32

Thirty-one, thirty. The seconds kept ticking away.

Three *shichen*—a full six hours—had already passed. We had stopped at a small village along the way to look for fresh horses, but all they had were small, painfully slow packhorses.

*We really do need to rest.*

We were trapped between a rock and a hard place.

We were already pushing as hard as we could, yet the time limit was still cutting it close. If we kept up this pace, the horses wouldn’t last.

*Do we have a choice?*

I was just about to suggest to Wolhwa and Jin Mukyung that we stop, even if only briefly, when—

“Hm?”

“Young Master Jin! Ahead!”

Even without Wolhwa’s shout, I had already seen them. Several dozen *jang* ahead, a group of dark figures blocked the main road.

Every one of them wore filthy clothes, with a curved saber jutting from his belt. There was no need to ask who they were.

*The Red Wind Band.*

They had surrounded a group of what looked like ordinary civilians and were threatening them. At the sound of hoofbeats, they whipped their heads around.

The mounted bandits spotted me riding well ahead of the others and grinned, baring yellow teeth.

“Well, look at that. Our next customer’s here already. Stop!”

“Oh, sure.”

They told me to stop, so I had to stop. What else could I do?

*Thud! Thud!*

“Gaaah!”

“Argh!”

My horse didn’t come to a halt until it had trampled the two men blocking the road. The mounted bandits—and even the travelers they had captured—stared at me wide-eyed.

“You—you bastard!”

I hopped down from the saddle and asked, “Just making sure. You’re with the Red Wind Band, right?”

“Who the hell are you?”

“Judging by that reaction, I guess you are. I’m short on time, so let’s finish this quickly.”

Without hesitation, I kicked the nearest man in the leg.

*Crack.*

His shinbone snapped with a sickening crunch, and he crumpled to the ground.

It all happened in an instant. The others stood stunned for a moment, then hastily leveled their curved sabers and spears at me.

“Kill him!”

“A man should always watch his back.”

“What?”

“I said, watch behind you.”

The instant I finished speaking, ten pairs of eyes turned to look behind them.

*Craack! Thud!*

Three fine horses charging at full speed swept them away.

* * *

The battle was over before it had even begun. The men struck by the horses had gone flying like bowling pins and lay sprawled on the ground, half-dead. The rest were subdued with ease.

“P-Please, spare my life.”

“I won’t kill you. But I’ll have to fix a few things first.”

“Eek!”

While Jin Mukyung methodically snapped the surviving bandits’ limbs, Hyuk Mujin brought over the horses tied beside the road.

“We can switch to these healthy ones. There are about ten, so we might as well take them all and change horses whenever one gets tired.”

I had been thinking the same thing. Unlike the last group we had encountered, these mounted bandits each had their own horse. That was fortunate.

*Still…*

How many of these Red Wind Band bastards were there?

According to the information we had obtained at the shrine, more than a hundred remnants had scattered throughout northern Shanxi.

Some hid in the foothills, others in remote villages or crowded towns, waiting for orders to assemble at the designated rendezvous point.

*These men aren’t stragglers.*

They were reserve forces carrying out an operation. The Red Wind Band Leader was replenishing his forces on the plateau and moving south while ordering the subordinates he had left behind to move north.

It wasn’t hard to see why Wolhwa had called him a dangerous man.

*The judgment to retreat swiftly when the tide turned against him. The foresight to prepare his next plan even while retreating. And the drive to put that plan into action.*

On top of that, I had heard his own martial arts were formidable.

At this point, it almost felt wrong to dismiss him as a mere mounted bandit.

*This has gotten seriously messy.*

Now I understood why the Quest Grade had risen to Peak. Things kept getting worse the more I learned.

Surely the Red Wind Band Leader wasn’t a Peak master too… right?

I asked Wolhwa just in case, and she immediately nodded.

“Yes. He is.”

“…Ah.”

“There’s always a reason someone rises to prominence so quickly. He isn’t widely known yet, but according to our sect’s intelligence, the Red Wind Band Leader is indeed a Peak master.”

I stared at her in disbelief.

“Why would a Peak master become a mounted bandit?”

“There are even Supreme Peak masters among mountain bandits and water bandits. Why couldn’t a mounted bandit be one?”

“Supreme Peak masters? Among mountain bandits and water bandits?”

“If you ever meet the Green Forest Alliance Leader or the Alliance Leader of the Yangtze River Channel League, ask them yourself. Not that you ever will.”

“…I sincerely hope not.”

A Supreme Peak master? I genuinely hoped I would never meet one.

Shaking my head, I mounted one of the horses we had just taken. Jin Mukyung and Hyuk Mujin followed after securely binding the Red Wind Band members and turning them over to the civilians.

> **System**
>
> **Time Limit:** 15:59:13

Even now, the time limit continued to tick down. Leaving the civilians bowing deeply behind us, we kicked our horses in the ribs.

“Giddyap!”

* * *

Deep within the inner grounds of the Mount Heng Sword Sect lay a garden that only a select few were permitted to enter. Cheol Mubaek, the Tiger of Mount Heng, was the only outsider ever granted that privilege.

“This is my first time here.”

“It was a special place. Whenever something weighed on Father’s mind, he would come to the garden.”

Lee Seowol walked through the snow-covered garden, her footsteps crunching softly. Then she stopped at the sight of a frost-covered flower.

“Mother loved this flower.”

“Did she?”

“Yes. Whenever she tended the garden, she would bring me along and teach me the names of the flowers.”

Lee Seowol fell silent in thought for a moment before continuing.

“But I can’t remember its name now.”

“It was a long time ago. Don’t let it trouble you.”

“Uncle Cheol.”

“Yes?”

“I don’t like flowers. I only followed Mother because she did. The truth is, I never cared about them. Father came to this garden for the same reason.”

Lee Seowol slowly surveyed the snow-covered garden. The flowers had withered, and the burial mounds at its center had grown from one to four.

Her gaze darkened as she silently stared at the mounds where her family lay.

*Whose fault was it?*

Perhaps it was her own fault for being born the daughter of a Murim martial artist.

Perhaps that was why, after losing her mother ten years ago, she had lost her father and two older brothers one after another.

*I should have tried harder to stop him…*

A memory from two months ago rose unbidden, blurring her vision.

It was a conversation between father and daughter, held alone late one night.

*“I intend to tie you to the third son of the Jin Family of Taiyuan.”*

*“The third son? Surely you aren’t thinking of marrying me to that good-for-nothing?”*

*“No. But it will cause a scandal you may find difficult to endure.”*

*“I see.”*

*“Is that all you have to say?”*

*“What else can I do? It’s my fault for having a heartless father.”*

*“I can never make sense of you. Does this truly not bother you?”*

*“If I said I didn’t want this, would you change your mind?”*

*“At the very least, I would look for another way.”*

*“So war with the Jin Family of Taiyuan is already a foregone conclusion.”*

*“Now is the perfect time, with the Strange Hero of Shanxi and the Heaven Shaking Sword both absent. An opportunity like this will never come again.”*

*“The Jin Family of Taiyuan is strong even without the Family Head and the Second Young Master. Please reconsider.”*

*“Impossible. My decision has already been made.”*

*“Then make certain you win. Become so powerful that no one in Shanxi would dare breathe a word about any scandal involving me.”*

*“…Had you been born a man, I would have made you the Young Sect Leader.”*

*“Then I’m glad I was born a woman. I have no interest in becoming the Young Sect Leader of this sect.”*

Her fears soon became reality.

Had even a fortnight passed before Lee Seogeun returned as a cold corpse? Not long after, she lost Lee Cheonbaek. In the end, even her eldest brother, Lee Seogwang, was gone.

*Now I’m all alone.*

And so, Lee Seowol, the last surviving blood relative of the Blood Wolf Sword Lee Cheonbaek, became the new Sect Leader.

As she stared silently at the burial mounds, a warm hand settled gently on her shoulder.

“I’m sorry. I should have come sooner…”

“Uncle Cheol, please don’t say that. If you hadn’t come, our sect wouldn’t have held out this long.”

The Mount Heng Sword Sect had been helplessly driven back by the Red Wind Band’s relentless assault. If the Tiger of Mount Heng—a Peak master who had rushed over after belatedly learning of Lee Cheonbaek’s death—had not arrived, the enemy would never have retreated.

“I swear I’ll tear that bastard limb from limb.”

Thinking of the Red Wind Band Leader, Lee Seowol shook her head.

In a life-and-death duel between Peak masters, Cheol Mubaek held the advantage.

The two had already clashed once, and Pung Yang had retreated with a minor internal injury.

*But he’ll never give Uncle Cheol that chance again.*

The Mount Heng Sword Sect had always gathered information on the mounted bandits of the northern plateau and kept a close watch on their movements.

Dozens of mounted-bandit groups roamed the plateau, but even among them, the Red Wind Band led by Pung Yang had grown at a frightening rate.

Pung Yang was exceptionally strong and meticulous, even among the chieftains of the plateau.

*There’s no way a man like that would fight Uncle Cheol in a life-and-death duel. If Uncle Cheol tries to confront him rashly, Pung Yang will turn it against him.*

Lee Seowol turned toward Cheol Mubaek.

“Uncle Cheol, could you tell me about Pung Yang’s martial arts once more?”

“He has just entered the Peak realm. His saber arts and throwing-knife techniques have both reached an advanced level. However…”

A deep furrow appeared between Cheol Mubaek’s brows.

“Every one of his forms was utterly insidious. I suspect he has learned demonic, heterodox arts.”

“Demonic, heterodox arts…”

Ever since the Great Faction War, the words *demonic, heterodox arts* had become synonymous with death in the Central Plains. Unorthodox factions might present themselves as orthodox, but no one openly declared themselves unorthodox.

“For now, it is only a suspicion. I’ll know once we clash again.”

“I trust you, Uncle Cheol. But don’t underestimate the Red Wind Band Leader. He has no shortage of subordinates willing to die in his place.”

The enemies pressing in from both north and south numbered close to three hundred.

Meanwhile, even after recalling every martial artist stationed at its branches, the Mount Heng Sword Sect had less than half that number.

Given the situation, morale was low among the ordinary martial artists and newly appointed senior members alike.

“Seowol. May I say something?”

This was a benefactor who had risked his life out of loyalty to his dead friend. Lee Seowol bowed respectfully.

“I’ll take your words to heart.”

Cheol Mubaek spoke in a heavy voice.

“Leave.”

A single word, laden with meaning.

But Lee Seowol answered without the slightest hesitation.

“I’m sorry.”

“It isn’t too late. You must survive.”

“It isn’t over yet. And I will survive.”

“If you think Cheonbaek would have wanted this…”

“Uncle.”

Her firm voice silenced Cheol Mubaek. Steely resolve shone in Lee Seowol’s clear eyes.

“This is what I want—as the Sect Leader of the Mount Heng Sword Sect.”

“Whew…”

Cheol Mubaek answered with a sigh.

“I’m already deeply indebted to you, Uncle. Even if you leave now, I won’t resent you.”

“Will you only be satisfied once you hear me say it?”

Lee Seowol shook her head. Cheol Mubaek had cherished her like his own child since she was young. In some ways, he had been more of a father to her than her actual father.

“I will never forget what you’ve done for us. Uncle Cheol, you are the benefactor of both me and the Mount Heng Sword Sect.”

“I’ve watched you grow up, but… you truly are a sly child.”

“I was sly as a child. Now I’m a ruthless bitch.”

Cheol Mubaek could only sigh again as Lee Seowol smiled.

“Do we have any chance of winning?”

“If we fought now? Ten percent.”

“What?”

“But if reinforcements arrive, fifty percent. Perhaps more.”

“Reinforcements? From the Jin Family of Taiyuan?”

“A messenger pigeon from the Lower District Sect’s Jeongyang Branch arrived two *shichen* ago.”

“How many are coming? A hundred? Two hundred?”

“Four. One of them is the Heaven Shaking Sword, Jin Mukyung. And another is…”

Lee Seowol let out a wry laugh as she remembered the bad blood between them.

“The Sleeping Dragon of Shanxi, Jin Taekyung.”
