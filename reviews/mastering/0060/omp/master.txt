# Chapter 60

The Head Elder thought,

*Have I lived too long?*

The Murim he remembered was not like this. Martial artists were not like this.

Ganging up on an opponent was shameful. Ambushes were condemned. Anyone who got so much as dirt on their back became a laughingstock.

And yet…

“Throw dirt! Keep throwing!”

In eighty years of life, he had never seen anything like this. What? Throw dirt?

It was a low, cowardly trick that even the Demonic Cultists he had fought like mortal enemies decades ago had never resorted to.

*Is that bastard truly a martial artist?*

Even more astonishing was the reaction of his so-called subordinates.

“Squad Leader’s orders! Throw dirt!”

“Keep your distance! Watch the Sword Energy!”

Watching them obey without a word made him dizzy.

*What a pack of bastards begging to be killed.*

These were not martial artists.

No, they were not even human.

This was an insult to martial artists—and to the Head Elder himself, who had devoted his entire life to the sword.

“How dare you…”

He was drawing his sword when—

“You idiots!”

A stern shout rang out half a beat sooner. A young man whose face was swollen as if he had been stung by bees swept a fierce gaze around him.

“What the hell do you think you’re doing?”

He looked ridiculous, but he had real spirit. Yes. That was how a martial artist ought to act.

*At least there’s one decent man among them.*

The Head Elder was just nodding to himself when—

“You think dirt’s going to cut it? Mix in rocks too!”

“…!”

The Head Elder shuddered like a man struck by lightning.

Overwhelmed by a humiliation unlike anything he had ever experienced, he even forgot to swing his sword as a clod of dirt flew into his face.

Splat!

He could say this with absolute certainty: in all his eighty years, he had never taken an attack so humiliatingly or with his guard so completely down.

As the dirt pattered to the ground, he spotted a hefty rock carefully mixed in with it.

“Uh-heh. Uh-heh-heh-heh.”

The Head Elder laughed like a madman.

Then his laughter abruptly stopped.

At the same moment—

Tssssss.

Sword Energy surged up, brimming with sixty years of internal energy.

“Are you prepared?”

Watching him, Jin Taekyung muttered,

“Shouldn’t have mixed in the rocks…”

Before the words had even left his mouth, the Head Elder charged like a lion. The dozen or so sheep scattered with shrieks.

“Scatter! Scatter!”

“Keep throwing dirt!”

Even while fleeing, they did not forget to throw dirt.

* * *

Fwoooosh—

A fierce gale tore from the tip of the Head Elder’s sword. Sand, dirt, stones—it made no difference. Before such overwhelming power, they could only shatter and scatter.

*How the hell are we supposed to fight this guy?*

Head-on?

Calling us melee damage dealers was putting it nicely. If I sent the reconnaissance squad against the Head Elder, they’d be dying in piles before I could blink.

I’d gotten attached to the bastards, though. I couldn’t let them die meaningless dog’s deaths.

“Squad Leeeader!”

That bastard deserved to die, sure.

*Why the hell did you have to mix in rocks?*

The Head Elder’s first target was Hyuk Mujin. Naturally. He had boldly struck the opening blow.

I heaved a deep sigh and hurled myself toward him.

Shiiiiing!

I drove my spear toward the Head Elder’s chest with everything I had.

At the same instant, the Sword Energy about to carve through Hyuk Mujin’s back changed direction.

Shing.

That damned Sword Energy.

There was no time to check the spearhead that had been sliced clean off. I snatched the terrified Hyuk Mujin by the scruff of his neck.

“Run!”

But the Head Elder was not about to give up that easily.

Swoooosh!

The air shrieked, and my side went hot. The attack had only grazed me, yet it tore away a handful of flesh and sent blood spraying.

*Kh.*

It hurt like hell. In this situation, though, even screaming was a luxury.

*I need to buy time.*

I shoved Hyuk Mujin aside and turned around. The Head Elder raised an eyebrow.

“You?”

One word, but the meaning came through perfectly.

*You think the likes of you can stop me?*

I pretended to be calm.

“Yeah. Me.”

“Do you not value your life?”

“If I say it’s damn precious, will you let me live?”

“Heh heh. What a mouth on one so young.”

“Gramps, can you still get it up?”

Tssssss.

It rose ramrod straight.

Large, beautiful Sword Energy.

“…Still going strong, I see.”

My grip on the spear grew slick with sweat.

*Looks like I’ll have to pull out every last trick I’ve got if I want to survive this.*

I had far more secrets than the Head Elder knew.

Attacks that used my Inventory.

A Skill that poured several times my strength into a single blow—One Annihilation.

*I’ve never shown it to anyone, precisely for a situation like this.*

Was this what Murim called a final trump card?

One thing was certain. Neither the Head Elder nor anyone else in Murim would see it coming.

And on top of that…

“Encircling formation. Spread out.”

Shff.

Hyuk Mujin and the reconnaissance squad began closing in from every direction.

It was a flimsy net for a fish as big as the Head Elder. But with a sharp enough harpoon, we had a chance.

“You think this will be enough?”

“To catch one old man? More than enough.”

The Head Elder smiled, amused.

“What a fearless child. You need a beating to knock some sense into you.”

But he never got the chance. A voice suddenly cut in.

“I must have misheard you. Say that again.”

A mountain of a man stood there, his eyes fierce beneath thick brows. The voice emerging from between his heavy lips was cold as ice.

“You said you were going to lay a hand on our boy?”

Fwoooosh.

Sword Energy blazed along his blade like wildfire and pointed at the Head Elder.

Jin Wikyung had appeared like a savior. He winked at me.

“Well? Big brother looks cool, doesn’t he?”

Still the same.

* * *

A face I had not seen in a month.

The moment Jin Wikyung appeared, all the tension in my body melted away.

I was glad.

Just glad.

*Thank goodness he’s alive.*

Wikyung was covered in blood, as though he had fought a brutal battle to get here. Wounds large and small showed through the tears in his clothes.

But that was enough. What mattered was that he was alive.

*There’s a lot I want to say, but it can wait.*

We could have our reunion after the war was over. Wikyung seemed to feel the same way. He turned his gaze forward.

At the end of our gazes, the Head Elder slowly spoke.

“If you have made it this far…”

Jin Wikyung’s icy voice cut him off.

“The Second and Third Elders are dead. The First Elder is only a matter of time.”

Good news. If Wikyung put it that way, the Elders must have played a major role in this war—which meant the Head Elder had just lost his hands and feet.

“Did the two of them… go peacefully?”

“Probably.”

The Head Elder nodded without protest.

“They were always saying they wanted to die fighting like martial artists. They got their wish, so that is fortunate.”

“They’ll be remembered as traitors, not martial artists. Every last one of you.”

“History is written by the victors.”

The Head Elder pointed his sword at us. Even with dozens of people facing him, including Jin Wikyung and me, he stood utterly composed.

“Now, let us decide who the victor will be.”

Jin Wikyung opened his mouth as if he had something to say, then pressed his lips firmly together.

The next moment, a thunderous roar burst from him.

“Strike down the rebels!”

Shing. Shrrring.

Dozens of weapons were drawn at once, all aimed at a single man. It was a magnificent sight.

“We obey!”

With a tremendous shout, dozens of martial artists charged as one. I was no exception. My grip tightened around my spear, and my chest felt ready to burst.

*We can win. No—we win, no matter what.*

It was an all-out clash with no formation to speak of. But we had Jin Wikyung, a Peak master, and dozens of martial artists following him.

*And we have me.*

The Head Elder had placed too much faith in his own strength. He had committed every last subordinate to the front lines without keeping even a minimal escort, and as a result, the black-clad men were now pinned down by the combined assault of the Jin Family of Taiyuan and the Mount Heng Sword Sect.

The Head Elder…

would die right here.

*It’s over!*

It did not take long for that certainty to shatter.

Shushushushush!

Dozens of tiny specks shot out of nowhere. They tore through the air faster and harder than anyone had expected.

“It’s the Finger-Flicking Technique!”

Jin Wikyung’s shout did not leave me any time to wonder what that was.

Pow-pow-pow-pow!

Clang!

“Aaargh!”

“Guh!”

Some people fell. Others knocked the projectiles aside. Those who fell never rose again.

*Don’t tell me…*

It lasted only an instant, but I saw it clearly. Tiny fragments embedded like buckshot around their hearts and across their faces.

*Stones?*

They were closer to shards now, but they had definitely been stones. Unable to withstand the Head Elder’s internal energy, they had shattered the instant he fired them at us.

*That’s possible?*

Goose bumps rose across my skin, but it was not over.

The Head Elder was reaching toward us.

“Scatter!”

This time, Wikyung and I shouted together. The martial artists, already wound tight, scattered in an instant.

*Good. We weren’t too late this ti—*

My relief lasted only a moment.

Swoooosh!

What shot toward us this time was not the Finger-Flicking Technique.

It was the Head Elder.

He plunged among the martial artists and rampaged like a wild beast.

Slash. Slash. Slash.

“Grrk.”

“Hhk.”

Each time his sword flashed through the darkness, another life vanished. Wherever the Head Elder passed, he left only stifled screams and corpses behind.

“Head Elder—!”

Jin Wikyung charged at him in a fury. By then, the Head Elder had already killed five people. Yet not a single drop of blood stained his pale face, making him look like a ghost.

“You’ve come?”

“How dare you!”

“You’re all so clumsy. Peace must have lasted too long.”

There was one fact none of us could deny.

The Head Elder was a hero of the Great Faction War.

He was stronger and more experienced than anyone present—a Peak martial artist honed through countless battles.

Shiiiiing!

The Sword Energy Jin Wikyung unleashed stabbed through empty air. The Head Elder avoided it with nothing more than a slight tilt of his head, then flicked a finger.

His target was me.

Shhk!

I could not see anything. But instinct told me what it was.

*Qi.*

That was exactly what it was—a mass of qi itself.

I threw myself into a roll without hesitation.

Pshk.

“Guh.”

I should have blocked it with my spear, but the attack was too unfamiliar for me to think that far ahead.

An ally whose name I did not know paid the price.

I felt sick.

“A Lazy Donkey Roll? You really are an ass.”

I spat out the dirt in my mouth.

“When you’re trying to live, there’s nothing you won’t do.”

“Haha! You’ve already become a true man of Murim.”

Clang! Clang-clang-clang!

What kind of monster was this bastard?

Even as he spoke, the Head Elder knocked aside every last one of Jin Wikyung’s attacks.

Thud!

At the same time, his internal-energy-laden fist smashed the head of a martial artist charging at him from behind.

“You’re still twenty years too early.”

The battle had barely begun. Not even enough time had passed to drink a cup of tea, yet nearly twenty martial artists had already become cold corpses.

“You bastard!”

Shiiiiing!

A middle-aged warrior whose face looked vaguely familiar lost his head to Sword Energy.

“Die!”

Boom!

A baby-faced young man who could not have been much older than twenty dropped to his knees, his chest caved in.

They were not NPCs.

They were real people.

Faces I had passed at least once in the Jin Family of Taiyuan. Faces belonging to people whose hands I had shaken.

I gritted my teeth.

*I’m sorry.*

I was not apologizing because I lacked the strength to save them.

I was apologizing because I had used their deaths.

Shhk!

Another man fell to the Head Elder’s Finger-Flicking Technique like a puppet with its strings cut. But just as the corpse seemed about to topple backward, it sprang upright like a roly-poly toy.

Then it pitched toward the Head Elder.

“Cheap trick!”

Boom! Bang!

With one hand, the Head Elder blocked Jin Wikyung’s sword. With the other, he launched a palm strike at the corpse.

And then…

*Now.*

Hidden behind the corpse as it flew through the air, I thrust out my spear.

“One Annihilation.”

The next instant—

Goooooong.

A deafening roar erupted.
