# Chapter 692

Sometimes instinct beats reason.

Like the moment I saw the Water God Dragon’s Origin Essence nestled so prettily in the Black Tiger’s mouth.

“You bastard. When did you steal that?”

—……

The words slipped out before I could stop them.

The Black Tiger gave me a cold look, then sent a thought my way.

—“Steal” is a strong word.

It was a little strong.

Even if he really had stolen the Water God Dragon’s Origin Essence, he could swallow it whenever he wanted. Under the circumstances, I was the one who had to apologize.

“Fine. I’m sorry.”

I held out both hands politely.

“Now spit it out. You can see my palms, right? Right here. Come on.”

—It was astonishing.

“Yeah, I’m pretty shocked too. I get it, so first, could you just…”

—I felt the power within it. Then I saw a vast stretch of time.

I stopped. “Time?”

—Yes. Several hundred years of time and memory, visible only to one of its own kind. Everything there was to know about an imugi that had failed to become a dragon.

“…!”

—At the end of those ancient memories, there was a human. Young, rough, and endlessly reckless.

The Black Tiger’s blue-white eyes, so much like Muyaho’s, rested on me. Then another thought echoed through my mind in what sounded like a man’s calm voice.

—Be grateful to that imugi. If I had not seen his memories, you would all have met the same end as that unpleasant human.

The world was full of unpleasant humans. Turn the whole place upside down and shake them out, and there’d be enough to fill the Yangtze and then some. But I had a pretty good idea who the Black Tiger meant.

“Don’t tell me… Heugung?”

—I do not know his name. Unlike you, though, he had only one forepaw.

Technically, it was a hand. That wasn’t important right now.

I looked at the Black Tiger with fresh eyes.

“Should I start by thanking you for saving me?”

—It does not matter. It was my choice.

“Then I’ll thank you anyway. I mean it.”

The Black Tiger glanced at me, turned, and began to walk. I fell into step behind him.

“I have a few questions.”

—How tiresome.

“What? You’ll answer them all sincerely even though it’s a bother?”

—No.

“Oh. Thanks for agreeing so readily.”

—…Human communication is truly strange. Have too many years passed?

I ignored that and asked the most important question first.

“How much time has passed?”

His tail hung stiffly, plainly showing his displeasure. Still, the answer came.

—You have been here for one day, by human reckoning.

“One day?”

Ordinarily, that would be no time at all.

Not now.

The Great Snow Fiend and the Black Hand Fist Demon had warned of the bloodbath about to sweep Nanman. With even the Beast Miao King’s whereabouts unknown, no one was left to stop the Southern Heaven Demon Empress.

*Damn it.*

I couldn’t have helped losing consciousness. But thousands, even tens of thousands, could have died during that single day.

I clenched my teeth, swallowed a curse, and forced out the words.

“An exit. Tell me how to get out of here.”

—You intend to leave?

“Yeah. I’m grateful you saved me, but I can’t waste any more time.”

—You intend to save your own kind from danger. Recklessly, as you did before.

“You…?”

—Do not worry. I cannot read human memories. I saw you in the imugi’s memories and made a guess. I imagine you fought so fiercely for much the same reason.

“…You were watching?”

—Yes. From beginning to end. As always, I watched everything that happened here.

The Black Tiger turned his head toward me, his thoughts as calm as ever.

—But I saw no reason to involve myself in human affairs. Not until you brought out *that*.

“…!”

My eyes widened. Not because I knew he meant the Water God Dragon’s Origin Essence.

I remembered the chill wind that had blown from nowhere when, in a last desperate attempt to survive, I’d been about to consume it. I had paused, sensed something wrong, and put the Origin Essence down. Muyaho had arrived moments later and saved my life.

“Then that wind…”

My voice trailed off. The Black Tiger’s answer sounded in my head.

—You were about to do something foolish. To defy the ordained order is to defy heaven. Had I left you alone, the power the imugi left behind would have overwhelmed and killed you.

I watched him walk along the endless cliff.

“Not once, but twice.”

—What do you mean?

“You saved me twice.”

The Black Tiger gave a small snort.

—I only pitied the one who gave everything he had to a foolish human. If not for him, you would deserve to die.

“That’s a little unfair. I don’t know why you hate humans, but I’ve never done anything bad enough to deserve that.”

—Even after you tried to take innocent lives and set fire to their peaceful home?

“What?”

Instead of answering, the Black Tiger nodded toward the foot of the cliff.

Countless beasts, their bodies blackened in places, drank from an unfamiliar stream. There were thousands of them by my rough estimate. With all the others that might be sheltering somewhere in this seemingly boundless place, I couldn’t begin to guess the total.

*What the hell…?*

I stood on the cliff, staring down at them.

—The fire you started is still devouring the mountain. If I had let it spread, everything would have burned by now.

“…”

—Yes, I led them here. Just as I did seven nights ago.

Seven nights ago. The day the Beast Miao King and I had received the urgent report and headed for Ailao Mountain.

I searched my memory, and realization struck.

*The beasts.*

I remembered clearly. Of the three hundred or so warriors stationed at Ailao Mountain, more than two hundred had returned alive.

The beasts hadn’t.

I hadn’t seen them anywhere—not on Ailao Mountain, not in the Poisonblood Grounds.

“That was you too. You saved them.”

—Monsters that should never have been there were set loose. I had not interfered in human affairs for hundreds of years, but that time I could not stand aside.

“Set loose?”

—You did not know?

Faint contempt entered his blue-white eyes.

—Humans did all of it. Your kind has done such things since long ago, and I have remained here and watched.

*Damn it.*

I’d only been able to guess what had happened at Ailao Mountain. Now the Black Tiger had confirmed it.

Dark Heaven had been behind that, too.

*If only we’d found out a little sooner.*

The regret came too late. I looked at the Black Tiger and finally began to understand his actions that day. I could still picture him appearing and disappearing ahead of us, again and again, when he could easily have gotten away.

“You weren’t attacking us.”

—No. That is not my duty.

“You were trying to show us where the Thousand-Year Spider was.”

—I could not merely watch a foul monster disturb this mountain. Humans planted the seed. It is only right that humans reap what grew from it.

He walked on. The path along the cliff grew narrower and steeper, and birds nesting on rugged peaks above the clouds watched us with curious eyes.

How long had we been walking? Clouds and mist surrounded us, and the ground below seemed impossibly far away.

*I’m sure this place wasn’t this big.*

When I first woke, I’d thought it was a forest around a small pond. Now I could see vast mountain ranges, valleys, and green pastures.

*What the hell is this place?*

I could no longer begin to guess. Staring at the impossible landscape, I finally asked, “Where are we?”

—A land filled with life. A place where all things live in harmony.

What was he talking about?

For some reason, I remembered the church deacon who used to give me spicy rice cakes when I was little while shouting, “Jesus, heaven! Unbelievers, hell!”

“This can’t be heaven.”

—The kingdom of heaven? A fitting description, in its way. But this place lies deep within the land, where no one can enter without my permission. It is very close to where you were.

“Then… are we in the Poisonblood Grounds?”

A few paces ahead, the Black Tiger gave a small nod.

—Yes.

“But this place…”

The words stuck in my throat. The Poisonblood Grounds?

I wouldn’t have believed we were anywhere on Ailao Mountain. There were no raging flames here, none of the eerie feeling that hung over the mountain.

—Do not try to understand it. This is a place you humans can neither enter by force nor comprehend.

He was right. Murim had all manner of strange things, Mystic Gate Formations among them, but what lay before me went far beyond anything they could do.

“Ha. The Poisonblood Grounds. This is the Poisonblood Grounds.”

Laughing hollowly, I followed him up the peak.

As we neared the summit, his thought reached me again.

—Poisonblood Grounds is its name now. Humans have changed many things with the passage of time, just as they changed what they called me.

“What was it called before?”

—The Sacred Land.

“What?”

—That was in a past as distant as the imugi’s memories. Beasts, not humans, ruled this land then. The few humans lived in groups within their own territories and worshiped us.

“Huh.”

I had no words.

This forbidden place, where no one could set foot, had once been called the Sacred Land. And if there had been so few people in Nanman back then… how long ago was that?

Five hundred years? A thousand?

*This is driving me crazy.*

Even if I’d put every point into Intelligence from the start, I’d have felt like an idiot right now.

I shook my head. The Black Tiger had stopped walking, and I turned to him.

“Then you. What exactly are you?”

—Did you not call me an apparition yourself?

“I’m asking what they called you when this place was the Sacred Land.”

He fell silent. Then he shifted his massive body, and something wistful passed through his blue-white eyes.

—A guardian spirit.

“What?”

—Long ago, that was what people called me. The mountain lord[^1] who guarded Ailao Mountain. A guardian spirit born alongside the sacred stone.

[^1]: “Mountain lord” is a traditional epithet for a tiger.
