# Chapter 46

Sizzle.

The meat hit the grill. Thick, red, and marbled with white streaks like snowflakes—it was the finest Hanwoo beef.

The sight, the sound, the smell. All of it was intoxicating. The only thing I didn’t like was the price…

“Will this be enough? We can order more afterward. Some special cuts, too.”

A rich C-rank Hunter was paying, so whatever.

*How long had it been since I’d last had Hanwoo?*

Food in Murim was spicy, salty, or bland. And more often than not, I hadn’t even been able to eat properly. For thirty days, I’d abused my stomach with beef jerky, fasting pills, and rice balls.

Chomp. Chomp-chomp.

The best thing about beef was how quickly you could eat it. As soon as it looked more or less done, it went straight into my mouth.

Every bite felt like walking on clouds. This piece, that piece, that one too—every last one tasted like heaven.

“Hnnngh.”

Team Leader Choi watched me with that peculiar look of his.

“Would you like some more?”

“No. I shouldn’t overeat.”

“We’re on our twenty-fifth serving…”

“Twelve servings each isn’t all that much.”

“I’ve only eaten three.”

“Oh, can we order some yukhoe?[^2]”

“…Yes.”

“Rice, too.”

“…”

And so the storm of a meal finally came to an end. At last, Team Leader Choi spoke.

“I told them I killed both the Priest and the Great Warrior.”

“Oh. Thank you.”

“Don’t mention it. Let’s call it a fair deal. If anything, I have something to thank you for as well.”

A fair deal.

He wasn’t wrong. I’d bought myself time to think, and he would gain a reputation. After all, he had supposedly led five low-rank Hunters and killed two mid-grade Rare Monsters.

The fact that no one had died in the process would be a huge boost to the Guild’s publicity, too.

*What if Synchronization hadn’t restored my strength?*

Who knew? Someone probably would have died. Maybe none of us would have made it out alive.

“You’d already thought that far ahead.”

He wasn’t just some eccentric. Not if he was that quick on the uptake.

I smiled awkwardly.

“It works out well for both of us.”

“For both of us. Both of us…”

Team Leader Choi muttered to himself, then abruptly asked,

“Would you like to join the Guild?”

“Pffft!”

Team Leader Choi raised the tablecloth to block the spray, then produced a business card with practiced elegance.

> **Peace Guild, Team 1 Leader Choi Minwoo**

What the hell? For a moment, I was completely thrown.

“Y-you’re trying to recruit me? Right now?”

“That’s right. We’re always looking for talent.”

I took the card and found myself strangely moved.

It seemed like only yesterday that I’d been bowing like a shrimp at job interviews…

*Live long enough and you really do see everything.*

An actual recruitment offer—and he was even treating me like talent. F-rank Hunter Jin Taekyung, you’d come a long way.

“Our Guild is still new, and we don’t have many members, but we’re excellent where it counts. For example…”

Team Leader Choi elegantly swirled his wineglass. When had he even ordered that?

“Our finances are extremely solid.”

“Ooh, finances!”

“Which means our employee benefits are excellent.”

“Ooh, benefits backed by solid finances!”

“Our Guild Master is a B-rank Hunter.”

“Ooh, a high-ranking Hunter—the source of those solid finances!”

“And there’s no need to worry about restructuring.”

“Ooh, job security!”

Team Leader Choi smiled the smile of a wealthy man.

“Will you join us?”

I scratched my head.

“No. That’s a little…”

“…Pardon?”

“I have circumstances that make it difficult at the moment. I need some time.”

If I followed my heart, I’d sign the contract right away—signature, seal, thumbprint, even a kiss mark.

*But what if the System disappears tomorrow?*

Then I’d have nothing.

Overnight, I’d go from being called talent to being called a human disaster.[^3]

“Is it a money problem?”

Money was always a problem.

But this was more important. I couldn’t let the wad of cash dangling in front of me blind me into making a snap decision.

“It’s difficult to explain. I’m sorry, but this isn’t something I can decide right now…”

“One hundred million won.”

“A hundred million?”

“That’s just the signing bonus. Everything else will be set to at least the standard terms for a C-rank Hunter.”

That was dangerous. This time, it was really dangerous.

But I resisted with superhuman patience. Hadn’t I learned long ago that life was never that easy?

That money might come back to choke me.

“I’m sorry.”

Team Leader Choi studied me in silence, then nodded.

“I’ll wait to hear from you.”

* * *

One person left, and one remained.

Team Leader Choi—no, Choi Minwoo—silently stared at the seat Jin Taekyung had vacated, then took out his phone.

Beep. Beep. Click.

“—You bastard, are you psychic? I was just about to call you.”

“How did that thing I asked about turn out?”

“—I looked into him because you asked, but… is there something special about this Jin Taekyung guy?”

“That’s what I called you to find out. So? What did you learn?”

“—It’s a dime-a-dozen case. Awakened at twenty, seven years ago, and assessed as F-rank. There’s a record of him graduating at the top of his class from the Hunter training center…”

Jin Taekyung’s past seven years spilled from the other end of the phone. Then Choi Minwoo’s eyebrows twitched.

“What? The Sangdong Station Mutated Gate?”

“—Yeah. You know about that incident, right?”

How could he not? It had happened only two years ago, and Choi Minwoo remembered it clearly.

“—He was the only survivor. I was surprised when I confirmed that part myself.”

Choi Minwoo took a drink of water. His throat had gone dry at the thought that he might have found a clue to how an F-rank Hunter had killed a mid-grade Rare Monster alone.

“And?”

“—He took six months off. The Hunter Administration kept sending investigators, and I guess he spent the time trying to pull himself together. You know how serious the incident was.”

“And then?”

“—That’s it. He returned to his Guild, ran Gates like hell for a year and a half, then got fired. Exactly three days ago.”

“Why?”

“—Technically, restructuring, but what kind of restructuring does a tiny little Guild like that need? The incident probably had a lot to do with it. They kept looking over their shoulders at the Administration, then finally pushed him out. He must’ve been uncomfortable for them to keep around.”

“That’s all?”

“—As far as I can tell. Want me to send you the file?”

“Send it now. I’m hanging up.”

“—Hey, wait!”

Click.

Choi Minwoo tapped his long fingers against the table.

Jin Taekyung. F-rank Hunter. The sole survivor of the Sangdong Station Mutated Gate.

And…

*At least a C-rank Hunter.*

That was the absolute minimum. He could still see Taekyung overwhelming a C-rank Rare Monster by himself with sheer strength and skill.

*And yet he’s F-rank.*

There were only two possibilities. Either he had been hiding his strength, or he had recently reawakened.

Choi Minwoo suspected the latter, but even that defied common sense.

Countless Hunters retired without ever rising a single rank.

A reawakening that took someone from F-rank to C-rank was, without question, extraordinarily rare.

*This isn’t some game. What the hell is he?*

Choi Minwoo shook his head. He felt as if he’d been bewitched by a ghost.

*I’ll have to look into him some more.*

As he rose from his seat, the restaurant owner approached and held out the bill.

“That will be 1,937,000 won.”

“…”

Now he really felt as if he’d been bewitched by a ghost.

* * *

“Fuck. I’m screwed.”

I dropped to the ground in the filthy recycling area. The one thing that should have been there was nowhere in sight.

“It’s gone. It’s gone. My capsule is gone.”

I’d been anxious ever since parting ways with Team Leader Choi. But I hadn’t expected someone to take it in less than half a day. I shouted into the empty air.

“Who the fuck took it?!”

And an answer came back.

“Me, you son of a bitch.”

Jinho was smoking on the roof of the goshiwon building,[^4] in the same spot where I’d seen him that morning. He exhaled a wistful stream of smoke and continued.

“I spent ten years crying, regretting it, and making vows…”

“You want me to make you cry and regret it for real?”

“You’re no fun. You haven’t seen this movie, have you?”

“Quit joking around. This is serious.”

“What, did you come up empty today?”

“No.”

All the strength drained from my voice.

“The capsule.”

“…Huh?”

“Some bastard took my capsule.”

“Cough! Cough-cough!”

Jinho must have inhaled wrong. He coughed like a maniac before finally managing to speak.

“D-didn’t you throw it away because you didn’t need it?”

“I did.”

Until the System came back.

I hadn’t expected the situation to change this much in just a few hours.

*I should’ve kept it for one more day.*

Where was I even supposed to start looking? I let out a deep sigh.

“Hyung, you didn’t happen to see who took it, did you?”

“Uh… well.”

Jinho scratched his head.

“If I saw them, then I saw them. If I didn’t, then I didn’t.”

Was that supposed to be an answer, or was it just crap?

When I glared at him, he smiled sheepishly.

“Look, it’s not like I’m hoping for a finder’s fee or anything…”

He was definitely hoping for a finder’s fee.

Anyway, that wasn’t important. I shot to my feet.

“You saw them? You’re sure?”

“If I had to choose, I’d say I did.”

“Who? Where did they go?”

“I’m not asking for a finder’s fee, but roughly how much were you thinking?”

“…One hundred thousand won?”

“Oh, dear. I must be getting old. My memory’s a little hazy.”

“For fuck’s sake.”

“Right, then. Good luck in this heat.”

“The finder’s fee is one hundred and eighty thousand won.”[^5]

Apparently satisfied with the amount, Jinho beamed.

“Your capsule. I picked it up.”

“…?”

It took me exactly three seconds to understand.

*What a goddamn daylight robber.*

I was speechless. He’d really stabbed me in the back like this?

“But I didn’t have anywhere to put it. My room’s too small, you know.”

“So?”

“I put it back in your room. I did good, right?”

How could I hit this man so thoroughly that word would spread about what a fine job I’d done?

My fists trembled.

* * *

“It really is here.”

The capsule occupied half the studio as if nothing had happened. All I could do was let out a hollow laugh.

*Should I call this lucky?*

Of all the people who could have taken it, it had been Jinho.

I opened the lid, picked up the user manual that had been tossed onto the worn seat, and flipped to the last page.

> **Main Features**
>
> - A customized capsule for one person! Once a user is registered, the capsule becomes permanently bound to that user and remains so until their death.

…Come on. No way.

*It has to be a coincidence.*

But I couldn’t shake the unease. I glared at the capsule, the source of everything that had happened.

*What the hell is this thing?*

I’d thrown it into the recycling area that morning because I wanted to forget. My life was hard enough already. I’d wanted to write the whole thing off as a nightmare and go back to living my life.

But things were different now.

*Because I have the System.*

The System…

Then something occurred to me. I placed my hand on the capsule’s surface and murmured,

“Item check.”

Ding.

Just as I thought. The corners of my mouth had begun to rise when—

> **System**
>
> This Item cannot be read.

“…It can’t be read?”

That had never happened before.

*Is it because I’m not in Murim?*

Flustered, I started checking random objects around the room. The television, a ballpoint pen, even my pillow. Every time, the System displayed accurate information.

There was only one exception.

It couldn’t read the capsule.

“Wow. This is driving me nuts.”

Just in case, I picked up the user manual. The result was the same.

Ding.

> **System**
>
> This Item cannot be read.

I flopped onto the bed and stared blankly at the old, yellow-stained ceiling.

*What’s going on?*

None of this made sense yet. But one thing was certain.

*I’ve become stronger. Far stronger than before.*

Power permeated every fiber of my body. Internal energy stirred in my dantian.

Synchronization had given me strength. Strength far beyond that of an F-rank Hunter. Enough strength to defeat a C-rank Rare Monster alone.

*Would you like to join the Guild?*

It was the first recruitment offer I’d ever received. But I had turned it down. The fear of being recognized had outweighed the joy.

*Can’t blame me.*

I’d endured seven years with the label of F-rank Hunter. I was a caterpillar that had spent its whole life crawling through the dirt, only to suddenly sprout wings called the System.

Of course I was afraid.

*But what if I can keep using this power? What if I can keep using the System?*

My heart pounded at the mere thought.

At the same time, the past seven years flashed through my mind. The label of F-rank, branded onto me despite all the work I’d put in. The memories from two years ago, when I’d trembled under other people’s contempt, my guilt, and my helplessness.

“Fuck…”

I couldn’t take it anymore. I shot to my feet, raced out of the goshiwon, and flagged down a passing taxi.

“Where would you like to go?”

I already knew my destination.

“Take me to the Bucheon Branch of the Hunter Association.”

A Hunter rank reassessment.

F-rank Hunter. I’d start by breaking the loathsome shackle that had tormented me for so long.

*Let’s do this.*

As I clenched my fist, the taxi driver said,

“This is a Seoul taxi.”

“Oh.”

[^2]: Yukhoe is seasoned Korean raw beef.

[^3]: The Korean words for “talent” and “human disaster” share the same pronunciation, though they use different characters.

[^4]: A goshiwon is a very small, inexpensive room-for-rent housing arrangement.

[^5]: In Korean, *sip-pal* (“eighteen”) echoes the swear he just used, and he switches abruptly to stiff politeness.
