# Chapter 750

Deep beneath the sea.

Countless wrecks and corpses darkened the water until it was impossible to see an inch ahead. Then blue-white flames erupted.

*Fwoosh! KRAAAAAASH!*

The current split, and the water vaporized.

The flames carried an energy that defied the laws of nature. No—a spear shot forward like a ray of light.

Toward the enormous shadow moving at an unbelievable speed.

Toward a calamity that could never be allowed to live.

But the mythical monster defied the laws of nature just as surely as the energy in the spear did.

*KRRRRRUMBLE!*

The sea shook.

Whirlpools large and small rose from the depths, blocking the spearhead as it raced forward.

*KOOOOONG!*

The collision sent waves of force spreading outward, one after another.

By the time the shock and thunderous roar had subsided, the flames had pulverized every obstacle in their path. Now they, too, were fading, their strength spent.

The spear had failed to reach the monster before its body vanished into the darkness far ahead.

*Swish. Clack.*

The sea had already grown quiet.

Someone caught the spear as it drifted helplessly with the current.

The man stood with his back to me, staring silently in the direction the monster had gone. He seemed ready to erupt like an active volcano.

No. Let me correct that.

He didn’t merely *seem* ready to erupt. One touch, and he would explode.

He’d been that way when the monster he could never allow to live had escaped.

And he was that way now.

“Enough.”

The sharp word escaped my lips before I realized it.

*Flash.*

A vivid green light spread through the water, and everything around me melted away.

The sea, dark as night. The enormous wreckage of high-rise buildings. Even the nameless corpses that had died with their eyes wide open.

In place of the vanished hologram, several dozen unfamiliar faces came into view.

Men in military uniforms and expensive-looking tailored suits.

The old man seated opposite me, at the head of the table, spoke with a grave expression.

“To think Leviathan got away.”

I’d never met him before today, but according to Team Leader Choi, he was one of the three most powerful people in Japan.

No—if you counted the authority he actually wielded, he might be number one.

Defense Minister was an immensely powerful position even in peacetime. With the country at war, it carried more power than ever.

But looking at the old Defense Minister put me in a foul mood.

To be honest, I wanted to deal with that damned old man before Leviathan, who wasn’t even here.

Why? Simple.

An hour ago, he’d eliminated our last chance to catch it.

Now that I knew what had happened, I wasn’t about to be polite.

“Yeah. Shame it got away. If someone had helped at the end, we might have caught it.”

“……!”

The conference room went still. While the other Japanese men glanced nervously at one another, the Defense Minister fixed me with an offended stare.

“That sounds as though you’re blaming Japan. Or have my old ears misheard you?”

“I heard you were over eighty, but you’re still in good shape. Your hearing’s pretty sharp for an old man.”

“What?”

“Has it suddenly gone bad? You heard me. Why keep asking?”

“How dare—!”

*Bang!*

The Defense Minister slammed the table and turned to Team Leader Choi.

“Are you going to stand there and allow this insolence?”

Normally, Team Leader Choi would have given me a warning look by now.

Normally.

“By ‘insolence,’ Defense Minister, do you mean striking the table during a meeting like this?”

“What?”

“Japan formally requested assistance through the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. We answered that request and risked our lives to come here. And yet…”

Team Leader Choi fell silent. His gaze turned cold.

“Why did you not deploy additional forces?”

“……”

“As I understand it, at least two thousand Hunters had been assembled by the time Jin Taekyung attacked Leviathan. The Ground and Air Self-Defense Forces were standing by as well.”

“That was…”

“I know most of them would not have been much help in a fight. But if you had deployed Japan’s Hunters, even then, they could have slowed Leviathan’s escape for a little while.”

Team Leader Choi had laid out the facts clearly.

That little while was exactly what I’d needed.

A bit of support—and sacrifice—to distract the wounded monster and delay it as it dragged itself away.

Instead, after risking my life to go down into that deep water, I’d had to watch its back grow smaller in the distance.

Team Leader Choi and the roughly two hundred Hunters who’d disembarked from the aircraft had been ready to enter, too.

“I could no longer stand by, so I tried to lead the Hunters in myself. Your side stopped us. You even invoked your authority over field operations.”

“……!”

“If you wanted to keep your own losses down and claim the credit, you should have done the job properly. Then we wouldn’t be in this situation.”

The Defense Minister pressed his lips together.

“The encirclement… The encirclement was perfect.”

The Skeleton King looked at me with genuine curiosity.

“If it was perfect, shouldn’t Leviathan have failed to break through?”

“Yeah. It should have.”

“But it broke through?”

“Yep.”

“And you lost track of it because of the magical power it released?”

“Yep.”

Having listened to all my helpful answers, the Skeleton King muttered, “What? Are they fucking idiots…?”

“……!”

“……!”

The temperature in the conference room seemed to drop below freezing.

But watching everyone fall silent and glance nervously around gave me no satisfaction at all.

*Fucking lunatics.*

Those bugs were worse than monsters, and they were sitting there calling themselves the operation’s command staff. I couldn’t even laugh.

*It’s not like I can say “fuck this” and quit.*

The casualty count so far was roughly a hundred thousand.

It was only that low because, by some stroke of luck, the tsunami Leviathan had caused had stopped around the outskirts of Tokyo.

If I left and it came back, they might as well rename Tokyo Atlantis.

*And if it gives up on Japan and goes after somewhere else…*

That would be a problem too.

Of course, the idiots here—including that old monkey chief—would probably dance for joy because *they’d* survived.

“……”

The worst part was, they really would.

I let out a sigh of pure, undiluted exasperation and addressed the Defense Minister, who had finally shut his mouth.

“Enough. Get me someone else in charge.”

“S-someone else?”

“The Prime Minister. Or the King of Japan. Whoever it is, they’ll be better than you.”

“The King of Japan? How dare you speak of His Majesty the Emperor of the Great Japanese Empire—”

“Oh, give me a break. How long are you going to keep up the empire crap? Your empire got flattened by two atomic bombs. You should’ve given up the title of Emperor while you were at it.”

“That was a savage act of destruction by the American Yankees!”

I plugged my ears as the Defense Minister shrieked.

“I’m going to get radiation poisoning. I’m going to get radiation poisoning. I’m going to get radiation poisoning. I’m going to get radiation poisoning.”

“Chikshō!”

Maybe he’d lived long enough not to care. Or maybe radiation contamination had robbed him of all fear.

As the Defense Minister charged at me, flailing despite his age, the Skeleton King reached out and caught him by the back of the neck.

“Stop, you old and insignificant hu—no, Yellow Monkey.”

“You wretch! Release me at once! How dare a fur-covered Yankee lay a hand on the Defense Minister of the Great Japanese Empire!”

“You dare insult the great United States in front of me?”

“I have not forgotten what you bastards did to this land and the innocent subjects of our empire!”

“Vengeful spirits of Pearl Harbor, descend upon me!”

*These fucking lunatics…*

A Japanese imperialist from the ’70s-and-’80s generation arguing with an American monster from the Demon Realm.

The sight of those two horrifying oddities made my chest swell with emotion. Unfortunately, it wasn’t over.

*Fwoooooosh.*

“……?”

What the hell?

A chill ran through me, and the fluorescent lights began to dim.

Even the people struggling to separate the Defense Minister and the Skeleton King shuddered in the cold.

*What’s happening now?*

I turned my head. My eyes met Team Leader Choi’s across the room.

That was when I realized it. My mouth fell open.

A shout from moments earlier rang through my ears again.

*Vengeful spirits of Pearl Harbor, descend upon me!*

*No. Fuck. Don’t tell me…*

*Whoooooosh! KRAAASH!*

The eerie energy became a gale, shattering the fluorescent lights overhead.

I was already running through the sudden darkness toward the Skeleton King.

“Look closely, and feel their resentment—!”

“Hey, you crazy bastard!”

*Crack!*

* * *

Thank goodness.

Thanks to a time-honored promotion system that still valued family and connections over ability in the twenty-first century, most of the high-ranking officials and generals in the conference room were ordinary people. They were too frightened even to hear the Skeleton King’s shout.

And apparently my demand for someone in charge had gotten through. Before long, the manager—no, the Prime Minister—arrived.

“You came all this way to help our country! I am most grateful, Jinsang!”

“Ah, yes. Nice to meet you, Prime Minister Koizumi.”

I never thought I’d meet this man in person.

I’d only ever seen collections of his quotes online. Seeing him now gave me a brief jolt of excitement, like I’d met a celebrity.

Of course, this wasn’t the time to show it. We needed to deal with Leviathan.

At least, that was what I wanted to do.

*I* did.

“I’ll get straight to the point. To track Leviathan—”

“I hear the Defense Minister was rude to you, Jinsang. Once this is over, I shall reprimand him severely, if only to ease your hurt feelings!”

“Uh. Thanks. I appreciate it.”

“Why the look, Jinsang?”

“It’s just… We need to talk about Leviathan. And there’s the way you keep addressing me.”

“The way I address you, Jinsang?”

“……”

“Ah, I see. It must sound like something else in Korean. Then shall I call you Gyeongsang, using the last syllable of your name?”

How would that help?

I’d dodged a pile of shit only to get pissed on.

*Is he wishing me a minor injury next time I fight Leviathan…?*

I weighed Jinsang against Gyeongsang, then sighed.

*Well, at least it isn’t Severe Injury.*[^1]

[^1]: Japanese *Jin-san* (“Mr. Jin”) sounds like Korean *jinsang*, an obnoxious person. *Gyeongsang* means “minor injury” in Korean, while *jungsang* means “severe injury.”
