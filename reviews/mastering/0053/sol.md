# Chapter 53

Ding.

> **System**
>
> - Sleep Mode has ended.

“…O-oppa!”

I gasped and opened my eyes.

The first thing I saw wasn’t the ceiling of my goshiwon[^1], but Hayeon’s face.

*Oh, right. I came home yesterday.*

“Did you have a nightmare?”

“Huh?”

“You’ve been screaming. And you’re sweating like crazy.”

*I was?*

Before I could ask, I realized my whole body was drenched in sweat, and my throat stung as though I’d swallowed sand.

*Why do I feel like this?*

The System didn’t only work while I was awake. Sleep Mode let me sleep deeply while restoring my condition to its peak.

Nothing like this had ever happened in Murim, or since Synchronization. And a nightmare, on top of that?

*What did I even dream about?*

My head throbbed, but I couldn’t remember the dream at all.

Hayeon asked in a worried voice, “Has something bad happened lately?”

“No. Nothing like that.”

“If there is, tell me. Don’t suffer alone.”

“Yes, nuna.”

“I’m not joking.”

Her little fist thumped me in the chest. Faced with Hayeon’s serious expression, I had nothing to say. I scratched my chin.

“Are you sure? Nothing worrying you? Nothing giving you a hard time?”

“I told you, there’s nothing.”

It was a lie. There had been things seven years ago, and there would still be things seven years from now. There had been days when I locked myself in my room and cried, and days when I drank myself senseless with Jinho hyung just to forget.

*That’s enough.*

There were things I couldn’t tell my mother, who had worked until the cartilage in her knees wore down raising two young children, or my little sister, a high-school senior now preparing for her college entrance exam.

Enduring and overcoming things alone—I was used to it by now.

I flashed her a grin and pretended nothing was wrong.

“My life’s finally about to take off. What would I have to worry about? Ah, there is one thing. How I’m supposed to spend all this money. Something like that.”

“Show-off.”

The mood lightened a little. I deliberately made a face.

“Show-off? Don’t you remember yesterday? Want me to show you the bundles of cash again?”

“I’ll give you that. You’re obnoxious, but I can’t argue.”

“I’ve hit the peak of my twenty-seven-year life graph. Nothing’s going on, so you just study hard.”

“My grades are in the top 0.1 percent nationwide, okay? I’m doing more than well enough, so don’t worry.”

Hayeon pouted and started to leave, then stopped and turned back.

“Oppa. By the way…”

“Yeah?”

“Who’s Jin Wikyung?”

“…What?”

A name I’d never expected to hear, at a moment like that.

My whole body went rigid.

* * *

Crunch.

I bit into a piece of freshly made ponytail radish kimchi. It was Mom’s cooking, the food I’d wanted so badly, but I could barely taste it.

It was because of the conversation I’d just had with Hayeon.

“Where did you hear that name?”

“From you. You kept calling that name in your sleep.”

And then her last question.

“Someone you know? If they show up in your dreams, you must be close.”

I couldn’t answer. I hadn’t found that answer in Murim or in reality.

No. I no longer had any reason to look for it. I had returned to reality, and Jin Wikyung was in Murim.

*Then why did Jin Wikyung suddenly appear in my dream…?*

My head was a mess. Was it an aftereffect of Murim? The term PTSD came to mind—post-traumatic stress disorder.

*This is driving me crazy.*

My expression must have hardened without me realizing it. Mom asked carefully, “Have you lost your appetite? I made all your favorites.”

“Oh, no. When did you make the kimchi? And this doenjang-jjigae is perfect.”

I hurriedly made an excuse and picked up my spoon. For the first time in ages, the three of us were sitting together at one table. I couldn’t ruin this precious moment.

*It’s nothing. It has to be nothing.*

Slurp.

Even so, the savory doenjang-jjigae tasted faintly bitter.

* * *

The unease lingering in one corner of my mind soon faded.

I spent the whole day laughing and talking with my family. After a long nap, it was already evening.

It was time to go back.

“Stay a few more days. I was going to make boiled pork tomorrow.”

“Our Mrs. Kim is starting again. I know how to eat boiled pork too, you know?”

Hayeon grumbled at the naked reluctance in Mom’s voice.

“She’s already packed you a ton of side dishes, so why is she so worried? That’s enough to open a side-dish shop.”

“…That’s true.”

The shopping bags Mom had prepared by the front door were packed with side dishes. Even this was a compromise I’d managed to reach only after talking her down.

*I don’t have anywhere to put them.*

If I put a fridge in my three-pyeong goshiwon room, there really wouldn’t be anywhere left to stand. No—there wasn’t even room for the fridge itself.

I already had a capsule the size of one.

*Should I start looking for a new place?*

I was thinking about that as I slung the backpack I’d packed the night before over my shoulder.

“…?”

Why was it so heavy? All I’d put inside were a few outfits I’d bought yesterday.

The moment I set the backpack down, my family suddenly grew frantic.

“Son, you’ll be busy starting tomorrow, right? Hurry back, wash up, and get a good night’s sleep.”

“…A minute ago, you told me to stay a few more days.”

“Oppa, you’ll miss your bus.”

“I’m taking a taxi.”

“The night surcharge. You’ll have to pay the night surcharge.”

By then, I had a pretty good idea what was going on.

“When did you put it in?”

“P-put what in?”

“The money.”

Their expressions answered for them. I sighed.

“I told you to keep it and use it when you need it.”

“….”

“I make plenty of money. And I’ll keep making plenty from now on.”

It was half truth, half lie.

The average annual salary of a C-rank Hunter was five hundred million won. I’d met a generous employer in Team Leader Choi and received a bonus beyond anything I’d imagined, but if the System disappeared, all of it would vanish like smoke.

That was why I’d wanted to give even more to my family, but…

“That’s money you risked your life to earn. Spend it on yourself. All right?”

“Mom.”

“Son.”

Mom’s next quiet words stopped me cold.

“Don’t push yourself. And don’t get hurt. That’s all Mom needs.”

What else could I say?

A little later, I stepped out into the humid summer night with shopping bags full of side dishes and a backpack stuffed with bundles of cash.

Vroom.

Throughout the taxi ride back to the goshiwon, I thought about Mom’s last words and the warmth in them.

And about someone’s voice in a memory that was growing fainter and fainter.

*Survive. Run without looking back. That’s your mission.*

* * *

Sshhk—

Blood sprayed. Not the green blood of a monster, but the red blood of a human. My thigh burned as I rammed my spear into the Lizardman Chieftain’s chest.

“Keee…”

Ding.

> **System**
>
> - You defeated **Lv. 50 Lizardman Chieftain**!
>
> - You gained EXP!

The Gate was cleared. A mana field leading outside formed over the Lizardman Chieftain’s lifeless body.

That was when Team Leader Choi pushed away from the tree.

“Three.”

“Pardon?”

“The number of times you’ve been injured today, Mr. Jin Taekyung.”

One long, firm finger indicated the injuries across my body.

The nape of my neck and my arm, already treated with potions, and my thigh, which was still bleeding.

“I’m fine. They’re only grazes. A low-grade potion is more than enough…”

“You’re not fine.”

He cut me off in a firm tone. Team Leader Choi’s expression was always unreadable, but this time, something was different.

One thing was certain. What I saw in his expression wasn’t merely concern.

“You weren’t injured once last week. The same Gate, the same monsters. If this is happening, there can only be one reason.”

His clear eyes settled on me.

“Mr. Jin Taekyung. Is something wrong?”

* * *

One day. Two days. Three days.

Time passed, but things didn’t improve. On the fourth day, I ended up with five separate injuries.

“Not in your current condition. Go home.”

Leaving Team Leader Choi’s words behind, I headed for the goshiwon with my thoughts in turmoil.

*What’s wrong with me?*

Everything had been going well. The System hadn’t disappeared, and I had nearly three hundred million won sitting in the bank. All that remained was to keep riding high as a C-rank Hunter, but…

*Those damn dreams.*

That was when it had started. Ever since my first night at my family’s house, I’d been having nightmares.

The scenes in them grew clearer by the night, and whenever a dream ended, I woke drenched in sweat. Even when I circulated my qi and restored my physical condition, my mind remained unstable, and my mistakes kept piling up.

*I can’t go on like this.*

My body was in reality, but my mind was still trapped in Murim. I was wondering whether I should see a psychiatrist when I reached my goshiwon room.

Click.

“Oh, you’re back?”

The greeting was so natural that I almost wondered if I’d walked into the wrong room.

I stared at him in disbelief.

“What are you doing?”

Jinho hyung answered, “Disassembly and reassembly.”

He was sitting in front of the capsule with a screwdriver in hand. For a moment, my vision flashed yellow.

*This bastard isn’t actually—*

“Are you crazy? Move!”

“Hey, hey. Hear me out.”

Jinho hyung hurriedly waved his hands.

“I haven’t even started yet.”

“What?”

“I just got here too. Seriously.”

Judging by his expression, he didn’t seem to be lying. Only after confirming that the capsule was intact did I let out a sigh of relief.

“Phew.”

Jinho hyung looked bewildered by my reaction.

“Why are you making such a fuss over a junk capsule that doesn’t even work? Weren’t you the one who dumped it like a piece of unwanted luggage?”

“That was then.”

There was no way Jinho hyung could know about Synchronization. Nor could he know what that mysterious object—nothing but a junk capsule to him—meant to me.

“Anyway, don’t touch it. Got it?”

“You look ready to beat me to death.”

“I’ll tear you apart.”

“….”

I ignored Jinho hyung’s baffled expression and flopped onto the bed.

That brief commotion had drained all the energy out of me.

“Something going on?”

“Going on, my ass.”

“The complaints about you have been insane lately. The guy next door came over again today. I barely managed to calm him down and get him to leave.”

I could guess why.

Jinho hyung gathered the tools he’d spread across the floor and continued.

“He says he’s going crazy because you groan all night. Oh, and he asked who Jin Wikyung was.”

That name again.

I buried my face in the pillow.

“Just tell him she’s my girlfriend.”

His hand quivered around the monkey wrench.

“You got a girlfriend? You traitorous bastard.”

“….”

“Is she pretty? How old is she? Show me a picture.”

This man was thirty. It was tragic.

“The name sounds a little exotic, though. Is she an ethnic Korean from China? Or Chinese?”

“…Chinese.”

It wasn’t exactly wrong.

“This bastard hits C-rank and he’s already gone global. Anyway, introduce me to a girl. I like China. Nǐ hǎo ma? Wǒ ài nǐ. What else was there?”

“You fucking bastard.”

“Idiot. You got the tones and pronunciation all wrong. With that, you think you’ll even make it to a hundred days? Repeat after me. Nǐ chī fàn le ma?”[^2]

“You fucking bastard.”

“Again. Nǐ chī fàn le ma?”

“You fucking bastard.”

“…Wait, you little bastard!”

I ignored Jinho hyung as the realization finally dawned on him and pointed at the door.

“Get out.”

*Please. Just let me have some time to myself.*

* * *

Once the room was quiet, I sat up and walked over to the capsule. I tapped its old, grime-caked surface and muttered, “What the hell are you?”

Naturally, no answer came.

I’d been secretly hoping for one. Too bad.

*The System’s already synchronized with reality. Why couldn’t a machine talk too?*

Honestly, I wasn’t even sure it was a real machine. Reality might have turned into fantasy a long time ago, but wasn’t this a whole new genre?

If my situation were a novel, what genre would it be? Fantasy? A game novel? Or—

*Dimensional travel?*

A breathy chuckle escaped me. Dimensional travel? I really was losing my mind. There was no way something like that could be possible.

There was no way…

The thought hit me like a bucket of ice water. My mind snapped clear.

*…It is possible.*

Dimensional travel had happened in the past, and it still existed today.

The invasion of the Demon King Asmodeus and the Gates were proof.

For us, Gates were simply passages we used to leave and return to our world. But decades ago, an army of monsters had crossed through one from another dimension to Earth.

*The Demon World.*

A land of evil. The home of monsters. The Demon King’s domain.

An unknown dimension no human had ever been able to set foot in—or even glimpse. That was how humanity defined it.

But what if the capsule before me was a kind of Gate leading to another dimension? What if Murim was another unknown dimension?

*Murim is another reality.*

Everything I had seen and experienced there.

Water. Earth. Wind. Even the people.

*They weren’t NPCs.*

I stared at my vacant face reflected on the capsule’s faded surface.

For a long while after that.

[^1]: A *goshiwon* is a tiny, inexpensive room-for-rent housing arrangement; three pyeong is roughly ten square meters.

[^2]: *Nǐ chī fàn le ma?* means “Have you eaten?” In Korean, its pronunciation resembles a profanity, which is why Taekyung keeps answering with “You fucking bastard.”
