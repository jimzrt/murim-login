# Chapter 36

> **System**
>
> **Quest**
>
> **Logout**
>
> You must now make your way through this harsh Murim.
>
> Become even stronger, and become famous.
>
> For the day that will one day come…
>
> **Grade:** Main Quest
>
> **Restriction:** Jin Taekyung
>
> **Objective:** Reach the **First Rate** realm (Complete)
>
> Reach Lv. 30 (Complete)
>
> Reach Fame 500 (475/500)
>
> **Reward:** Logout

I closed the Quest Window. It was the middle of winter, but I felt a cold sweat coming on.

*If this keeps up, I’m completely screwed.*

My Fame had been climbing little by little, only to suddenly stop dead. That had been half a day ago. I’d bolted from my quarters and gone around shaking hands like a presidential candidate until my arms nearly fell off, but the notification I was waiting for never came.

*No, I did get a notification.*

> **System**
>
> — There is no one in the Jin Family of Taiyuan who does not know your Fame.

It was the System’s way of telling me I’d already milked all the Fame I could from this place and should knock it off. Even the System seemed to find me pitiful.

And so an entire day slipped away with nothing to show for it. Today was the long-awaited departure ceremony.

“Fuuuck…”

As I muttered the curse under my breath, Jin Wikyung climbed onto the platform.

Hundreds of pairs of eyes followed him—the martial artists of the Jin Family of Taiyuan and those from the small and mid-sized sects that had recently joined us. More than five hundred stood in formation across the main training ground.

Boom. Boom. Boom.

A tremendous rumble began to spread. Some stamped their feet; others struck their weapons. When five hundred martial artists wielding internal energy moved as one, the earth shook and a deafening roar filled heaven and earth.

*What is this…?*

I had never seen anything like it. No longer individuals, they had become a single army, waiting for one man’s command.

At last, Jin Wikyung spoke.

“I will not deny it. The enemy outnumbers us in both troops and Peak masters.”

Silence fell in an instant. But Jin Wikyung wasn’t foolish enough to crush morale with his opening words. The force of his presence was proof of that.

“However.”

His usual good-natured smile was nowhere to be seen. Right now, Jin Wikyung was both a Peak martial artist and the head of the Jin Family of Taiyuan.

“They are nothing but an overgrown pack of jackals. Common street rabble, wandering martial artists blinded by gold, and mounted bandits who prey upon innocent people!”

The air crackled beneath his fiery shout. Even my blood seemed to boil for a moment.

“They have neither cause nor justice.”

Cause. Justice.

The Mount Heng Sword Sect had lost the two most important things in any war.

That was also why the small and mid-sized sects of Shanxi Province, which had been watching the war between the two great powers from the sidelines, had sent reinforcements.

“This war will be over within three days.”

Fifteen hundred martial artists would clash on the same day, at the same hour.

It would be a hellish battle of killing and being killed.

“No one here can say whether they will live or die. However…”

Jin Wikyung’s gleaming eyes swept over them all.

“We will prevail.”

Then—

A roar erupted, loud enough to leave my ears ringing. As the fervor reached its peak, Jin Wikyung towered above them like a giant.

“Survival of the fittest! Put your lives on the line, fight, and survive!”

The cheering continued for a long time.

* * *

Our five hundred troops were divided into a vanguard, center, and rear guard. Most of our core fighting strength had been assigned to the vanguard and center, so the rear guard under my command consisted of only a few dozen martial artists.

There were some familiar faces among them.

“Squad Leader!”

They were members of the reconnaissance squad. Half glad and half puzzled, I asked, “Why are you guys here? Weren’t you at Medicine King Hall?”

“This battle will decide the fate of our family. We can’t sit it out over a few injuries.”

When I’d secretly visited them last time, some had still had cracked bones in their arms and legs. Murim people really were tough as hell.

“We asked to be placed under your command, and the higher-ups readily approved. We’ve been walking around with our chests puffed out ever since that day. Haha.”

“Well, look at you.”

A short laugh escaped me.

I’d spent several days with them on the reconnaissance mission, but there were still a few whose names I didn’t know. Even so, I felt close to them. We’d been through life and death together.

*They say three raids are enough to make sworn brothers.*

I smiled at the eight reconnaissance-squad members. Then a thought struck me.

*No, wait.*

Eight? Had I counted wrong?

I started counting again from the end, one by one. There had originally been nine of them, not counting me. Hyuk Mujin and Han Yeop were both seriously injured, so neither should have been here. That meant there should have been seven…

“…What are you doing here?”

Even while looking straight at him, I couldn’t be sure he was the same guy. His face was swollen like a steamed bun, and every patch of skin showing beyond his clothes was red and blotchy.

“Can’t you tell by looking?”

He was right. There was only one rude bastard like this on the reconnaissance squad.

“Hyuk Mujin?”

“Yes. Why? What?”

“Are you really Hyuk Mujin?”

“You can’t even recognize my face anymore?”

*Looking like that, even your parents wouldn’t recognize you…*

No, more importantly, why was this bastard here?

“Did you volunteer too?”

“I did.”

Then he added, “They wouldn’t take me, though.”

“Huh?”

“I told the physician I wanted to go, and he got angry and asked if I’d lost my mind and wanted to die. So I just snuck out.”

I gave him a serious look.

“Have you lost your mind? Do you want to die?”

“I want to live.”

Hyuk Mujin answered with a sour expression.

“Then why did you come?”

“I know my own condition. I can fight well enough.”

“Your face looks ready to burst.”

“The swelling is going down. My internal injuries are completely healed, so there’s no problem.”

“What about the external injuries?”

“They’ll heal on the way.”

“…”

“A few bones are cracked, but I can handle i—kh!”

Hyuk Mujin suddenly doubled over. Startled, I shouted, “Hey! What’s wrong?”

“Sometimes my chest hurts when I breathe… Ah, it’s fine now.”

“…”

Was this guy completely insane?

As I stood there speechless, Hyuk Mujin continued.

“Ah, Han Yeop couldn’t come. His internal injuries haven’t healed, so he’d only be a burden. Squad Leader? Squad Leader, you are listening to me, right?”

There was only one thought in my head.

*Logout. I need to log out as soon as possible.*

* * *

Night came early in winter. The sun went down, and after we had walked for who knew how long, the order to make camp finally came once we entered a wide basin.

“Ugh, every bone in my body hurts.”

Hyuk Mujin groaned. He clearly hadn’t fully recovered, and the march seemed to be taking its toll.

“It’s not too late. Want to turn back?”

“Are you saying that again?”

“I’m saying it because you look like you’re struggling.”

“You’re imagining things. Does Hyuk Mujin, a man among men, look like someone who’d get tired after walking for a mere half day?”

I nodded without the slightest hesitation.

“Yep.”

“Absolutely not!”

“Hmm. You’re really not tired? You’re fine?”

“Yes.”

“Then go help the others set up camp.”

At that moment, Hyuk Mujin, man among men, widened his eyes and sank to the ground.

“Ugh, the internal injuries Jopil gave me…”

“…”

*You said your internal injuries were completely healed, you bastard.*

I was considering whether to hit him when a voice cut in.

“Is he injured?”

A massive shadow stretched beneath the moonlight. Hyuk Mujin cautiously raised his head to see who it was, and his eyes flew open.

“Gasp! Lesser Family Head!”

If Jin Wikyung was a division commander, Hyuk Mujin was a private.

The next moment, Hyuk Mujin shot to his feet like lightning and stood at attention. Jin Wikyung burst out laughing.

“You still don’t look fully recovered. Stay down. Ah, but your internal injuries do seem completely healed. I’ll vouch for that.”

“Ah, I, that isn’t…”

Jin Wikyung lightly patted the flustered Hyuk Mujin on the shoulder, then turned to me.

“Shall we walk for a bit?”

I followed him to a secluded corner. He spoke first.

“How are you feeling?”

“Good.”

I’d reaped the full benefits of leveling up. My body had recovered at a terrifying speed, and allocating my points had made it even stronger.

The problem lay elsewhere.

*My Fame isn’t going up.*

No, it was going up. By about a rat dropping’s worth. Little by little. Very, very little.

At this rate, there was no guarantee I’d be able to log out before the fighting began.

*At least, not if things kept going like this.*

Jin Wikyung’s arrival was perfect timing. I brought up the idea I’d been mulling over.

“I want to take on a mission.”

His eyes widened at my blunt request.

“Hm? A mission?”

“Now that I’ve recovered, I want to distinguish myself for our family.”

I was proud of myself. To think I could deliver a line like that—a lie like that—with a straight face.

“I-is that truly what you think?”

“Yes.”

Jin Wikyung’s eyes trembled. He seemed overcome with emotion.

*This is actually making me feel kind of guilty.*

The reason I was putting on this unconvincing act was Fame. If I went out on even a reconnaissance mission and distinguished myself, I would gain more Fame.

Even better if I ran into a reconnaissance unit from the Mount Heng Sword Sect. Not only would they be excellent EXP, they might give me enough Fame to reach five hundred.

*Once the real battle started, I was done for.*

I had to log out before then, and fast. I spoke with determination.

“Just leave it to me.”

“How did you come to have such an admirable thought? Thank you, youngest.”

Jin Wikyung wiped the damp corners of his eyes with his sleeve, then continued.

“But no.”

“Then please give me a reconnaissance mission—what?”

“I appreciate the thought. Keep guarding the rear as you are now.”

*What is he talking about?*

I barely suppressed the urge to grab him by the collar and shake him.

“I-I really want to distinguish myself.”

“You’ve already done more than enough.”

“No, that’s not what I mean.”

“The merits you’ve earned so far have already been a great help to our family. So don’t trouble yourself over it.”

“I’d like you to give me even one reconnaissance mission. There could be enemies lying in ambush ahead…”

Jin Wikyung laughed softly.

“This battle will decide our family’s fate. Do you think I wouldn’t have accounted for something like that?”

“Couldn’t there be a one-in-ten-thousand chance that something was overlooked?”

“That is, quite literally, one in ten thousand.”

*Damn it.*

Nothing was going my way. What if I really failed to fill the rest of my Fame? Then I’d have to fight in a massive battle involving fifteen hundred people.

*I’m fucked.*

Something large and warm settled on my dejected shoulder. Jin Wikyung’s hand.

“Youngest.”

His voice was heavy and lonely. At the sudden change in mood, I fell silent and listened.

“Your mission is more important than anyone else’s.”

“What is my mission?”

After a long pause, he forced out a single word.

“Survive.”

“Huh?”

“Survive, no matter what it takes. If our family loses, run without looking back.”

“…”

“As long as the roots live, the tree can grow again. You and your second brother could become better roots than I ever could.”

I was speechless. For a long while, I could only stare at his face.

Survive. Become roots.

There was a gravity in his voice and gaze that I had never felt before.

“That is your mission.”

The hand on my shoulder slowly slipped away. I stared after Jin Wikyung as he left, unable to look away.
