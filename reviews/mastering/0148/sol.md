# Chapter 148

With the mood nicely warmed by a hundred thousand nyang, Jin Wikyung and Hong Jin exchanged greetings.

“I am Jin Wikyung of the Jin Family of Taiyuan. It is an immense pleasure to meet the Deputy Military Commissioner I’ve heard so much about.”

“To receive such a warm welcome from the Lesser Family Head of the great Jin Family of Taiyuan—I hardly know what to do with myself. Please, call me Comrade Hong from now on.”

“But how could I address a government official so casually?”

“Oh, you’re too formal. I told you, feel free.”

“Ha-ha. In that case, shall I, Comrade Hong?”

What was with the sudden communist atmosphere?

As I wondered whether I was in Pyongyang or the Murim, Jin Wikyung finished introducing himself and turned toward me.

“Hmm. Taekyung, you’re here?”

“Yes.”

His voice was more dignified than usual, so I read the room and answered politely.

Apparently, he understood that behaving as he normally did in front of outsiders would not only damage his own dignity but embarrass the entire family.

“So, did you properly greet His Highness?”

*I did more than greet him. I held a private autograph session.*

Hong Jin smiled and patted my shoulder.

“His Highness was delighted. He’s been wanting to meet Young Master Jin for quite some time.”

“Oh, is that so?”

“Yes. He was so happy, he simply wouldn’t let him go.”

“Uhehehe. It seems our youngest—no, my younger brother has thoroughly won His Highness over.”

“I can see why. He’s handsome, tall, and well-built. He’s strong in martial arts and has such an easygoing personality. Who could dislike him?”

“Ahem. It feels rather awkward to say this myself, but Taekyung truly is an extraordinary talent. If he had been born into one of the Five Great Families instead of our family, no one would be surprised if he became the greatest under heaven.”

Jin Wikyung got so carried away that he forgot all about acting dignified. As he chattered excitedly, Hong Jin’s expression hardened.

“The greatest under heaven? Lesser Family Head Jin, that joke goes too far.”

“Pardon? What do you mean?”

“You claim Young Master Jin has what it takes to become the greatest under heaven? I may have no ties to the Murim, but surely you don’t take me for a fool.”

“……Ahem.”

The atmosphere turned frigid in an instant, and Jin Wikyung coughed uncomfortably. Hong Jin promptly continued.

“A man like Young Master Jin could become the greatest of all time.”

“……!”

“Allow me to congratulate you in advance, Lesser Family Head Jin. For the greatest of all time to emerge from the Jin Family of Taiyuan—what a blessing for Shanxi Province.”

Jin Wikyung cried out, overcome with emotion.

“Comrade Hong!”

“Lesser Family Head Jin!”

“……”

They said Hong Jin had lived in the imperial palace for twenty years, and his way with words was certainly something else.

I clicked my tongue as I watched Jin Wikyung rejoice as though he had found his soulmate.

“This won’t do. We shouldn’t stand around here. I’ve prepared a place for us, so why don’t we share a drink?”

“What a predicament. I’m not very good with alcohol.”

“Ah, what a shame.”

“I can never get enough of it.”

“Comrade Hong!”

“Lesser Family Head Jin!”

“……”

“……”

Look at those two playing off each other.

They disappeared with their arms around each other’s shoulders, roaring with laughter. Wipeng stared after them in disbelief, then turned to me.

“Is that man truly the Deputy Military Commissioner?”

“Unfortunately, yes.”

“I heard he was once a palace attendant, but I never imagined he would be so frivolous.”

Well, then what did that make Jin Wikyung, a martial artist who had played along with every word?

Jin Mukyung, whose expression had been sour for a while now, finally spoke.

“That’s simply the kind of man he is. Last time, he casually stroked my shoulder. I barely stopped myself from breaking his arm.”

He tore the cloth in his hands to shreds and flung it to the ground. Looking much more relieved, he said,

“Then I have important business to attend to, so I’ll be going.”

“What business? You’re just going to train again.”

“Is anything more important to a martial artist than training?”

“……No.”

That shut me up.

As I stood there smacking my lips, a clear, ringing voice rang out.

“Wow, that’s exactly what my grandfather always says.”

Wipeng and Jin Mukyung’s gazes stabbed into Cheongpung like awls.

To ordinary people, Cheongpung was merely a young man with a slightly unusual air about him. To masters, however, he was something else entirely.

Both men’s eyebrows shot upward, and Cheongpung turned to me in confusion.

“Uh, Benefactor. Did I do something wrong?”

“Wrong? Of course not. They’re just intrigued. Right, gentlemen?”

Neither man took his eyes off Cheongpung. They merely nodded.

An extraordinarily young Peak master. Naturally, they were curious about this Cheongpung who had appeared out of nowhere.

“Since you’re all here, why don’t you introduce yourselves? This is Cheongpung.”

Before I had even finished speaking, Cheongpung bowed deeply.

“Hello, I’m Cheongpung! I’ve only been in Shanxi for a few days. Before that, I was in Henan, and before that…”

*Where the hell did this guy crawl out of?*

The question was written all over their faces.

Just as I’d expected. I explained Cheongpung’s identity as simply as possible.

“He’s the Sword Saint’s disciple.”

“……!”

“……!”

The name of Sword Saint Mae Jonghak was practically a cheat code among martial artists.

Both men gaped at Cheongpung, too stunned to speak. Cheongpung cautiously asked,

“Um, which of you is the Heaven Shaking Sword?”

Still reeling from the shock, Jin Mukyung stammered out a reply.

“I-I am the Heaven Shaking Sword. But are you truly Great Hero Mae Jonghak’s…?”

“Yes. He’s my grandfather.”

“Gasp!”

He looked several times more shocked than when he had seen the Flame Divine Palm martial arts manual.

A young man claiming to be the disciple—and grandson—of a Supreme Peak master believed to have gone into seclusion decades ago had suddenly appeared before them. Their reaction was understandable.

“This can’t be…”

“The Sword Saint’s successor…”

Cheongpung looked back and forth between the two astonished men, then smiled brightly.

“I didn’t know either until I came down the mountain.”

“Y-Young Hero. Did Great Hero Mae also descend the mountain…?”

The question brimmed with hope and excitement.

Both men were swordsmen who had trained with the sword their entire lives. Mae Jonghak had attained such heights in the Way of the Sword that he had earned the title Sword Saint. To them, he was practically a god.

But Cheongpung’s answer shattered their hopes.

“No. I snuck out alone. There were people I wanted to meet.”

“Ah…”

“How unfortunate…”

“But putting that aside…”

Cheongpung looked at the two crestfallen men and spoke again.

His gleaming eyes had been fixed on Jin Mukyung for some time now.

“Are you really Young Hero Jin Mukyung, the Heaven Shaking Sword? The one from the Ten Dragons and Phoenixes?”

“That’s right. I am Jin Mukyung.”

“Wow, I finally found you!”

“……Hmm?”

“I’ve been searching everywhere for you. From Heaven’s Gate Temple in Henan all the way here.”

*What? Jin Mukyung was the person this guy had been looking for?*

Wipeng and Jin Mukyung himself both looked bewildered.

“You were looking for me?”

“Yes. You were nearby, and I thought you’d be a good place to start.”

“Start? What do you mean?”

“My dueling tour.”

Cheongpung smiled faintly. It was nothing like the bright, innocent smiles I had seen from him before.

“I made a decision when I came down the mountain. I won’t return until I’ve defeated every one of the Ten Dragons and Phoenixes.”

“……!”

“My grandfather told me that martial artists have no need for words. We contend through martial arts alone.”

Sssss.

At that moment, a wave of heat washed over me. Violet light-flames had risen around Cheongpung’s entire body.

I had seen this once before.

*The Zaha Divine Technique.*

The Extreme Yang qi burned away the cold. The ground melted, and the soil blackened. Every trace of Cheongpung’s smile vanished.

“Shall we move somewhere else?”

“Is there any need?”

Jin Mukyung continued.

“Draw your sword.”

* * *

Jin Mukyung exhaled slowly. His racing heart gradually began to settle.

Breathing was crucial in battle. Only now was he finally ready to draw his sword.

He rested a hand on the hilt and recalled one man’s name.

*Sword Saint Mae Jonghak.*

Not a single day had passed since he first picked up a sword that he had forgotten that name.

A legendary swordsman said to have reached the ultimate realm of the sword—or perhaps a realm beyond it.

Everyone revered the Sword Saint, but Jin Mukyung was different.

*Someday, I will defeat him.*

Anyone who heard him say it would have snorted. They would have pointed at him and called him insane.

No matter how great a genius Jin Mukyung was, he could never reach the Sword Saint. No one had surpassed Mae Jonghak since the day he first earned that title.

Decades ago, Sword Saint Mae Jonghak had already written a new chapter in the history of the orthodox Murim and become the hero of a legend.

*It doesn’t matter. This is my goal.*

It was not reckless arrogance. It was a goal.

A goal he had etched into his bones and heart every day he trained with the sword.

And now, someone who had inherited everything from Sword Saint Mae Jonghak stood before him.

“My grandfather used to tell me, ‘Compared to the Ten Dragons and Phoenixes, you are nothing. Don’t become arrogant.’”

Cheongpung slowly stepped forward. A single blue-steel sword dangled haphazardly from his waist, and his steps were as light as if he were out for a stroll.

And yet…

*There are no openings.*

He looked utterly careless, but Jin Mukyung had no idea when or how he could attack.

Jin Mukyung licked his parched lips.

“I’ve never even met him… He praised me too highly.”

“No. Honestly, you surprised me a little. I mean that.”

Jin Mukyung knew that Cheongpung meant every word. That only made him feel stranger.

*Only a little?*

It had been more than twenty years since he began training with the sword. Talent and effort had brought him this far.

People called him a genius, gave him the martial title Heaven Shaking Sword, and counted him among the Ten Dragons and Phoenixes.

He had believed he had never once become drunk on such empty fame, but…

*I still have a long way to go.*

At some point, he must have grown accustomed to people looking up to him.

The wound Pung Yang had inflicted on him not long ago seemed to throb again.

“Do you know something?”

“What?”

“That you’re strong.”

“Until recently, I wasn’t sure. But now I know.”

“Because you met me?”

“Yes. Because I met you, Young Hero Jin. Now I know what the Ten Dragons and Phoenixes are capable of.”

“Is that so?”

Jin Mukyung gave a quiet laugh.

What an interesting man. He possessed martial arts rivaling—or perhaps surpassing—an Elder of the Nine Sects and One Gang, yet remained untainted. Pure and honest.

He was a martial artist, yet entirely unsuited to the Murim.

*He’s the exact opposite of someone I know.*

Someone suddenly came to mind.

A man who could probably survive no matter where in the Murim he was thrown, yet fought in the least martial-artist-like way imaginable.

“How old are you?”

“I turned twenty this year.”

“We’re even the same age. Coincidence? Or fate?”

“What?”

Instead of answering, Jin Mukyung shook his head.

In truth, he already knew how this duel would end. The qi of the Zaha Divine Technique surging around Cheongpung was simply that overwhelming.

His only regret was that he couldn’t display the full extent of his skill against an opponent of this caliber.

*What would that troublemaker do in a situation like this?*

Jin Mukyung glanced at his troublesome younger brother. Taekyung was grinning like a little devil and silently mouthing four words.

*You. Are. So. Fucked.*

*What a goddamn bastard.*

Jin Mukyung laughed hollowly and placed a hand on his sword hilt. The internal energy boiling within his dantian surged through every part of his body.

Cheongpung looked at Jin Mukyung’s sword and spoke.

“My grandfather told me something else. A duel has no need for an opening stance.”

“I agree.”

The next moment—

Violet light-flames and silver Sword Energy collided with a thunderous boom.
