# Chapter 952

The Double Ninth Festival was one of the continent’s biggest holidays.

It came when swallows flew south to warmer lands and snakes and frogs burrowed underground to prepare for winter.

At this time of year, people climbed mountains, drank chrysanthemum wine, recited poetry, and enjoyed the scenery.

Commoners who lived from one day’s wages to the next did it. So did high officials who wanted for nothing, and martial artists who lived in the turbulent martial world.

“Do you remember?”

A voice sounded suddenly behind him.

The young man gazing at the field of bright yellow chrysanthemums showed no surprise. He had known who was there for some time.

“Remember what?”

Jin Mukyung asked without taking his eyes off the flowers. A familiar presence came to stand beside him.

“Five years ago, when the three of us climbed the hill behind the house and spent the Double Ninth Festival together. We had such a wonderful time.”

“…You mean the Double Ninth Festival ten years ago?”

Jin Wikyung looked hurt at his younger brother’s doubtful response.

“What? Don’t you remember it?”

“I remember. I asked because your version sounds quite different from mine.”

“Different?”

“Taekyung, that reckless fool, got drunk and caused a scene. I wouldn’t call it a wonderful time.”

Jin Wikyung tilted his head.

“Really? You looked like you were enjoying yourself when you beat him with your scabbard.”

“I wasn’t smiling. I was disciplining my own flesh and blood to set him on the right path.”

“Then why were you grinning from ear to ear?”

Jin Mukyung fell silent.

Come to think of it, perhaps he had been smiling.

His youngest brother was always causing trouble, and their eldest brother doted on him. When an excuse to punish Taekyung finally presented itself, how could Mukyung not smile?

Of course, that was long ago. Only a small fragment of the day remained in his memory.

“The youngest cried as if the sky had fallen. You didn’t touch your food. You went off into the woods, away from everyone, and practiced with your sword until sunset. I can still see you there.”

“I don’t remember that, but I’m sure I did.”

Jin Mukyung’s reply was matter-of-fact.

Training was a matter of course for him. He had held a sword since childhood and practiced wherever he was, whenever he could.

Five years ago, the last time the three brothers of the Jin Family of Taiyuan had spent the Double Ninth Festival together, Mukyung had already reached the Peak realm, though he was barely twenty.

“I still remember the day you left home.”

“Ah.”

Jin Mukyung smacked his lips. He remembered Wikyung coming to see him off with a swollen face, as though he had spent the previous night crying.

*“Do you… do you really have to go?”*

*“Yes. I do.”*

*“Even if your older brother begs you to stay?”*

*“Not a chance.”*

*“What’s so special about Heaven’s Gate Temple that you’d abandon your family and go all the way to a distant land?”*

*“Even disciples of the great sects wait years to get into that Heaven’s Gate Temple you’re dismissing.”*

*“All right. If that’s what you want, I’ll escort you to Henan myself.”*

*“…You’re serious?”*

*“There’s something I need to tell the Hall Master in person.”*

*“What on earth would you tell him?”*

*“Just a few important precautions he absolutely needs to know. Foods you dislike, some of your habits… Mukyung? Where are you going, Mukyung?”*

He had run. With every last bit of strength he had.

It was the first time in his life he had put so much effort into his movement technique.

Jin Mukyung had only wanted to grow stronger at Heaven’s Gate Temple. He had no intention of becoming the fastest cadet ever to get expelled.

“I still remember watching you leave, hiding your tears behind your sleeve.”

“…”

Jin Mukyung kept his mouth shut.

Some truths were more beautiful left untold. That he had raised his sleeve to shield his eyes from the dust, for instance. Or that he had run to escape his overzealous brother.

Before he knew it, a quiet laugh escaped him.

“Why are you laughing?”

“It’s nothing. Just…”

Jin Mukyung managed to stop laughing and turned his head.

There was the face he remembered from about two years ago, when he had left once more to enter seclusion with no end in sight. Wikyung had watched him go with worry in his eyes.

Just as he was looking at him now.

“I’m laughing because I’m glad to see you. You haven’t changed, hyung.”

“Mukyung, you…”

“I’m glad some things are still the same.”

He had spent a little over two years in pitch-black darkness.

He had kept hunger at bay with bitter, astringent fasting pills and quenched his thirst with dew and rainwater collected in the training hall deep inside the cave.

Sometimes, after swinging his sword until he collapsed on the cold ground, he would find himself wondering.

Where was he? Who was he?

If he ever left that darkness, what would be waiting for him?

“Part of me was afraid. I thought the world I knew, and the people in it, might have changed beyond recognition.”

He had been half right and half wrong.

Everything around him had changed. The people he knew had not.

Surely that included his troublemaking youngest brother, wherever he was now.

“The day that brat left the family, he came to see me and said something.”

Mukyung had turned their conversation over in his mind thousands of times. No, tens of thousands. Taekyung’s voice, unusually calm that day, still lingered in his ears.

*“See you later.”*

It had not been a simple farewell.

The Star-Array Grand Banquet.

Taekyung had challenged him to meet there, among the countless stars from the Nine Provinces and Eight Wastes and the Four Seas and Five Lakes, and test their skills alongside Cheongpung.

As brothers born of the same mother. As martial artists.

After Taekyung left, Mukyung had stood staring at the empty space he had occupied and murmured the answer he had failed to give him.

*Yeah. See you later. No matter what.*

But they had not kept that promise.

In a place where he could not even feel time passing, Jin Mukyung swung his sword without rest. He slashed, stabbed, and tore at the pitch-black darkness before him.

Every time he grasped something new, another wall stood in his way.

He had to get over it. Break through it.

With the unkept promise tucked away in a corner of his heart, Jin Mukyung pressed on.

Until he found a faint light in the darkness.

Until that light grew as clear as the sun.

At last, he came back into the world.

He left behind the darkness that had enveloped him for two years and emerged into a world filled with another kind.

“Hyung.”

Jin Mukyung looked his brother straight in the eyes.

There was light in his gaze, and a directness that made it impossible to lie to him.

“Now tell me what I must do.”

“…!”

“No. Give me an order, as the Lesser Family Head of the Jin Family of Taiyuan.”

Jin Wikyung’s eyes trembled.

“You knew…?”

Jin Mukyung nodded.

The Jin Family of Taiyuan now stood at the head of Shanxi Murim. With the Family Head missing, Jin Wikyung led them all as its Alliance Leader.

Shanxi Province was already at war.

Li Feng, a lay disciple of Huashan and Shanxi Province’s Assistant Military Commissioner, had mobilized every force at the Shanxi Provincial Office to evacuate the people in the north. Some five thousand martial artists had gathered in the central region.

Every moment mattered.

Wikyung had not come looking for him solely to reunite with the brother who had emerged from seclusion after two years.

“Just as you know me well, hyung, I know you.”

“…It will be dangerous.”

“I don’t mind.”

Jin Mukyung went on calmly.

“I learned martial arts because I loved the sword. But I wanted to grow stronger for our family.”

Only strength could restore a fallen martial family.

His sword had carried more than a passion for martial arts all these years.

“Jin Mukyung, Second Young Master of the Jin Family of Taiyuan, will carry out the Lesser Family Head’s orders with all his heart.”

His younger brother cupped his hands in a salute, his voice unwavering. Jin Wikyung slowly closed his eyes.

After a brief hesitation, he opened them again. He was no longer speaking as Mukyung’s older brother, but as the Alliance Leader of Shanxi Murim and the Lesser Family Head of the Jin Family of Taiyuan.

“From this moment on, you are neither the Second Young Master of the Jin Family of Taiyuan nor the Lesser Family Head’s brother.”

He released the breath he had been holding.

Then Wikyung looked calmly at his brother—no, his retainer—and continued.

“I command Jin Mukyung, Commander of the Heaven Shaking Squad.”

Jin Mukyung, the Heaven Shaking Sword.

About five years earlier, the young prodigy from the frontier had reached the Peak realm at barely twenty and earned that epithet. His one older brother had named a fighting force after it.

He had hoped Mukyung would one day return to this place.

He had hoped, too, that Mukyung’s name would resound throughout the world and up to the heavens, just as *Heaven Shaking* promised.

“The enemy vanguard invading the north has split off some of its forces to go around Jeongyang. Take two hundred men from the Heaven Shaking Squad and blunt their advance.”

Jin Mukyung smiled.

“As you command.”

* * *

The next day.

The vast steppe army entered the north without resistance. What awaited it was not chrysanthemums but dark-red blood covering the mountain slopes—and one terrified survivor of its vanguard.

“K-Khan…”

Jamukha stared at the man trembling before him.

He knew at once that the thousand-man vanguard sent into Shanxi Province half a day ahead of the main force had been wiped out. The lone surviving fool had been sent back with a warning for the invaders.

“H-he told me to give you this, Khan…”

*Slide. Thump.*

The cloth slipped from his trembling fingers. The head of a Keshik centurion rolled out of the blood-soaked bundle.

Jamukha looked down at it in silence.

“Is that all?”

“He left a message. A message…”

His queue had come loose, and terror filled his face.

“He said, ‘Even five years from now, my brothers and I will climb the mountain together and spend the Double Ninth Festival—’”

“Bullshit.”

*Shhk.*

Jamukha struck without a moment’s hesitation. The man’s severed head rolled into the chrysanthemum field.

As Jamukha gazed at the flowers, now so red their original color was gone, Chinggen smacked his lips.

“Why not hear the rest? We don’t even know who he was.”

“Does that matter?”

The thousand-man vanguard had been annihilated.

No. What mattered was that all hundred Keshik among them had died in a single battle.

“Either way… we’ll meet soon enough.”

Jamukha stared south, his voice low.

A thought crossed his mind.

This war was supposed to be overwhelming and one-sided. It might not be as easy as he had imagined.
