# Chapter 130

Winter in the borderlands was brutal. A middle-aged man shuddered as the knife-sharp wind cut through his collar.

“Ugh, it’s cold as hell.”

The man, Seokchil, was a porter for the Seongun Escort Bureau in southern Shanxi Province.

He had spent more than a day and a half hauling a cart loaded with over a hundred geun of cargo, leaving him drenched in sweat. Whenever he stopped for a brief rest, as he had now, he had to contend with the bitter cold.

“Hyung, leave the cart and hurry over. Come warm yourself by the fire before you freeze to death.”

A fellow porter, already crouched in front of the campfire, called out to him. Seokchil answered gruffly as he walked over.

“Brat, I have five mouths to feed. I’ve got a long way to go before I can die.”

“True. You can’t die with a fox of a wife and rabbit-like children waiting for you.”

“What fox? She’s a bear. A bear.”

“Were those your last words? If your wife hears you, she’ll wring your neck.”

“You can curse the king behind his back. Didn’t you know?”

Seokchil moved closer to the campfire.

They were burning horse manure for fuel, so a foul smell hung in the air. But after nearly twenty years as a porter, Seokchil was as accustomed to it as he was to the smell of cooking rice.

“Ah, now I feel alive again.”

“But Hyung, aren’t you being a little stingy?”

“Huh? What nonsense are you talking about now?”

His fellow porter grinned and jerked his chin to one side.

“You should bring the rookie over, too. What kind of man rushes over here to save himself and leaves the kid behind?”

Seokchil turned while warming his frozen hands. A young man sat on a snow-covered rock in the distance, staring blankly into space.

*That kid’s doing it again.*

The young man was a new porter they had picked up in Henan. According to Escort Chief Song, who was in charge of this escort run, the young man seemed capable enough to earn his keep, so they had taken him on.

*Well, we’re always short on people.*

The problem was that no one could tell what the young man was thinking. He often sat there in a daze, just as he was doing now.

As Seokchil clicked his tongue, his fellow porter asked, “Why? Is he a little strange?”

“He works hard. He’s surprisingly strong for someone who doesn’t look like much.”

“Then what’s the problem?”

“What do you mean, what’s the problem? It’s frustrating to see a young fellow sitting around like that day after day. Back when I was his age…”

“You want to say you dreamed of making your mark on the world and worked hard every day?”

“Of course. A man should set himself a grand goal and strive toward it.”

“I assume that grand goal wasn’t becoming the greatest porter under heaven.”

“You little—”

At Seokchil’s outraged response, his fellow porter quickly changed the subject.

“By the way, what’s that fellow’s name?”

“Cheongpung.”

“Now that’s a fine name. Suits him, too.”

“That it does.”

Seokchil wasn’t particularly fond of Cheongpung, but he wholeheartedly agreed.

Something about the young man’s open, pleasant features and clear eyes put people strangely at ease and soothed their anger.

“Hey, rookie!”

Cheongpung turned at the other porter’s call.

“Me?”

“Who else here is a rookie? Come warm yourself by the fire. If you keep sitting there, your butt will freeze right off.”

“That might be an interesting experience.”

“Experience? What experience?”

“Having my butt freeze off. It’s never happened to me before.”

The porter fell silent for a moment, then whispered to Seokchil, “What the hell is wrong with him?”

“I don’t know. The kid’s a little strange. Maybe he ate something bad.”

Cheongpung tilted his head.

“I had two dumplings this morning.”

“…Sharp ears, huh? Fine, just come sit down.”

“Should I?”

Cheongpung trudged over and sat in front of the fire. The usual questions immediately began flying his way.

“Where are you from?”

“Henan.”

“So you’re from Henan.”

“I was in Hubei a month ago.”

“Hmm. Hubei’s nice, too.”

“Before that…”

The porter turned to Seokchil.

“This guy’s something else.”

“Right? Talking to him makes me feel like I’m going strange, too.”

“How have you put up with him beside you for over a month?”

“That’s why I stopped talking to him. Has it been about three days since our last conversation?”

Cheongpung answered with a serious expression.

“Four days and three shichen.”

“…”

“…”

The two men barely suppressed the urge to smack him over the head.

“So where are you from?”

“I lived in the mountains.”

“That’s not what I meant… No, never mind. Thank you for answering at all.”

“You’re welcome.”

Strangely enough, Cheongpung’s innocent smile made their anger melt away.

His unpredictable, bizarre remarks, coupled with a smile as pure as a child’s, only deepened their curiosity about this strange young man unlike anyone they had ever met.

“But you said you lived in the mountains?”

“Just like it sounds. I grew crops and gathered medicinal herbs there from the time I was young. I did all sorts of other things, too.”

The two men assumed Cheongpung had come from a slash-and-burn farming community.

Most slash-and-burn farmers had fled into the mountains either to escape the cruelty of vicious landlords or to avoid the authorities after committing some crime.

“You must have had a hard life.”

“I thought it was fun.”

“Oh, really?”

Could life as a slash-and-burn farmer possibly be fun? The thought crossed Seokchil’s mind, but he saw no reason to ask.

“Then what made you come down from the mountain?”

“Life there got boring. I wanted to see the world, and there were people I wanted to meet.”

“So that’s why you came to Henan.”

“Yes. It turned out to be a wasted trip, but… things aren’t bad now, either. Escort work is quite interesting.”

“You think escort work is interesting?”

Cheongpung beamed.

“Watching people is interesting. So are looking at the land, looking at the sky, and thinking.”

To Seokchil, who had spent so many years as a porter, they were all sights he was sick to death of.

People worn down by exhaustion and worries about making a living. Damp earth and knife-sharp winds blowing like mad. The only thought filling his head was how much he would be paid for this escort run.

*Well, he’s still young. That’s why he can say things like that.*

Besides, he had lived in the mountains all his life and came from a slash-and-burn farming community. His attitude was understandable.

Soon enough, he would learn about the harsh realities of life. As he grew older, wouldn’t he gradually end up just like Seokchil?

*I used to be like that, too.*

Seokchil regarded Cheongpung with a mixture of envy and pity before speaking.

“Just take this as the rambling of an old man.”

He knew he was meddling where he wasn’t wanted, but he wanted to teach this innocent young man something about the realities of life.

The boy had too much of his life ahead of him to begin and end as a porter.

“Any job seems worthwhile at first. But after ten or twenty years, you stop seeing much of a future in it. No matter how hard a porter works or how capable he is, he’s still a porter. You’d be better off learning some Third Rate martial arts at a local martial arts academy and starting as an escort. Much better off.”

Cheongpung blinked.

“Oh, really?”

“Not ‘oh, really?’ I’m telling you to do it. You’re a little old to start learning martial arts, but who knows? Maybe you have exceptional martial talent and could become a successful First Rate master.”

“A First Rate master…”

The other porter, who had been listening quietly, clicked his tongue.

“Aren’t you filling his head with too much nonsense? First Rate masters don’t exactly grow on trees.”

“I’m just saying it’s possible. Does it make any sense for someone his age to be satisfied with being a porter?”

“Well, you’re right about that. If I were ten years younger, I wouldn’t be doing this either.”

“See?”

Seokchil patted Cheongpung on the shoulder.

“You heard him. Save every coin you can for a year or two, then enroll in a martial arts academy. You’ll be much better off. Until then, I’ll teach you everything I know.”

Cheongpung tilted his head.

“A year or two?”

“What? Is that too long? You don’t know how the world works, so I suppose you don’t realize how expensive martial arts academy fees are. Even with the lowest estimate, it’ll take at least a year…”

“No, I’m going to quit before then.”

“You’re quitting? When?”

“Now.”

“Huh?”

“What?”

Cheongpung smiled brightly.

“I didn’t know the way to Shanxi Province. Then I found an escort run headed there, so I asked if I could tag along.”

“…And?”

“We’ll reach Taiyuan after one more day, so I was planning to part ways around then.”

Seokchil and the other porter exchanged baffled looks.

“What the hell? Didn’t Escort Chief Song say he signed a one-year contract?”

“That’s what I heard, too. That’s why they assigned him to you, the most experienced porter, so he could learn from you.”

Seokchil turned to Cheongpung in confusion.

“When you joined us in Henan, you signed something, didn’t you?”

“Oh, yes.”

“If you still have it, show me.”

Cheongpung pulled a yellowed bamboo slip from inside his clothes and held it out.

It was a contract stating that he would work as a porter for the Seongun Escort Bureau for one year and pay a penalty if he left before then.

“You can read, right?”

“I finished the Four Books and Three Classics when I was four.[^1]”

“Enough nonsense. Read this part. Yes, that section. Read it aloud, nice and clear.”

Cheongpung read the section Seokchil indicated in a crisp voice.

“Once signed, this contract cannot be revoked. In the event of unauthorized departure, the signer shall pay a penalty of fifty silver nyang or provide compensation of equivalent value.”

“You know how much fifty silver nyang is, right? Do you know what ‘compensation of equivalent value’ means?”

Cheongpung thought for a moment, then slapped his forehead.

“Does it mean I’d have to work it off?”

“That’s right, you idiot. Did you think the Escort Bureau was full of nothing but kindhearted saints?”

Seokchil’s blood pressure shot up, making the back of his neck throb. He wanted to crack open the top of the kid’s skull and see what was inside.

*How can someone like this even exist? Is it because he’s spent his whole life in the mountains?*

The dangers involved in transporting goods across the land were beyond imagination. Mounted bandits, river bandits, mountain bandits, every other sort of outlaw, and even interference from rival Escort Bureaus.

Even if they overcame every one of those obstacles, a single natural disaster could doom an escort run.

Escort Bureaus were as disciplined and hard-edged as most Murim sects—if not more so.

*And after putting his seal on a contract, this kid says what? “I’m leaving around here”?*

The young fool before him knew far too little about the world.

Determined to save the boy’s life, Seokchil spoke.

“I’m telling you this just in case, so give up any thoughts of running away. Work for a year and think of it as earning money. Understand?”

“A year is too long. I think I can work through tomorrow, though.”

“You little bastard!”

“Hyung, Hyung, calm down! If Escort Chief Song sees this, we’ll be in for a world of trouble.”

“Let go! I said let go!”

Just as Seokchil was about to snap, Cheongpung spoke.

“Uh, wouldn’t this be enough to cover the penalty?”

Clink.

The two men’s eyes widened at what Cheongpung held out.

The objects were shaped like horses’ hooves and gleamed silver, whiter than the snow.

“S-silver yuanbao?”

“And there are two of them!”

Two silver yuanbao, each worth fifty silver nyang.

A hundred silver nyang was an enormous fortune, more than an ordinary porter could earn after working himself to the bone for ten years.

And it had just emerged from the clothes of a young man they had taken for a slash-and-burn farmer.

“H-how…?”

“I was given some traveling money when I left home.”

Both men’s mouths fell open at Cheongpung’s innocent reply.

What kind of family gave their son a hundred silver nyang as traveling money? Judging by the money pouch sagging like a bull’s testicles, that wasn’t even all he had.

“At least it looks like this should cover the penalty…”

The two men nodded frantically.

“It will. Of course it will.”

“Why did you suddenly start speaking formally?”

“It’s simply more comfortable this way.”

“Exactly. Nothing in the world could be more comfortable.”

“Oh. If that’s what you prefer.”

Cheongpung gave the two men a puzzled look and handed them both silver yuanbao.

“I’ll be going, then. Please tell them this is for the penalty.”

“A-are you giving us both?”

“This is too much…”

“If there’s anything left over, you two can split it. I don’t really know how to spend money. Buy yourselves some warm clothes. Something with expensive fur.”

“…!”

As Cheongpung slung a bundle over his shoulder and prepared to leave, Seokchil hurriedly called after him.

“C-could I ask your name?”

“Cheongpung. Until half a month ago, I was from Henan. Last month, I lived in Hubei, and before that, I was in Shaanxi.”

With that, Cheongpung strode off toward Taiyuan.

The sky was blue, and early shoots were sprouting from the damp earth.

“Maybe spring will come a little early this year.”

He smiled brightly, hoping the plum blossoms would bloom in profusion again that spring.

Then he thought of Lotus Peak on Huashan, from which he had secretly run away not long ago.

[^1]: The Four Books and Three Classics are foundational Confucian texts.
