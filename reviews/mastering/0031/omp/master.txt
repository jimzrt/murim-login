# Chapter 31

“Well, look at you. This bastard’s got it made.”

I slowly opened my eyes to a familiar face.

“Did you come here for a picnic? Sleeping inside a Gate?”

I must have dozed off. I shamelessly denied it.

“I wasn’t sleeping. Who says I was?”

“Wipe the drool off your mouth before you start lying.”

“Tsk.”

“Oh, you little—”

He shook a fist at me, but a smile tugged at the corners of his mouth. I rubbed my stiff neck.

“Ugh, I’m tired.”

“Did you meet a woman last night? Why are you nodding off like a sick chicken in the middle of a raid?”

“When would I have time to meet a woman? You know my situation.”

“True enough.”

We’d been through thick and thin together for five years, ever since our first battle. He knew me as well as I knew him.

“Then what’s wrong?”

“What do you think? Money.”

“Money? Don’t tell me you’re moonlighting.”

His voice dropped. I looked past his shoulder at the team members resting nearby and nodded.

“Well, well. This bastard gets a little experience under his belt and starts lining his own pockets. And he’s the vice team leader, no less.”

“Raids have been scarce lately, and I’m strapped. Really strapped. You’ve done it yourself, hyung, so don’t give me that.”

Hunters with professional licenses were legally barred from taking second jobs. Even a D-rank probably wouldn’t have to live like this, but F-ranks like us had no choice. The Guild knew that, which was why they looked the other way.

“True, but… be careful. If someone files a complaint with the Management Agency, you’ll have a real headache on your hands.”

“It’s a trustworthy place. They pay cash the same day, so there’s no paper trail. You think I’d do it otherwise?”

“Really?”

Judging by his face, he was tempted.

“Do you need money too, hyung?”

“I’ve got three kids. I’d need an oil well to erupt in my yard.”

He launched into his usual routine with sorrowful eyes. I’d already heard it dozens of times. After venting about the misery of raising children, the rising price of formula, and the shamelessness of merchants, he patted my shoulder.

“Don’t get married.”

“I won’t.”

More precisely, I couldn’t.

I had no woman and no money. My goal in life was to buy a house in a safe zone within five years, so deep down, I envied him.

A loving spouse and children. A happy family. I had no idea when I’d ever have something like that.

“Marriage is a swamp.”

For all his talk, he was known as a devoted husband and a good father. I knew how fondly he smiled whenever he took out his family photos.

“It sucks your whole life in. By the time you come to your senses, you’re buried up to your chest and can barely breathe.”

“…”

Maybe I’d been wrong about him.

“So don’t spend all your time working. Take it easy now and then. Dating, hobbies—stuff like that’s good for you.”

“I don’t know. I still need the money.”

I scratched my head as I answered. He gave me a concerned look.

“I saw you sleeping earlier. You were drenched in cold sweat. Keep that up and you’ll drop dead from overwork.”

“Was I?”

Now that he mentioned it, my back was damp with cold sweat. I must have had a bad dream…

*I can’t remember.*

Probably just some stupid nonsense.

* * *

A Gate.

These days, Gates were how Hunters like me made a living, but their true purpose had been to serve as invasion routes for the Demon Realm’s army.

When Demon King Asmodeus fell, his mighty army was routed with him. Yet even decades later, the Gates remained.

“Defensive formation!”

At his command, three Hunters carrying massive tower shields moved to block the front. In a narrow cave, that alone was enough to stop most attacks.

*Clang! Clang-clang!*

“Giiiiik!”

About twenty goblins hurled poison needles, axes, and spears, but every one of them bounced off those big, beautiful tower shields.

“Archers!”

The tanks blocked every attack from the front while the archers rained arrows from the rear. We pressed forward and took down about half the goblins, drawing panicked shrieks from the rest.

“Gyaaaah!”

“Kieek!”

He gave the next order at just the right moment.

“Attack formation!”

The tanks dropped their tower shields and charged. I was even faster.

“Hah!”

I swung my iron spear in a wide arc. Green blood burst through the air, and the front rank broke apart. I plunged into the gap, stabbing and slashing at anything within reach, and their entire formation collapsed.

“Charge!”

The damage dealers and tanks joined in, and the goblin pack became a heap of corpses in the blink of an eye.

“Pretty easy today, huh?”

“Honestly, the vice team leader did half the work. He was flying around out there. When did he get that good…? Shh. The Team Leader’s pissed.”

Everyone chatting fell silent the moment he appeared.

“Hey, Jin Taekyung!”

That made me jump.

I’d been staring blankly into space, lost in thought.

“What?”

“Who told you to go off on your own? Are you a tank? Did you forget the attack order? And you still call yourself the vice team leader?”

“That’s not—”

“If you’re going to act like this, transfer teams. You’re putting everyone else in danger too.”

I looked at his grim face and sighed.

“I’m sorry. I don’t know why I did that. They just… suddenly didn’t seem like a big deal. I must have lost it for a second.”

It was a strange feeling. The moment I saw the pack of goblins, I’d thought I could wipe them out by myself.

No, it hadn’t been a thought.

It had been certainty.

“You…”

He swallowed the rest of his words. We’d been watching each other’s backs for five years, and this was the first time I’d ever done something so reckless.

“Don’t do it again. If something’s bothering you, tell me.”

He patted my shoulder and walked away. As I watched him go, a spot deep in my chest began to ache.

*Should I see a doctor?*

But before long, I had forgotten all about the pain.

* * *

“Magic Gem!”

“Got one!”

“Another!”

“It dropped! It dropped! It dropped!”

“Mom! Hayeon!”

Ah, that last shout was mine. His face was flushed with excitement too.

“Hey, what is all this…?”

Twenty Magic Gems of various sizes lay neatly arranged on the ground.

Magic Gems. They looked like red pebbles, but they were known as the flowers of a Gate. These concentrations of mana found inside monsters were a form of higher-dimensional energy—and the most valuable byproduct a monster could yield.

“At this rate, each one’s got to be worth more than a million won.”

That came from the Team Leader, an E-rank Hunter who’d survived ten years in this business. Everyone’s eyes glazed over at the intoxicating sight.

“Is it normally like this?”

At the new recruit’s question, everyone shook their heads vigorously.

“Absolutely not.”

An F-rank Gate yielded one or two on average. Even with incredible luck, you wouldn’t find five.

I was busy doing the math.

*At least twenty million from the Magic Gems alone, another five million from the byproducts and Equipment. Add all the various allowances and…*

*Fuck. How much is this?*

It was the biggest jackpot I’d hit since becoming a Hunter.

And the raid wasn’t even over yet.

“How far in are we?”

“Almost there. Turn right and the boss zone’s just ahead.”

The words *boss zone* made everyone’s eyes light up.

It went without saying, but a boss zone contained a boss. A boss monster was the strongest monster in its Gate—and the one carrying the most expensive byproducts, Equipment, and Magic Gems.

*What if we take down the boss monster too?*

A literal jackpot. A once-in-a-lifetime opportunity for an F-rank Hunter.

Everyone was grinning at the thought when he spoke.

“Wait. Let me think.”

What the hell was he talking about?

“What do you mean?”

“Think about it. Every monster we’ve encountered has been an ordinary goblin. So why are there this many Magic Gems? Something’s off.”

He sighed. His face was still flushed with excitement, but doubt was written all over it.

“I know how you feel. I do… but we’ve already made a fortune. Let’s be satisfied with this and head back.”

Satisfied?

Turn back now, after coming this far?

I looked at the other team members. Some had worked alongside us for two or three years, while others were new. But they all wore the same expression I did.

“I’m against—”

My breath caught.

*Damn it. The chest pain again.*

I tried to keep talking, but no sound came out. Now my ears were ringing too.

*What is this?*

The pain and ringing grew worse. I dropped to my knees, gasping for breath.

*Someone. Please, help me.*

I grabbed someone’s pant leg and clung to it.

It was him. The man who had looked after me like a brother and a father for the past five years.

*Hyung. Please save me.*

He looked down at me with an indifferent gaze.

“Me? Save you, after you ran away and left everyone behind?”

What?

“I wanted to live too.”

I forgot the pain and stared at him. His clothes and skin melted away, exposing bone. Everyone but me had become a skeleton sprawled across the ground.

*Ah. That’s right.*

They had all died.

That day two years ago, in the boss zone I had insisted we enter, every one of them had died.

*I was the only one who survived.*

I floundered, drowning in the memories. The ominous darkness that had settled over the boss zone. The foul smell and damp ground. That thing’s enormous wings.

Bodies ripped to pieces in midair. Terrified screams. People running for their lives.

*You fucking bastard!*

But even the Skill I’d poured everything into hadn’t been enough to kill it. As I waited for death, he pulled me to my feet.

*Taekyung!*

*Hyung, I’m sorry. It was all my fault.*

If it hadn’t been for me—if I hadn’t gotten greedy—they all could have lived. They could have gone home to their families.

I sobbed like a child, and he forced a smile.

*How is that your fault? Look at this kid. Now you’re even pretending to be the Team Leader.*

The massive creature floated through the cave. Stalactites rained down, and the last member of our team screamed as he died. In the darkness, the monster’s red eyes turned toward us.

*That arrogant bastard. Taekyung, you go first.*

*Hyung. Cheonsu hyung!*

My chest hurt. Pain crashed over me, enough to turn my vision black. It felt as though I had swallowed lava, as though everything inside me was burning away. The ringing in my ears became a monster’s roar.

*Kyaaaaau!*

* * *

“Hyung—!”

I woke with a scream.

But this wasn’t a Gate. There were no monsters or team members.

A man rose from his seat beside the sunlit window.

“Were you dreaming about my lord? He’ll be pleased when I tell him.”

His face radiated coldness.

Wipeng, Jin Wikyung’s right-hand man.

Seeing him drove home that I was still inside the game.

“Are you all right?”

“No. It was a nightmare.”

“Then I’ll leave that part out when I tell him.”

“Suit yourself.”

My whole body was drenched in sweat. Through the gaps in the bandages wrapped tightly around me, I could see scabbed flesh.

“How long was I out?”

“You were unconscious for five days. Your condition was so critical that the Medicine King Hall Leader concluded you wouldn’t last another day.”

“Really?”

“Yes. When my lord heard, he flew into a rage. If I hadn’t stopped him, he would have beaten the Medicine King Hall Leader to death.”

“Ah.”

I remembered the old man from the meeting a few days ago, the one who had threatened to shove a giant needle into the White Tiger Hall Leader’s anus.

*So he’d practically pronounced me dead.*

“Did anything else happen?”

“A great deal. Among it all, there is good news and even better news. Which would you like to hear first?”

“The good news.”

“First, the reconnaissance squad and the survivors of the Sakju Branch returned safely. Two of them suffered fairly serious injuries, but their lives are not in danger.”

*Survivors.*

The word made my heart sink.

*Number Seven.*

I remembered his face as he breathed his last, one dagger buried in his throat and another between his brows. He couldn’t have been more than twenty. Far too young to die.

“Are you thinking of the dead?”

“The body—did they recover his body?”

“We recovered it and gave him a proper burial. He was an orphan with no one in the world, so he had no surviving family.”

“…”

“May I say something?”

Wipeng didn’t wait for an answer. He stepped toward me and continued.

“Third Young Master, do not reduce your subordinate’s death to a meaningless one.”

“What do you mean…?”

“Martial artists are not people meant to be protected. They fight their enemies and prove themselves. He may have died facing an enemy far too strong for him, but he did not simply die. He fell in battle.”

The idea that dying on a battlefield somehow made it an honorable death was the biggest load of bullshit I’d ever heard.

There was no such thing as an honorable death. Even now, I could vividly hear the screams of my comrades who had died two years ago. I could still see Number Seven’s wide-open eyes as he breathed his last.

“The fact that he died hasn’t changed.”

“There are people who cling to facts that can never be changed. I won’t say who.”

“…”

“Do you regret it?”

“Of course.”

“Then live the life he did not get to.”

Wipeng continued in a gentler voice than I had ever heard from him.

“I’m not telling you to forget the dead. Bury them in your heart and carve them into your mind. Use that regret as your foothold, then soar to the place they dreamed of reaching. That is the path you must take, Young Master.”

*The path I must take…*

The words alone made something stir deep in my chest. I turned them over in my mind for a while, then laughed weakly.

“Damn. I’ll break my legs before I get there.”

“It will take a lifetime.”

“If I devote my whole life to it, will I make it?”

“I don’t know. I don’t even know how far my own path will take me, so how could I know yours?”

“What’s waiting at the end of your path, Great Hero Wipeng?”

“Number One Under Heaven.”

Was that a joke?

No. Wipeng was more serious and resolute than ever.

“That won’t be easy.”

“Because it is a dream.”

He was right. Dreams were always difficult to achieve.

Even more so when you carried the dreams of those you had lost.

“Great Hero Wipeng. May I ask you one thing?”

“Anything.”

“That guy. What was his name?”

“His name was…”

The moment Wipeng opened his mouth, a cold winter wind rattled the window.

*Whoooosh.*

Beyond the chill sound of the wind, I heard Number Seven’s name.

“That’s a cool name.”

“I heard he chose it himself. His dream was just as grand.”

“What was it?”

“Number One of All Time.”

“…”

“You’re going to have a hard time.”

“Yeah. This is ridiculous.”

A laugh slipped out. Only then did I feel the weight lift from my heart.

It was all thanks to Wipeng.

“You’re finally back to your old self.”

“Thank you.”

“Don’t mention it.”

Wipeng gave a slight nod and spoke.

“Now, the even better news remains.”

Ah. Right.

Good news and even better news.

Brimming with anticipation, I waited for him to continue.
