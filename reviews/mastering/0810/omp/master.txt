# Chapter 810

Perhaps intuition was another name for wisdom.

The feeling of an instinct etched into every cell suddenly waking. Of countless thoughts tangled in your head coming together as one.

“Michael Silbert. Michael Silbert. Michael…”

The name should have stayed in Jin Taekyung’s mind. Instead, it slipped from his lips without him noticing.

A question filled his mind in an instant. New thoughts followed, one after another. Each time a question came back to him with an answer, he sent it out again carrying another question.

And each time, the same words came first.

*What if. Suppose. Maybe…*

He questioned and questioned without pause, until the thoughts tangling in his head took on something like a coherent shape.

He had no idea how much time passed.

Or how close someone’s footsteps had come.

*Crunch.*

Sand shifted underfoot. Jin’s senses snapped awake.

The hairs on his body stood on end, and instinct moved him before his brain could give the order.

*Whoosh.*

He spun, slashing downward with his spear.

He’d made that move thousands—tens of thousands—of times in training and combat. It was etched into him, terrifyingly fast and precise. Fast enough to make the intruder in the darkness think of death.

But the intruder was no pushover.

*Vwooom.*

A flash of light accompanied an invisible ripple of qi. The space around them brightened, and Jin’s eyes widened as he recognized who stood before him.

“……!”

Recognition brought control. His strike, hurtling forward with tremendous speed and force, stopped as if someone had pressed pause.

The spearhead halted a hair’s breadth from a faintly glowing, translucent barrier. A fierce gust swept through.

*Fwoooosh!*

Sand that had somehow escaped the blood flew into the air. The intruder spat some of it out of his mouth.

“Do me a favor. If you’re going to kill me, tell me first. Give me a little time to prepare.”

Jin lowered his spear. “Damn it. I’m sorry, Johnson. Are you hurt?”

“……As you can see.”

Magic Johnson dispelled his defensive spell. He barely managed to get the words out. In those few moments, cold sweat had gathered on the back of his neck, glistening in the faint moonlight.

“I’m fine. Though I might’ve had a little accident.”

“What?”

“Before you get the wrong idea, everything about me is big. Just this once, it was only a little.”

Jin let out a relieved breath at the joke.

That had been close. If he hadn’t stopped his spear at the last moment, there would have been blood.

“I really am sorry.”

“I said I’m fine. But Jin, what the hell was that?”

“What was what?”

“Why you mistook a wonderful friend and comrade-in-arms like me for an enemy. What were you thinking about just now?”

“Ah, well…”

Jin started to answer, then closed his mouth.

Johnson studied him for a moment and shrugged. “You don’t have to tell me. If our young boss can’t talk about it, I’m sure he has his reasons. But there’s one thing I do have to say.”

“What’s that?”

“We finished regrouping nearly thirty minutes ago. Whatever’s troubling you, if we delay the pursuit any longer, we’ll lose them for good. That’s why I came.”

*What? Already?*

Jin checked the time and bit his lip. Johnson was right.

*When did that much time pass?*

It felt as though Choi Minwoo had stopped by less than a minute ago, but the sky had grown darker.

*I must’ve had a lot on my mind.*

No-self wasn’t a state that belonged only to martial arts. Jin had been so absorbed in one question after another that he hadn’t noticed time passing. Johnson had no way of guessing why he’d stayed rooted to the spot.

“I know their deaths are weighing on you. That’s why I gave you some time. But Jin, if we lose the monsters now or fail to find The Prophet… remember that everyone who died will have died for nothing.”

*Pat, pat.*

Johnson gave Jin’s shoulder two pats with his large hand, then turned away.

“Everyone’s waiting. We need you, Boss.”

That was all.

Jin watched the Grand Mage walk away. How much truth did the questions he’d come up with hold? What was a lie, and what was real?

He steadied his breathing.

“No. Either way, there’s only one path.”

He’d barely managed to grasp a single clue. With no other choice, there was no point in thinking it over any longer.

Jin took a heavy step into the darkness surrounding them.

* * *

The desert night was cold, dark, and vast.

No one complained. They walked in silence.

One hundred and eighty-five.

We’d fought back-to-back with them, if only for a short time, and lost them. Some had been strangers to us; others had been close. We’d had to bury their bodies in the dunes and leave them there.

In place of gravestones worthy of heroes, we planted their bloodied weapons in the sand. We swore we would return to these coordinates and bring them home to their families.

That promise was why the survivors kept moving. I led the way.

*Whooooosh!*

Countless figures sped across the desert.

Sand carried on the wind stung my face. But sand wasn’t all the wind carried.

*Blood. And a stench.*

The smell was sickening, but familiar. Sticky liquid coated the plants and rocks we passed.

Blue-green blood. Monster blood.

*Where are you?*

I spread the qi coiled inside me across a wide area. Then I sensed faint breathing and traces of unpleasant magical power.

“Shen.”

I stopped. Xiao Shen understood at once, drew his sword in a flash, and drove it deep into the ground beneath his feet.

*Squish!*

Blood spurted from the caving sand. The ground around us rippled like waves.

*Shhhhh!*

Monsters appeared for an instant amid the flying sand. An urgent shout rang through the night.

“Ant Lion!”

An antlion.

Even the smallest was about the size of a midsize car, and these powerful monsters were classified as at least B-rank. But the one surfacing now had larger pincers and stronger mana than any Ant Lion I’d seen.

*One of the monsters that fled the battle.*

It was among the hundred or so A-rank monsters that had saved themselves by leaving the battlefield.

I moved as soon as I realized it.

*Tap.*

The twenty meters between us vanished. As the Ant Lion lunged to swallow the nearest Hunters, I brought my spear down.

*Shhk!*

One strike. Both pincers, harder than steel, sliced off like tofu.

Before it could scream, I twisted the spear shaft in midair and slammed it into the monster’s body.

*Crack!*

Blood sprayed out with a nauseating stench. The blow wasn’t enough to kill it.

I rested the spearhead against the spot I took for its brow and spoke in a language not of this world.

The language of monsters.

—A few questions. Think carefully before you answer.

—……!

Scholars who studied the Demon Realm language existed. Humans who could speak it fluently were rarer than named monsters.

The Ant Lion’s pain-clouded eyes flew wide.

—You. Human. How?

I tightened my grip on White Flame instead of answering.

*Shhk.*

The spearhead slid through skin as hard as armor.

—I answer! Anything! Ask!

Its halting Demon Realm language suggested it wasn’t very intelligent. It was quick to catch on, though. I stopped the spearhead.

—Where are the others?

—Gone. All. Where sun falls.

—West?

—Yes! There. Many!

I had no reason to doubt it. The Ant Lion was stupid enough that everything it said rang true.

*West.*

Some monsters must have fallen behind or scattered, but most of those that had fled the battlefield had headed west.

What I really wanted to know was something else.

—What about The Prophet?

—The Prophet?

—Yes. The Prophet.

The Ant Lion blinked its bulging eyes.

—What is that? I don’t know.

I clicked my tongue as I realized my mistake. The Prophet had revealed that name to the world himself. Unless the monster army had gathered somewhere in the desert to watch TV, this creature wouldn’t know it.

—Let me rephrase. Not The Prophet. Your leader—the commander leading you and the other monsters.

—Commander? Leader? Leads us?

It repeated my words haltingly, then looked up at me with fear and confusion in its eyes.

—Dead. You. You all killed them.

—What?

—One. Two. Three. Four. All dead. Me, everyone, scared. So ran.

I looked into its eyes and confirmed once more that it wasn’t lying.

—Right. I see.

—Me, me, believe! Didn’t lie!

—I know.

I drove the spearhead deep between the Ant Lion’s eyes.

—I know how many people died because of you, too.

*Crunch. Gurgle.*

The monster’s enormous body shuddered as blood bubbled from it.

A System notification announced another death. I shook the blood and bodily fluids from my spear.

Johnson came over once he saw the interrogation was finished. “What did it say?”

“No luck,” I told him. “It didn’t even know The Prophet existed.”

“……Damn it. I suppose it’d be strange for an A-rank monster to know all that. Did you find out where the others went?”

“Yes.”

“Where?”

I looked at Johnson without answering. Then, as if it were nothing, I said, “East.”

Over his shoulder, the Skeleton King stared at me, eyes wide.
