# Chapter 39

“Ah.”

I blinked. It was hot and humid. My breath fogged my vision. When I felt around my head, my fingers touched something hard.

*The VR helmet.*

I pulled it off, revealing the dusty interior of the capsule.

I sat in the old capsule chair, its cushions worn flat, and tried to calm my pounding heart.

*Reality? Is this really reality?*

I had logged out after exactly thirty days. A sudden fear rose inside me.

Was this really the real world? When I pressed that button and the capsule opened, would I find that too much had changed? With my heart in my throat, I opened my mouth.

“Open Status Window.”

Silence.

No familiar notification. No System Window.

Only then did it finally sink in.

I had successfully logged out.

“Huuu.”

I took a deep breath and pressed the button. The capsule door opened with a click, and stale air greeted me.

“…Huh.”

A dark, cramped room. A small TV on the desk. A single bed. And someone asleep, reeking of alcohol.

“Khrrr. Khrrooo.”

Who would’ve thought those bizarre snores could sound so welcome?

*I’m back. Back in reality.*

Everything was exactly as I remembered it, as if the last thirty days had been a dream.

After staring blankly around the room for a while, I walked over to Jinho hyung. Then I slammed the edge of my hand into his throat.

Whack!

“Khrrr… gack!”

If it hadn’t been for this bastard’s snoring, I never would’ve crawled into that capsule. I grabbed Jinho hyung tight as he thrashed around.

“Take one more. No, two.”

Whack! Whack!

“Gack! Grrrgh!”

* * *

“You of all people should know better. And you’re supposed to be the goshiwon manager, no less.”

“…I’m sorry.”

“Let’s not give anyone a reason to be embarrassed again, all right?”

Bang.

The door closed. Jinho hyung turned to me with a sigh.

“Are you insane?”

Before he could say anything worse, I beat him to it.

“For the record, that was self-defense.”

“What the fuck are you talking about? Have you lost your mind?”

“I’m perfectly sane.”

“Then why the hell did you chop a sleeping man in the throat and—!”

Thump, thump, thump.

The man next door pounded on the wall. Judging by the rhythm and force, the message was clear: *I want to kill you.*

Jinho hyung lowered his voice.

“Why the hell are you making a scene? At this hour, too.”

“This hour?”

“Yeah, you lunatic. It’s only three in the morning.”

Jinho hyung held out his phone, looking incredulous.

July 25. 3:02 a.m.

I checked the date and time, and my mouth fell open.

*Only three hours have passed?*

I’d spent a month in Murim. Exactly thirty days. Yet only three hours had passed in reality.

“…Hyung.”

“Don’t talk to me. My throat hurts.”

“What’s the usual time ratio for a game capsule?”

“You’re like Hong Gil-dong, you bastard. Your conversation flashes east one second and west the next. It’s all over the place.”

“What’s the ratio?”

“Huh?”

My grim expression threw him for a moment, but he answered.

“The latest capsule came out last month, right? Five to one, I think.”

“What exactly does five to one mean?”

“What do you think? Five hours pass in the game for every hour in reality.”

This was driving me crazy.

“Is there anything higher?”

“No. That’s the limit of current technology… Why are you asking?”

Because something impossible had happened.

*How am I supposed to make sense of this?*

Even at a second glance, the capsule was ancient. That piece of junk, built more than twenty years ago, had left modern technology in the dust.

Thirty days in only three hours. What kind of time ratio was that? My head was too scrambled to calculate it.

“This is insane.”

“What is?”

There was no way I could understand it on my own. Maybe Jinho hyung could come up with some kind of answer. After a moment’s hesitation, I began.

“A month ago—no, three hours ago—I went into that capsule…”

I’d been through so much that there was plenty to tell. Even after hearing the whole story, Jinho hyung remained silent for a long time.

Then he said one thing.

“This is insane.”

“Exactly. How does any of this make sense? Isn’t that some kind of illegally modified capsule?”

“No. Not the capsule. You.”

“Huh?”

Jinho hyung gave me a grave look.

“Just apologize and be done with it. Say, ‘Sorry. Your snoring was so loud that I hit you.’ Own it like a man, you bastard.”

“…”

“You couldn’t log out, so you spent a month risking your life in a game? Go write a novel.”

*Right. I knew things were going too smoothly.*

I sighed.

“I’m telling you, it’s true.”

“Taekyung. Let’s think about this rationally.”

Jinho hyung adopted a serious tone.

“Some bastard chops a sleeping man in the throat to wake him up, then claims he was trapped in a game for a month. But when he checks the clock, only three hours have passed? And the capsule is an antique that belongs in a museum?”

“I know it sounds insane. I get it. But…”

“Then try to understand me too. My head hurts from the hangover, my throat hurts, and looking at you makes my heart hurt.”

“No, just listen to me!”

Thump, thump, thump, thump.

I lowered my voice before the man next door smashed through the wall.

“Then try it yourself.”

“What?”

“If you try it, you’ll believe me.”

Nothing worked faster than experiencing something firsthand. If I pulled him out after about ten minutes, he’d have no choice but to believe me.

Jinho hyung stared hard at me, then spoke.

“Fine. Let’s give it a try.”

He climbed into the capsule and even put on the VR helmet. As he complained about the sour smell inside it, I said,

“I’ll pull you out soon. Stay put at Honghwaru.”

“Honghwaru, my ass. Just close the capsule door.”

I wondered what his face would look like when I saw him again. I closed the door, took out my phone, and opened the stopwatch.

The instant I pressed start, the numbers began climbing.

*One second, two seconds… ten seconds.*

Ten seconds in reality had to be minutes, maybe even hours, inside the game.

*By now, he must be losing his mind at Honghwaru, trying to figure out what’s going on…*

Clunk.

“Huh?”

The door opened.

*What? Why is he out already? No, how did he get out?*

Jinho hyung sighed when he saw me standing there in confusion.

“What are you doing?”

“Uh… what?”

“The power isn’t even on. How am I supposed to play a game?”

What the hell was he talking about?

“The power isn’t on?”

“Move.”

As I stood there stammering, Jinho hyung climbed out, bent down beside the capsule, and picked something up.

“What does this look like to you?”

A cord.

An unplugged power cord.

Thinking I might be seeing things, I rubbed my eyes furiously. Nothing changed.

“Why is this…”

“Taekyung. Jin Taekyung. You poor, pitiful soul.”

Jinho hyung looked at me mournfully.

“Go to a mental hospital as soon as the sun comes up. I’m going back to my room.”

He dropped the power cord and walked away. I stared blankly after him.

It hadn’t even been plugged in.

*Then what the hell was the game I played?*

I felt as though a ghost had bewitched me. Goose bumps rose all over my body.

* * *

I lay on the bed and thought.

*Am I crazy?*

I had played a game for thirty days in a capsule that hadn’t even been plugged in.

I could understand Jinho hyung’s reaction. But everything that had happened there…

*It was all real.*

Jin Wikyung, Wolhwa, Hyuk Mujin, and the Head Elder. I remembered every NPC’s face, the way they spoke, and how they acted. It couldn’t have been a delusion I’d dreamed up on my own.

*Then what’s the problem?*

There was only one answer.

The problem was the game capsule built twenty-seven years ago—a piece of junk that should have been retired long ago and put in a museum.

Even its date of manufacture was suspicious.

January 1, 2020.

The day the Demon King Asmodeus fell at humanity’s hands.

*Who the hell was making game capsules back then?*

It was an era when hundreds of millions had died in the five-year Great War and monsters roamed city streets.

In my opinion, anyone who heard the breaking news that the Demon King had fallen and said, *All right, time to start making game capsules!* before firing up a factory belonged in court.

*Back then, I thought it was a misprint.*

I froze.

*A misprint?*

*The product manual!*

How could I have forgotten something so important? What an idiot.

I shot to my feet and started tearing the room apart. At last, I found the familiar little booklet under the bed.

> **Product User Manual**
>
> **Product Name:** Virtual Reality Access Device  
> **Model Name:** Ark - 2020  
> **Manufacturer:** H Soft  
> **Date of Manufacture:** January 1, 2020

Then the next page.

> **Precautions**
>
> - The player cannot log out at will.
>
> - If the player dies during play, resurrection is impossible.

A month ago—no, three hours ago—I had thrown the booklet aside at this point.

This time was different. With trembling hands, I turned to the final page.

> **Key Features**
>
> - A custom capsule made for one person only! Once a user is registered, the capsule is permanently bound to that user until death.
>
> - Time-ratio adjustment for comfortable play! Upon login, time in reality passes very slowly. The reverse is also true.
>
> - Character Synchronization System! By synchronizing with their character, the user experiences a greater sense of unity.

“What the hell is this?”

My eyes could read the words, but my brain refused to process them. I started again from the beginning.

*First, permanent binding.*

It meant exactly what it said. The capsule was mine until I died. Once I got some answers, I was going to smash that goddamn thing to pieces.

*Next, the time ratio.*

That was what I had been most curious about. But instead of an exact figure, it only gave the vague explanation that time *passes very slowly*.

It said the reverse was also true, so time in the game had to be passing now, however slowly.

*Then is the war still going on?*

Jin Wikyung came to mind. The Head Elder’s betrayal was already a foregone conclusion, and he would try to stab my brother in the back at a critical moment. Had Hyuk Mujin and the reconnaissance squad managed to warn him?

*Ah, that’s still a long way off. The time ratio has reversed.*

More importantly, I had immediate problems of my own. I examined the last key feature, but unlike the others, it still made no sense no matter how many times I read it.

*Synchronize with a character?*

Just in case, I even looked it up in a Korean dictionary. It meant exactly what I thought it did.

That only made it sound crazier.

*How the hell do you synchronize with a game character?*

I couldn’t make any sense of it. In the end, all I had done was add another question.

One thing was certain, though.

I wasn’t crazy.

I flopped onto the bed and read the front of the booklet again.

“Manufacturer: H Soft.”

In the end, every road led to the same place. If I investigated these bastards, something was bound to turn up.

I searched the internet for H Soft, but aside from a porn studio with the same name, I couldn’t find a thing.

“…”

First, I needed to lock the door and think.
