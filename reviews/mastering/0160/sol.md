# Chapter 160

“Was Grandfather right?”

“No. Not at all.”

There was a simple reason I was flatly denying the words of Mae Jonghak, the great martial artist known as the Sword Saint.

*Look at the Sword Saint trying to con me.*

*Nothing special, my ass.*

They said the view changed depending on where you stood. Beyond the wall of the Peak realm I had barely managed to cross, a rugged mountain range awaited me.

*I still have a long way to go.*

They said there was no end to learning. Martial arts were no different.

I was nothing more than a beginner who had barely gained enlightenment from a casual remark tossed out by the max-level Sword Saint.

Still…

“Thank you.”

When I bowed with those heartfelt words, Cheongpung panicked.

“Benefactor, why are you suddenly doing this?”

“Because I’m grateful. That’s all.”

There were as many martial artists as grains of sand on a beach, yet only a tiny fraction of them ever reached the Peak realm.

Even someone with superb martial arts and internal energy would spend their entire life stuck in place without the enlightenment to match.

*If not for Cheongpung, I would have wandered in circles for a long time.*

But I had been lucky. Cheongpung had devoted nearly ten days to helping me train, and he had even passed on the Sword Saint’s teachings.

That was far too generous a return for a few candied hawthorn skewers.[^1]

“Please don’t do this. I barely did anything.”

“Barely? I may be shameless, but I’m not an ungrateful bastard.”

“Huh? Really?”

“…”

Just what did this guy think of me?

When my eyes narrowed, Cheongpung hurriedly waved his hands.

“No, that’s not what you’re thinking.”

“What am I thinking?”

“I mean, it’s just…”

Cheongpung’s gaze darted around in search of an escape. Then he spotted Hyuk Mujin snoring in the corner of the training ground and shouted.

“Young Hero Hyuk! Wake up! This isn’t the time to be sleeping.”

“…”

This was genuinely pathetic.

Hyuk Mujin’s eyes flew open at Cheongpung’s shout. The moment he spotted me, he spoke as though nothing had happened.

“As expected. I believed in you.”

“…”

This bastard was taking it even further.

“I, Hyuk Mujin, stood guard with the single noble purpose of protecting my Captain.”

“…”

“Enlightenment is hard-won and easily broken. So I stood right here! Eyes wide open! Making sure not even a single rat could get through!”

*Forget the rat. You’re the fucking bastard here…*

I stared at the dried drool around his mouth and spoke quietly.

“Mujin.”

“Yes, sir!”

“I’ve become a Peak Master, you know?”

“Congratulations!”

“Yeah. And now my body is overflowing with strength. My fists are itching too.”

“…”

“Standing guard? Rats? If your snoring had been any louder, I might’ve suffered qi deviation, you bastard.”

“…You heard me?”

“Did you think I wouldn’t?”

He had been snoring so loudly that even my mother in Ilsan probably heard him.

As he scratched the back of his head and grinned sheepishly, I spread my fingers wide.

“This is your last chance. I’m counting to ten, so get out of my sight. One, two, three…”

*Dadadadada!*

Cheongpung watched Hyuk Mujin flee at incredible speed and exclaimed in admiration.

“Wow, training really does work! He’s incredibly fast!”

So that was how he interpreted it.

I turned to Cheongpung, who was clapping like a seal and looking immensely proud of himself.

“Then let’s start again.”

“Huh?”

“The duel. It isn’t over yet.”

“I’m fine with that, but… Aren’t you tired?”

“Not at all.”

It was a lie. My body, having entered a new realm, was overflowing with vitality, but my mind was utterly exhausted. It felt like the strength had gone out of me after an all-out sprint.

But my desire to continue sparring with Cheongpung was stronger.

*I want to test it.*

Gaining enlightenment was worlds apart from raising my stats with points.

Right now, I felt as though I could unleash martial arts that were entirely new—not merely faster or stronger.

“I’m perfectly fine.”

I said it again, but Cheongpung shook his head.

“My grandfather said rest is also training.”

“Not even one round?”

“No. Benefactor, you need to rest now. And…”

“And?”

Cheongpung giggled before continuing.

“I’m going to win anyway. So what’s the point?”

“…!”

“You know that now too, don’t you?”

“That… is true.”

I didn’t want to admit it, but it was a fact.

You could only see as much as you knew. After reaching the Peak realm, I realized all over again just how much of a monster Cheongpung was.

I also realized that, as I was now, I could never defeat him if he fought at full strength.

*Unless he went easy on me.*

But that was not what I wanted. Winning a duel against an opponent who was not giving his all would only leave a bitter taste.

“Don’t be impatient. For the time being, you’ll have your hands full just absorbing the enlightenment you gained today.”

“Then…”

“Let’s postpone our duel until next time. It’s a shame. I was having fun too.”

*Ding.*

> **System**
>
> - Quest *Sword Saint Training: A Secondhand Experience—2* has been canceled.
>
> - Because the Quest proposer made this decision voluntarily, no penalty will be imposed.

“Get plenty of rest today!”

I watched Cheongpung walk away for a long time before returning to the pavilion.

Hearing the System notification had reminded me of everything I had put off.

*Check messages.*

*Ding. Ding. Ding.*

Endless System notifications rang out as all the messages I had yet to check unfolded before my eyes.

My mouth fell open as I stared at the translucent System windows.

*How many of these are there?*

It looked like rest would have to wait.

* * *

Hot water sloshed inside the large wooden bathtub.

I had gone several days without washing properly and desperately needed a bath. The moment I sank into the steaming water, a moan escaped me.

“Ahhh. That’s the stuff.”

I finally felt alive again. After closing my eyes and savoring the sensation for a while, I opened the System window.

“Check messages.”

*Ding. Ding. Ding.*

System messages poured out like water through a broken dam. I read them one by one.

> **System**
>
> - You have successfully completed Quest *Beyond the Wall*!
>
> - Your class has changed to **Peak Master**!
>
> - You have acquired a massive amount of EXP!
>
> - Level up!
>
> - Level up!
>
> - Level up!
>
> - Level up!
>
> - Level up!

*Oh. I gained five Levels at once?*

Some people might have thought that was stingy, but I certainly didn’t.

The Murim was another reality, but here, I was practically a game character.

Back when I was a beginner, killing a single bandit could raise my Level. Things were different now. The amount of EXP required kept increasing, and my leveling speed had been slowing down accordingly.

*This is more than enough.*

But the rewards did not end there.

> **System**
>
> - The realm of *Jin Family’s Cultivation Technique* has risen to the ninth stage.
>
> - You can now acquire more internal energy with greater stability.
>
> - The realm of *Qi Sense* has risen to the seventh stage.
>
> - You can detect targets at Level 90 or below within 70 meters.
>
> - You have acquired EXP due to the dramatic advancement of your martial arts.
>
> - Level up!
>
> - Level up!

I was already satisfied that Jin Family’s Cultivation Technique and Qi Sense, both of which had stagnated for so long, had advanced. Gaining two more Levels on top of that was an unexpected windfall.

The messages I had read so far already amounted to seven Level-ups.

*Now that’s a proper reward.*

I smiled contentedly and continued reading.

> **System**
>
> - A Peak Master is a powerhouse capable of leading an entire school. You can create your own martial art and establish a new sect.
>
> - You have gained insight into methods of manipulating internal energy that you did not previously understand. Through training, you can use the new Skill *Sound Transmission*.

Creating my own martial art and founding a sect.

The System was certainly giving me the full Peak Master treatment. It was worlds apart from how I had been treated as a first-rate martial artist.

*Well, I am a Peak Master now.*

Thousands of martial artists crowded Shanxi, yet there were fewer than twenty Peak masters among them.

I didn’t know how things were in the Central Plains, but at least in Shanxi Province, that was what it meant to be a Peak master.

*But none of that is very useful to me.*

I didn’t know about creating my own martial art yet, but I certainly had no intention of founding a new sect. Why would I go anywhere when I had the dependable Jin Family of Taiyuan?

More importantly…

*Sound Transmission is good. Very good.*

Sound Transmission. A technique for secretly transmitting sound through internal energy.

I couldn’t think of any immediate use for the first two, but Sound Transmission had considerable practical value.

It would require training, but that was no problem.

*Only three left now?*

The countless message windows had all disappeared, leaving exactly three.

With equal parts relief and regret, I checked the remaining messages.

*Ding.*

> **System**
>
> - *Ten-Thousand-Year Cold Iron* has been added to your Inventory.
>
> - A martial artist and their weapon are one. Finding a weapon capable of drawing out the full extent of one’s strength is also a martial artist’s duty.
>
> - Quest *Find the Craftsman* has been created.

“…Huh?”

* * *

Northern Gaoyuan.

This land had once been home to the nomadic empire founded by a great ruler. But it had changed with the passage of time.

The vast pastures covered in green grass were gradually disappearing, and wooden buildings from the Central Plains were beginning to replace the gers that served as the nomads’ homes.[^2]

The nomads’ customs still survived, but the clothing and culture of the Central Plains were slowly infiltrating the plateau.

The man draped in sheepskin did not like it.

“Han Chinese bastards swarming the sacred grasslands.”

“Temur, let it go. This is nothing new.”

“Chinggen. We are descendants of the khans. We must never forget that.”

“I haven’t forgotten. I’m telling you not to forget what kind of gathering this is.”

“Damn it. An inn instead of a perfectly good ger.”

Temur and Chinggen, two chieftains who each commanded a hundred tribespeople, entered the inn.

It had been built by Han Chinese who called themselves mounted bandits. It was the only inn in Northern Gaoyuan, and correspondingly enormous.

The moment the two chieftains stepped inside, they saw mounted bandits packed throughout the building all the way to the upper floor. At the center of the crowd sat two men.

“Ha-ha-ha! The chieftains of the Great Steppe have arrived! Come, sit. I’ve warmed some mare’s-milk wine for you.”[^3]

Unlike the middle-aged man who welcomed them with both arms spread wide, the other man did not so much as nod. He merely gestured.

“Sit.”

“…!”

“…!”

Veins bulged on Temur and Chinggen’s foreheads.

Who were they? They were descendants of the Great Khan who had once crossed the steppe and ruled the Central Plains.

That had been centuries ago, but their pride had never died.

“You Han Chinese bastard, lower than a dog!”

Temur was hot-tempered and reckless by nature. Before Chinggen could stop him, his hand seized his curved saber.

“I’ll offer your head to the god Tengger—”[^4]

And that was when it happened.

“I said sit.”

The voice was cold, and the eyes were like an abyss. Even Temur, who led a tribe across the harsh steppe, found himself unable to draw his saber.

Seizing the moment, Chinggen hurriedly grabbed Temur by the shoulder and whispered so quietly that he could barely be heard.

“Temur, don’t act rashly. You know who he is, don’t you?”

Temur swallowed.

Four people were to meet here today. They were all strangers to one another, but each was a powerful figure who represented one of the major forces of Northern Gaoyuan.

Yet only one of them could be a master capable of intimidating him so thoroughly.

*The Human Butcher.*

No one knew his surname. No one knew his name.

No one knew where he came from or what he had done.

He was Han Chinese, and he roamed the steppe with only fifty subordinates. He had earned the epithet Human Butcher by slaughtering people as though they were livestock.

In other words, he was a butcher of men.

“You’re a slow one, friend.”

[^1]: Candied hawthorn skewers are traditional fruit skewers coated in hardened sugar.

[^2]: A *ger* is a traditional round felt dwelling used by nomadic peoples of the Central Asian steppe.

[^3]: Mare’s-milk wine is a traditional alcoholic drink made by fermenting mare’s milk.

[^4]: Tengger is a sky deity in traditional steppe belief.
