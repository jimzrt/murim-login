# Chapter 20

The hour-long negotiation was finally drawing to a close.

“Half the shops owned by the Mount Heng Sword Sect, and exclusive rights to the pleasure district. Is that correct?”

“Correct. And your sect?”

“From this moment onward, we will sell no information to any sect in Shanxi other than the Jin Family of Taiyuan. We will also devote all our resources to gathering and delivering information about the war.”

They had already settled on the terms after dozens of rounds of back-and-forth.

Jin Wikyung and Wolhwa carefully reviewed the agreement one last time for any loopholes, then drew up and exchanged the documents.

“We’re officially in the same boat now. I look forward to working with you, Lesser Family Head.”

“Likewise.”

The moment they shook hands—

Ding.

> **System**
>
> - The **Jin Family of Taiyuan** and the **Lower District Sect** have formed an alliance.
>
> - The alliance will remain in effect for the duration of the war.

Even this got a notification. Then again, something similar had appeared when the war with the Mount Heng Sword Sect began.

*With the Lower District Sect joining us, have our odds of winning gone up a little?*

The Lower District Sect specialized in information.

Wolhwa had confidently claimed that all information in Shanxi passed through her hands. If that was true, their help would be invaluable.

*Nothing is more important than information.*

I didn’t know how things worked in Murim, but I knew that much. Depending on how it was used, information could be a sword, a shield, or even a bomb capable of turning the tide of a war in a single stroke.

Hopefully, Wolhwa would bring us information like that.

“Then I’ll take my leave.”

“I hope you’ll understand that I cannot see you out far with so many eyes upon us.”

“Of course.”

*It’s finally over.*

Wipeng bowed with his usual impassive expression, and I gave an awkward half-bow of my own.

“Goodbye.”

Wolhwa’s eyes curved into crescents as she looked at me.

“Young Master Jin is coming with me.”

“Huh?”

“Aren’t you going to see me off?”

“…Why would I?”

“Because we spent a hot night together?”

The fastball she drilled straight into my ribs left me momentarily stunned. Seriously, I’d told this woman to bring us information, so why was she starting by spreading that kind of adults-only information?

In front of everyone, no less.

“Oh?”

“A—a hot night? Together?”

Wipeng looked back and forth between Wolhwa and me with interest, while an earthquake shook Jin Wikyung’s pupils.

“Taekyung, is that true? Is it really true?”

After a long silence, I answered.

“I’ll see her out.”

I grabbed Wolhwa by the wrist and dashed from the room. Behind me, Jin Wikyung’s mournful voice echoed through the hall.

“My baby brotherrr!”

* * *

Wolhwa was a rare beauty. Between her slender figure and her extravagant clothes, she couldn’t help but draw attention.

“Did our family have a beauty like that?”

“Of course not. She’s an outsider.”

“An outsider?”

“Yeah. A friend among the gate guards said she’s a courtesan from Honghwaru.”

“Ah, that ridiculously expensive Honghwaru. I know the place. Wait, why would a courtesan come here now?”

“They say she came to collect the Third Young Master’s unpaid tab. She probably wants her money before the war turns into a full-scale conflict.”

“…Huh. The Third Young Master really does get up to everything, doesn’t he?”

Their whispers wormed their way into my ears. Wolhwa had been walking ahead of me, humming to herself, but she suddenly turned around.

“Young Master Jin, why are you so far behind?”

I answered sullenly.

“If we walk together, people will get the wrong idea. There are already ridiculous rumors going around…”

“Oh, the unpaid tab?”

Yeah. That.

“That rumor isn’t actually false.”

Wolhwa smiled brightly.

“When the gate guards asked why I was here, I told them I’d come to collect an unpaid tab. It’s not a complete lie.”

“What?”

“Were you planning to announce our alliance to the whole world?”

“…No.”

But why did she have to use my name? My image was already a complete disaster.

If a rumor like this spread when a war had just broken out, there would be no denying it. My reputation as a piece of trash would be set in stone.

“Considering you’ve already been falsely accused of poisoning someone, being known as a kept man isn’t so bad. Given your usual behavior, everyone will find it believable.”

That was strangely persuasive. I nodded without thinking, then noticed something odd about what she had said.

“Falsely accused?”

Come to think of it, she hadn’t mentioned Lee Seogeun even once. She had negotiated like a fastidious businesswoman, then formed an alliance as though it were the most natural thing in the world.

*What exactly is she relying on?*

Wolhwa smiled at my suspicious look.

“Young Master Jin, have you forgotten who I am?”

“The Lower District Sect’s Shanxi Branch Leader…”

My eyes flew open.

Information. She had information.

Information proving that I wasn’t the culprit!

I hurried to Wolhwa’s side.

“Can you clear my name? Do you really have information like that?”

Lee Seogeun’s poisoning had started this war. If we could prove that someone else had orchestrated everything, we could stop it.

But Wolhwa’s next words shattered my hopes.

“No. I don’t.”

What?

“Then why did you form an alliance with us?”

“Because I’m a merchant.”

Wolhwa continued in a light, cheerful tone.

“We of the Lower District Sect are merchants at heart. We act strictly according to profit and loss.”

“Then this makes even less sense.”

“Oh? Why?”

“Because the Mount Heng Sword Sect is stronger than us. If you’re weighing profit and loss, shouldn’t you side with them?”

“You’re honest. Or should I say naïve?”

Wolhwa gave a quiet laugh.

“This is an investment. I placed both sides on a scale and analyzed them objectively.”

“So as long as you profit, nothing else matters? Even if I really did poison Lee Seogeun?”

“Young Master Jin, what do you think it means that the Lower District Sect’s Shanxi branch devoted all its resources to investigating the matter, yet still couldn’t uncover the truth?”

I hesitated, but Wolhwa didn’t wait for an answer.

“It’s simple. Either Young Master Jin is truly innocent, or you concealed the truth so well that even we couldn’t uncover it.”

“Ah.”

“Either way, I don’t lose. If it’s the former, then your side has legitimacy, which suits us just fine. If it’s the latter, then it proves the Jin Family of Taiyuan is exceptionally capable.”

I was more than a little surprised. So that was another way of looking at it.

A ruthless calculation of profit and loss. Now I understood exactly what Wolhwa meant when she called her people merchants.

Knowing someone like her was on our side was reassuring.

But…

“You’re overlooking one important thing. If I’m innocent, then who actually poisoned Lee Seogeun?”

That was the heart of this war. The biggest missing piece of the puzzle, and one even Wolhwa had failed to find.

If the Lower District Sect had discovered the culprit’s identity, she would have brought that information today.

Wolhwa sighed.

“I’m ashamed to say we haven’t uncovered anything yet. But our sect is doing everything it can. Ah, we’re almost there.”

I still had plenty of questions, but I had no choice but to fall silent when I noticed the gate guards nearby.

*Our alliance with the Lower District Sect is still a secret.*

A four-horse carriage—apparently the one Wolhwa had arrived in—came into view, with gate guards surrounding it in a wide circle. Even from here, I could feel the strange tension in the air.

*Did something happen?*

I channeled internal energy into my eyes and ears. My enhanced senses soon picked up the gate guards’ tense voices.

“He’s a master. No doubt about it. At least Super First Rate, perhaps even a Peak master. See the whip at his waist? He must wield it like a demon.”

“A master of that caliber volunteering to be a coachman? A peerless beauty really is something else.”

The martial artists speaking in such grave tones appeared fairly experienced. A younger martial artist cautiously murmured,

“But doesn’t his posture have too many openings for someone like that? He’s thin, too…”

The senior martial artists clicked their tongues.

“Hey, my friend and I have spent ten years guarding this gate. We can tell just by looking into someone’s eyes. What would a little shit like you know, butting into your elders’ conversation?”

“Tsk. Openings? Those look like openings to you? Can’t you see the ease and naturalness that only true masters possess?”

“Then what about his temples? Masters with profound internal energy have bulging temples.”

“That’s because he’s achieved Returning to the Origin…”

Unfortunately, the conversation ended there. The gate guards noticed us standing in front of the main gate and scattered.

Thanks to them, I learned the identity of the Peak master who wielded a whip like a demon—the romantic martial artist who had volunteered to be a coachman after falling for Wolhwa’s beauty.

“Young Master, do you remember me?”

“…”

As if I could forget.

That deep voice. The sharp gaze that carried the unmistakable air of a master.

He was the coachman who had brought me to the Jin Family of Taiyuan a few days ago.

I never expected to see his face here. As I stood there speechless, Wolhwa spoke up.

“I hear you two are acquainted. Last time, you crossed the boundary between life and death together and forged a deep friendship that transcended age and status…”

*No. That isn’t what happened. Please stop.*

I tugged at Wolhwa’s sleeve, but it was already too late. The coachman smiled gently and opened his mouth.

“Even now, whenever I close my eyes, the memories of that day remain vivid. The Heavenly Axe… He was a truly formidable bastard.”

The gate guards, who had been listening intently, exclaimed in amazement.

“Oh!”

“The Heavenly Axe… Surely he means that Heavenly Axe of the Eighteen Strongholds of Green Forest?”

“Wasn’t he an infamous Peak master of Green Forest? To kill a man like that… As expected, this gentleman must be…”

“…”

They seemed to have gotten something terribly wrong.

I didn’t even know where to begin correcting them—or where to stop.

As I stood there blankly, the coachman grabbed me in a hug.

“If it hadn’t been for you, Young Master, I would have been in serious trouble.”

This man certainly had a strange way of putting things. Without me, he would have been drinking a cup of makgeolli atop Mount Beimang by now.[^1]

“Please let go of me first, and then we can talk…”

I was just about to pry him off when someone muttered,

“Yama Whip. The master of the whip arts who vanished without a trace more than ten years ago. It’s him. It must be.”

A ripple passed through the gate guards.

“Yama Whip? You mean the Peak master who roamed the realm, hunting down remnants of the Demonic path?”

“I’ve heard that name, too. A master who stood between the orthodox and unorthodox paths, with neither his sect nor his past known… Come to think of it, didn’t his trail vanish somewhere near Shanxi?”

“Then the Third Young Master—no, our Young Master—is acquainted with Great Hero Yama Whip.”

“Not only that. He must have played a major role in defeating the Heavenly Axe.”

“Oh! Ohhh!”

Fervent gazes poured in from every direction. The coachman must have sensed that something was wrong, because he tried to pull away, but my hand clamped firmly around his shoulder.

“Young Master?”

I gave him the brightest smile in the world.

“To think we would meet again like this, Great. Hero. Yama. Whip!”

My words were like oil thrown onto a fire.

“Woooah!”

The excited gate guards stomped their feet while Wolhwa doubled over, desperately trying to hold back her laughter.

Ding.

> **System**
>
> - Rumors about the **Poisoner** are dying down!
>
> - Rumors about the **Sleeping Dragon of Shanxi** are gaining credibility!
>
> - **Fame** increases by 20!
>
> - Passionate supporters have appeared!

As the beautiful System notifications rang out, a famous saying came to mind.

*The perfect lie… is a true story.*

* * *

The young man stared at the ceiling with his eyes wide open. The light had faded from his once-bright black eyes, and his face was twisted with fear and pain.

“Seogeun. My son.”

A large, rough hand caressed the young man’s face. Fierce poisonous energy seeped through the man’s skin, only to be stopped by the internal energy that instinctively surged within him.

“How did this happen to you?”

The middle-aged man mourned.

Orphaned and alone, he had spent decades in Murim.

He had met countless people and watched just as many leave. From his days as an inexperienced twenty-year-old wandering martial artist to the day he became the master of a sect, he had accumulated more memories than he could count.

“Did it hurt? Were you so wronged that you couldn’t even close your eyes?”

He gazed down at his son’s wide-open eyes. Every tiny blood vessel had burst, staining them red.

His son had barely reached twenty. Bloody tears flowed from the eyes of the father standing before his poisoned child.

“My son.”

The poisonous energy that had entered through his hand was spreading throughout his body. Within only a few breaths, his head began to spin and his limbs went numb.

Such a deadly poison.

He could vividly picture his son’s final moments—his whole body stiffening until he couldn’t even struggle.

“I will remember this pain.”

The next moment, immense internal energy surged like wildfire and drove back the poisonous energy. The poison consuming his insides vanished in an instant, as though torn apart by a pack of hundreds of wolves.

“I will repay them a hundredfold. A thousandfold.”

Blood Wolf Sword Lee Cheonbaek, Sect Leader of the Mount Heng Sword Sect, turned his back on his son’s corpse and strode away.

When he opened the pavilion door, he saw the black night sky and the flickering torches beneath it.

Around two hundred fully armed men stood outside. The man at their head bowed.

“Father.”

Lee Cheonbaek nodded to his eldest son.

“Go. Tonight… our revenge begins.”

That night, around two hundred martial artists departed the Mount Heng Sword Sect.

[^1]: A Korean image for being dead; Mount Beimang is the legendary mountain of the afterlife.
