# Chapter 622

Even counting only the time I had spent in Murim, well over a year had passed.

One unexpected incident after another had taken me out of Shanxi Province and sent me wandering from place to place. Along the way, I had met all kinds of people and visited the sects they belonged to.

But I could say this much for certain: none of those sects, however large or powerful, could compare to the sight before me.

“Huh.”

“Holy…”

Exclamations broke out all around me.

I’d known the place covered an enormous area when I saw it from the mountain peak. I hadn’t expected *this*.

As the clouds and mist cleared and we drew closer, my jaw dropped farther and farther.

*Wait. This is a sect?*

It was astonishing for two reasons. The first was its sheer size. The second was…

**Nanman Beast Palace**

A fence around the vast grounds, and a wooden sign someone had scrawled on in a clumsy hand.

Not a high, sturdy wall or an iron gate. A fence. Just a fence.

*…Wait. This is a sect?*

Same thought, different reason.

The fence wasn’t even intact.

I studied a half-collapsed stretch for a moment, then turned to Namho.

“Elder Namho, surely this isn’t really…”

“You’re asking if it’s the Nanman Beast Palace?”

“Ah, yes.”

I nodded, and Namho answered calmly.

“Can you read?”

“Of course.”

“Then read that sign. No, perhaps I’d better read it for you.”

He pointed at the clumsily lettered board.

“Nan. Man. Beast. Palace.”

“…”

“That’s what it says. The Nanman Beast Palace begins here.”

I knew what the sign said. The problem was how far the place was from anything I’d imagined.

From above, I’d thought there must be an enormous building here. But up close, out of the mist, it was practically an empty field.

“Captain, shouldn’t we call this the Nanman Ranch instead of the Nanman Beast Palace? All I see are fences.”

Ju Hwaran looked around and nodded at Hyuk Mujin’s dubious assessment.

“This is my first time here, so I can’t say I know much about the Nanman Beast Palace, but… it does seem a little lacking for a palace.”

Song Ilseom and Sama Pyo, who had been watching in silence, each weighed in.

“There’s certainly something strange about it.”

“As Hyuk Mujin said, the lack of people bothers me. I don’t see any other pavilions or buildings, either. What do you think, Taishan?”

“My lord. Taishan is hungry. Can Taishan eat that mushroom?”

Sama Pyo shook his head at once.

“No.”

“Then the cow? That cow walking over there looks tasty.”

“You’re the Young Sect Leader of the Black Dragon Demon Gate, aren’t you? Do me a favor and shut that brute’s mouth. If I hear one more word, I think I’ll burst a blood vessel and die.”

“…I understand.”

“One more thing. Every blade of grass and every animal you can see belongs to the Nanman Beast Palace. Please remember that. And remember I’m about to burst a blood vessel.”

Looking considerably older than he had a moment ago, Namho gave Sama Pyo that stern warning, then pointed across the vast grounds.

“I understand why you’re confused, but I never said this place *was* the Nanman Beast Palace.”

“What? You said exactly that a moment ago.”

“No, I didn’t.”

“Ah. Have you gone senile?”

“…”

His expression darkened so quickly that it seemed certain. I gave him a sympathetic look.

“Oh dear. Still, I’m sure you’ll be all right. Someone close to me suffered from the infirmities of old age too, and he’s almost completely recovered.”

“Three things. First, I do not suffer from the infirmities of old age. Second—and I ask only because you’ve made me curious—how did this acquaintance of yours recover?”

“He Returned to Youth.”

“…An excellent method. Apart from the fact that I’m an eighty-year-old man who’s never learned martial arts.”

“Isn’t eighty when life begins?”

“I’d like to end *your* life here, but I lack the strength.”

Namho swallowed his anger and went on.

“Third, what I said before still stands. I never said this place *was* the Nanman Beast Palace. I said the Nanman Beast Palace begins *here*.”

“Oh.”

Similar words, very different meanings. Now that I understood, I asked, “So what is this place?”

“One of the lands under the Nanman Beast Palace’s rule. Unlike the rest of Nanman, it’s governed directly by the Palace Lord. Does that make more sense?”

“Like the Emperor appointing vassal kings and City Lords throughout the realm. This place is effectively the capital.”

“Exactly. Of course, the Palace Lord doesn’t have the authority of the Son of Heaven, and Nanman’s tribal chiefs have more authority than City Lords.”

Ju Hwaran, who had been listening, gave a quiet exclamation.

“Now that you mention it, I remember reading something like that in my grandfather’s records. He wrote that Nanman was a single kingdom, and the Nanman Beast Palace was like an enormous ranch spanning five counties.”

“As expected of the Escort King’s granddaughter. But even Great Hero Ju Gongsan never crossed this fence. People who aren’t of Nanman’s ethnic groups aren’t allowed inside.”

“It’s that strict?”

“It is.”

If Namho was telling the truth, even the Sichuan Tang Clan—a sect as closed to outsiders as any in the world—had to yield to the Nanman Beast Palace.

I shook my head and looked past the seemingly endless fence and pasture.

“But we need to get in.”

“Yes.”

“Is there another way? Since you’re Miao, perhaps you can enter…”

“I said outsiders aren’t allowed in. I don’t recall saying it was easy for the local peoples, either.”

“Oh.”

“Here, I’m nothing more than an ordinary, powerless old Miao man. If Dark Heaven hadn’t reached its claws across the world, and you hadn’t come looking for me, I would have quietly lived out my days… You son of a bitch!”

I flinched at his sudden shout.

“Elder Namho, why are you calling yourself that? What’s wrong with quietly living out your days?”

“Not me! That bastard! Catch that fucking son of a bitch!”

At Namho’s frantic yell, we turned.

A huge backside was crawling over the fence. Beyond it, a yellow calf grazed peacefully.

“Oh. Oh, no.”

“Surely he isn’t…”

“When did that bastard get over there?”

“Young Hero Tae!”

“Taishan!”

Sama Pyo’s cry came last. Taishan flinched.

*Moo?*

The calf sensed something was wrong and jerked its head up. Taishan was already close. An exclamation mark seemed to appear in its large, clear eyes as its not-yet-fully-grown hooves dug into the grass.

*Pat-pat-pat!*

A quick decision and an attempt to flee—but Taishan was a step faster.

“Beef! Stop right there!”

With that determined shout, his enormous body flew through the air.

The calf was no tiger or leopard. How was it supposed to evade a Peak master who’d decided to catch it?

Taishan landed with a heavy *thud*, and the calf pinned beneath him screamed.

*Moooooo!*

It was a plaintive cry, enough to bring tears to your eyes just hearing it. But after days of forced marches and meager rations, all it did was make Taishan’s mouth water.

“Taishan! Beef!”

“Get that bastard off it!”

Before Namho finished shouting, I’d vaulted the fence and reached Taishan.

I grabbed him.

“Let go! Beef tartare!”

“Stop it, you heartless lunatic. Haven’t you seen *The Sound of the Bell*?”[^1]

“No!”

“…Ah.”

Well, of course he hadn’t.

That made sense, but it changed nothing. I seized him by the back of the neck and hauled him away.

His mountain-sized body toppled backward. The beef—no, the calf—scrambled to its feet and ran.

*Thud-thud-thud-thud!*

Grass flew up beneath its hooves, dust rising in its wake. Taishan watched its rump disappear beyond the pasture and muttered mournfully.

“No. Taishan’s rump meat…”

*Was this guy born in Majang-dong or something?*[^2]

While I questioned his origins, Namho erupted.

“You brute!”

“Elder Namho, calm down. Please.”

“How am I supposed to calm down? I warned you again and again not to touch anything on the Nanman Beast Palace’s land! That great hulking glutton ignored every word I said and went after a calf?”

Sama Pyo hurried between Namho and Taishan.

“I’m sorry, Elder Namho. I looked away for just a moment, and… Taishan, apologize to him at once.”

Taishan looked Namho straight in the eye.

“Taishan is hungry.”

“That fucking bastard. Let go of my hand. Let go! Are you going to let go or not? Did you think I spent over fifty years getting bitten by mosquitoes in Nanman just to see this?”

Life really did begin at eighty. Namho had never learned martial arts, yet the aura he was giving off might have made even Jeok Cheongang back down for once.

The old Hidden Shadow Pavilion agent raged like an active volcano for another fifteen minutes before finally calming down.

“Whew. I’m fine now. Let go of my hand.”

“Are you sure?”

“My insides have turned a full circle, but I’m not about to die.”

A 180-degree turn would have been a problem. A full 360? That was fine.

I nodded to Hyuk Mujin, and he released Namho’s hand. Namho sighed, looking as though he were on his deathbed.

“At least nothing happened in the end. If someone from the Nanman Beast Palace had seen that, we’d have been in trouble before we even began.”

At another sect in the Central Plains, someone might have thought, *It’s only one cow.* But cultures and people differed. Just as nomads treasured their horses like their own lives, livestock was a valuable asset here, where the boundaries between tribes were sharply drawn and relations between them often strained.

*It’s called the Nanman Beast Palace, after all.*

Even the name sounded animal-friendly. Namho had warned us from the start not to touch anything for a reason.

“So what do we do now?” Hyuk Mujin asked. “We can’t wait here forever for someone to show up.”

“Entering the Nanman Beast Palace’s territory without permission would cause trouble. We’ll have to find another way.”

Namho reached into his bundle and took something out.

“What’s that?”

“An emergency signal firework. If it goes off, someone should at least come out to investigate.”

He smiled confidently and lit the fuse with his fire striker.

*Crackle.*

The fuse burned rapidly toward the end. Then, with a tremendous noise, the firework shot upward.

*Fwoooosh—boom!*

The problem was that its sparks fell onto the pasture instead of scattering harmlessly in the air.

“…Huh?”

“…Huh?”

*Whoooosh—roar!*

The pasture went up in flames. Around me, everyone stood stunned.

I watched the livestock flee in every direction and muttered, “…Someone will definitely come out now.”

“…That wasn’t supposed to happen.”

Wasn’t supposed to happen, my ass. We were fucked.

I stared miserably at the burning pasture.

*Roooar!*

A savage roar rang out, and a figure appeared beyond the hill on the far side of the flames.

[^1]: *The Sound of the Bell* is a Korean documentary film about an elderly farmer and his ox.

[^2]: Majang-dong is a Seoul district known for its livestock and meat markets.
