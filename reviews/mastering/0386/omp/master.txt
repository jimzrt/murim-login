# Chapter 386

“I’m too late. Have you all been waiting long?”

Lee Jungryong swept his gaze over the assembled group. Some faces were familiar; others he was seeing for the first time.

Magic Johnson and Pai Chen, whom he had encountered several times during the Great Cataclysm. The young British prince. And China’s problem child.

Whenever his eyes met theirs, the S-rank Hunters gathered in the underground bunker greeted him first.

“It’s been a while, Lee. You’ve become even more attractive since we last met. Every time I see you, I feel like confessing my love.”

“Please restrain yourself, Magic Johnson. You’re not my type. Though I might make an exception for Pai Chen over there.”

At Lee Jungryong’s nonchalant reply, Pai Chen swept back her long hair.

“Oh my. I didn’t realize Mr. Lee felt that way about me. What a shame. I found out too late.”

“What’s there to regret? You’re still in your prime.”

“Thank you for saying that, but if the youngsters heard us, they’d laugh. They’d wonder what a pair of old people like us thought we were doing.”

“Is that really how it is?”

When Lee Jungryong looked at him, Wu Heixing hurriedly waved his hands.

“O-Of course not, Mr. Lee. How could it possibly be?”

“I’ve heard a lot about you. You’re remarkably talented and, hmm… a young man who’s honest about everything, I’m told.”

“T-Thank you.”

Wu Heixing bowed, looking deeply honored.

Normally, his hatred of Korea ran so deep that he constantly spat out the word *bangzi*, an insult for Koreans. But he lacked the nerve to show that side of himself in front of Lee Jungryong.

A bead of cold sweat rolled down the back of Wu Heixing’s neck.

“I’d heard you were coming, but I never thought you actually would.”

“Of course I had to come. When your neighbor is in trouble, shouldn’t you help?”

“It’s an honor to meet you, Mr. Lee.”

Even Magic Johnson and Pai Chen, heroes of the Great Cataclysm, could not compare to Lee Jungryong.

He was the sworn brother of Cheon Taemin, hailed as the Jesus of the twenty-first century and the savior of the world. He was also the true leader of the Ares Guild.

One of the most powerful Guilds in the world. One of the three strongest S-rank Hunters alive.

Lee Jungryong.

Anyone who stood before him owed him the proper respect.

Not even the arrogant Wu Heixing or a prince of noble blood was an exception.

“Pleased to meet you. A pleasure to meet you, Jungryong Lee.”

“Ah. So you’re that prince.”

“How dare—”

Prince Felix raised a hand to silence his secretary, then inclined his head with regal dignity.

“Felix. You may call me Felix. You may.”

The Korean produced by the translation device was awkward, and his level of formality was strangely ambiguous.

But with that, Felix Alexander Louis—third in line to the British throne—made the difference between Lee Jungryong and everyone else perfectly clear. He had actually given Lee Jungryong permission to omit the title *Your Royal Highness*.

Of course, Lee Jungryong did not care in the slightest.

*They’re all just brats.*

The only ones Lee Jungryong acknowledged were Magic Johnson and Pai Chen.

Whatever their personalities, Wu Heixing and Prince Felix were both hailed as geniuses by the public. But in Lee Jungryong’s eyes, they were nothing more than chicks that had only just learned to walk.

And yet…

The people who truly irritated Lee Jungryong were elsewhere.

*The Peace Guild.*

Lee Jungryong’s gaze shifted to one side. A deep smile formed on his lips as he looked at the two men standing there in silence.

“I’ve been seeing you two quite often lately. Have you both been well?”

At Lee Jungryong’s greeting, Jin Taekyung and Choi Minwoo exchanged glances and shrugged.

“Not really.”

“Not particularly.”

“…?”

What?

Lee Jungryong faltered without realizing it.

It had been less than a month since he had last seen them. Yet the way the two men treated him had changed drastically.

*Hostility.*

That was it.

He no longer felt the obvious hostility and wariness they had shown him before.

As Lee Jungryong studied their utterly calm faces, he soon realized why.

*They’ve grown stronger. Far stronger than before.*

There was no doubt about it. Choi Minwoo’s qi was far greater and more refined than before—so much so that Lee Jungryong wondered how he could have grown this much in such a short time.

And the other man, Jin Taekyung…

*…What is this?*

He could feel it through his skin and his sixth sense. The tremendous qi contained within Jin Taekyung’s entire body. The deep stillness in his eyes.

It could mean only one thing.

*He crossed the wall.*

The realization struck Lee Jungryong like a blow to the back of the head. He had to struggle with all his might not to show his agitation.

*How?*

Jin Taekyung had already been powerful. Perhaps he had even stood above the countless A-rank Hunters across the world.

After all, he had defeated two named monsters alone. It was only natural for the public and the media to proclaim the birth of a new S-rank Hunter.

But Lee Jungryong had known the truth.

Jin Taekyung had not yet crossed the wall.

It should have taken him a very long time to overcome the wall blocking his path and become a true powerhouse.

And yet…

*How could something this absurd happen?*

Mere surprise could not begin to describe it.

As Lee Jungryong met Jin Taekyung’s impassive gaze, he remembered emotions he had long forgotten.

Anxiety. Impatience.

It was the first time Lee Jungryong had felt such unease since taking complete control of the Ares Guild.

Only a few months ago, Jin Taekyung had been nothing more than an amusing nuisance.

Yet now, that same man had awakened anxiety and impatience within him.

*You…*

The smile on Lee Jungryong’s lips vanished without a trace. Wei Penghu regarded his stiff expression with puzzlement.

“Mr. Lee? Is something wrong?”

“…It’s nothing.”

“Hmm. Then may we begin the meeting?”

Lee Jungryong nodded silently, his gaze fixed on one person the entire time.

Reclining at an angle against the back of his chair, Jin Taekyung muttered as if to himself.

“Well, it certainly looks like something to me.”

“…!”

“Or not. Never mind.”

Without realizing it, Lee Jungryong clenched his fists.

* * *

The meeting in the underground bunker dragged on for a long time.

The monster wave had caused at least hundreds of thousands of casualties over the past week. It was already a massive catastrophe in its own right, and they were unquestionably at war. Naturally, they had to proceed with the utmost caution.

“Accordingly, we should divide our forces into five armies and continue pressuring the enemy…”

Someone interrupted Wei Penghu as he spoke with a grave expression.

“Comrade Minister of Defense.”

“Speak, Senior General Liao.”

The middle-aged man known as Senior General Liao wore a uniform dripping with medals. Stroking his beard, he began to speak.

“Listening to you, Minister of Defense, has become so frustrating that I must say something. At this rate, how long will it take us to deal with those monsters?”

*Wow…*

His tone was unbelievably obnoxious.

He was talking that way to Wei Penghu—the Chairman’s right-hand man and the military’s highest-ranking power broker.

As if he had read my mind, Team Leader Choi sent me a message through Message Magic.

- The Communist Party has factions too. Senior General Liao is a pure-blooded member of the Princelings, the Communist Party’s largest faction, going all the way back to his grandfather’s generation. They’re rivals of the Shanghai clique, which Chairman Xiao and Minister of Defense Wei Penghu belong to.

- The Princelings and Shang… what?

- …Simply put, the Princelings are number one and the Shanghai clique is number two.

- Ah.

Until now, I had assumed that a one-party system meant the Communist Party was completely united. Apparently, they fought each other tooth and nail too.

- But is that allowed? If Chairman Xiao just points at someone and says, “That man is dangerous,” wouldn’t they behead him in Tiananmen Square?

- They do it because they’re allowed to.

- …Right. When you put it that way, I’ve got nothing to say.

- The factions reached an agreement. During the Great Cataclysm, the Chairman from the Princelings made so many mistakes that they couldn’t shamelessly keep clinging to power.

- Ah. Pingping?

- Yes. Pingping.

That damn Great Cataclysm really had changed all kinds of things.

While I listened to Team Leader Choi’s explanation, Senior General Liao—the pure-blooded scion of the Princelings—made an absolutely brilliant proposal.

“Let’s use nuclear weapons.”

“…”

“…”

“Let’s submit a formal request to Chairman Xiao and launch dozens of nukes across all of Sichuan. That should resolve everything cleanly, shouldn’t it?”

“…”

“…”

What a nuclear-crazed lunatic.

Everyone, myself included, stared at each other in disbelief.

Wei Penghu’s expression was especially impressive. He looked like a man in desperate need of a pistol.

“Rejected.”

“Why? Are you dismissing me because I’m not part of the Shanghai clique?”

“Say something that makes sense. Something that makes sense! What about the people who are still alive or haven’t been harmed? What about the land we would leave barren?”

“The sacrifice of the few for the sake of the many is unavoidable!”

Forget all that. He just sounded like an ox to me.[^1]

Magic Johnson, who had been listening quietly, abruptly spoke in his deep voice.

“The damage if the nuclear attack succeeds would be bad enough, but what will you do if the enemy uses spatial teleportation magic to move the warheads? To Beijing, for example.”

“That’s why we have an Archmage like you…”

“Me? Our opponent isn’t an ordinary Lich. If this Arch Lich is a better mage than I am, then we’ll have an irreversible catastrophe on our hands. Something similar happened several times in the early days of the Great Cataclysm, remember?”

“B-But even so… sacrifices on this scale…”

“Hey. Motherfucker.”

Bang!

I nearly jumped out of my skin. Magic Johnson shot to his feet, his bronze muscles rippling.

“Knock it off. I like Asian men too, you know. I might have to punish you.”

“…!”

“…!”

That was the scariest threat I had ever heard.

Senior General Liao’s face turned deathly pale as he looked around for help. But even the officials who appeared to belong to his faction avoided his gaze. Wu Heixing, his last line of defense, looked away as well.

*So this is how it gets resolved.*

His opponent was an American national hero and the nation’s gay icon.

Everyone seemed desperate not to become the Asian man Magic Johnson took a liking to.

Of course, the ridiculous bullshit Senior General Liao had been spouting also played a major role.

“Enough, both of you. And Senior General Liao, stop talking such utter nonsense.”

With Wei Penghu—the Chinese man who had just put a chink in his place—taking charge, the meeting moved forward quickly.

After gathering the proposals already raised and weathering the ensuing arguments, Wei Penghu finally secured everyone’s agreement. He spoke with a weary expression.

“We will assign the six S-rank Hunters present to six separate fronts. Following the official organization, each front will receive three Army and Air Force divisions, along with Hunters from the Public Security Armed Forces.”

Three Chinese divisions and the Public Security Armed Forces.

I had no idea exactly how many people that meant, but the sheer numbers would be enormous.

When it came to the number of Hunters it possessed, China was always competing for first or second place.

*Of course, the other side is no pushover either.*

The estimated casualties alone numbered in the hundreds of thousands.

If the Arch Lich had resurrected the dead as undead…

An unfathomable number of enemies would be waiting for us.

“This concludes the meeting. Please move out as soon as possible.”

Wei Penghu said he would provide the exact numbers and unit composition in writing. Just as everyone rose from their seats, a voice pierced my ear.

- Come see me for a moment.

It was not Message Magic, but unmistakably Sound Transmission.

[^1]: The Korean word *so* can mean both “the few” in the general’s maxim and “ox,” turning his solemn justification into an insult.
