# Chapter 28

*Hoo.*

I sat cross-legged and steadied my breathing. The breath I exhaled still carried qi I hadn’t managed to contain.

*What a waste.*

Circulating qi meant drawing in qi from outside, cycling it together with the qi inside me, and accumulating it. But only a small amount remained in my body.

Most of it returned to nature.

*In novels, doing just a little of this sends your cultivation soaring.*

The greatest martial art under heaven? A legendary elixir?

Not here. The Jin Family’s Cultivation Technique was Peak-grade, but it was hopeless at accumulating internal energy. And elixirs? Bullshit. I hadn’t even laid eyes on one. All I’d done was suffer like hell.

*Is that my only hope?*

Another mass of internal energy crouched in a corner of my dantian. If I could make all of it mine, it would be a huge help in the fights ahead.

The problem was that no matter how many times I tried, it refused to budge.

*Move. Move!*

As if something that had sat there immovable as a boulder this whole time would suddenly start moving. I tried drawing it up with my internal energy, but it gave me no response. I sighed and got to my feet.

“Are we leaving?”

Hyuk Mujin had been keeping watch over our surroundings.

“Yeah.”

“Understood.”

He promptly got the reconnaissance squad ready, then scooped Soyul into his arms. I could only stare.

*What the hell did this bastard eat?*

Why was he suddenly so on the ball?

This was the guy who used to move only when I shoved a fist in his face. Somehow he seemed like a different person.

*Not that I’m complaining.*

I pushed the stray thoughts aside and hoisted Gong Yacheong onto my back. As the poisoning worsened, his face had turned a dark bluish-black.

Hyuk Mujin asked worriedly, “Will he be all right?”

“He has to be.”

I’d already done everything I could. All that remained was to hope Gong Yacheong held on. I murmured to the unconscious man,

“Not much farther. Just hold on a little longer.”

Sunlight pierced the dim dawn. I took a long stride toward it.

No—I tried to.

- Awoooooo!

At first, I thought it was the wind. But those were the cries of living beasts. Hyuk Mujin muttered,

“The wolves must be hungry.”

“Wolves?”

“Of course. It’s hardly strange to find wild animals in the mountains.”

This was a game set in ancient China. It wouldn’t be strange for wolves or tigers to jump out from anywhere. I’d heard wild animals crying several times over the past few days.

But…

*Something feels wrong.*

This was different from before. Just hearing those howls made my chest tighten and my fingertips tingle.

The instincts I’d honed over seven years seemed to whisper to me. Something was lurking beyond that forest, where the sunlight had yet to reach.

“Prepare for battle.”

“Then we’re leaving now… Huh?”

“Form a defensive formation.”

Hyuk Mujin quickly came to his senses and relayed my order. Three reconnaissance squad members carrying shields faced with thin iron blocked the snow-covered path.

A narrow trail with the high ground atop a hill. An advantageous position.

- Awoooooo!

A second howl rang out. Closer—and all the more ominous for it. Hyuk Mujin spoke cautiously.

“It sounds like nothing more than a pack of wolves…”

“Then that’s even better.”

“Won’t this delay us too long?”

I shook my head. We’d been running for two full days. If this brief pause was enough to get us caught, then that was fate.

“Hold. We’ll wait a little longer.”

The beasts seemed to understand me. For nearly fifteen minutes, the howls continued without pause, drawing closer all the while. Snapping branches and paws pounding over snow echoed through the mountains at dawn.

“Doesn’t sound like only one or two.”

Hyuk Mujin glanced at me.

“Judging by the noise, there must be dozens… Wolves may be pack animals, but this is still strange.”

No sooner had he finished than the pack appeared. Perhaps they hadn’t been able to find prey during the winter; most were so emaciated that their ribs showed. But they were predators all the same. We couldn’t let our guard down.

*Starving predators, at that. The kids could get hurt.*

Yet contrary to my thoughts, a strange sense of relief washed over me. This place might be crawling with martial artists, but surely even the animals hadn’t learned martial arts.

Forget gut feelings. These would be easy opponents.

*I skip a few raids and I’ve already lost my touch.*

I clicked my tongue and stepped forward. The dozens of wolves charging toward us already looked like chunks of EXP.

“Let’s finish this quickly. Yeah?”

I crooked a finger at the wolf leading the charge in the distance. Judging by its size, that one was the leader.

- Grrrraaaah!

Since when did wolves roar like lions? Don’t tell me it was some kind of spirit beast. Was I seriously about to lose to an animal?

*Not happening.*

I swallowed and raised my spear. I couldn’t afford to lose this battle of wills, so I glared hard and lowered my voice.

“Come.”

The effect was incredible!

- Grrrrr…

The charging wolf abruptly veered aside and plunged into the forest. Its dozens of subordinate wolves rushed after it and vanished.

No—they had fled. A cold wind swept across the countless pawprints stamped into the snow.

*What the hell was that?*

They came charging as if they were going to eat me alive. Why did they run?

Then a famous comic popped into my head. The one about a rubber man with hyperactivity disorder smashing the maritime authorities.

“D-don’t tell me it’s that?”

That ability that let someone frighten enemies—or even knock them unconscious—with nothing but their aura.

This game was insane enough for that to be entirely possible. In which case, the System ought to ping me right about now…

Ding.

There it was! Buzzing with anticipation, I waited for the System voice.

Ignoring the reconnaissance squad’s bewildered expressions, I muttered incessantly.

“Come on, come on, come on!”

Something appeared.

The Quest Window.

> **System**
>
> A Quest has been created.
>
> **Quest**
>
> **Jopil, One Question, One Kill**
>
> The relentless chase has come to an end. You have come face-to-face with these cruel and tenacious pursuers, and you must confront Jopil, One Question, One Kill.
>
> May you rest in peace… No, may fortune favor you in battle.
>
> **Grade:** Peak
>
> **Limit:** Jin Taekyung
>
> **Task:** Survive — Incomplete
>
> **Reward:** ???
>
> **Failure:** Death
>
> - You do not have the authority to choose this Quest.
>
> - The Quest has been forcibly accepted!

“…Huh?”

What was happening? Where was I, and who was I?

Countless questions flashed through my mind and vanished. Then I heard someone’s voice.

“We finally meet.”

A man stood beside a bare tree, some twenty zhang away.[^1] Far if you called it far, close if you called it close. The problem was that no one had known he was there.

Not even me.

*When the hell did he…?*

The higher my Level and martial realm rose, the sharper my five senses became. Yet I still hadn’t sensed that man. I hadn’t even heard his footsteps.

If he hadn’t spoken first—if the Quest Window hadn’t appeared—I would have turned around without ever knowing he was there.

*The wolves.*

Those beasts had fled. Not from me, but from that man.

The unease that had refused to leave me finally took shape. I didn’t even need to use Qi Sense.

I already knew his name.

“Jopil?”

The man—Jopil, One Question, One Kill—smiled broadly and nodded.

* * *

I had met three Peak masters so far.

Jin Wikyung, Wipeng, and the Head Elder. All three looked the part. Jopil, One Question, One Kill, did not.

*He looks ordinary.*

He wasn’t a giant like Jin Wikyung, didn’t have sharp eyes like Wipeng, and didn’t wear a silver beard like the Head Elder.

Jopil was of average height and had an unremarkable face.

That only made him seem more dangerous.

“Nice to meet you.”

The instant Jopil smiled brightly and stepped forward, I sprang back without a second thought.

“Nimble. Good reactions, too. I like you.”

My heart hammered. Maybe it was the tension, but my voice came out hoarse.

“Don’t come any closer.”

“My apologies. I was so pleased to see you that I got carried away.”

Well, I wasn’t pleased at all.

“Don’t be so tense. I only want to talk.”

“Talk?”

“Yes. I’ve been wanting to meet you.”

Of course he had. I’d killed twenty of his men only a few days ago.

*Fuck. I’m fucked.*

The thought of a master like that pursuing me day and night for two days because he wanted to tear me apart made my stomach churn.

“Don’t give me that bullshit, Jopil!”

When I shouted, I felt the air behind me freeze. Jopil smiled as if he already knew what I was doing.

“What difference will telling them who I am make? Second Rate, Third Rate. They’re all idiots and trash.”

He’d pegged the reconnaissance squad’s strength exactly. It was almost enough to make me wonder whether he could use the System too.

“Why don’t we have a brief conversation instead? There’s a great deal I’d like to ask you.”

“A conversation? You’re not just buying time?”

“Buying time? What do you mean?”

“You know what I mean. You’re waiting for your men.”

Jopil’s nose twitched as though I’d hit the mark. That was it. For whatever reason, he was alone. If I was willing to accept a few sacrifices, then maybe…

“Waiting? Me? For those pathetic weaklings?”

“…What?”

“Didn’t I already tell you? They’re all idiots and trash. Hopeless lives without effort or talent.”

“…”

“Black Mountain Blade was passable, but he had no eye for talent, so he deserved to die. I tore out his eyes before I left—his punishment for failing to recognize a master.”

Correction. Jopil, One Question, One Kill, didn’t merely look dangerous.

He was a fucking dangerous bastard.

*This guy’s completely insane.*

I’d never met anyone like Jopil, either in reality or in a game. That perfectly casual tone and manner. He was a psychopath who treated people like tools.

“Anyway, what you’re worried about won’t happen. Those slow bastards will need half a shichen to catch up.[^2] By then, everything will be over.”

One thing was certain. In the ending Jopil envisioned, his own death had no place.

“Well?”

“And if I refuse?”

Jopil smiled gently.

“I’ll be disappointed in you. Very disappointed.”

I could more or less picture what would happen if he became disappointed.

“Young friend, I bear you no grudge. Quite the opposite—I’m rather fond of you. Answer a few questions truthfully, and I might even let you go.”

“Wait. Let me go?”

“Yes. I won’t harm you in any way. I’ll send you back in one piece.”

“…Really?”

“I’ll stake my neck on it. Will that suffice?”

The sincerity was plain on Jopil’s face. He might have been an unpredictable psychopath, but perhaps all of us could make it out of this alive.

“All right.”

Even if the worst happened, what else could I do but fight? For now, I planned to buy some time and search for a weakness.

“Good. A friend who listens to reason. Hahaha.”

Jopil clapped and laughed. The scabbard at his left hip swayed.

*Right-handed. Swordsman.*

I filed away each piece of information.

“First, I’d like to ask your age.”

“Twenty.”

Jopil’s eyes widened.

“My. To have reached that realm at twenty. Impressive.”

I’d spent seven years as a Hunter without ever escaping F-rank, yet in this game, people treated me like a martial arts genius.

It was a strange feeling.

“Judging by your clothes, you seem to belong to the Jin Family of Taiyuan.”

I nodded readily.

“Super First Rate at twenty. You can’t be the famous Heaven Shaking Sword, so… what is your name?”

“Jin Taekyung.”

“Jin Taekyung. Jin Taekyung. I’ve heard that name somewhere before. Ah!”

After turning it over in his mind, Jopil exclaimed,

“The wastrel Third Young Master! That’s you?”

“I’m not a wastrel. These days, they call me the Sleeping Dragon.”

“Puhahaha! I knew it. The Jin Family of Taiyuan? Those rigid fools would never resort to poisoning someone. I don’t know who set this whole thing up, but matters are taking an interesting turn.”

Jopil regarded me with satisfaction.

“I’d already heard plenty of rumors about you. Was it all a disguise?”

“…Something like that.”

“Good. A hidden blade, then. I like it. When did you begin learning martial arts?”

“Seven years ago.”

It wasn’t entirely a lie. By Murim standards, a Hunter’s combat methods could be considered a kind of martial art.

“Seven years. And your master?”

“Don’t have one.”

“No master?”

He studied me for a while before saying,

“You don’t appear to be lying.”

“You promised to spare me if I answered honestly. That was your promise, wasn’t it?”

“Yes, it was. What an absurd yet entertaining story. A direct descendant of the Jin Family of Taiyuan, without a master, reaching that realm at twenty… My, my.”

My mouth had gone bone-dry. I gripped my spear and scanned Jopil’s body. He seemed to be riddled with openings.

But was what I saw really all there was?

*He might be baiting me into attacking first.*

The thought went no further. Jopil suddenly burst out laughing.

“Hahaha! Good. I like it. I’ll keep my promise.”

He would keep his promise?

The thing I’d thought impossible was actually happening. I stared at Jopil in a daze.

“No need to look at me like that. To be honest, I wanted very badly to kill you at first… But now that we’ve met, I find myself wanting to watch you a little longer.”

Jopil continued, his voice brimming with goodwill.

“It would be a waste to kill such outstanding talent. Especially with things as they are.”

“What do you mean?”

“Ah. You may not know yet. You’ll find out when you return. Go on, then. I hope you’ll have grown a little more by the next time we meet.”

I could leave? He meant it?

Without dropping my guard, I slowly backed away. Jopil merely watched me with a smile.

He looked like a fisherman releasing a minnow.

*They say even in a tiger’s den, you can survive if you keep your wits about you.*

Who would have thought Jopil’s unpredictability would become my way out?

Once I’d put a safe distance between us, the breath I’d been holding burst out. But there was no time to catch it. I had to get away from here as quickly as possible.

“We’re moving. Hurry!”

Then—

“Hold on, young friend.”

Jopil stared at me, puzzled.

“What are you doing?”

“What do you mean? We’re leaving, just like you promised…”

“I only gave permission for you to leave. Alone.”

“…What?”

“I may not look it, but I’m in someone’s employ. I still have to complete the mission I was given.”

The mission. Don’t tell me.

“The three survivors of the Sakju Branch. And those pieces of trash you call your subordinates. Leave them behind. I ought to be paid for two days’ work, don’t you think?”

His eyes, clear and innocent as a child’s, flashed. In the next instant, they became the eyes of a predator.

“I’ll warn you now. If you refuse, I’ll be very disappointed.”

I stared blankly at Jopil, the reconnaissance squad, the young siblings, and the dying Gong Yacheong. The moment was brief, but my mind cycled through the choice dozens of times before the words finally burst out.

“Then be disappointed, you fucking bastard.”

Jopil laughed savagely.

[^1]: A zhang is a traditional Chinese unit of distance, roughly 3.3 meters.
[^2]: A shichen is a traditional time period of roughly two hours.
