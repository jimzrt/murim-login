# Chapter 121

It had been a fierce battle—fierce enough to leave mountains of corpses and rivers of blood—yet most of the Mount Heng Sword Sect’s buildings remained largely intact.

Including the pavilion I was sitting in.

I collapsed into a chair.

“Ugh. I’m dying.”

Exhaustion always followed a major battle like the one we had just fought.

Leveling up could restore my body, but it couldn’t do anything about mental fatigue.

And it was even worse after a day spent skirting the brink of death.

*That was seriously dangerous.*

Jopil. The Head Elder. Pung Yang.

Nothing good had ever come from getting tangled up with Peak masters. More than once, I’d found myself wishing I had five lives.

“Squad Leader, thank you for your hard work.”

“Yeah, yeah.”

“Whew, your shoulders are really tense.”

Hyuk Mujin sidled up and began massaging my shoulders.

He and Wolhwa had stayed outside to protect Cheol Mubaek, then joined us during the survivor search once things had mostly settled down.

“If I’d been there, I would’ve really laid into that bastard Pung Yang. You know what I mean, right?”

“Of course. You would’ve gotten yourself killed on the spot.”

“……”

“What? Massage harder.”

Hyuk Mujin grumbled, but dug his fingers more firmly into my shoulders.

“What about Jin Mukyung? I mean, my second brother?”

“We’ve already moved him to a separate room. The physicians said there’s nothing to worry about. The other wounded are recovering quickly too.”

“Really? That’s a relief.”

“Are those physicians quacks or something? I heard the Second Young Master and Great Hero Cheol both suffered fairly serious Internal Injuries.”

“They were sent by the Lower District Sect. Let’s trust their skills.”

In truth, it wasn’t the physicians I trusted. It was the Items.

Without the **Superior Wound Medicine**, which could heal most wounds within a few days, and the **Ten-Year He Shouwu**, which was exceptionally effective against Internal Injuries, some of them would already have crossed the River Jordan.

*I’ve given them the minimum emergency treatment. The physicians can handle the rest.*

The Lower District Sect—or rather, Wolhwa—had acted quickly without any of us realizing it. Several days ago, when she sent one of her subordinates back from the shrine, she had apparently issued a mobilization order to a nearby branch.

The Lower District Sect’s reinforcements arrived half a day after the battle ended and immediately began dealing with the aftermath.

*While everyone else was looking ahead, she was thinking about what came after.*

The support force had been assembled for relief work, not combat.

They had brought physicians, cooks, and even laborers. Their foresight and preparation were enough to make me marvel.

*She really isn’t an ordinary person.*

The Lower District Sect was both an information organization with branches throughout the land and a Murim sect.

Wolhwa had become a Branch Leader in a sect of that size while still in her mid-to-late twenties. There was no way she was ordinary.

*Come to think of it, Wolhwa isn’t even her real name.*

The name I’d discovered through Qi Sense was Eun Sowol. As for why she had gone out of her way to hide it from us, I figured it was something like using a code name in a spy movie.

One thing was certain: making an enemy of someone that capable would be exhausting.

*Don’t get too close. Keep a reasonable distance and maintain proper boundaries. Yeah, that should be about right.*

Fortunately, that wouldn’t be too difficult. Wolhwa had shown me inexplicable goodwill and curiosity from the very beginning.

Whether that interest was genuine or merely the curiosity of a veteran information merchant remained to be seen.

“Mujin.”

“Should I massage harder?”

“No, not that. What do you think of Young Lady Wolhwa?”

“She’s pretty.”

“And?”

Hyuk Mujin thought for a moment before answering.

“She’s extremely pretty.”

“……”

“Massage my forearms instead.”

I was the idiot for asking him.

Hyuk Mujin looked wounded and was about to protest when light footsteps approached and stopped outside the door.

*Wolhwa?*

No. The qi beyond the door was far weaker than Wolhwa’s. After a brief silence, the unexpected visitor spoke.

“Young Master Jin, may I come in?”

The voice was clear and distinct.

It was Lee Seowol.

* * *

Once Hyuk Mujin left the pavilion, Lee Seowol and I were alone.

I gazed out the window at the slowly darkening sky and cleared my throat for no reason.

“Ahem. Ahem.”

Being alone at this hour with a woman—especially a stunning beauty—was torture in its own right.

Worse yet, she was the Sect Leader of the Mount Heng Sword Sect, whom we had been fighting like sworn enemies until recently.

They said new wine belonged in new wineskins, but she was still the only daughter of Lee Cheonbaek, the man who had tried to bring down the Jin Family of Taiyuan. On top of that, she and I were already entangled in a scandal.

*What the hell am I supposed to say?*

*May the deceased rest in peace?*

No. That would make things far too gloomy. As if losing her family recently hadn’t been enough, she had also lost most of her subordinates in the battle against Pung Yang.

After agonizing over it, I finally opened my mouth.

“Have you eaten?”

“……”

“I know you’ve been through a lot, but at times like this, it’s even more important to keep your strength up…”

I stopped.

“Sorry.”

*Damn it. I should’ve just kept my mouth shut.*

As I regretted speaking from the bottom of my heart, Lee Seowol rose and bowed deeply to me.

“Lee Seowol of the Mount Heng Sword Sect pays her respects to her benefactor.”

It happened before I could stop her. Flustered, I hurriedly helped her to her feet.

Benefactor?

It was accurate, but hearing it made me cringe.

“Oh, come on. What are you doing? You don’t have to call me your benefactor.”

“No. Anyone with a conscience should know gratitude after receiving kindness. Please do not make me feel ashamed, Benefactor.”

Her tone was so resolute that I couldn’t argue any further.

*She’s not wrong.*

Without Jin Mukyung and me, the Mount Heng Sword Sect would have closed its doors for good today. A deep bow wasn’t enough. They could erect statues of us and still owe us.

Maybe they could designate today as the Day the Sleeping Dragon of Shanxi Came and make it an annual Mount Heng Sword Sect holiday—

*Okay, that might be taking things too far.*

“Now, calm down and sit.”

“I will follow my benefactor’s instructions.”

“Could you stop calling me that?”

“Yes, Benefactor.”

*This is driving me crazy.*

Only after Lee Seowol calmly sat down did I finally learn why she had come.

“I came to give you my answer regarding the letter.”

“The letter? Oh.”

She meant the invitation.

It was a polite request to share a meal at the Jin Family of Taiyuan on the approaching New Year’s Day—or rather, a summons disguised as a dinner invitation.

Now that the Jin Family of Taiyuan had Shanxi Murim firmly in its grasp, it was obvious that any sect refusing the invitation would face an unpleasant future.

The Mount Heng Sword Sect, which had only narrowly escaped annihilation, was no exception.

“So, what’s your answer?”

“We will gladly accept your sect’s proposal.”

It was the answer I’d expected.

But Lee Seowol continued.

“Furthermore, as an apology for what happened, I will transfer every right held by our sect to the Jin Family of Taiyuan.”

“Rights?”

“Yes. Every right pertaining to the territory currently held by our sect.”

In other words, she intended to hand all of northern Shanxi over to the Jin Family of Taiyuan.

*She’s going this far?*

The Mount Heng Sword Sect had already bowed its head to the Jin Family of Taiyuan. Jin Wikyung would demand a substantial amount in compensation, but even he couldn’t swallow the sect whole.

Wolhwa had said something similar during our previous conversation.

*And now she’s serving it up to us without even being asked.*

After calling me her benefactor over and over, it seemed she hadn’t merely been paying lip service.

Right. Gratitude shouldn’t end with words. Hm.

“Thank you. My eldest brother will be pleased.”

“There’s more.”

*What? She isn’t finished yet?*

Lee Seowol drew three books from inside her robes and held them out to me.

I slowly read the titles on their covers.

“Blood Wolf Sword Technique, Blood Wolf Footwork, and…”

“Shura Annihilating Fist. The sword technique and footwork were created by my father himself, while the Shura Annihilating Fist is Uncle Cheol’s secret ultimate technique. All three are outstanding Peak martial arts.”

“Peak martial arts…”

I swallowed hard.

One of the lessons Murim had beaten into me was the importance of martial arts. Even with the System, I was only barely making up for my shortcomings. For ordinary martial artists, it went without saying.

To them, an outstanding Peak martial art was a priceless treasure.

*These are worth even more than the rights to northern Shanxi.*

If those rights were the branches of a tree, the three martial arts manuals before me were its roots.

Lee Seowol had placed her father’s legacy—the most valuable possessions of the Mount Heng Sword Sect—on the scales.

“Are these a gift too?”

“No. This is a transaction.”

*I knew it.*

A transaction.

Jin Wikyung would find some way to accept it. Three Peak martial arts were at stake, after all.

*What the hell is she going to demand?*

Wealth? A guarantee of safety? Or something else?

Lee Seowol—or rather, the Mount Heng Sword Sect—was in a situation beyond desperate. Even if they were offering their martial arts for next to nothing, whatever they wanted in return was sure to be a difficult demand.

I cautiously distanced myself from the matter.

“I’m curious about the transaction, but I don’t know if you’re aware—I don’t have that kind of authority.”

Lee Seowol gazed steadily at me with eyes as clear as a lake.

“Is that so?”

“Yes. I don’t hold any particular position either. I think it would be best if you discussed this separately with my eldest brother later.”

“I disagree, Benefactor.”

“Excuse me?”

“This is a transaction you are fully capable of deciding, though naturally, there would need to be extensive discussion.”

“A transaction worth three Peak martial arts… Then what would we have to give you?”

“A person.”

“A person?”

For an instant, a smile flickered across Lee Seowol’s lips.

It was the second time I had seen her smile, and this time, I knew I hadn’t imagined it.

“Please marry me.”

* * *

“Well, I’ll be going.”

Hyuk Mujin, who had been pacing around the pavilion’s front courtyard, turned at the sound of a woman’s voice behind him.

A beauty whose mere appearance made something tickle inside his chest was descending the pavilion steps.

*Good heavens. She’s breathtaking.*

Though she was dozens of paces away, it almost seemed as if the cold midwinter wind carried the scent of flowers.

*Our squad leader sure is blessed.*

He was handsome, the youngest Young Master of the Jin Family of Taiyuan—the universally acknowledged foremost family in Shanxi—and an outstanding martial artist to boot.

When he stood beside Wolhwa, they looked like the perfect couple.

And now the Sect Leader of the Mount Heng Sword Sect had been added to the list.

Hyuk Mujin let out a deep sigh as he watched Lee Seowol disappear into the distance.

*I loved you, however briefly, Young Lady Lee.*

When Hyuk Mujin returned to the pavilion, he found Jin Taekyung sitting there in a daze.

“Squad Leader, what’s wrong?”

“……”

“Squad Leader. Snap out of it!”

Only after Hyuk Mujin grabbed him by the shoulders and shook him did his unfocused eyes finally clear.

Hyuk Mujin asked with a worried expression, “Did something happen? Why are you suddenly acting like this?”

Jin Taekyung swallowed hard and barely managed to speak.

“Mujin.”

“Yes.”

“Do you know how old Lee Seowol is?”

“What do you mean, ‘Lee Seowol’? You should call her Sect Leader or Young Lady.”

“Unless you want people calling you the late Hyuk Mujin, shut up and answer.”

“……”

Hyuk Mujin thought for a moment.

“How old was she again? I think she was about marriageable age.”

Then he slapped his forehead.

“Oh, I remember.”

“H-How old is she?”

“Seventeen.”

Jin Taekyung’s mouth fell open.

“Fuck, she was still a high schooler?”
