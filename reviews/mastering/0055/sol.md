# Chapter 55

*This is driving me insane.*

The world went yellow before Hyuk Mujin’s eyes.

Assassins disguised as disciples of minor sects. The Head Elder’s betrayal. That alone was enough to make his head explode, but…

*Why the hell did this man collapse too?!*

Jin Taekyung had suddenly lost consciousness. The reconnaissance squad members had taken turns slapping his cheeks and shaking him by the collar, but he showed no sign of waking up.

*He’s definitely still breathing.*

It made no damn sense. And with things as they were, Hyuk Mujin—the deputy squad leader—had somehow ended up in charge of this colossal mess.

“You, and you.”

Hyuk Mujin pointed to two of the reconnaissance squad members still standing there in a daze.

“You two, return to the family at once and report what happened.”

The Head Elder.

Hyuk Mujin didn’t know why one of the family’s most respected elders, a hero of the Great Faction War, had devised such a scheme or what he hoped to gain from it.

But they had to prevent the worst from happening.

“Everyone else, move out for the main force. Hurry!”

Hyuk Mujin hoisted the unconscious Jin Taekyung onto his back.

After roughly two hours of running, the world went yellow before his eyes again—this time for a different reason.

*I’m going to die.*

It had only been a few days since Jopil’s brutal palm strike had left him with internal injuries. He had boasted to Jin Taekyung that he was almost fully recovered, but in truth, he needed at least two weeks of complete rest.

“Huff, huff.”

His entire body was drenched, and his legs were trembling.

He would have struggled to keep up even without a burden. With the solidly built Jin Taekyung on his back, he felt ready to drop dead.

*Ah, Mother. Father.*

Now he was even hallucinating his parents—both alive and well—beckoning to him. The middle-aged woman standing beside them looked familiar from the temple prayer hall.

*Guanyin Bodhisattva?*

“Child. You have suffered enough. You may rest now.”

Her benevolent smile slowly put Hyuk Mujin’s heart at ease.

*Yeah. I’ve done enough. The squad leader keels over at the most important moment, and these bastards don’t even offer to carry him, whether I drop dead or not.*

*Fuck it. Whatever.*

Just as Hyuk Mujin was about to let go—

“Whew… haaah.”

Warm breath tickled his ear. He knew who it was without turning around. Tears of relief sprang to Hyuk Mujin’s eyes.

“Squad Leader!”

Jin Taekyung grinned and whispered,

“Candy in your ear.”[^1]

* * *

Clang! Clang!

“Kill them! Kill them!”

“Graaah!”

Blood sprayed and screams erupted on every side. A blood-soaked martial artist crawled on all fours between the corpses.

His still-youthful face was twisted with pain and terror.

“Hnnh… hhhk…”

He tried to hold back his sobs, but they kept bursting out. He had spent ten years gritting his teeth and honing his martial arts, yet it had all proved useless on this blood-soaked battlefield.

The senior he had relied on was dead. So was the comrade who had been like a brother to him. Weapons had poured in from every direction, splitting open bellies and hacking limbs apart.

“Mother… Mother…”

He called for his mother over and over until his face suddenly went rigid.

Shunk—!

A spearhead had sprouted from his chest.

His eyes flew wide for an instant, then the light faded from them. A wandering martial artist with a bandit’s beard pulled out the rusty spear and grinned.

“What’s a brat doing here?”

But even a wandering martial artist who had survived countless brushes with death could not evade the blade that came flying at him the next instant.

Slice—

A silver line flashed, and the wandering martial artist’s head flew into the air.

A wandering martial artist killed a martial artist. Another martial artist killed the wandering martial artist.

Nameless men killed and were killed on every side, over and over again.

That was a battlefield.

*Yes. This was what it was like.*

The Head Elder gazed across the battlefield, lost in memory.

Eight Spring Gorge. A fierce battleground that had earned a single line in the history of the Great Faction War decades ago. How many people had died in that narrow gorge?

*I was young then, too.*

His body had been strong, and his heart had burned hot. It was a time when that embarrassing word—*chivalry*—had still seemed grand.

But the Great Faction War had changed the Head Elder. He had grown weary of the endless fighting and afraid of death.

*What good is a chivalrous warrior? Once you die, you’re nothing but a ghost.*

That realization had been a kind of enlightenment. In the end, he had survived and become a hero. After long years of bitter endurance, he had finally reached this point.

Now it was time to reap the fruits of his labor.

The Head Elder turned his shrewd old eyes toward the man standing beside him.

*What a waste. Such a waste.*

Jin Wikyung was an exceptional talent. He was young, highly accomplished in martial arts, and above all, possessed the dignity and judgment required of a leader. At times he allowed sentiment to sway him, but perhaps because of that, he was deeply respected within the family.

*He is more than capable of restoring the family to greatness.*

If Jin Wikyung became the Family Head, and if his two younger brothers—each already distinguishing himself—stood firmly behind him…

The Head Elder caught himself and let out a quiet laugh.

What a strange thing to be thinking after coming this far. Besides, Jin Taekyung was surely dead by now, reduced to a lonely ghost.

“I’ve grown old.”

Jin Wikyung reacted to the quiet murmur.

“Did you say something?”

“Nothing of importance. Old men tend to talk to themselves. More importantly, how does the situation look to you, Lesser Family Head?”

“The battle is going in our favor, but…”

Jin Wikyung’s expression darkened slightly. They held the advantage in both troop quality and terrain, but their casualties continued to mount.

There was no way he could watch members of his family die and remain unmoved.

“Lesser Family Head. I understand how you feel, but you must not act rashly.”

The Head Elder’s warning was stern.

“A leader must know when to step forward. The longer the battle drags on, the more impatient our enemies will become. That is the moment we must seize.”

“The Mount Heng Sword Sect will soon commit its full strength.”

“They’ll place their Peak masters at the front. Their Sect Leader, Lee Cheonbaek, may even come himself.”

“Then everything will be decided.”

The Head Elder nodded.

“If you, Wipeng, and I hold off their leaders while arrows rain down from the cliffs, their morale will plummet.”

“It’s a shame we can’t use fire. I resent the heavens.”

The narrow terrain of Eight Spring Gorge was perfect for a fire attack, but the heavy snowfall several days earlier had left snow piled everywhere.

Yet even if the heavens had sided with Jin Wikyung, what he hoped for would never have happened.

The men drawing their bows atop the cliffs would obey the Head Elder’s commands.

“I resent the heavens as well.”

The Head Elder meant it.

He had waited far too many years. He resented Heaven’s mandate for arriving only now.

* * *

“Candy in your ear.”

“Squad Leader!”

“Was it sweet as a dream?”

“…Are you insane?”

A rush of relief swept through me at the sight of Hyuk Mujin’s ugly face. I felt the same about the other reconnaissance squad members who crowded around when they heard I was awake.

“Squad Leader’s awake!”

“What happened? Are you all right?”

“Wasn’t he dead?”

I’d deal with the bastard who’d just said that later. I memorized his face and name, then grinned at everyone.

“Been a while. You guys been well?”

“Huh?”

“What’s that supposed to mean?”

“Did he hurt his head?”

“His head! Check his head!”

Exactly the reaction I’d expected.

Still, it was a greeting I’d genuinely wanted to give them at least once.

…Even if they did treat me like a lunatic for it.

“Anyway, that’s enough greetings. How long was I out?”

Hyuk Mujin dropped to the ground with a thud and answered.

“Definitely more than an hour, but a little under two.”

I’d spent two weeks in reality, so the timing roughly lined up. I nodded and moved on to my next question.

“Was I breathing?”

“Why are you suddenly asking that? Seriously.”

“We don’t have time. Just answer me.”

“You were breathing, so you woke up. Otherwise, you’d be dead.”

Fair enough.

Apparently, logging in or logging out left the body on the other side in a deathlike state.

*If I’d logged out before dealing with the assassins, there wouldn’t have been a body left to come back to.*

I could think about that later.

There was a more urgent problem.

“The main force?”

“We were already heading there. I split off two men and sent them back to the family.”

“Good. How much farther?”

“At least another two hours.”

“Two hours…”

“Uh, Squad Leader. I’m sorry to say this, but… I think we have to consider the worst-case scenario.”

Hyuk Mujin and every member of the reconnaissance squad fell silent. No one needed to ask what the worst-case scenario meant.

*I’ve thought about it too.*

According to what Gwak Jun had said just before he died, this betrayal had been meticulously planned for a long time.

But the plan contained exactly one mistake.

*Me.*

The Head Elder had underestimated me.

No—maybe, in a way, he had overestimated me. He’d sent dozens of First Rate masters just to kill me.

Even that hadn’t been enough. I had survived.

*He made a mistake.*

On top of that, when Gwak Jun first revealed his true colors, he’d said they needed to start moving soon if they wanted to make it on time.

Which meant…

“It’s not too late.”

I met each squad member’s gaze and spoke firmly.

“Don’t think about the worst-case scenario. No matter what it takes, I’ll change the outcome.”

“Squad Leader…”

“So, Mujin.”

I gave the emotional Hyuk Mujin a warm smile.

“Get up. Now.”

“I’m dying here.”

“Want me to beat you to death instead?”

“…”

“Run one more step while we still have time. Haven’t you ever heard of marathon spirit?”

“I haven’t.”

Ah. Right. This was Murim.

“Anyway, get up. We’ll be there in another hour.”

Hyuk Mujin gripped his trembling legs and forced himself upright, then cocked his head.

“An hour?”

“Yeah. An hour.”

“I already told you. Even at full speed, it’ll take two.”

“Exactly. One hour.”

“What are you even talking about…?”

“Mujin.”

“Yes?”

Hyuk Mujin blinked at me, guileless as an ox. I smiled even brighter.

“Ever heard of grit?”

Whether it should take two hours or four didn’t matter.

We were getting there in one. Period.

“Run like hell. That’ll do it.”

“Huk.”

Every face in the reconnaissance squad went deathly pale.

* * *

“We’ve been blocked.”

“Our casualties are mounting. Sect Leader, please take action.”

Despite the continuing reports, Blood Wolf Sword Lee Cheonbaek, Sect Leader of the Mount Heng Sword Sect, kept his eyes closed.

*I was too hasty.*

The war had begun in a rush, and their preparations had been equally inadequate. Their provisions were rapidly dwindling, morale was falling, and men were deserting one after another.

*They saw right through me.*

Rage over the loss of his son and impatience with the situation had clouded his judgment. He had ignored his subordinates’ advice and chosen the fastest route.

That was how he had ended up confronting the Jin Family of Taiyuan in a narrow gorge whose very name was unfamiliar to him: Eight Spring Gorge.

“Sect Leader!”

At his subordinate’s shout, Lee Cheonbaek slowly opened his eyes.

The formidable vision of a Peak master swept across the battlefield.

“Gaaah!”

“Wipe them out! They’re nothing but rabble!”

Well over a thousand of his men were jammed at the narrow entrance, unable to advance. The wandering martial artists and mounted bandits he had placed at the front had numbers on their side, but individually, they were inferior even to the Jin Family’s rank-and-file warriors.

*Sword fodder, at best.*

Just as he clicked his tongue inwardly, several dozen wandering martial artists suddenly began peeling away from the front line. The middle-aged wanderer leading them bellowed at the top of his lungs.

“We’ll die like dogs at this rate! Brothers of the Blood Rain Group, fall back!”

Those became the middle-aged wandering martial artist’s last words.

Whoosh—

A light breeze brushed past him. The wandering martial artist felt nothing else.

He never realized that Blood Wolf Sword Lee Cheonbaek had already passed him, or that his men had frozen in terror.

He only thought that his neck suddenly felt hot.

“Uh…”

His cleanly severed head dropped with a dull thud. The headless body staggered several more steps before collapsing like a rotten old tree.

“The Blood Rain Group, was it?”

Lee Cheonbaek pointed his sword at the petrified wanderers. Not a single drop of blood stained its blade.

“Get back in there.”

The killing intent of a Peak master lashed out like a blade. The wandering martial artists charged toward the front even faster than they had retreated.

They had decided that fighting on the front line was better than throwing themselves at the Peak master before them.

“Rat bastards.”

Lee Cheonbaek followed after them. His blazing gaze was fixed somewhere ahead, where the Jin Family of Taiyuan’s leaders had to be.

“The Sect Leader is taking the lead!”

“Everyone, charge! Wipe out the Jin Family of Taiyuan!”

As Lee Cheonbaek stepped forward, the Mount Heng Sword Sect’s core forces followed.

Three Peak masters and dozens of First Rate masters surged toward the front.

[^1]: A riff on the Korean pop song “My Ear’s Candy”; Taekyung follows it with a parody of the lyrics.
