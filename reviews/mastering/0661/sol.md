# Chapter 661

Two days from now, at noon.

Execution.

Those two short phrases slipped from Baeksang’s lips and tore through my mind like bullets.

*Execution in two days? Mine?*

I swallowed the question rising in my throat. I couldn’t let Baeksang see me shaken. Not here.

Instead of giving in to despair, I tried the escape route that had already shut me out several times.

*Logout.*

Beep!

> **System**
>
> - **Logout** failed!
>
> - **Logout** is unavailable in certain situations!

*Fuck. Still doesn’t work.*

I made myself speak calmly.

“Two days. Sooner than I expected.”

“My late father always said that once you’ve made up your mind, you should draw your sword without hesitation.”

“You’re not drawing that sword alone, are you?”

“I was the first to grasp its hilt, but I won’t be alone when it swings for your neck. Not even the Palace Lord can stop it.”

*He won’t be alone…*

I considered what he meant, then muttered almost to myself, “You must have been busy, reaching out to people all over the place in such a short time.”

“Twenty tribal chieftains, including me, have already joined together,” Baeksang said dryly. “And the matter on the agenda for the final day of the Tribal Grand Council will be…”

“Jin Taekyung of the Jin Family of Taiyuan. No—my execution, now that I’m a criminal whose sins outrage heaven and humanity.”

“You understand.”

“I’d have to be a fucking idiot not to, now that it’s come this far. Is that why you threw me into the underground prison first?”

“Of course. It was the easiest way. Though I didn’t expect you to surrender so readily.”

Baeksang made no attempt to deny it. He looked down at me, bound by iron balls weighing ten thousand geun, like a hunter surveying his captured prey.

“To catch a tiger, you must go into the mountains and risk your life. But a tiger locked in a cage is another matter, wouldn’t you say?”

“I suppose so. Especially when there are twenty hunters instead of one.”

Baeksang had always had plenty of tribal chieftains behind him. But twenty?

That was more than half the thirty who remained after Heugung and Yohi disappeared. Some of the chieftains who had stood with the Beast Miao King had switched sides.

“What did you promise them?”

“Revival for their tribes. Greater influence and enormous wealth.”

“Well. I figured it was something like that, but it’s damn simple.”

“And effective.”

Maybe he was so candid because he knew we were alone. Whatever the reason, Baeksang was more honest today than I’d ever heard him. Worse, I couldn’t deny what he said.

The Nanman Beast Palace was an alliance of tribes. Each chieftain had sized up the situation and bet on the side most likely to win.

For the future of the tribe he ruled. For the wealth and glory he stood to gain.

And…

“The one who stands to gain the most is right in front of me. You’re going to take all of Nanman, aren’t you?”

Baeksang said nothing. I let out a hollow laugh.

“That’s something. Rotten right down to the roots.”

Baeksang had been watching me as though he could read my mind. Now he spoke without warning.

“Have you ever lived through a war?”

“What?”

“I asked if you have ever lived through a war.”

Why bring this up now?

I had no idea what he was after, but as the tiger in the cage, I didn’t have much choice except to listen.

“I lived through all of it. More than fifty years ago, I arrived in the Central Plains with the Palace Lord and ten thousand warriors. The first thing I saw was hell on earth.”

He went on, slowly and evenly.

“Fields were trampled flat. Blue rivers filled with blood and corpses. Every day—every moment, it seemed—people died across the land. Children who had lost their parents to the war were so hungry they tore bark from trees and ate it without even noticing their teeth being pulled out.”

War was always horrific. Someone might win in the end, but the road to victory was strewn with screams and death. Neither side could avoid it.

“It was a hellscape unlike anything the Palace Lord or I had ever seen. But we had a reason to fight, and none of us retreated. If the Demonic Cult took the Central Plains, Nanman would be next. We were there to protect our homeland, where our families and friends lived.”

Baeksang’s voice echoed through the dark underground prison.

“More lives were lost than I could count. Some died fighting the enemy. Others sat staring at the sky after a battle and took their own lives. Sometimes we had to strike a lethal acupoint for a comrade writhing in pain.”

“…”

“Battle after battle, some died and the rest grew weary. But as the war turned in our favor, the hope we carried grew stronger. Soon we could go home. See the women we loved, our aging parents, or the children who must have grown so much while we were away.”

I hadn’t lived through it, but I could feel it. I hadn’t seen it, but I could picture it.

Ten thousand warriors making their way through jungles, across the Yangtze, over plains and mountains, until they finally reached the distant Central Plains. Then fighting the Demonic Cult’s hundred thousand followers of the Demonic Path as they surged in from every direction, day after day.

“When I counted one day, fewer than a tenth of our ten thousand warriors were still alive. The war, which had slowly been turning our way, had reached a stalemate. The Palace Lord and I began to wonder why we were still there.”

No one could stand against time. It could move mountains and dry rivers, and even a Supreme Peak master was powerless before it.

*It must have worn them down. Slowly, until they couldn’t bear any more.*

As I thought it, Baeksang continued.

“Then, more than a decade into the war, the Martial God defeated the Heavenly Demon.”

Two absolute masters had clashed in a life-and-death duel that shook heaven and earth. The Martial God won. The Demonic Cult, which had once swallowed half the world, lost the Heavenly Demon at its center and collapsed like a sandcastle.

“The remnants of the Demonic Cult retreated west. The Murim Alliance intended to pursue them to the end and wipe them out. With the Martial God—whom no one could stop now—at the fore, the Three Saints, the Ten Kings, and countless orthodox martial artists of the Murim Alliance advanced from every direction. We went with them.”

The balance had already been shifting toward the orthodox factions. With the Heavenly Demon dead, there was hardly a contest left. All they had to do was pursue the hundred thousand followers of the Demonic Path, now a defeated army, and root them out of the Central Plains.

“The war was as good as over. No. It should have been.”

Baeksang fell silent and stared into empty space.

Water seeped through a hairline crack in the ceiling and struck the cold floor.

Drip. Drip-drip.

I waited, but he said nothing. At last, I spoke.

“Baekhwi. Wasn’t that his name?”

“……!”

Baeksang went rigid, his eyes trembling. I’d never seen him so shaken. I gave a small nod.

“So it was. I thought I might have remembered the name wrong.”

Crunch.

Something cracked between his clenched teeth. He looked at me, his eyes cold.

“Who told you that name?”

It had been Yayul Mok, but Baeksang didn’t need to know that.

“Does it matter? What matters is that someone still remembers Baekhwi.”

“Someone like you has no right to speak that name.”

Whoosh!

Blade-sharp killing intent surged from Baeksang and shot through the gaps in the iron bars.

Strangely, I wasn’t afraid.

Maybe I felt a little compassion for a father who had lost his young child long ago. Whatever else Baeksang was, that was how I felt toward him now.

*Baekhwi.*

*Baek, white. Hwi, shining.*

A person’s name. Baeksang’s only child, whom he had cherished so dearly. Someone who would have become the Beast Miao King’s son-in-law if he had lived.

*If he had lived.*

I ignored Baeksang’s killing intent and asked, “Did he die while you were pursuing the Demonic Cult? Or—”

Boom! Rumble!

The rest of my question vanished beneath a roar. A tremendous shock wave shook the prison.

Baeksang had pulverized a section of wall with a single punch. He stared at me with eyes like frost.

“Do you want to die here and now?”

Given my situation, that was a serious threat. Hyuk Mujin would probably have pissed himself and taken a vow of silence.

But there were exceptions. Cheongpung might be a little excited because dying would be a first for him.

And I’d been knocked around so much that my liver had probably swollen to twice its size.

“That wouldn’t be so bad. Though you can’t kill me right now.”

“What?”

“There’s a reason you put my execution off for two days. And I doubt you made that decision alone. Was it an order from the Southern Heaven Demon Empress? I hear you exchange missives on the first day of every month.”

“……!”

“So quit posturing and tell me the story. It gets lonely and boring down here by myself. At least the Sichuan Tang Clan had other prisoners.”

“You bastard!”

His shout rang through the prison. Trembling with rage, Baeksang reached out and seized the iron bars.

Creeeak!

Steel as thick as an adult’s forearm bent like taffy. Baeksang stepped through the gap, his eyes cold enough to sting.

“Hwi. Is that child’s death so amusing to you? Are you that curious?”

His aura was vicious enough that he looked ready to draw his sword and cut me down. I didn’t flinch.

He feared the Southern Heaven Demon Empress too much to kill me here, and there was something I needed to know.

“I don’t find it amusing. But I am curious about one thing.”

“You truly—”

“Tell me.”

I cut him off, my voice flat.

“Not how your only child died. I want to know what kind of lunatics turned you into one.”

“……!”

“Tell me that.”

A heavy silence followed. Every second felt like fifteen minutes before Baeksang finally opened his mouth.

“Gansu. Great Snow Mountain.”

Haa…

His breath trembled. White vapor spilled into the freezing air of the underground prison, like the heavy snow that must have fallen that day.

“Back then, we were leading the Southern Army alongside the Zhongnan Sect.”

I swallowed the words that tried to burst out of me.

*Oh, fuck. The Zhongnan Sect.*
