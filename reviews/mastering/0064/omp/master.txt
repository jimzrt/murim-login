# Chapter 64

*Slowly. Don’t rush.*

Jin Wikyung steadied his breathing. This was a crucial moment. One mistake could ruin everything.

*I’ve done well so far. I just need to keep it up.*

A bead of cold sweat slid down his cheek, but Jin Wikyung was concentrating so fiercely that he didn’t even notice.

His pupils contracted. His faint, trembling breath stopped.

*Now!*

His eyes flew open, and his hand shot out like lightning.

Bang!

“My lord!”

Wipeng burst into the study with a thunderous crash.

“There’s chaos outside… What’s wrong?”

A trembling voice escaped Jin Wikyung’s lips.

“I was almost finished.”

“What?”

“I poured my heart and soul into it for two hours. All for this one moment.”

“I don’t know what you’re talking about, but that’s not important right—”

“It is important!”

With an anguished cry, Jin Wikyung clutched at his hair.

“It was important! Who are you to decide otherwise?”

Tremble, tremble.

He looked so grief-stricken and resentful that Wipeng forgot the urgency of the situation and stared at his lord.

*I was too hasty.*

It had been five days since the war ended. Jin Wikyung had been buried under an unprecedented workload dealing with the aftermath. Of course his nerves were frayed. If Wipeng had driven a normally generous man to such anger, the fault had to be his.

“I apologize. I was in such a hurry that I lost my head.”

Wipeng’s sincere apology softened Jin Wikyung’s anger.

“Please be more careful next time.”

But it did nothing to ease the sorrow saturating his voice. Feeling even guiltier, Wipeng said, “If it’s something I can take care of, let me help.”

“No need. What’s done is done. I’ll have to start the drawing over from the beginning.”

“I can draw it for you inst—what?”

Wipeng approached Jin Wikyung with a sinking feeling. A huge sheet of paper covered the entire table.

“What is this?”

His voice was drier than desert sand, but Jin Wikyung didn’t notice.

“I tried to capture the battle from five days ago in a painting.”

“Not the battle. The Third Young Master.”

“Same thing. Anyway, it would have been perfect once I finished Taekyung’s eyes…”

“But I barged in and made your brush slip.”

“No. Now that I think about it, this was for the best.”

Jin Wikyung sighed.

“Heaven must have refused to let my paltry skill portray Taekyung. Don’t you agree?”

“…”

Wipeng silently picked up the painting.

Rip! Rip-rip-rip!

“No! My *Birth of a Hero*!”

“You even gave it a title?”

Wipeng pressed a hand to his forehead as Jin Wikyung wailed in anguish. Whenever—very, very occasionally—his lord acted like this, Wipeng felt the urge to retire to the countryside.

*Should I just open a martial arts school?*

Watching his lord gather the scraps with a devastated expression made retirement seem more appealing than ever.

“I was going to keep it in my private collection!”

“Forget your private collection. You need to get outside right now.”

“Why?”

“Young Master Jin has returned.”

Jin Wikyung stopped picking up the scraps and tilted his head.

“Did the youngest go somewhere?”

“Not that Young Master Jin.”

Disbelief entered Jin Wikyung’s eyes. Wipeng nodded.

“Yes. The Second Young Master has returned.”

“Mukyung!”

His face lit up. His second younger brother had returned after three years. He wanted to run out and greet him at once.

“Where is he now?”

“At the Third Young Master’s residence.”

“Mukyung hated the youngest, yet he went to see him the moment he arrived? Maybe he’s finally ready to act like an older brother. He’s grown up. Hahaha!”

Jin Wikyung burst into hearty laughter.

Rumble-rumble-rumble.

A sudden thunderous crash shook the estate, followed by screams.

—The building is collapsing! Everyone, get out of the way!

—Get more people over here!

—The Third Young Master’s residence is collapsiiiing!

Jin Wikyung blinked.

“I could have sworn I just heard that the youngest’s residence was collapsing.”

“Brothers grow up by fighting, don’t they?”

“What are you talking about… Don’t tell me—”

“It’s nothing. Go ahead and start another painting.”

Wipeng, wearing the serene expression of a man who had attained enlightenment, spread a fresh sheet of paper across the desk.

“I think you should call this one *The Hero Getting Beaten Like a Dog by His Second Brother*.”

Whoooosh!

Jin Wikyung unleashed a Peak-level movement technique and raced away like the wind. Wipeng watched him disappear and sighed.

*Should I really open a martial arts school?*

The question had been weighing on him more and more lately.

* * *

Jin Mukyung.

Twenty-three years old. Known by the epithet Heaven Shaking Sword.

A martial arts genius who had reached the Peak realm at barely twenty.

More importantly, he was the second older brother of this body—Jin Taekyung’s body. Naturally, I’d been curious about him.

*I can’t remember what Jin Mukyung—or rather, my second brother—was like.*

Amnesia made an excellent excuse. When I asked Jin Wikyung, he told me everything about him.

*You won’t be able to see him anytime soon. He’s very far away.*

*Where is he?*

*At Heaven’s Gate Temple in Henan. The heartless brat hasn’t shown his face once in three years.*

Despite his words, Jin Wikyung had looked proud.

Like a parent whose child had gotten into Harvard.

*What’s he like?*

*Hmm. He’s kind. People often misunderstand him, but he’s definitely a good kid.*

*Were we close?*

*…Of course. I think you were? Yes, you were close.*

*Ah. Right.*

I hadn’t thought much of it at the time. Once I learned how far Henan was from the Jin Family of Taiyuan, I lost interest altogether.

*It’s not like I’ll still be in Murim by then.*

The journey took several weeks of nonstop riding.

That was also why Jin Mukyung hadn’t shown so much as the tip of his nose before the war ended. And yet…

*I never expected to meet him like this.*

His appearance had caught me completely off guard.

I gave him an awkward smile and held out my hand.

“Long time no see, hyung.”

The “hyung” who had been staring at me silently took my hand.

“Yes. It has been a long time.”

*He seems nicer than I expected.*

Just as Jin Wikyung had said, people must have misunderstood him because of his fierce eyes.

The thought eased my tension, and my smile became more natural.

“How’ve you been?”

Jin Mukyung gave me a faint smile in return.

“Well enough. But, youngest.”

“Yeah?”

“You’ve gotten awfully casual with me.”

Whoom!

The next thing I knew, I was flying toward the wall. Jin Mukyung had hurled me with tremendous force.

*What the hell?*

I twisted in midair, lightly kicked off the wall, and landed on my feet. Jin Mukyung watched me and smirked.

“Well, well.”

I didn’t like where this was going.

I scratched the back of my head.

“Weren’t we close?”

“We were. My fist and your body.”

“Ah.”

I was insane to believe Jin Wikyung. The man doted on his younger brothers like his life depended on it.

*Shit. He should’ve explained that properly.*

Jin Mukyung held out his fist.

“This is your oldest friend. Say hello.”

“Hello.”

Ominously, his smile widened.

“Our youngest has certainly grown. Acting cocky in front of his big brother.”

Whoooosh!

Jin Mukyung charged like lightning and threw a punch. The air tore before his fist.

*He’s serious?*

The blow carried no internal energy, but the raw force behind it was immense.

I jerked my head aside in alarm.

Bang!

The wooden wall exploded. Fists rained down through the splinters scattering in the air.

Bababang!

Face, chest, shoulder, stomach.

The punches seemed wild and random, but his movements flowed smoothly, and the area they covered was as tightly woven as a net.

“A fist technique?”

“Been a while since you had a taste of the Reformation Fist, hasn’t it?”

*Fuck, what kind of name is that for a fist technique?*

As I cursed inwardly, the first form of the Reformation Fist slammed into my abdomen.

Whump!

“Hup.”

“I’m not finished.”

I fought through the breath-stealing pain and blocked the next punch with my forearm. The dull impact made my bones throb.

“You blocked it?”

Babababam!

It hurt. It hurt like hell.

Jin Mukyung outclassed me in every way—strength, speed, everything.

But…

*This is more manageable than I expected.*

Was it because he wasn’t using internal energy?

At first, all I could do was take a one-sided beating. But after a while, his attacks slowly began to come into focus.

Whoosh!

Jin Mukyung’s fist sliced through empty air. A precise read. A clean dodge.

He looked at me in surprise.

“You have improved quite a bit.”

I steadied my breathing and grinned.

Since it had come to this, I figured we might as well have a proper fight.

“Not quite a bit. A lot. Haven’t you heard the rumors?”

“I have. More than enough.”

Jin Mukyung smirked.

“Then show me how much of them is true.”

Shwaaak!

His hand shot toward my wrist with a sharp whistle.

*Like hell!*

My eyes widened as I struck at the incoming hand.

Or tried to.

Tap, tat-tat-tat!

Five attacks and defenses passed between us in less than a second. Then the match was decided in an instant.

Clamp!

“What the hell is this pathetic grappling technique?”

After a series of strange movements, Jin Mukyung caught my wrist and twisted it into a lock. He stared at me as though I were hopeless.

Getting caught so easily had already made my blood boil. His next words poured fuel on the fire.

“Again.”

“…What exactly are you trying to do?”

“I already knew the rumors were nonsense. Now I need to teach some manners to a little brother who doesn’t know his place.”

Jin Mukyung released my wrist and crooked a finger.

“Come at me. I won’t go easy on you this time.”

I stared at him in silence.

Jin Mukyung was unquestionably a master far beyond me. The wall of the Peak realm was too high for my current abilities to overcome.

I knew that. I knew all of it.

*This is pissing me off.*

And suddenly, I wanted to know.

How far could I go?

Just how strong was Jin Mukyung, the genius whose reputation had spread so far?

It was a desire to test myself—not as a Hunter, but as a martial artist of Murim.

*Let’s do this.*

Jin Mukyung was the first to notice the change in me.

“I didn’t know you could make a face like that.”

“This is how I always look.”

He smiled, intrigued.

“Fine. That’s all well and good… but you’re still speaking awfully casually.”

At that moment, Jin Mukyung’s fist blurred.

Whoosh! Whump!

My vision flashed. Even with every nerve focused, I hadn’t managed to evade the attack completely.

Jin Mukyung looked from his fist to me and back again.

“That shouldn’t have happened.”

The punch had been aimed at my temple. If it had landed cleanly, it would have ended the fight.

He hadn’t used internal energy, but I had still managed to partially evade a full-powered blow from a Peak master.

“See? I’ve improved a lot.”

“I’ll admit that much. You’re still not even a quarter as good as the rumors claim.”

“Don’t worry. I’ll start catching up now.”

“You’ll catch up to me? How long do you think that’ll take?”

I answered.

“Starting this very moment.”

“Last fifteen minutes, and I’ll call you my big brother.”

Jin Mukyung’s form blurred once more. But this time, I was one step ahead.

*Assign ten points to Agility.*

Ten level-ups gained through the war. A hundred points lying dormant in my Status Window.

Ten of them answered my command.

Shwaaaak!

The change was instantaneous. At the same time, certainty filled me.

I could perfectly evade Jin Mukyung’s fist as it flew toward my face.

Whoosh!

*Too slow.*

Without hesitation, I turned my head.

Whump!

*…Damn it. I should’ve used ten more points.*
