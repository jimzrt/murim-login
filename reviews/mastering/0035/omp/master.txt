# Chapter 35

A hospital room in Medicine King Hall.

Hyuk Mujin, wound tight in bandages from head to toe, let out a groan.

“I’m dying.”

Han Yeop, lying beside him in much the same state, replied,

“It’s a miracle we didn’t die.”

“Right?”

“Right.”

A brief silence followed. Both of them were remembering that day.

A single palm strike from Jopil. An attack they couldn’t have blocked even if they’d known it was coming. Whenever Hyuk Mujin closed his eyes, he saw that bastard’s red-hot palm. It might haunt his nightmares for the rest of his life.

“That monster. How is something like that even possible?”

“He’s a Peak master.”

Unlike the dejected Hyuk Mujin, Han Yeop sounded calm.

“Hey, punk. Doesn’t it bother you at all?”

“What?”

“That…”

Hyuk Mujin found himself at a loss for words. Right. Maybe it didn’t have to bother him.

“No, that’s not what I meant…”

“I know.”

“Huh?”

“I know what the deputy squad leader is thinking. I know what you want to say.”

“…”

“When I first came to, a lot of things went through my mind. The relief of being alive. The helplessness I felt that day. The despair over my own martial arts.”

Hyuk Mujin fell silent. Han Yeop had it exactly right. Jopil’s Flame Divine Palm had broken bones and torn heart meridians, but the real wound lay elsewhere.

One question had been circling through his head ever since that day.

*Can I reach that realm?*

Even *overwhelming* wasn’t enough to describe it. All his effort, all his pride in his martial arts, had been ripped up by the roots.

“You’ve shaken all that off?”

Han Yeop shook his head.

“Then?”

“I admitted that I’m weak. It wasn’t even hard. I’ve always known. But…”

“…”

“I’ll get stronger. As strong as Jopil. No—far stronger than Jopil.”

Han Yeop continued, his voice firm.

“Once I started thinking that way, I felt happy. If I become a Peak master, I can get that strong too. That sort of thing.”

“A Peak master…”

The Peak realm was a domain granted to only a rare few. Coming from Han Yeop, who was barely second- or third-rate, the declaration was almost laughable.

But Hyuk Mujin didn’t sneer.

*You’ve changed too.*

So much had changed. The situation. The people.

Even Hyuk Mujin, once a timid second-rate martial artist, had joined that current before he knew it. His heart suddenly surged, and the words burst out before he could stop them.

“I’ll get even stronger.”

Han Yeop’s eyes widened, then he flashed a grin.

“You will. First, though, we have to catch up to one person.”

They both thought of the same person.

Jin Taekyung.

He was already racing far ahead of them. What was he doing now?

Behind the two men, lost in thought, the door that had been left slightly ajar slid shut.

* * *

I closed the door and turned away, a shudder running through me.

“Ugh, shit.”

These idiots were filming a teenage coming-of-age drama in a hospital room. I’d stood there quietly listening to hear what they were talking about, and what a spectacle it had been.

If I’d gone in, I might’ve ended up swearing brotherhood with them.

*Still, I have to admit it’s kind of admirable.*

Hadn’t we gone through life and death together, in our own way? Besides, those two had risked their lives to help me. Saying I didn’t feel so much as a shred of affection for them would have been a lie.

*Could this be the last time?*

In two days, I would set out in charge of the main force’s rear guard, while those two and the rest of the reconnaissance squad would remain at the family estate to recover from their injuries. And probably…

*By then, I’ll have logged out.*

I was close to meeting the last condition of the Logout Quest: 500 Fame. Today’s visit to Medicine King Hall was a farewell of sorts.

You could call it the nostalgia filter of a sergeant nearing discharge.

*Well, there’s no need to announce anything. Seeing their faces is enough.*

I’d already looked in on the other members of the reconnaissance squad. Now that I’d seen Han Yeop and Hyuk Mujin too, there was only one more place to stop by…

Huh?

“Oh!”

A rag doll in one hand. Snacks in the other.

A little girl with her hair tied in a cute ribbon shouted, her eyes round.

“It’s the mind-reading uncle!”

“…”

Couldn’t you call me Big Brother? I gave Soyul a sad little wave.

* * *

“Young Master Jin.”

“Benefactor!”

The reactions came the moment I entered the room. Gong Yacheong, still pale, tried to rise, and I stopped him. Then I pulled Socheon, who had dropped into a full bow, back to his feet.

“Stay lying down. You too, punk. Get up. Do I look old enough to take a bow from you?”

“A hundred bows wouldn’t be enough to repay what you’ve done for us.”

Tears were already welling in both their eyes. Soyul, who didn’t understand any of it, hugged her rag doll tight and scampered over to cling to her brother.

“Big Brother, are you wearing the Benefactor’s kindness? Show me too. Is it pretty?”

*Uh. That’s not clothing.*

Leaving the chattering Soyul behind, I spoke to Gong Yacheong.

“How are you feeling?”

“Couldn’t be better. I’ll need to recuperate for a while, though.”

A faint smile spread across Gong Yacheong’s lips.

“It’s all thanks to you, Young Master.”

“I didn’t do it for praise. I even left you behind once.”

“That was my choice. And you came back.”

I remembered the night Jopil had chased us. Gong Yacheong had been badly poisoned and had wanted to be left behind. Just as he had, I’d had to make a choice.

After agonizing over it, I had decided to go back for him.

*I regretted it like crazy.*

It had been insane. Gambling my life for a mere NPC in a game. But now I thought I understood why I’d made that choice.

What had I seen in those young siblings who’d lost their parents? Who had Gong Yacheong and the reconnaissance squad reminded me of…?

“Young Master Jin?”

Gong Yacheong’s voice pulled me back.

“It’s nothing. I just—just had something on my mind.”

“Ah, I heard the news as well. Is that what this is about?”

“What news?”

“They say there will be a major battle soon.”

*Wasn’t that military intelligence?*

If Gong Yacheong, who never left his hospital room, knew about it, then anyone in the Jin Family of Taiyuan with eyes and ears must already know.

If there was even one spy among us, we might as well set up a loudspeaker aimed at North Korea.

*Is this war really going to be all right?*

I shook off the thought. What did it matter to me? Soon enough, none of this would have anything to do with me anyway.

The System alerts ringing even now were proof.

Ding.

> **System**
>
> — Rumors about you continue to spread.
>
> — Fame increases by 1.

They said rumors had no feet, yet traveled a thousand li. After I’d taken down Jopil, my name seemed to have started spreading in earnest.

I opened the Quest Window for a quick look. I had about fifty Fame left to go.

Logout was practically a done deal.

“It will be a battle with our family’s fate at stake.”

Of course, they had no way of knowing my situation. I listened quietly to Gong Yacheong and Socheon for a while, then rose from my seat.

“I think I should be going.”

“Benefactor.”

Socheon looked disappointed, but Gong Yacheong stopped him.

“Let him go.”

“But…”

“That’s enough.”

Socheon lowered his head glumly. Soyul, who had been playing with the doll by herself, looked up at me with huge eyes.

“You’re leaving already, mister?”

“I said Big Brother.”

“Okay. Mister.”

I gave her chubby cheek a light pinch and was about to turn away when Gong Yacheong called out to me.

“Young Master Jin. Even if you must leave, shouldn’t you take what you left behind?”

“What I left behind?”

I thought about it for a moment, but there was no way I’d left anything. Thanks to the cheat known as Inventory, I always traveled light.

“There’s nothing like tha—what is this?”

Gong Yacheong was holding a long bundle.

“You were in no state to collect it at the time. Now that its owner is here, returning it is only right.”

*What is it?*

Still bewildered, I accepted the bundle. It was fairly heavy. Just as I was about to check what was inside, Gong Yacheong spoke.

“Unwrap it in your quarters. Don’t let anyone else see.”

*Did he put a golden calf in here?*

* * *

I unwrapped the bundle the moment I reached my quarters. Then I understood what Gong Yacheong’s last words had meant.

*These really are the kind of things people would covet if they saw them.*

An old booklet. A small box. And a familiar sword.

To someone in Murim, these were worth far more than any golden calf. And I had the ability to judge their value more accurately than anyone.

*Check Item.*

Ding.

> **System**
>
> **Item Window**
>
> **Flame Divine Palm**
>
> **Type:** Martial Arts Manual
>
> **Grade:** Supreme Peak
>
> **Restriction:** Holder of Scorching Yang Qi
>
> **Description:** One of the Fire Gate Clan’s secret techniques. A martial art based on powerful fire qi.
>
> **Effect:** Acquired Flame Divine Palm
>
> **Item Window**
>
> **Fire Divine Elixir**
>
> **Type:** Spiritual Elixir
>
> **Grade:** Peak
>
> **Restriction:** None
>
> **Description:** A spiritual elixir made according to the Fire Gate Clan’s secret formula.
>
> **Effect:** Grants thirty years of internal energy when consumed. However, if the user cannot control the powerful fire qi contained within the elixir, they may meet a horrific end.
>
> **Item Window**
>
> **Nameless Sword**
>
> **Type:** Sword
>
> **Grade:** None
>
> **Restriction:** None
>
> **Description:** Made of ten-thousand-year cold iron, this sword is exceptionally sharp and durable. After drinking a great deal of blood over many years, it transformed of its own accord. Special conditions are required to draw out the sword’s power.
>
> **Effect:** Unknown

“This is insane.”

It really was insane. A Supreme Peak martial arts manual. A spiritual elixir that granted thirty years of internal energy. And a sword I didn’t fully understand but that looked ridiculously good.

They said a tiger left its pelt behind when it died, but Jopil had left three treasures behind.

*Shit. It always gives me the good stuff after everything’s over.*

Goddamn trash game. If it was going to give me something, it should’ve done it sooner. Coughing it up only after everything was finished made my blood pressure spike.

*Still, these items are seriously good.*

Just reading the descriptions was enough to tempt me. What if I absorbed the elixir and learned Flame Divine Palm? If fire started shooting out of my hands like it had for Jopil…

*That would be fucking cool.*

But the thought lasted only a moment. They said a soldier nearing discharge had to watch out even for falling leaves. I didn’t want to take the elixir, screw something up, and hold my own self-immolation ceremony.

*Remember this. Safety first. Safety first.*

Talking about safety at this point was pretty funny, but I wasn’t stupid enough to gulp it down on the spot.

*It’s almost over.*

One slip and I’d be done for. I stuffed all the items into my Inventory. Somewhere in the dead of night, someone must have been talking about me over drinks, because a System alert rang out.

Ding.

> **System**
>
> — Fame has increased by 1.

* * *

At that hour, the Head Elder was walking through the garden. This was the appointed date and place. The man in the darkness was never late.

“The moon is very bright.”

“So it is.”

As he had said, tonight’s full moon was exceptionally bright.

“When I was young, I loved the moon… but the older I got, the more I found myself thinking.”

“Thinking what?”

“That I wished there were no moon. Something like that.”

“A world without charm.”

“What’s wrong with a little less charm? I make my living at night, so I’d be delighted if the moon disappeared.”

*Nightlife, huh.*

The man prattled on in the flippant, cheerful tone of a kept man, but the Head Elder knew the truth. He possessed formidable martial arts and was a superb assassin.

The wind seemed to carry the smell of blood.

“Ah, right. How is the work progressing?”

“Smoothly. Even the troop deployments are complete.”

“Don’t overdo it. If the clever Lesser Family Head catches a whiff of anything, the whole plan will fall apart.”

“Don’t worry. I didn’t even need to make a move.”

“Heaven is helping us.”

“And your side?”

“You’re asking the obvious.”

The light reproach in his words made the Head Elder fall silent.

*Of course.*

They were like sea fog. Their identities lay hidden beneath the mist, and even if he reached out and stirred it with his hand, there was no substance to grasp. All that remained was a damp palm and an unpleasant feeling.

*But they have power.*

The power to make him Family Head of the Jin Family of Taiyuan and the sole hegemon of Shanxi.

It was an ambition he had harbored for half his life. The Head Elder was prepared to do anything.

“All preparations are complete.”

“Mine as well.”

On the day the two great sects that divided Shanxi between them clashed…

Everything would end, and everything would begin anew.
