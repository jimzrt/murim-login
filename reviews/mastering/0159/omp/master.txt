# Chapter 159

Fifty points to Agility. And fifty points to Strength.

A hundred points in total. I had poured them all in at once—a massive number that would normally take ten Level-ups to earn—but I didn’t regret spending a single one.

*Points really are the best. Always thrilling. Always new.*

The foot kicking off the ground and the hand gripping the spear shaft told me the same thing.

I was far stronger than I had been only a few seconds ago.

And at last, I had taken the first step toward winning this duel.

*I’ll end this quickly.*

*Whoooooosh!*

The instant the spearhead came crashing down with a terrifying roar, a violet haze rose from Cheongpung’s entire body.

*The Zaha Divine Technique.*

Proof that Cheongpung had finally begun fighting at full strength. But one crucial factor still remained in this duel.

*Whoooosh!*

Sure enough, a streak of Sword Energy surged from Cheongpung’s waist.

Sword Energy, the exclusive domain of Peak masters, possessed enough cutting power to slice through steel.

I hadn’t yet crossed the wall into the Peak realm. I couldn’t meet it head-on.

I had to either retreat or continue my attack knowing the spear would be destroyed.

But…

*What is this feeling?*

A strange sense of déjà vu came over me. My heart pounded, and my vision sharpened. In a world that seemed to have slowed down, the cool spear shaft in my grip trembled.

*Vrrr. Vrrrr.*

A cool head. A hot heart.

That was how I had fought for seven years…but this time was different. This was instinct. Nothing but instinct ruled my entire being.

*I can do it.*

As though possessed, I put more strength behind the descending spearhead.

Forty-five years of internal energy raced through my fingertips and into the spearhead. The trembling in my grip intensified, and Cheongpung’s violet Sword Energy blazed before my eyes.

That was all.

*Boom!*

Two enormous forces collided.

A thunderous roar. A brutal blast of wind. Cheongpung’s eyes flying wide.

And then…

*It’s fine.*

The snow-white spearhead was still there.

It pressed down on Cheongpung’s Sword Energy without so much as a hairline crack, trembling all the while.

*Vrrrrrrr.*

It sounded like hundreds of bees beating their wings.

*How is this possible?*

Was this another effect of raising my Strength? Or simply a coincidence?

At that moment, the answer rang out.

> **System**
>
> - You have finished preparing to step into a new realm.
> - Your weapon is resonating with your qi.
> - Quest *Beyond the Wall* has been generated.

*Ah.*

It wasn’t an effect of raising my Strength. Nor was it a coincidence.

This was the natural order of things. After enduring countless hardships, I had finally come face-to-face with the wall. Now I had to cross it.

The wall of the Peak realm—a wall countless others had failed to overcome.

*A Peak master.*

True superhumans who wielded Sword Energy, or Aura.

A shiver ran through me at the mere thought. Memories flashed before my eyes like a cheap monochrome film.

A mere F-rank Hunter, one of countless others, had made it this far. There had been countless crises, and someone had sacrificed themselves along the way.

> **System**
>
> **Quest:** *Beyond the Wall*
>
> Would you like to accept this Quest?
>
> **Y / N**

I had crossed the boundary between life and death countless times. If I couldn’t cross a mere wall to the Peak realm, I would be ashamed of everything I had lived through.

*Obviously yes.*

> **System**
>
> - You have accepted the Quest!

*Ding.*

As the cheerful System notification rang out, a grin spread across my face.

My thoughts had taken a while, but barely any time had passed. I whispered to Cheongpung, who was staring up at me in bewilderment.

“Now for spicy Puramyeon.”[^3]

I’ll show you what Korean spice tastes like.

* * *

*Krrrnnng.*

My Strength had risen by twenty-five percent compared to roughly a minute ago.

The difference was like heaven and earth. Cheongpung was visibly flustered by my sudden transformation.

“B-Benefactor. Where did this sudden strength come from?”

“It’s the power of Korean rice. Especially gukbap.”[^1]

“Pardon?”

“I used to eat two bowls of pork-bone hangover soup every morning. With five bowls of rice.”

“What does that even—gasp!”

*Krrrnnng.*

Cheongpung gasped as my force continued to build.

He had Sword Energy and the Zaha Divine Technique, but neither was having much effect. Filled to the brim with my qi and resonating with it, the spear had grown sturdy enough that even Sword Energy could no longer cut through it.

*I have a real shot at winning.*

But Cheongpung wasn’t going down that easily.

“Hup!”

With a short shout, he unleashed tremendous force and knocked the spearhead upward.

It had only risen a handspan, but Cheongpung had escaped the pressure for an instant, and he didn’t waste the opportunity.

“I’m sorry. I underestimated you, Benefactor.”

Before he had even finished speaking, an invisible force shot from his palm.

The Taeeul Miri Palm. A secret ultimate technique of Huashan, personally taught to him by the Sword Saint.

“Hngh!”

*Bam-bam-bam!*

Sharp pain shot through me as I staggered backward five steps in succession.

Thank goodness I had improved my bones and muscles as well as my Sinews and Meridians with every Level-up. Otherwise, I would have been in serious trouble.

“Damn, that hurt.”

Cheongpung stared at me with eyes as wide as a startled rabbit’s.

“A Seven-Star Taeeul Miri Palm…”[^2]

“Don’t say that. You’ll make me crave cider.”

“Pardon?”

There it was. Exactly the reaction I had expected.

The instant Cheongpung asked what I meant, my spear was already thrusting toward his chest.

A perfectly timed surprise attack. But there was no way it would end with just that.

As if answering my thoughts, Sword Energy slammed into the spearhead.

*Clang!*

*I wasn’t expecting it to work.*

With no expectations came no disappointment. My emotions remained steady, and so did my forms.

*Next, the waist.*

*Whoooosh!*

Cheongpung twisted at the waist as the spear moved.

The gust raised by the spearhead silently sliced through his shirt. A faint line of blood appeared across the exposed skin.

A small gain was still a gain.

*Next, the neck.*

Following through with the turn, I swung the spear shaft at Cheongpung’s neck.

*Boom!*

That was not a sound a human body should make when struck by a steel spear.

But Cheongpung had martial arts. He had the Zaha Divine Technique, one of the greatest internal-energy cultivation techniques under heaven as well as a protective qi art.

I clicked my tongue as the violet energy around him grew denser.

*Even so, how can he take absolutely no damage? This is outright cheating.*

Talk about being born with a plum-blossom silver spoon. How was anyone supposed to duel with a guy like this?

But I didn’t have time to complain. Cheongpung had closed the distance in barely half a step, and the Sword Energy he scattered filled my vision.

*Whoooosh!*

Flowers bloomed from the tip of his sword.

The movement was so fluid it was beautiful. But if I let myself become mesmerized, I would soon find myself standing before a sign for Mount Beimang.

The Plum Blossom Sword Technique I had watched until now was a frightening martial art composed of extremely intricate, complex forms.

When Cheongpung wielded it, I felt as though I were trapped in a rainstorm.

But…

*I can see it.*

I opened my eyes wide. My breathing slowed, and my vision sharpened. My heartbeat sounded like thunder, and the spear trembled in my hands.

*Vrrrr. Vrrrrr.*

The spear was crying out.

At that moment, the spear and I were practically one.

Its shaft no longer felt cool in my hands.

*Shishishishik!*

Sword and spear blurred together.

I continuously deflected and knocked aside the Sword Energy pouring down like rain.

Ten times, twenty, maybe thirty…

Cheongpung was more tenacious and precise than anyone I had ever fought.

When I countered the Sword Energy driving toward my waist, the Taeeul Miri Palm came flying at me. When I avoided the Taeeul Miri Palm, the Crouching Tiger Fist aimed for my chest.

*Thud!*

The shoulder I had used to block the Crouching Tiger Fist throbbed.

A flash of pain slowed me for an instant. Cheongpung opened his clenched fist.

Air burst from the tips of all five fingers.

*Ah, damn it. Right, he had that too.*

The moment I realized it, I bent backward like a bow.

The Plum Blossom Five-Point Finger. He had shown it only once during his duel with Jin Mukyung.

*Pip-pip-pit!*

Five streams of finger force grazed the bridge of my nose and my earlobe by a hair.

*He really is strong.*

It struck me again: Cheongpung was no ordinary swordsman.

He wasn’t merely the heir of the Sword Saint Mae Jonghak. He had mastered Huashan’s secret techniques as well. In other words, he was a complete gift set of Huashan martial arts.

I wiped away the blood seeping from my wounds and spoke.

“You’re coming on a little strong, aren’t you?”

“That’s what I’d like to say to you, Benefactor. Honestly, you surprised me.”

“Why? Because someone who seemed like no big deal is fighting much better than you expected?”

“Yes!”

“…”

“You’re much better than I thought. Seriously. And…”

“And?”

“This is fun.”

Watching the faint smile on Cheongpung’s lips, I realized something.

Competitive pride aside, this guy simply loved martial arts themselves. He was showing me just how frightening a genius who enjoyed what he did could be.

“Aren’t you the same as me, Benefactor?”

“Young Hero Cheong, do you know what my dream is?”

“…”

“To retire in one piece with a building in my name. I’ll collect rent and spend my old age in comfort as a landlord.”

“A landlord?”

“Put simply, um… Right. Think of it as someone who lives up in the heavens.”

“Ah-ha.”

Cheongpung nodded as though he understood.

“You want to become the Martial God.”

“No, that’s not what I meant. Where did the Martial God come from?”

“Is that not it?”

“No. It’s completely different.”

“But you do seem to like martial arts.”

“Me? Not that much.”

“You look awfully happy for someone who doesn’t.”

Only then did I realize that the corners of my mouth had been raised this whole time.

*Since when?*

As I stood lost in thought, Cheongpung smiled brightly.

“If you’ve caught your breath, shall we start again?”

“…How long have you known?”

“I’ve used that trick on my grandfather many times.”

People really did think alike. I burst out laughing.

“You’re more perceptive than I expected.”

“My sword is faster.”

*Tsssss.*

The moment violet Sword Energy surged from Cheongpung’s blade, the spear in my hands trembled violently.

* * *

*Clang!*

The First Form of the Jin Family’s Spear Technique.

The spear thrust straight ahead was blocked by the blade of Cheongpung’s sword.

The Jin Family’s Spear Technique was an honest martial art. Simple and rough.

It was clearly a First Rate martial art, but its underlying principles were far too basic.

*Compared to Huashan’s secret techniques, it falls hopelessly short.*

Martial arts ultimately came down to combinations of movements.

Thrusting, striking, cutting.

By circulating internal energy, adjusting the angle by the slightest degree, and linking one form into the next, one could create endless variations.

I had finally mastered them after arduous effort, but the Jin Family’s Spear Technique and the Jin Family’s Manoeuvre Technique lacked that sort of subtlety.

*Whoooooosh! Clang!*

In that respect, my opponent was a terrible match for me.

Cheongpung was a Peak master with an eye sharp enough to see through my martial arts at a glance.

As if he had decided to stop holding back altogether, he began pressing the attack in earnest.

*Whoosh! Bam-bam!*

*Sword techniques, fist techniques, palm techniques, hand techniques, even claw techniques.*

He really was a martial arts genius. He chained together roughly ten different martial arts, yet every movement meshed perfectly with the next like interlocking gears.

His eyes shone like morning stars, and his breathing remained slow and steady.

*So this is the Sword Saint’s Disciple.*

An excellent Master. Excellent martial arts.

And talent on top of it all.

The world had always been unfair. And over the past seven years, I had learned how to adapt to that unfair world.

*Persistently and patiently. Never giving up.*

That was my way.

I had never wanted to be the tortoise that caught up with the rabbit far ahead.

Crossing the finish line was enough.

Surviving and retiring. That was the greatest dream a lowest-rank Hunter could have.

*I had to claw my way to survival.*

I learned everything in the Gates. Goblins armed with rusty knives and poison needles had been my sparring partners.

Unlike a duel in Murim, every fight put my life on the line. But that was exactly why I learned so quickly.

And then…

*I came to Murim.*

*Clang-clang-clang! Slash!*

My side burned. I could feel the blood flowing, but I didn’t make the stupid mistake of checking the wound.

The human body was weaker than you might think, yet stronger in its own way. Even if I bled for a while, it would stop once the blood clotted.

“Ugh!”

Funny enough, Cheongpung’s weak stomach gave me an opening.

I charged the moment he faltered.

*Second Form of the Jin Family’s Spear Technique.*

*Whoooooosh!*

Had it been about a week after I fell into Murim?

I had discovered two martial arts manuals in a dust-covered archive: the Jin Family’s Spear Technique and the Jin Family’s Manoeuvre Technique.

If anyone asked why I had learned them, the answer was simple.

*To survive.*

To me, Murim was another Gate.

I had to learn if I wanted to survive.

That was how I entered the world of martial arts. Before I knew it, several months had passed.

And everything had changed.

*Shishishishik! Bam!*

*Hngh.*

The Plum Blossom Five-Point Finger.

The instant I avoided the five streams of finger force, the Taeeul Miri Palm struck me.

I had poured in a full hundred points, but I still hadn’t caught up to Cheongpung.

However, against someone who already knew the Jin Family’s Spear Technique inside and out, I had no choice but to keep driving straight ahead.

*Third Form of the Jin Family’s Spear Technique. Fourth Form.*

The Jin Family’s Spear Technique grew more powerful the farther it advanced. Its footwork was designed to match.

Its essence lay in pressuring the opponent with simple forms tailored for actual combat.

*If my teeth won’t do it, I’ll use my gums.*

My understanding of martial arts might be lacking, but I was not inferior when it came to stats.

For every step I advanced, Cheongpung retreated.

Riding the momentum, I thrust and slashed with all my strength. Even as I panted until my mouth tasted sweet, I suddenly recalled the conversation I had just had with Cheongpung.

*But you like martial arts, don’t you?*

*Me? Not that much.*

*Then why have you been smiling this whole time?*

That was obviously because…

*It’s fun.*

*Whoooooosh! Boom!*

Sword and spear collided.

The bluestone tiles laid neatly across the training ground shattered into powder and scattered through the air.

As the thick cloud of dust cleared, Cheongpung came into view.

His smile was clear and bright, completely at odds with the situation we were in.

“Martial arts are fun, aren’t they?”

After thinking for a moment, I nodded without a word.

“Why did you say they weren’t earlier?”

“Let’s just call it a difference in how we’ve lived.”

For seven years, I had lived as though something invisible were chasing me.

Guilt, perhaps. Or a sense of responsibility.

Or maybe it was simply self-satisfaction—a way to rationalize it all.

As he watched me hesitate, Cheongpung spoke.

“My grandfather once told me something. He said, ‘Martial arts? They’re nothing special!’”

“Hmm? The Sword Saint said that?”

“It’s true.”

Cheongpung continued with an aggrieved expression.

“He said it wasn’t martial arts, but empty space.[^4] Since it’s empty to begin with, you just accept it as it is and fill it.”

“Accept it as it is and fill it.”

“I’m serious. You can ask my grandfather later…”

Cheongpung’s voice gradually faded before cutting off altogether.

I closed my eyes.

In the pitch-black darkness, forgetting both the passage of time and where I was, I muttered a single sentence as though possessed.

*Accept it as it is and fill it…*

Why did those words keep catching at my heart?

The more I repeated them, the harder my heart pounded and the more my entire body tingled.

It felt as though the boulder weighing down my chest had begun to shift.

*It isn’t martial arts. It’s empty space. Accept it as it is and fill it…*

I didn’t know whether several seconds or several hours passed.

In the darkness, even time itself seemed to have stopped.

A day, two days, three days—even a year could have passed in that suffocating darkness without seeming strange.

Then I opened my eyes.

“Benefactor, how do you feel?”

Instead of answering Cheongpung’s question, I looked around.

Hyuk Mujin was dozing in the corner, while the ink-dark sky was filled with stars that looked ready to come pouring down at any moment.

Was this really the world I knew?

“Was my grandfather right?”

His second question.

I answered in a hoarse voice.

“No. Not at all.”

Martial arts were fucking hard.

Still…

*Ding.*

> **System**
>
> - Quest *Beyond the Wall* has been successfully completed!
> - Your class has changed to **Peak Master**!

Now I knew one thing.

[^1]: *Gukbap* is soup served with rice; *bone haejangguk* is a hearty pork-bone soup traditionally eaten as hangover food.

[^2]: The Korean word for “seven-star” also appears in *Chilsung Cider*, a Korean lemon-lime soft drink, setting up Taekyung’s next line.

[^3]: Puramyeon is an instant-noodle brand. Taekyung uses its spicy flavor as the next step in his escalating flavor joke.

[^4]: Mae’s line is wordplay on two Korean terms pronounced *mugong*: “martial arts” and “empty space.”
