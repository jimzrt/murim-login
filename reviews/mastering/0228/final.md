—Let me update you on the progress of the foundation…

“Team Leader Choi.”

I cut off Team Leader Choi’s voice over the phone in a trembling voice.

“That’s enough. Please don’t say any more.”

—…If it pains you that much to part with the money, should I cancel the foundation?

“No. Absolutely not!”

—Is it like looking at a rice cake in a painting?[^2]

“No, it’s a rice cake in my hand. I’m starving to death and barely stopping myself from eating it.”

[^2]: “A rice cake in a painting” is a Korean expression for something desirable but beyond one’s reach.

The secret Christie’s auction attended by wealthy people from around the world had ended not long ago.

Exactly twenty-four hours later, an endless string of zeros appeared in my bank account.

*Five hundred billion.*

To be exact, after every fee had been deducted, it came to roughly 530 billion won.

The unit wasn’t hundreds or thousands. It was *eok*—hundreds of millions.[^3]

It was literally an *eok*-inducing sum, and I nearly cried out myself.

[^3]: *Eok* is both the Korean unit for one hundred million and an exclamation of shock.

*How much money was this, exactly?*

It was an astronomical amount—enough to change my life, no, enough to let me and my descendants live in luxury for generations.

But I had already decided where the money should go and how it could be used in the most proper and worthwhile way.

“So please keep going. I’m leaving it all in your hands, Team Leader Choi.”

—Understood. We’ll begin with the people on the list you gave us last time.

“Please take good care of them.”

The families of the teammates whose lives had stopped on that day three years ago would be the foundation’s first recipients.

The money couldn’t fill the empty spaces left by their lost family members, but it could help them pursue their dreams without having to worry about anything.

—Next are the veterans of the Great Cataclysm and single-parent families. Is that correct?

“Yes. Please proceed that way.”

After exchanging a few more words, I ended the call.

When I left the room, I found Mom and Hayeon sitting at the kitchen table eating fruit.

The moment I approached, a fork with a piece of apple speared on it suddenly appeared in front of me.

“Son, have some apple. It’s sweet.”

“Uh, okay.”

Crunch.

The sweet flesh broke apart in my mouth. I quietly ate the fruit while listening to the two of them talk.

Friends, studying, someone who lived nearby, dramas…

Sometimes I laughed quietly. Other times I nodded along.

Then, without warning, I spoke.

“Everyone’s okay, right?”

“Hm? What do you mean, son?”

“What?”

“I mean… with everything that happened. Anything at all.”

Hayeon cocked her head.

“Are you trying to talk about the money?”

“……”

“I knew it. Mom, didn’t I tell you yesterday? I said this guy would definitely be like this.”

Mom smacked Hayeon’s twig-thin forearm.

“You! How can you call your brother, who’s eight years older than you, ‘this guy’?”

“Ow! That hurts. Stop hitting me.”

Hayeon grumbled, folded her arms, and leaned back in her chair.

“Excuse me.”

“…Excuse me?”

“What would you rather I call you? My family-register roommate?”

She was my one and only little sister, eight years younger than me. Normally, I would have flicked her forehead for the fun of it, but somehow, today wasn’t the time.

Hayeon looked at me and let out a deep sigh.

“Oppa.”

“Hm?”

“What does Mom say more than anything else?”

I wondered. What was it?

I didn’t have to think long. I heard it whenever I came home—or at least once every few days over the phone.

“Be careful?”

“Right. You know it well.”

She poked the apple and held the fork out to me.

“Eat. Eat, and be careful.”

“……”

“What good would hundreds of billions do us? Forget not being able to spend it all. We obviously wouldn’t even know how to spend it properly.”

I mechanically chewed the apple she had put in my mouth.

I mechanically chewed the apple in my mouth. It was sweet, and it stung like fire—enough to make the tip of my nose tingle.

*When did she grow up so much?*

This had been a difficult choice for me.

The truth was, I wasn’t particularly good or righteous.

I was just an ordinary older brother who wanted to give his only sister everything she asked for, and an ordinary son who wanted his mother, after all the years she had struggled alone, to finally live in comfort.

And hundreds of billions of won was more than enough to make every fantasy I had ever imagined a reality.

*With this much money…*

I had agonized over it. Even after making my decision, I kept agonizing.

But all of it had been pointless.

People praised me as both a hero and a big-hearted man, but they were wrong.

My sister, eight years younger than me, had a far bigger heart than I did.

She had inherited all of Mom’s best qualities. Even her eyes curved into the same crescents when she smiled.

“Son, as I always say…”

“No parent in the world could spend money their child earned by shedding blood. Yes, I know.”

“Good. You know it well.”

“But you’ll have to start spending some now. I plan to earn a lot more.”

“Then buy me a massage chair.”

“A massage chair?”

“Yeah. I wondered what I’d want first if my son became successful and offered me a present, but that was the only thing I could think of.”

I laughed along with Mom.

My heart felt lighter, as though I had finally set down a heavy burden I had carried for a long time.

“Then let’s go look at one together tomorrow. We can look at furniture while we’re at it.”

“Furniture?”

“Come to think of it, the furniture in our house is pretty old, too. Mom, when someone offers you something like this, you’re supposed to accept it.”

I wondered what expression she would make when she learned that the furniture was for the house we would soon be moving into.

Even more so when she discovered it was the place where the three of us had lived back when we were a family of four.

*I’d love to go there right now, but…*

I wanted to save that pleasure for tomorrow—or rather, for what came next.

Realizing the time had finally come, I rose from my seat.

“Where are you going?”

“Son, there’s still plenty of fruit left.”

I smiled broadly at the two disappointed faces and answered,

“It’s okay. I think I’ll take a nap.”

This time, I was going to sleep for a very long time.

“Who’s there?”

“It’s me.”

“…Is that you?”

At first, he hadn’t been able to tell.

In the span of only one or two shichen, the young man’s footsteps had grown as light as his voice.

*Has he gained some kind of insight?*

Before, the old man had been able to sense his presence from fifty paces away. This time, he hadn’t noticed him until he came within thirty.

He wasn’t sure whether to call that impressive or bizarre.

Though a smile tugged at the corner of his mouth, his voice was gruff.

“Did I not tell you to come at daybreak?”

“I couldn’t wait. I already know my answer, so why waste time?”
