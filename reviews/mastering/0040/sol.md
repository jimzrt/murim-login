# Chapter 40

I was flying through the sky, enormous wings spread wide as I cut through the wind.

Far below lay a high, steep gorge shaped like a bottle gourd. Hundreds of people stood inside it.

A man at their center shouted at the top of his lungs.

“Don’t fight for the Jin Family of Taiyuan!”

Then the ground shook. Trees trembled, and mountain birds took flight.

I glanced over my shoulder. An enormous cloud of dust was sweeping through the gorge toward us.

“Fight for yourselves! Fight for your family and loved ones, who will be trampled by the enemy!”

He drew his sword and roared.

“Stand and face them like martial artists! I will do the same!”

Hundreds of weapons were drawn in unison. The man strode forward and took his place at the front. The cloud of dust rolling through the gorge scattered, revealing countless people.

— Waaaaah!

— Wipe out those Jin Family bastards!

The man suddenly looked up. When he spotted me, he grinned.

“I’ve got a good feeling about this.”

The moment I saw his face, the strength drained from my wings. I plunged into the depths of consciousness.

* * *

“Blub, blub-blub!”

I flailed desperately with my wings—or rather, my arms—before realizing what had happened.

*It was a dream.*

Thank God. I thought I was a goner. Only after catching my breath did I take in the situation inside the room.

— This is a breaking news report. A new Gate has appeared at Exit 3 of Hapjeong Station. Mana measurements have confirmed it as a C-rank Gate, and…

A small TV sat on the desk, showing an announcer. And then there was—

“What the hell was that?”

Jinho hyung. He held a pot lid in one hand and chopsticks in the other, staring at me like I was out of my mind.

“Some kind of performance art?”

“Shut up. I was dreaming.”

“About swimming?”

“About falling.”

“Good for you. You’ll grow taller.”

He delivered the line without a trace of sincerity, then slurped up his noodles as if he owned the place. For a moment, I wondered if this was even my room.

“This is my room, right?”

“Probably.”

“Then why are you here?”

“Is this anything new?”

That sounded surprisingly reasonable. I almost bought it.

“Turn the TV off or something. People are trying to sleep.”

“Some inconsiderate bastard even hits a guy in the Adam’s apple while he’s sleeping.”

“…”

No one could beat that bastard in an argument.

“If you’ve got nothing to say, eat some ramen. I cooked five packs because I thought you might wake up.”

Talk about foresight. Overcome with emotion, I accepted the chopsticks.

Was this ordinary ramen? This was my first ramen in a month.

The mouthwatering smell, the perfectly cooked noodles, the spicy broth loaded with freshly sliced Cheongyang chili peppers…

*This is insane. Absolutely insane.*

Slurp.

By the time I came to my senses, it was all over. Jinho hyung stared blankly as I licked the pot clean.

“I thought you were filming a commercial. Have you never eaten ramen before?”

“It’s my first ramen since I came back.”

“You’re still going on about that?”

“Try eating nothing but Chinese food for a month, then have some ramen. This deserves a Michelin star.”

“Stop. It’s not funny anymore.”

He looked thoroughly fed up. This time, though, I had something to back me up.

“Look this over, then we’ll talk.”

“What is this?”

“What do you think? A product user manual.”

“…”

“Wait, is this…”

“Yeah. It was inside that capsule. Read it.”

“Who throws out a piece of junk more than twenty years old with the manual still inside?”

Jinho hyung tilted his head, then started reading. A few seconds later, he looked up.

“This is a misprint. The date of manufacture is January 1, 2020.”

I’d thought the same thing at first.

At first.

“That might not be a printing error.”

“Huh?”

“No, never mind. That’s still just a guess. What about the rest? Have you ever heard of the model or manufacturer listed there?”

Jinho hyung was crazy about electronics, especially capsules.

He was a big name on all the related forums and had even run an IT blog.

But his immediate answer sent my expectations crashing down.

“No.”

Well, that was only natural. Even an internet search hadn’t turned up anything. Still, I couldn’t help feeling a little disappointed.

“You really don’t know? You know this field inside out.”

“Yeah. But I don’t know this.”

Jinho hyung scratched his head.

“An illegally modified capsule? Or maybe a custom job? Honestly, I’ve never seen a design like that.”

The more he talked, the bleaker things looked.

“Fine, set the design aside. But I know every model from the earliest ones to the latest. Every capsule ever released in Korea.”

“And?”

“The model name written here. The manufacturer. I’ve never seen either one before.”

“Couldn’t it be from an overseas manufacturer?”

“Oh, you hopeless idiot. You dumbass. You moron.”

Looking thoroughly exasperated, Jinho hyung thrust the manual at me.

“Read the first line.”

“Product User Manual?”

“Exactly. It’s in Korean. Korean!”

“Oh.”

“Whether H Soft is a domestic manufacturer or an overseas one, if they went as far as producing a Korean user manual, there’s no way I wouldn’t know them. It’s not like there are hundreds or thousands of capsule manufacturers in this business.”

I felt like a complete idiot. Then again, it wasn’t like I knew anything about capsules.

Jinho hyung suddenly spoke.

“Wait a second.”

He pulled out his smartphone and started tapping the screen. He seemed to be searching, but the result was obvious.

“Fuck. Nothing but a porn site.”

*Yeah, that one was pretty good.*

“What, is it a ghost company? Why isn’t anything coming up?”

“Read the rest, too.”

By the time he reached the last page, he’d really feel like he’d seen a ghost. Jinho hyung turned the pages with a serious expression.

Once.

Then once more.

“Isn’t it incredible?”

“Yeah. Incredible.”

His voice was hollow.

“You told me to look at blank pages. That’s incredible.”

“Huh?”

“No wonder I thought this manual was so slapdash. No explanation of the capsule’s parts, no operating instructions, and even the model name, manufacturer, and date of manufacture are a mess.”

“Blank pages? What are you talking about?”

“Well, well. Look at this liberal-arts dumbass playing innocent.”

I snatched the manual from him and hurriedly read it. Everything I’d seen before falling asleep was still there. Precautions on the second page. Key features on the last.

“You can’t see this?”

“Cut it out. This is starting to get scary.”

That look. That tone.

He meant it.

The writing I could see was invisible to Jinho hyung.

Or maybe…

*Only I can see it.*

I stood frozen for a long time.

* * *

Pshhh—

I climbed out of the capsule on shaky legs. Its glossy exterior made it look like a giant egg. This was the latest model, released only last month.

“Oh, you’re out already. Did you try the game I recommended?”

Still half out of my mind, I answered the capsule café owner sitting behind the counter.

“Yes.”

The virtual-reality game he’d recommended was a megahit with ten million concurrent players. It had incredible graphics, outstanding freedom, and more than seventy percent of the market, he’d said.

“The graphics are insane, right?”

I logged into the game. Then I saw the graphics and thought:

*Am I insane?*

*This is the most popular virtual-reality game there is?*

The graphics were good. I could give it that. But that was as far as it went.

The NPCs’ appearances and movements, their dialogue patterns, the five senses I experienced through my character—all of it felt unnatural. It was a *game*, but it never felt like *reality*.

“Do you have any games set in wuxia?”

“Ah, so you’re into wuxia? There are quite a few. What’s the title you’re looking for?”

“Murim.”

“Murim Online?”

“No. It’s an open-world game. Single-player.”

“Is there a wuxia game like that?”

Figures. There was nothing more to hear. I staggered out the door, and the owner called after me.

“Come again!”

I wouldn’t.

Not ever again.

* * *

Hope Goshiwon.

I sat beneath the old, rusted sign and pulled out my smartphone. The other end picked up almost before it could ring.

Click.

“Yeah. Why?”

My one and only nemesis—no, my younger sister, Hayeon. The moment I heard that uniquely bratty voice of hers, my throat closed up.

“Hello?”

“…Yeah.”

“Why’d you call?”

“Just because. I wanted to hear your voice.”

Silence.

Dead silence.

“I’m hanging up.”

“No, wait. Wait!”

“Three seconds. What do you want?”

*That damn girl…*

Right. This was Jin Hayeon. Any tears welling in my eyes instantly dried up like sand around the pyramids.

“What’s Mom doing?”

“She went out. Said she had an errand. Call her if you’re curious.”

I deliberately didn’t. Even this brat’s voice was enough to choke me up. If I heard Mom’s, I’d probably bawl like a little kid.

I quickly changed the subject.

“What about you?”

“What’s a high-school senior with a hundred and twenty days left until the college entrance exam supposed to do? Study.”

Her tone was sharper than usual. Exam stress must have been hitting her hard.

“How’s studying going?”

“I bombed the July mock exam. I was off my game and got even the easy questions wrong. God, the more I think about it, the more annoyed I get.”

“It’s fine. Just do well on the real thing. How many did you miss?”

“Two.”

“That’s still Grade 1.[^1] What about the other subjects?”

“Two across all subjects.”

“Huh?”

“One in Korean history and one in math.”

“…Two in total, across every subject? Are you serious?”

“Obviously that’s what I meant.”

*Smart little brat…*

I knew she was good at studying, but I hadn’t realized she was this good. Thinking back on my own grades, it really seemed possible for one child to hog all the good genes.

“You’re pretty good at studying, huh?”

“From your perspective, isn’t that amazing?”

“W-what kind of nonsense is that? I was pretty good at studying too, you know. You just don’t remember because you were in elementary school…”

“Last week, while we were deep-cleaning, I found your report card. There were so many sevens, I thought it was a slot machine hitting the jackpot.”

“You need some allowance, right? How much does makeup cost these days?”

“That’s just sad. Seriously.”

*Cruel girl…*

The call lasted more than ten minutes. I mostly listened. Hayeon rattled on about studying, school, and a boy she was interested in, her voice much brighter than it had been at first.

A strange sense of sentimentality came over me.

*I really did come back.*

Had I been dreaming? Or had I lost myself in some delusion?

Only a day had passed in reality, yet utterly incomprehensible things had happened to me.

But I decided to stop trying to understand them.

*I’m back in reality now.*

And I had to live in reality.

My family was here. I was here. That was enough. I’d simply had a strange dream for a little while—the kind that would fade from memory with time.

“So I…”

“Yeah.”

Listening to my little sister chatter, I stood. It was time to step out from beneath the old, rusted sign and head back to my room.

* * *

Bzzzt. Bzzzt.

Seong Jinho cracked his bleary eyes open. The smartphone beside his pillow was vibrating.

Six in the morning. The signal that started his day.

“Oh man, I feel like death.”

It had been five years since he’d left home. By now, the moldy smell that crept into his nose every morning had become thoroughly familiar. Eyes half shut, Seong Jinho shoved his cigarettes and lighter into his pocket and left the room.

*Nothing wakes you up like a cigarette.*

He shuffled up to the rooftop in his slippers. He had just put a cigarette between his lips when—

Thud.

“Huh?”

What was that?

He leaned over the railing and spotted a hunk of metal in the recycling area directly below.

Then he noticed the burly young man standing there, staring at it.

“Hey! Jin Taekyung!”

At Seong Jinho’s shout, Jin Taekyung looked up.

“What?”

“You throwing that away?”

The hunk of metal was the junk capsule Taekyung had picked up the day before. After playing some ridiculous prank with it, he seemed to be throwing it out again.

*He was awfully serious for a prank like that… Ah, whatever. It was nonsense.*

Seong Jinho let out a quiet laugh.

“Why throw it away? Not going back to Murim?”

“You actually believed that?”

Jin Taekyung smiled back. But to Seong Jinho, who had known him for a long time, the smile looked strangely awkward.

*What’s with him?*

Something felt off. As Seong Jinho stood there uneasily, Jin Taekyung waved and started down the hill.

“Where are you going, you punk? Aren’t we eating breakfast together later?”

“I have to work!”

Jin Taekyung left without looking back. Seong Jinho took a drag from his half-burned cigarette.

“That bastard really works hard…”

Soon, Jin Taekyung disappeared from sight. Seong Jinho stubbed out his cigarette in a flowerpot and was about to leave when the junk capsule caught his eye.

*He said the manufacturer was H Soft, right?*

It was probably just some half-assed prank, but there was no harm in looking into it.

[^1]: Korean mock exams use a 1–9 scale; Grade 1 is the highest.
