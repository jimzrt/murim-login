# Chapter 293

Sss. Hooo.

Only the soft sound of breathing drifted through the hospital room. Standing guard at the door, I watched the others focus on circulating their qi and regulating their breathing.

Their cross-legged postures had been awkward at first, but they were gradually getting the hang of it.

*Not bad.*

Team Leader Choi, Butler Kim, Song Song, and finally Im Kkeokjeong.

Before sunset, all four had completed a full circulation. Not long afterward, they became capable of performing the Small Circulation on their own.

*Not bad for a first step. No, this is better than I expected.*

None of the others possessed energy as vast as Team Leader Choi’s, but Butler Kim and Song Song were mages. They were already adept at handling mana and adapted quickly.

The biggest surprise, however, was someone else.

Im Kkeokjeong.

*I thought he’d struggle for a while…*

I’d forgotten. Im Kkeokjeong was the only Second Awakener among everyone I knew. Had it come from grueling effort? Or a stroke of heavenly luck?

Whatever the reason, he had already broken through the limits imposed upon him once before.

*Maybe there was a reason he underwent a Second Awakening.*

Judging by what he had shown me, Im Kkeokjeong might have the greatest potential for growth.

He was still a D-rank Hunter. He had plenty of steps left to climb.

*I won’t have to worry about the aftereffects of his injuries, either.*

Repeatedly circulating his qi would naturally improve his Muscles and Bones and Sinews and Meridians.

As long as Im Kkeokjeong could overcome the mental scars left by his trauma, he would be able to return to the Guild before long.

And by then…

*The Guild will have changed quite a bit, too.*

The changes had already begun. Lee Jungryong had drawn the knife hidden up his sleeve and turned it against us. Now he owed us compensation for it.

With that compensation as its foundation, the Peace Guild would achieve astonishing growth. These people needed to stand at the forefront.

Someone had to run the Guild. Someone else had to identify talented, trustworthy people and turn them into loyal members of the Peace Guild.

And those people would be given martial arts.

*We need blades of our own.*

The moment that thought crossed my mind, Team Leader Choi finished his Small Circulation and opened his eyes with an exhale. A sharp gleam flashed through them.

Starting with him, the others opened their eyes one after another.

Ding.

> **System**
>
> - Thanks to your excellent instruction, several people have reached **One Star** in the **Jin Family’s Cultivation Technique**.
>
> - The Title **Trainee** has changed to **Training Instructor**!

A martial arts drill instructor, huh.

Was this heaven telling me to work them to death for a while?

I grinned at the people still savoring the afterglow of circulating their qi.

“We’ll be having dinner inside a Gate.”

Murmurs rose from all around at my declaration.

“Excuse me?”

“Huh?”

“Sorry, but did you hear what he just said?”

“I thought he said we’d be eating dinner inside a Gate. I must have misheard.”

“Ah, right.”

I didn’t even need to look at their faces. The ones having that innocent conversation were the new hires on their first day.

The people who had joined only two or three weeks earlier, however, reacted differently.

“I knew this would happen.”

“Ah, fuck…”

“Good thing I brought potions.”

“Hyung, do you have any arrows to spare?”

“Yeah. I brought an extra two hundred for emergencies, just in case.”

“Then could I get a hundred?”

They hadn’t joined even a month apart, but their reactions were worlds apart.

The veterans swore under their breath like they were sighing, then swiftly pulled armor and equipment from their backpacks.

Clack! Clack! Clack!

Was this how a teacher felt?

A satisfied smile spread across my face at the results of repeated training.

I turned to the greenhorns, who still had no idea what was happening.

“You all brought everything with you, right?”

The new hires nodded.

“Uh, yes. You told us to bring it all, so…”

“Put your bags down, take out your equipment, and put it on.”

“Excuse me? Is this some kind of mock battle?”

“Mock battle, my ass. You don’t improve much doing that. Real combat is best.”

“…?”

Suspicion crept into their innocent eyes.

“Then, perhaps…”

“Are we really going into a Gate?”

“Yes. A raid to celebrate joining the Peace Guild! What do you think? Sounds great, right?”

“…”

“The new people don’t seem too excited. What does everyone else think?”

The people I’d worked to the bone for the past few weeks gritted their teeth and shouted.

“That’s a wonderful idea!”

“As expected of King Taekyung.”

“Wow. This. Is. Great!”

Hmm. Just as I thought.

This, too, was the result of training. They clearly remembered what had happened to the few brave souls who had openly challenged my decisions.

The logic of *we can’t be the only ones who suffer* had probably played a part as well.

*Exactly. Everyone should suffer together.*

If the new hires were Pomeranians, the others were already Tosa mastiffs.

Faced with an atmosphere blazing with determination to die together, the new hires recoiled and protested.

“B-But nobody told us about this! The notice said we’d only be doing a simple workshop for the first week after joining the Guild…”

“Yes. This is the workshop.”

“What Guild holds a workshop inside a Gate? For an entire week!”

“The Peace Guild.”

“That’s ridiculous!”

“Does everyone else think so, too?”

I turned my head slightly. The veteran Tosa mastiffs who had spent the past few weeks tasting hell bared their teeth and shouted.

“Why is it ridiculous? It works! Of course it works!”

“Obviously this is a workshop!”

“Who rents a vacation house for a workshop these days? You’re supposed to bond by killing monsters together inside a Gate!”

“We can’t be the only ones getting fucked!”

That last shout was so blatant it bordered on desperate. Hearing it forced me to reflect deeply.

*I guess I need to work them even harder.*

They couldn’t be allowed to think of this as suffering. I planned to condition—or rather, train—them until they accepted all of it as perfectly natural.

“All right, everyone quiet!”
