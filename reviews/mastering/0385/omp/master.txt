# Chapter 385

“Isn’t that right, you peninsula bangzi?”[^1]

“What are you saying, you mainland chink bastard?”

A heavy silence fell over the room.

Even with translation magic, foreigners like Magic Johnson had no idea exactly what *bangzi* and *chink* meant, leaving them bewildered. Team Leader Choi clicked his tongue quietly, while one man’s face hardened like stone.

“What?”

“What, asshole?”

“Say that again, bangzi.”

“Sure. Chink.”

“You fucking—”

“Fine. Let’s get this straight. I’m a dog. You’re a chink.”

“…!”

I watched his face twist. He shoved back his chair and was about to leap to his feet when the woman beside him raised a hand to stop him.

“Enough.”

She was a beautiful woman in her early twenties, with a crisp, refreshing air about her. Her voice remained calm.

“I’d appreciate it if you both toned it down.”

*Tone it down, huh…*

I studied her for a moment, then shrugged.

“Sure, if you insist.”

Perhaps my answer caught her off guard. One eyebrow arched slightly.

“I heard you weren’t easy to deal with, but you’re more agreeable than I expected.”

“I have to be. You’re the one asking, after all.”

“You fell for my feminine wiles, didn’t you? Ah, being beautiful is such a burden.”

She coyly swept back her long hair, drawing a quiet laugh from me.

“Did you just laugh?”

“You’re more entertaining than I expected, Ms. Pai Chen.”

Pai Chen. That was her name.

I couldn’t let her looks and fresh-faced air fool me into thinking she was some university brochure model. She might never have married, but she was already well past fifty.

She was also a Senior from a generation far beyond mine—and one of the heroes of the Great Cataclysm.

“So you knew who I was from the start. Hmm. I thought kids these days might not recognize me.”

She had a point. Compared to other S-rank Hunters, Pai Chen rarely appeared in the media.

There were even persistent rumors that she had assumed a new identity and started a new life somewhere out of the public eye.

But I had known about her since I was little.

“My mother’s been a fan of yours for years. You starred in that romance movie that came out about twenty years ago, didn’t you?”

“Oh my. How long ago was that? Still, it’s nice to meet someone who remembers.”

“Could I get your autograph later? I think my mother would love it if I brought one home.”

“Of course. And from now on, just call me Chen. My full name sounds too formal. If you want to be even more casual, you can call me *older sister*.”

“Excuse me? What are you talking about? I looked you up online, and you’re five years older than my mother.”

“…You really are a handful. Just as I heard.”

But the real handful was someone else.

“You fucking turtle-dick…!”

His voice boiled with rage. The man we had briefly forgotten during our conversation glared at me with furious eyes.

“How dare a bangzi from some weak little country ignore me! Do you even know who I am?”

“…Wow. That line was fucking terrible.”

Pai Chen propped her chin on one hand and looked between us with interest.

“It really was. I’ve been wondering, though. Do you even know who this boy is?”

Pai Chen wasn’t the only one waiting for my answer.

Everyone was staring at my mouth except Team Leader Choi, whose expression had reached a state of near enlightenment.

The man huffing like a seven-year-old who had lost an argument, America’s Archmage Magic Johnson, and even the young man from Britain. Their attendants were no exception.

Under their collective gaze, I nodded.

“Of course I do. Wu Heixing.”

He competed for first or second place in media exposure even among S-rank Hunters. There was no way I wouldn’t recognize him.

Wu Heixing—the man who had been picking a fight with me and calling me a bangzi—stared wide-eyed.

“You knew who I was, and you still dared to act that way?”

“Even a stray dog would know there’s a complete piece of human trash among the S-rank Hunters.”

“W-What did you say?”

“What? Everyone knows your hobby is chasing attention and your specialty is committing crimes, then playing the media afterward.”

“…!”

“Come to think of it, didn’t you cause some trouble last year? You posted a raid photo on your official social media with some bullshit caption about ‘the only drug my country allows me.’ Then a month later, you got caught using actual drugs. Was it marijuana?”

“Mr. Jin Taekyung.”

“Team Leader Choi, don’t stop me.”

“It wasn’t marijuana.”

“Huh?”

Team Leader Choi cut in and calmly corrected the Taekyung Wiki.

“It was cocaine and methamphetamine.”

“Oh, I see. Thank you for clarifying.”

“You’re welcome. Now that things have come this far, what’s left that we can’t say?”

Look at that enlightened expression.

Team Leader Choi had relinquished all worldly attachments and seemed ready to ascend to immortality. Leaving him behind, I beamed at Wu Heixing and continued.

“Damn, how did you think of setting up foreshadowing like that? This bastard is Wu Heisunwon at minimum. *Purple Raid*.”

“W-What did you say?”

“Now that I think about it, there was something else too. Five years ago, you got caught sexually assaulting someone after drugging them at some club called Burning Moon. How did you get acquitted? Under Chinese law, it wouldn’t have been strange if you’d been executed two or three times over for that.”

“…!”

“I heard rumors that you’re the son of someone at the very top of the Communist Party. Did money and power get you off? Look at this clever bastard.”

“Sh-Shut your mouth! How dare a lowborn bangzi speak to me that way!”

Wu Heixing’s face was bright red as he shouted. Then someone who had been silently watching the whole debacle finally spoke.

“You’re loud.”

An arrogant voice flowed smoothly through the room.

“Frivolous and vulgar. I hear you’re descended from a Chinese aristocratic family. Did the elders of your house never teach you proper etiquette?”

Wu Heixing bit his lip when he recognized the speaker.

“Y-You’re…”

“You’re?”

Faintly green eyes beneath neatly groomed brown hair swept slowly over Wu Heixing, then moved away.

“William.”

At the young man’s call, a graying middle-aged man stepped forward from behind him.

His shoes were spotless, his suit pressed sharp as a blade. He stood tall, surveyed the room, and spoke in a pleasing baritone.

“Everyone, rise and pay your respects to His Royal Highness Prince Felix Alexander Louis, Duke of Cambridge, Earl of Strathearn, Baron Carrickfergus, Knight of the Garter, and Knight of the Thistle.”

*Duke of Cambridge, Earl of Strathearn… Fuck, how many titles does he have?*

Anyway, Prince Felix-whatever sternly added,

“You may all simply address me as His Royal Highness Prince Felix.”

“…”

“…”

Look at their faces. Everyone looked more uncomfortable than they had ever been in their lives.

Naturally, the most spectacular reaction came from Wu Heixing, who had just called me a lowborn commoner.

And why wouldn’t it? If this came down to social status, how was he supposed to beat a member of the British royal family?

“S-So, this is…”

“Ah.”

Prince Felix studied the flustered Wu Heixing, then nodded as if he had finally understood.

“I see. Do you have a stutter?”

“A st-stutter?”

“If not, why can’t you speak properly? Did your family neglect to provide you with speech training?”

Long live His Royal Highness the Prince.

I didn’t even need to step in. The third in line to the British throne was injecting pure carbonation straight into my veins, and I could feel it fizzing all the way down to my duodenum.

I was grinning so hard my mouth threatened to split when Prince Felix suddenly turned his gaze on me.

“You.”

“Hm? You mean me?”

At my question, the middle-aged man standing behind Prince Felix opened his mouth.

“When His Royal Highness Prince Felix Alexander Louis, Duke of Cambridge, Earl of Strathearn, Baron Carrickfergus, Knight of the Garter, and Knight of the Thistle, addresses you—”

“That will do, William.”

Prince Felix raised a hand, silencing the parrot. Then he gazed at me benevolently.

“I have cast aside my dignity and personally intervened because I have always believed that the barriers between social classes must be broken down.”

“…?”

“What does it matter whether one is a commoner or a noble? Beneath God, we are all equal. So don’t let that man’s words wound you too deeply.”

*What the fuck is he talking about?*

I was momentarily speechless. Team Leader Choi leaned over and whispered discreetly,

“I believe he thinks you’re a commoner.”

“…!”

*Has that prince bastard lost his mind?*

As I stared incredulously at Prince Felix, he slowly rose and extended the back of his hand toward me as if it were the most natural thing in the world.

“Come, then.”

“…Come? What is it this time?”

The middle-aged man—secretary or parrot, I still couldn’t tell—spoke with a pleased smile.

“Kiss the back of his hand and express your gratitude for the benevolence His Royal Highness Prince Felix Alexander Louis has shown you.”

“…”

*Are these people completely insane?*

*Did these eighteenth-century fuckers all travel here in a time capsule?*

My head was still reeling when a message spell from Team Leader Choi reached my ear.

- No.

I answered through Sound Transmission.

- What do you mean, no?

- Whatever you’re thinking, no. Just smile and let it pass. Prince Felix is already famous for being rather eccentric.

- If I break the back of his hand right now, wouldn’t that make him even more famous?

- No. Absolutely not!

- Just the back of his hand. Or one finger, at least.

- I said no!

I hadn’t known Message Magic could carry this much emotion. Team Leader Choi must have been desperate.

*Fine. He’s a British prince. I’ll let it go.*

I was trying to swallow my anger when—

“Hey, Sibeol-jwa.”

*What now?*

Magic Johnson had suddenly approached me. I regarded him warily.

I hadn’t expected much from Wu Heixing, who was infamous for his anti-Korean sentiment and assorted scandals. Even so, the thrill of seeing in person the S-rank Hunters I had only ever watched on television was rapidly fading.

“…Please. Call me by my name, not Sibeol-jwa. Would you like it if I called you Fuckson instead of Johnson?”

“Hmm. Now that you mention it, I see your point. Jin, then?”

“That’s much better. What do you want?”

“Nothing much. You aren’t planning to kiss the prince’s hand, are you?”

*That sounds like plenty.*

I stared at him and immediately asked,

“Of course not. What is this, the Middle Ages? Would you want to do it, Johnson?”

“I would.”

“What?”

A bolt of enlightenment flashed through my mind.

I had completely forgotten. Magic Johnson was an American national hero—and the nation’s most famous gay man.

*Time* magazine’s choice for “the world’s most influential LGBT person” looked at me with a perfectly serious expression and made a proposal.

“So I’d like to express your gratitude to the prince on your behalf.”

“…”

What a way to package it.

Unlike Magic Johnson, however, Prince Felix quietly withdrew his hand.

“Beneath God, we are all equal human beings. Antiquated customs such as this must disappear. Magic Johnson, I shall accept your kind offer another time.”

“…”

*Was that really something the bastard who’d just held out his hand to me could say?*

Pai Chen had been listening too. Apparently just as dumbfounded, she muttered,

“Oh my. He’s shameless. He may not be a criminal, but he might be even worse than Wu Heixing.”

“Even so, he’s better than Wu Heixing. That bastard should have been executed under the law.”

“That’s true.”

Wu Heixing had shriveled in the presence of one heavyweight after another, but now sparks flew from his eyes.

“Y-You, you, you bangzi bastard!”

“Enough with the bangzi routine. Even the national anthem gets tiresome after the second verse. The same goes for me putting up with you.”

“…Putting up with me? You? Me?”

His tone suggested he genuinely couldn’t comprehend it. I nodded.

“Yeah. I’m sure now.”

I already had a good idea of Wu Heixing’s level. He, meanwhile, hadn’t realized the slightest thing about me.

A fight began with sizing up your opponent. This one had ended before it even started.

“So quit picking fights and walk away while I’m still asking nicely. Let’s just focus on our own jobs.”

“You’re a mere A-rank Hunter, yet because Comrade Chairman expects great things from you, you’ve forgotten your place and started running wild—!”

“Ah.”

I’d been wondering why he had acted like an asshole from the moment we met. So that was it.

The reason was so obvious and childish that I couldn’t help laughing.

“I’d call it cute, but you’re way too fucking old for that.”

“…!”

“What are you going to do? You want all the Chairman’s attention and expectations for yourself, but some Korean guy stole your thunder.”

“You…”

Wu Heixing’s face burned with shame and rage when he realized I had seen through him.

By now, everyone’s attention was fixed on us. He must have felt their contemptuous stares all the way to his bones.

And when people like him found themselves in situations like this…

*They always crossed the line.*

My prediction was dead on.

A faint movement.

His fingertips slid almost imperceptibly toward his sword’s hilt.

As a gesture of trust, the magnanimous Chairman Xiao Yang had not asked anyone present to disarm. But that consideration was about to become poison for Wu Heixing.

*Draw it. Don’t hesitate.*

No matter how reckless I might seem, I did at least consider the circumstances.

Until now, I had held back because I had come here at the Chairman’s request to help China. But if Wu Heixing drew his weapon, I would have all the justification I needed to rough him up within reason.

*That’s it. More. Just a little more.*

As though I were controlling him, Wu Heixing’s hand crept toward the hilt. Just as his fingers were about to close around it—

Step. Step.

Several sets of footsteps echoed through the corridor outside the silent underground bunker.

Wu Heixing’s hand stopped at almost the same instant the tightly shut door swung open.

The moment I saw the man who entered alongside Minister of Defense Wei Penghu, Wu Heixing’s existence vanished from my mind.

“I’m terribly late. Have you all been waiting long?”

Bold features that looked painted with a brush, and a powerful build his clothes couldn’t conceal.

He looked like a middle-aged man barely into his forties, but beneath that skin lurked an old tiger and a cunning snake.

*Lee Jungryong.*

Our eyes met, and a deep smile spread across Lee Jungryong’s lips.

[^1]: *Bangzi* is a derogatory Chinese slur for Koreans.
