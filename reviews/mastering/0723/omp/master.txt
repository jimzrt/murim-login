# Chapter 723

One day. Two days. Then three.

The rain that had come to be called the Sacred Rain still fell across Nanman. Beneath the benevolent grace of the Earth Mother Goddess, nothing could stop its newly united people.

*Thud! Craaack!*

A middle-aged man felled a massive tree with a single swing of his ax. A thin woman hoisted a mountain of firewood as though it weighed nothing.

Watching from the window, I muttered, “Wow. They got one hell of a buff.”

“A b-buff?”

The question came from behind me. I already knew I had a visitor, so I turned to answer.

“Nothing. What brings you here? Shouldn’t you be swamped right now?”

“I was passing by. Besides, I’m not as busy as I was. Everything is being put right in the blink of an eye.”

“Really?”

For an uninvited guest, Yayul Mok was a welcome sight. I looked at the Young Palace Lord of the Nanman Beast Palace and went on.

“That’s good news. Who do they have to thank?”

Yayul Mok shrugged.

“Father says it’s thanks to some Han Chinese man. He must be mistaken.”

“I don’t think he is.”

“He must be. A despicable Han Chinese man would never help us.”

“True. You can’t trust anyone from the Central Plains.”

“Now you’re speaking sense.”

A brief silence passed. Then, as if on cue, we both let out a quiet laugh.

“It’s all thanks to you, Jin Taekyung.”

“We all worked hard, but you’re right. I did do most of it.”

The smile on Yayul Mok’s face deepened at my shameless reply.

“Are all Han Chinese as lacking in humility as you?”

“No. Just me.”

“That’s a relief.”

“Why?”

“Because I think I could grow to like Han Chinese more than I do now.”

“Oh…”

I looked at him with fresh eyes.

I never thought I’d hear him say that. I’d noticed the change little by little, but the hostility toward the Central Plains that once filled him was gone.

“Don’t like them too much. Go there and you’ll find plenty of madmen.”

“You take me for a child. I know people are much the same wherever you go. In Nanman and the Central Plains alike.”

“Really? You didn’t seem to know that when we first met.”

“…That was because you turned the fields into a sea of fire.”

Looking a little embarrassed by the memory, Yayul Mok came over to the window. He gazed out through its half-open shutters, then spoke.

“It still doesn’t feel real. That all this could happen in the place where I was born and raised.”

“The disaster? Or the miracle?”

“Both.” After a moment, he added, “But this, too, must be a god’s will.”

“…You believe in the Earth Mother Goddess too?”

“I don’t know about that. But I believe a god exists. I’ve seen things I can’t explain any other way.”

Anyone who encounters something beyond understanding is bound to think of a god. You might not know who’s up there in that impossibly distant sky, but you’d figure they must be someone impressive.

*I’m no different.*

I kept that last thought to myself and changed the subject.

“So, how’s the cleanup going?”

“If you mean the restoration, we’re already in the final stages. We should see the finished results within a few days at the latest.”

The Inner Palace had been devastated, and hundreds of houses and pavilions had collapsed in the Outer Palace alone. The speed was astonishing, but instead of expressing surprise, I nodded.

*Well, that buff is incredible.*

The rain falling even now carried the powers of life and purification. It was an area-wide buff that kept people full of energy and enhanced their physical abilities.

*It’ll wear off when the rain stops, of course. But a few days is plenty.*

They say even a sheet of paper is easier to lift when two people do it together. Here, tens of thousands of Nanman people were working day and night to rebuild their homes.

And their efforts were bearing fruit at an incredible pace.

“What about the other matter?”

Yayul Mok understood what I meant.

“We abolished the Great Chieftain system to remove the hierarchy and divisions that remained between the tribes. The Miao people and all four of the other major tribes agreed.”

“Good. Oh, what about Yohi?”

He nodded.

“She was the first to propose abolishing it.”

The tribal chieftains who had joined forces with Baeksang had been executed before countless witnesses. Yohi alone had been pardoned.

She had repented of her wrongdoing, however late. More importantly, she had led the beasts in helping the tribespeople escape the Outer Palace. No one had raised any real objection to her pardon.

“She said we couldn’t allow another Baeksang, Heugung, or Yohi to come after them.”

“She’s right. The Great Chieftains had too much power.”

“Father and the other tribal chieftains agreed. From now on, the Nanman Beast Palace will answer to the Palace Lord alone. The Tribal Grand Council will remain in case of an emergency. And…”

As Yayul Mok continued, I realized once again how thoroughly the Nanman Beast Palace had changed.

The Beast Miao King was now both Palace Lord and priest, with authority far greater than before. The appearance of the Earth Mother Goddess had calmed the people, and they had come together regardless of tribe or faith.

For hundreds of years, since the founding of the Nanman Beast Palace, this land had been divided into thirty-two pieces. At last, it was united.

*You really could call it a small kingdom now.*

Tens of thousands of tribespeople. Nearly ten thousand warriors. Add the beasts they commanded, and it was no exaggeration. Even if Nanman’s warriors fell short of the martial artists of the Central Plains, they were a formidable force.

I looked at Yayul Mok with fresh interest.

“…Why are you looking at me like that?”

“Now that I think about it, you do look kind of noble. Turns out you’re a prince, or something.”

“…A prince?”

He repeated the word doubtfully, then let out a quiet laugh.

“What’s so funny? It’s not that far off.”

“I was just thinking what an honor it is.”

“What?”

“To be called a prince by the Apostle of the great Earth Mother Goddess. What else would you call it?”

“…?”

What was he talking about?

While I blinked at him, Yayul Mok took something from inside his robe and held it out.

“Take it.”

“This is…”

“We decided to call it the Mother Goddess Scripture. Father asked me to give it to you.”

I took the bundle of bamboo strips, still bewildered.

*Ding.*

> **System**
>
> — You have acquired Mother Goddess Scripture.
>
> — Mother Goddess Scripture is a scripture containing records of the Earth Mother Goddess.
>
> — A god can exist only when someone believes in it.
>
> — The Earth Mother Goddess has become known as the One God of Nanman!
>
> — Countless followers of the Earth Mother Goddess are rejoicing!
>
> — Through the records of Mother Goddess Scripture, your name has become known in a new light!
>
> — You have achieved Religious Reformation, an achievement that makes one wonder if anyone else would go this far!
>
> — You have acquired the Title Apostle of the Earth Mother Goddess!

Holographic windows filled the air along with the System notifications.

I stared at them for a moment, then hurriedly unrolled the bamboo strips. After reading the tiny writing on them, I burst out laughing in disbelief.

“What the hell is this?”

Yayul Mok shrugged.

“Exactly what it says. The Earth Mother Goddess has been with this land since ancient times. Whenever Nanman faced a crisis, she sent an apostle to save its people…”

“And that’s me?”

“Yes. Three hundred years ago, her apostle was my ancestor, the first Palace Lord. Two hundred years ago, it was the Sect Leader of your Fire Gate Clan.”

They’d managed to fit it all together.

Sure, I’d given them a few hints. I just hadn’t expected to find my name written out in full.

No, not just *my* name. *Our* names.

*Rustle.*

I skimmed the rest and found plenty I recognized. Jeok Cheongang, of course. The Fire Dragon Pavilion members. Even Namho.

This was supposed to be something like a Bible. Could they really put us all in it?

“I know you threw it together in a hurry, but come on. Who the hell wrote this backstory?”

“Father.”

“No wonder it’s so good. How did he make it all fit together so neatly?”

“…”

I avoided Yayul Mok’s gaze and tucked the bamboo strips into my robe.

*The Apostle of the Earth Mother Goddess.*

I’d somehow become an apostle of a god, a role that certainly hadn’t been written in my fate. But I didn’t feel bad about it. In fact, right now it was a major gain.

*It should ease some of the resentment Nanman’s people hold toward the Central Plains.*

Yayul Mok suddenly spoke, interrupting my thoughts.

“So, are you ready?”

“Hm?”

“To leave Nanman.”

“…!”

After a moment’s silence, I smacked my lips.

“You’re sharp.”

“I’d have to be blind not to notice.”

“How did you find out?”

“Even if I ignored everyone else, that huge fellow has been frantically gathering food since yesterday. As if he’s preparing for a long journey.”

There was no need to ask who he meant. I sighed.

“Taishan, you crazy bastard. I told you to keep it quiet.”

“Your master, Fire King Jeok Cheongang, made off with five jars of fruit wine.”

“…”

Oh, come on, Old Master.

I squeezed my eyes shut. Yayul Mok’s voice reached me anyway.

“You should at least say goodbye before you go. Everyone’s waiting.”

* * *

It wasn’t until we neared the North Gate that I realized Yayul Mok hadn’t been exaggerating when he said *everyone*.

*This is…*

Thousands of people. Maybe tens of thousands.

The field beyond the gate was covered in fresh grass and flowers, brought to life by the rain. Amid the crowd filling it, I spotted familiar faces.

Jeok Cheongang stood in front of a cart loaded with jars of fruit wine, pretending nothing was out of the ordinary.

Taishan held meat in both hands, looking solemn. Beside him were the Fire Dragon Pavilion members, each mounted on a beast.

And then…

“You’ve finally arrived.”

Yayul Cheok, the Beast Miao King and master of the Nanman Beast Palace, spoke with a stern face. He stood here on behalf of everyone in this land.

“Why were you going to leave without a word?”

I scratched the back of my head.

“I was going to tell you.”

“You were?”

“Well… in a letter.”

“Just a letter?”

“You looked so busy…”

“Do you think that makes sense?”

“…”

Was this what they called death by a thousand questions?

I couldn’t find an answer. The Beast Miao King, a full head taller than me, gazed down at me.

“Blazing Flame Divine Dragon Jin Taekyung.”

His voice was low. Then, in a stern tone, he continued.

“You came to our homeland for a reason. You had a purpose here, did you not?”

“…”

“And now you mean to leave without a word, though you have not achieved that purpose. You faced death countless times in an unfamiliar land and saved countless lives. Yet not once did you ask for the reward you were owed.”

His gaze and voice were fixed on me, but everyone gathered there could hear him. Everyone was watching.

“Why?”

I found myself answering.

“I was just one person trying to save other people.”

“…!”

“I didn’t do it for a reward. And I couldn’t ask anyone to make another sacrifice. That’s why I was going to leave.”

Silence fell.

Tens of thousands of people stood together, yet I couldn’t hear a single breath. Against that silence, the rain falling on our heads sounded louder than ever.

Beyond its curtain, the Beast Miao King moved.

*Rustle.*

His back was as firm as a pillar—the back of the Palace Lord of the Nanman Beast Palace, a man who should never have had to bow to anyone. Now it bent toward me.

Before all his people, he offered me a deeply respectful clasped-fist bow.

“I, Yayul Cheok, the Beast Miao King, on behalf of everyone in this land, ask Great Hero Jin Taekyung, Fire Dragon Pavilion Master, to admit us into the Murim Alliance.”

The rain was beginning to ease.
