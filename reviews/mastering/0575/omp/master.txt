# Chapter 575

I didn’t need to see it up close or rush over to check for a pulse.

The moment the blue-flame-wreathed spearhead drove into the Kraken’s body, I knew.

*It’s over.*

*Slash! Boom!*

The spear cut through it, and fire qi exploded from within.

The monster’s head, some ten meters tall, burst like a balloon. Blue blood that hadn’t evaporated rained down over the sea.

And then—

*Whoooooosh—*

As the Kraken’s enormous body finally collapsed, a clear chime rang in my ears.

*Ding.*

> **System**
>
> You have defeated Lv. 140 ‘King of the Black Sea’ Kraken!
>
> You have gained a large amount of EXP!
>
> **Myriad-Poison Ring** has detoxified all poison within your body!
>
> Status Abnormality: **Poisoned** disappears!
>
> Status Abnormality: **Paralyzed** disappears!

Even with the System telling me I’d defeated the Kraken, my mouth felt as gritty as if I were chewing sand.

*Damn it.*

I should have kept it alive. Somehow, I should have kept it alive long enough to learn who was really behind this.

But one moment of carelessness had brought about consequences I couldn’t undo. The Kraken was dead, and the question remained unanswered.

If it had attacked me directly, I might have had a chance to capture it, but…

*Too late now.*

The Kraken had been smarter than I’d thought.

It knew I was stronger than it, even poisoned. It knew it couldn’t escape.

That was why it had gone after the survivors instead of me. It would rather die than be captured.

Faced with its final desperate move, I’d had only one choice.

“…Phew.”

I sighed, looking at the Kraken’s corpse half-submerged in the sea.

There had been no other choice. If I hadn’t killed it with that one strike, someone would have died right in front of me.

“Sacrificing the few for the many” sounded noble enough. But it was still a sacrifice. If I had the power to stop someone from dying for nothing and stood by instead, that was all I’d be doing.

*I shouldn’t have let my guard down. Not until it was over…*

But what was done was done.

I was anxious about failing to uncover who was behind the disaster, and I regretted letting my guard down. I didn’t regret killing the Kraken.

People were alive because I had.

“Over here! Please, help us!”

“Cough! Please, my child…”

“Mom! Mooooom!”

Maybe fighting as far away from them as I could had helped.

Hundreds of people had been killed or injured before I’d even arrived, but there were still more survivors waiting to be rescued.

Their desperate cries came from the choppy water and the broken Gwangan Bridge. I forced down my exhaustion and shot toward them.

*Fwoooooosh!*

The briny, refreshing smell of the sea filled my nose.

I raced across the water like a gust of wind. Passing the Kraken’s floating corpse, I reached the survivors and began pulling them up.

“It’s dangerous out there, so everyone needs to stay here. If you still have the strength, help me rescue the others first.”

A young man who looked relatively unharmed spat out seawater.

“Cough. B-but shouldn’t we run somewhere else…?”

“Run?”

“Y-yes.”

I raised a hand and pointed. Black smoke rose between the densely packed buildings all around us.

Ordinary civilians couldn’t hear what I could, but my senses had already told me what was happening.

—Hssssss!

—Tank! Three steps forward!

—The building! It’s coming down! There are civilians inside!

—Aaaaaagh!

*Rumble! Boom!*

Faint screams and crashes. Humans and monsters were fighting in the streets not far away—and in several places at once.

“There are monsters in every direction. Still want to go?”

“…No. I’ll stay here.”

At least he caught on quickly.

The Kraken was dead, but this was far from over. If I was going to get this chaos under control, I had a busy day ahead of me.

*Fwoooooosh! Boom!*

The survivors stared, dazed, as I kicked off the water and shot into the sky.

* * *

Everyone ate. But who you ate with, and where, made a difference to how formal the meal was.

By that measure, the breakfast shared by the two people sitting across from each other had been formal indeed.

They had started late, but quiet conversation had stretched the meal to a full two hours. Now beautifully garnished sujeonggwa[^1] sat on the white tablecloth.

“With Seollal coming up, I had them prepare tteokguk… I hope you enjoyed it, Team Leader Choi.”

Choi Minwoo wiped his mouth with a napkin before answering the gentle, courteous voice.

“It was excellent, Mr. President. Thank you for taking such care.”

“Ha-ha. I’m glad.”

The middle-aged man across from him, President Baek Hanseong, smiled warmly.

“You’ve been so busy lately that I worried you weren’t eating properly. I told our head chef here at the Blue House to take special care. I said we had an important guest.”

“Important guest” was no empty compliment.

An invitation to breakfast at the Blue House already marked a person as powerful and influential. A meal alone with the President meant something more.

Baek Hanseong valued Choi Minwoo that highly.

“It’s good to see you eat so well. Makes me feel I did something right, too.”

Choi Minwoo dipped his head.

“Thank you. But I’m not skipping meals as often as you think. I’m sorry to have worried you.”

“Oh? Well, I suppose you have a personal chef.”

“No. Someone has been making my meals for me lately.”

“Someone making your meals… Ah, do you mean…?”

Choi Minwoo gave a small nod.

“Mr. Jin Taekyung’s mother cooks for me.”

“My goodness. His mother does it herself?”

“Yes. I’ve tried to refuse—it doesn’t feel right—but she won’t hear of it.”

President Baek let out a quiet sound of surprise.

He already knew Jin Taekyung’s family was staying temporarily at Choi Minwoo’s estate. Hearing Choi himself speak about it, though, gave that fact new significance.

*If she’s even cooking for him… They’re closer than I thought.*

Jin Taekyung and Choi Minwoo. Choi Minwoo and Jin Taekyung.

To President Baek, they were two enormously valuable men he could not afford to lose. They were also his only means of keeping the immensely influential Ares Guild in check.

Jin Taekyung, with his extraordinary standing. Choi Minwoo, Cheon Taemin’s maternal grandson and only living blood relative.

With those two on his side, Baek could rise still higher. After becoming the youngest president in Korea’s history, he might well become the first to win reelection under the amended law.

*Whatever it takes, I have to bring them both over to my side.*

While President Baek’s thoughts ran to politics, Choi Minwoo sipped his coffee and thought about something else entirely.

*She said she was making dinner tonight… Kimchi stew, was it?*

Strange. He had just finished breakfast, yet his stomach felt empty.

He wanted Jin Taekyung’s mother’s kimchi stew more than the tteokguk the Blue House head chef—a master of Korean cuisine—had prepared with such care.

Fluffy white rice from a pressure cooker of a kind rarely seen these days. Pork cooked until tender, and well-aged kimchi.

And people sitting close together around one table, too busy eating to put down their spoons.

*Hmm.*

Choi Minwoo caught himself smacking his lips and gave a quiet, rueful laugh.

*So that’s what it is.*

He wasn’t particularly fond of tteokguk or kimchi stew. Nor was he looking forward to dinner because her cooking was better than the Blue House chef’s.

He simply liked being there. The warm food, shared with warm people.

*Home.*

And family.

Choi Minwoo had lost both before he was as tall as the chair he sat in now. He had thought he could never have them again.

*No. I do still have one family member.*

A hero who had saved humanity. A living savior.

But a man who had been unable to protect his own family.

Choi Minwoo thought of the last time he had seen his maternal grandfather—a meeting he could no longer remember.

*Soon… I’ll see you again.*

At the thought of the grandfather who had shut himself away for decades, a feeling he couldn’t name rose within him.

President Baek noticed the change in his expression.

“Is something troubling you?”

“Ah. No. It’s nothing.”

“Ha-ha. I see. Well, now that we’ve finished eating, there’s something important I’d like to discuss with you, Team Leader—”

The door flew open.

President Baek stopped mid-sentence. He frowned at the aide who had entered without knocking.

“I thought I told everyone not to come in.”

“I’m sorry. I tried to avoid it, but you weren’t answering…”

“Answering? Of course not. My phone’s on silent.”

With an important conversation under way, taking out a smartphone would have been discourteous, given who was at the table.

“Huff… I’m sorry. But it’s urgent.”

The aide was breathing hard, sweat beading on his forehead.

Sensing something was wrong, Choi Minwoo took the smartphone from inside his suit jacket. He had more than ten missed calls from Butler Kim and a string of text messages.

Dread rose in him.

*Don’t tell me…*

He opened the messages. One of his neatly groomed eyebrows twitched.

> Butler Kim
>
> Butler Kim
>
> Emergency Monster Wave in Busan
>
> Extra-large named monster sighted
>
> Young Master, where are you?
>
> Young Mster

The messages were from Butler Kim. They were short, but there was nothing small about the news.

*A Monster Wave? In Busan?*

Busan was Korea’s second-largest city, home to millions. An extra-large named monster could cause an enormous loss of life just by doing a forward roll.

And what the aide said next made matters worse.

“The emergency alert was delayed by a little over ten minutes. The Monster Wave happened so suddenly, and the Gate management office and the surrounding facilities were all destroyed…”

“You idiot! Do you hear yourself?”

“I-I’m sorry.”

Sweat poured down the aide’s face as he continued.

“But according to a report from one minute ago, the extra-large named monster has been subdued. Hunter Jin Taekyung happened to arrive at the scene…”

“What? Are you certain?”

“Yes. The remaining monsters are being dealt with, and survivors are being rescued.”

“…Phew. At least there’s that.”

President Baek let out a sigh of relief. Choi Minwoo rose without hesitation.

The worst of the immediate crisis might have passed, but this was no time to sit and talk.

“I’ll be going, Mr. President.”

“Of course. We’ll speak another time.”

They exchanged brief farewells. Choi Minwoo had just left the Blue House when—

*Bzzzzzt.*

His phone, now turned back on, vibrated forcefully.

Team Leader Choi thought he knew who was calling and answered at once.

“Yes, Butler Kim. I’m on my way ri—”

—It’s me.

“…!”

Choi Minwoo stopped dead.

Song Cheonwoo’s aged voice came through the phone.

—We need to talk. It’s urgent.

[^1]: Sujeonggwa is a traditional Korean cinnamon punch, often served chilled with dried persimmons and pine nuts.
