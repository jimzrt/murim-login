# Chapter 1072

At that moment, only one thought ran through the black-robed man’s mind.

*Am I seeing things?*

It was a reasonable question.

He was a sorcerer. An experienced one, and highly skilled.

He had neither internal energy measured in jiazi nor martial arts exceptional enough to be called divine. What he did have was the ability to control hundreds of monsters perfectly.

And yet…

*What the hell is this guy?*

He stared blankly at the monster before him—or rather, at the intruder who had clearly pulled a monster’s hide over himself.

Only after pinching his arm hard enough to confirm he wasn’t dreaming could he force out the question he’d been holding back.

“Who are you?”

The intruder flinched and answered haltingly.

“I-I’m a monster.”

“…You’re talking like a person.”

“A-aren’t there monsters that talk like people?”

“…No, there aren’t.”

Faced with the expert’s thoroughly logical rebuttal, the intruder fell silent for a moment. Then he opened his mouth.

“Grrroooar.”

“…”

“Ahem. Grrroooar.”

“…”

“G-grraaaah.”

Now he was even raising both arms like a jiangshi as he continued his uncertain attempts at a monster’s roar. The black-robed man, more confused than ever, shouted, “Stop!”

The intruder gasped. “Why?”

“What the hell are you doing?”

“I was trying my best to imitate them… Wasn’t that last one pretty close?”

“Well, the last one was a little close.”

“Wow, I knew it! Thank you!”

An exchange that strayed this far from common sense could paralyze the mind.

While the black-robed man struggled for words, the intruder added proudly, unable to hide his delight, “All that practice for two days straight paid off. Grandpa was right—hard work doesn’t betray you.”

The black-robed man blinked, still floundering in confusion.

“W-what did you just say?”

“Hm?”

The intruder tilted his head. His eyes, visible beyond the rotten hide, were clear and bright.

“Oh, about hard work not betraying you? My grandfather’s been telling me that since I was little…”

“Not that!”

“Oh, I know what you mean. The big people. Well, I guess it’s not quite right to call them people, but anyway, I hid among them and kept practicing.”

“S-so you mean…”

“That’s right. Like I said, I’ve been doing it for two days.”

“…!”

The answer was innocent enough, but the black-robed man felt a shock run down his spine.

*For two days? And I never noticed?*

It made no sense.

After grueling training, he’d gained the ability to control some five hundred monsters perfectly. He could detect the life energy of a living human with his eyes closed.

But…

*He’s not lying.*

He could feel it instinctively. Everything the intruder had said was true, without a single lie.

As far as the black-robed man could tell, the fellow wasn’t intelligent enough to lie convincingly. And after being caught so plainly, he had no reason to keep anything hidden.

That realization finally brought the black-robed man to his senses. Until now, he might as well have been bewitched.

“You… You’re an orthodox faction lackey.”

The intruder gasped and hurriedly waved his hands.

“N-no, I’m not!”

“Shut up, you madman.”

“Madman? That’s a very vulgar and bad word. My grandfather told me never to say it.”

“You little piece of—!”

The black-robed man felt his blood rush to his head. However unexpected the situation, he’d let this lunatic play him, if only for a moment.

He shook the evil bell in his hand, putting all his anger into the motion.

*Jingle!*

He hadn’t imagined the extra force in its sound.

The death energy possessed by sorcerers who commanded monsters made the ringing clearer and more sinister. That chilling note carried two messages.

First, it alerted the other sorcerers stationed nearby to what was happening.

And second—

*Whoosh! Thud!*

It ordered the hundred or so monsters he’d brought as guards to deal with the madman.

“You have one chance left to receive my mercy.”

The monsters swept around them with speed that belied their enormous frames, surrounding the intruder and forming an iron wall in front of the black-robed man. From behind it, he ground out his offer.

“Surrender quietly and tell me everything you know. I’ll give you a relatively quick and painless death.”

He wasn’t relying on numbers alone.

Each of his guards had power comparable to a master somewhere between Supreme First Rate and Peak. Their strength and speed surpassed human limits, and their tenacious vitality was the stuff of nightmares.

And there were a hundred of them.

Even if the lunatic before him possessed tremendous skill, the black-robed man was certain the outcome wouldn’t change. The intruder had more than these monsters to contend with.

“Struggle all you like. In a few moments, you won’t even be able to do that.”

Thinking of his fellow sorcerers, who should have received the signal and be rushing here already, the black-robed man curled one corner of his mouth.

“But why haven’t they answered?”

The intruder’s sudden question caught him off guard.

“What?”

“Answered. Uh, should I call it an answering ring? Anyway, I watched you for two days. You always send a bell signal back, even for the smallest thing.”

“…Huh?”

The black-robed man looked around in a hurry.

No. Looking would do him no good. He focused every bit of his attention on listening for his fellow sorcerers’ reply—for the sinister ringing of bells like his own.

But all he heard was the wind blowing in from far away and the breathing of the monsters around him. No answer came.

Nothing at all.

“…!”

His heart lurched.

Something was wrong. Terribly wrong.

As dread swept through him, his eyes turned toward the intruder. Despite being surrounded by monsters, the fellow had his ears pricked and his hands cupped around them like a trumpet, listening too.

“Oh. I really can’t hear them. Nothing at all.”

“You… You…”

What could he say? How was he supposed to make sense of this?

He couldn’t even form the words. Beyond the discolored hide, he saw the intruder’s eyes curve into crescents.

“What a relief. I knew I could count on Little Grandpa.”

“Little… Grandpa?”

“Yes. He teaches me all sorts of things. He gets really angry whenever I call him Little Grandpa, but sometimes I think he secretly likes it, too.”

The intruder caught himself rambling and snapped his mouth shut with a gasp.

“Oh, don’t tell Little Grandpa I said that. He’ll get angry again.”

The black-robed man didn’t answer. He no longer had the presence of mind to.

He stared at the intruder, who had finally stopped talking, and barely managed to force out the question that had just come to him.

“Who are you…? No, who are you people?”

His vision swam. What he’d just heard had made him realize a truth he desperately didn’t want to believe.

The other thirty or so sorcerers who had come here with him were already dead.

Perhaps their killers had slipped close the way this intruder had. Perhaps they’d used methods even quieter and deadlier.

If any were still alive, they wouldn’t be for long.

Not if the man whose sobriquet had just flashed through the black-robed man’s mind was here.

“Th-that Little Grandpa of yours… Don’t tell me he’s…”

“What did you just say?”

The emotionless voice pierced his ears. Shock struck him like lightning through the crown of his head.

He was here.

Little Grandpa—no, *that man*.

Right behind him.

Yet the black-robed man hadn’t sensed his breathing. No breath had brushed the back of his neck when the man spoke. He’d detected neither the slightest presence nor a trace of life energy.

The black-robed man stood rigid as a statue. The breathless voice came again.

“I asked you. What were you babbling about?”

The black-robed man forgot how to speak. He even forgot how to breathe.

All he could do was make one final, instinctive struggle to survive.

He didn’t even realize a line had crossed his wrist before he could move the old, bloodstained evil bell that was practically everything he had.

*Slice. Plop.*

Everything came a moment too late.

The severed wrist hit the ground. Only then did its owner notice it was gone.

The pain arrived last. The black-robed man felt it and tried to scream in terror.

“Guh…!”

*Tap. Thud.*

His Sleep Acupoint was pressed, and his body crumpled helplessly.

Unable even to finish his scream, he plunged into pitch-black darkness with no end in sight. The voices of two people drifted after him, faint as a dream.

“I told you I’d kill you if you called me that one more time.”

“I’m sorry, Little Grandpa.”

“…You’re driving me insane. Let’s go find that guy.”

“Yes! Little Grandpa!”

Right to the very end, it was a nightmare.
