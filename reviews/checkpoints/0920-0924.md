# Review

The reading copies and active state are broadly consistent. Three passages create avoidable contradictions about the rebels’ fate and the imperial family.

## C01 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.99
- **Source:** Chapter 921, after the battle
- **Current:** That was how long it took for all three thousand rebels to be wiped out.
- **Defect:** “Wiped out” implies all the rebels were killed, but the same scene shows survivors surrendering.
- **Replacement:** That was how long it took to defeat all three thousand rebels.
- **Rationale:** The replacement includes those killed, captured, and surrendered without contradicting the following paragraphs.

## C02 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.98
- **Source:** Chapter 923, account of the restoration
- **Current:** The fourth prince, who had been far removed from the throne, became a usurper and a parricide in every sense.
- **Defect:** This states the Emperor was genuinely a parricide, despite the account establishing that his father was poisoned and later abdicated in his favor.
- **Replacement:** The fourth prince, who had been far removed from the throne, was branded a usurper and a parricide.
- **Rationale:** It preserves the accusations against him without treating them as fact.

## C03 — Minor

- **Confidence:** 0.93
- **Source:** Chapter 923, Prince Shangshan's approach
- **Current:** Toward the enemy who had killed his parents and brothers.
- **Defect:** The unqualified “brothers” implies that all the prince’s brothers were killed, though the Emperor is his surviving elder brother.
- **Replacement:** Toward the enemy who had destroyed so much of his family.
- **Rationale:** It retains the prince’s personal stake without contradicting his living brother or specifying which relatives died.
