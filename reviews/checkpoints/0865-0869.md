# Review

The reading copies and state are broadly consistent. Three passages need correction: two create location or referent confusion, and one word-count joke does not work in English.

## C01 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.96
- **Source:** Chapter 867, Baek Yeon questioning the twins at Qianqing Palace
- **Current:** “Don’t tell me he got inside the palace.”
- **Defect:** The intruder reached Qianqing Palace, which is already within the imperial palace. This question makes the twins’ answer contradict their account.
- **Replacement:** “Don’t tell me he got inside Qianqing Palace.”
- **Rationale:** Specifying the building preserves the distinction between reaching its entrance and getting inside.

## C02 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.98
- **Source:** Chapter 867, the Emperor asking Baek Yeon about Jin Taekyung
- **Current:** “Zhu Bao. The Murim martial artist who came with him.”
- **Defect:** The phrasing momentarily identifies Zhu Bao as the martial artist, rather than the person the martial artist accompanied.
- **Replacement:** “The Murim martial artist who came with Zhu Bao.”
- **Rationale:** This makes the Emperor’s intended subject unambiguous without naming Taekyung before the narration does.

## C03 — Minor

- **Confidence:** 0.92
- **Source:** Chapter 869, Taekyung teasing Jeong Hogun about the brothers’ reunion
- **Current:** “Right. Four characters: brothers reunited.”

“You’re a lawless thug from the martial world, so it seems you can’t understand. This is not merely a reunion between brothers. It’s…”

“Oh, I get it.”

I nodded with a solemn expression.

“A somewhat complicated reunion of brothers. Right?”

“……”

“Not quite? Too long? How about a chaotic reunion of brothers? One word shorter, but pretty much the same thing.”
- **Defect:** The English phrase has two words, not four characters; the two subsequent alternatives have the same word count despite the claim that one is shorter.
- **Replacement:** “Right. Four words: a reunion of brothers.”

“You’re a lawless thug from the martial world, so it seems you can’t understand. This is not merely a reunion between brothers. It’s…”

“Oh, I get it.”

I nodded with a solemn expression.

“A complicated reunion of brothers. Right?”

“……”

“Not quite? Too long? How about a chaotic brothers’ reunion? One word shorter, but pretty much the same thing.”
- **Rationale:** The proposed phrases have four, five, and four words respectively, so the escalating interruption and final word-count jab work in English.
