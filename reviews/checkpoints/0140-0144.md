# Review

The packet is coherent and preserves its active hooks, but it contains repeated title drift from “prince” to “king,” two capitalization inconsistencies, one dated slang choice, and an active-state sentence that incorrectly implies the luncheon itself has ended.

## C01 — Minor

- **Confidence:** 0.99
- **Source:** Chapter 140, final scene after Cheongpung names Mae Jonghak
- **Current:** I was shocked that Cheongpung had vomited in the very place where the king was about to arrive, but Li Feng seemed unfazed.
- **Defect:** The narration calls Prince Shangshan a king, contrary to his established title and the packet’s terminology decision reserving literal “king” for Cheongpung’s usage.
- **Replacement:** I was shocked that Cheongpung had vomited in the very place where the prince was about to arrive, but Li Feng seemed unfazed.
- **Rationale:** Zhu Bao is consistently established as Prince Shangshan; neutral narration should identify him as the prince.

## C02 — Minor

- **Confidence:** 0.90
- **Source:** Chapter 142, Cheongpung insulting the Three Hands of Zhongnan
- **Current:** “Get lost, you boomer bastards!”

“……!”

“……!”

*Boomer?* They didn’t know exactly what it meant, but that wasn’t important. It had been followed by the word *bastards*.
- **Defect:** “Boomer” is dated internet slang and creates a conspicuously contemporary localization in a passage whose joke only requires an unfamiliar insult for an old person.
- **Replacement:** “Get lost, you old-fart bastards!”

“……!”

“……!”

*Old fart?* They didn’t know exactly what it meant, but that wasn’t important. It had been followed by the word *bastards*.
- **Rationale:** “Old fart” preserves the age-based insult and the characters’ confusion without relying on generational internet jargon.
