# Review

The reading copies retain a consistent action-comedy voice and preserve the packet’s major hooks, but the checkpoint summary misattributes the scripture’s creation, the continuity section contradicts the surviving subordinate gods, and Chapter 723 contains a System-panel formatting drift. One awkward Chapter 721 sentence also warrants correction.

## C01 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.99
- **Source:** Checkpoint summary, Plot
- **Current:** Yayul Mok completes the Mother Goddess Scripture, which records Nanman’s history and names Jin an Apostle of the Earth Mother Goddess, triggering his Religious Reformation achievement.
- **Defect:** The summary incorrectly credits Yayul Mok with completing the scripture. Chapter 723 only establishes that he delivers it on his father's instructions; it does not identify him as its author or compiler.
- **Replacement:** The Mother Goddess Scripture is completed and delivered to Jin by Yayul Mok; it records Nanman’s history and names Jin an Apostle of the Earth Mother Goddess, triggering his Religious Reformation achievement.
- **Rationale:** Removing the unsupported attribution preserves the revealed facts without inventing authorship.

## C02 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.98
- **Source:** Checkpoint summary, Continuity
- **Current:** The Earth Mother Goddess is now Nanman’s sole publicly recognized deity.
- **Defect:** This contradicts Chapter 722, where the traditional local gods remain recognized as subordinate gods under the Earth Mother Goddess.
- **Replacement:** The Earth Mother Goddess is now Nanman’s supreme deity, with the traditional local gods subordinated beneath her.
- **Rationale:** The replacement preserves her supremacy while retaining the religious compromise explicitly established in Chapter 722.

## C04 — Minor

- **Confidence:** 0.99
- **Source:** Chapter 721, reconstruction scene before the Sacred Rain is recognized
- **Current:** Rain from a clear day without a single cloud? Even in Nanman, with its unpredictable climate, this was unusual.
- **Defect:** “Rain from a clear day” is an unnatural English collocation; rain comes from a sky, not a day.
- **Replacement:** Rain from a cloudless sky? Even in Nanman, with its unpredictable climate, this was unusual.
- **Rationale:** The replacement is natural contemporary English and preserves the observation that rain is falling despite the absence of clouds.
