# Review

The reading copies are broadly consistent in voice and continuity, but there are two terminology slips, one untranslated form of address, one quotation-formatting inconsistency, and two places where the checkpoint state prematurely identifies the final door explosion as the Red Wind Band’s attack.

## C01 — Minor

- **Confidence:** 0.99
- **Source:** Chapter 105, Taekyung’s reflection after defeating the mounted-bandit scouts
- **Current:** He definitely said captain, didn’t he?
- **Defect:** The defeated man said “squad leader,” so changing the title to “captain” within the same scene creates an internal terminology mismatch.
- **Replacement:** He definitely said squad leader, didn’t he?
- **Rationale:** Using the title that was actually spoken preserves the callback and avoids implying a second rank or position.

## C02 — Minor

- **Confidence:** 0.96
- **Source:** Chapter 106, Taekyung declining Wolhwa’s proposal
- **Current:** As his younger brother, I can’t go around splashing filth on my hyung’s path.
- **Defect:** The untranslated Korean address “hyung” conflicts with the English-only reading-copy standard and redundantly follows the explicit phrase “his younger brother.”
- **Replacement:** As his younger brother, I can’t go around undermining what he’s trying to accomplish.
- **Rationale:** The replacement preserves Taekyung’s familial loyalty and refusal to interfere with Jin Wikyung’s plan while reading naturally in contemporary English.

## C03 — Minor

- **Confidence:** 1.00
- **Source:** Chapter 109, former deputy leader reporting his affiliation to Pung Yang
- **Current:** Until recently, I was the deputy leader of the Toho Band.
- **Defect:** “Toho Band” contradicts the packet’s binding rendering “Earth Tiger Band,” which Pung Yang also uses immediately afterward.
- **Replacement:** Until recently, I was the deputy leader of the Earth Tiger Band.
- **Rationale:** Using the established name makes both references unambiguously identify the same mounted-bandit group.

## C04 — Minor

- **Confidence:** 0.98
- **Source:** Chapter 109, message delivered by the Lower District Sect’s hawk
- **Current:** The Red Wind Band is moving south across the plateau. Their numbers are estimated at approximately two hundred.
- **Defect:** The letter is presented as an isolated plain paragraph rather than as a normal Markdown quotation, unlike the packet’s required treatment of non-System written material.
- **Replacement:** > The Red Wind Band is moving south across the plateau. Their numbers are estimated at approximately two hundred.
- **Rationale:** A normal blockquote clearly distinguishes the letter from narration without incorrectly presenting it as a System panel.

## C05 — Major

- **Confidence:** 1.00
- **Source:** Checkpoint summary, Plot section, final sentence
- **Current:** As its leaders despair, the main hall doors suddenly explode, marking the attack’s beginning.
- **Defect:** The reading copy ends before revealing who destroyed the doors or whether the explosion is the Red Wind Band’s attack, so the summary converts an unresolved cliffhanger into a confirmed event.
- **Replacement:** As its leaders despair, the main hall doors suddenly explode, but the chapter does not reveal who destroyed them.
- **Rationale:** The replacement records the visible event without resolving the packet’s final hook or introducing unsupported certainty.

## C06 — Major

- **Confidence:** 1.00
- **Source:** Durable state, active_continuity entry for the Mount Heng main hall
- **Current:** The Mount Heng Sword Sect's main hall doors have exploded as the attack begins.
- **Defect:** The active state prematurely identifies the unexplained door explosion as the beginning of the attack, although Chapter 109 withholds the cause and identity of whoever is outside.
- **Replacement:** The Mount Heng Sword Sect's main hall doors have exploded, but who destroyed them remains unknown.
- **Rationale:** Durable continuity must preserve the cliffhanger’s uncertainty so later chapters are not constrained by an inference presented as fact.
