# Review

The reading copies remain coherent in voice and terminology, but the checkpoint state misstates two revealed facts, drops the Great Snow Fiend’s expectation that Dark Heaven can address his lost arm, and Chapter 683 has a chapter-wide apostrophe-style inconsistency.

## C01 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.99
- **Source:** Checkpoint continuity, Southern Heaven Demon Empress bullet
- **Current:** - The Southern Heaven Demon Empress ordered the Great Snow Fiend to capture Jin alive if possible because Jin may become a major future threat.
- **Defect:** This attributes the Great Snow Fiend’s threat assessment to the Southern Heaven Demon Empress, even though Chapter 682 explicitly says he does not know her full reason and independently decides Jin is too dangerous to leave alive.
- **Replacement:** - The Southern Heaven Demon Empress ordered the Great Snow Fiend to capture Jin alive if possible; the Great Snow Fiend does not know her full reason and independently judges Jin too dangerous to leave alive.
- **Rationale:** The revised continuity preserves the unresolved motive behind the capture order while distinguishing it from the Great Snow Fiend’s decision to kill Jin.

## C02 — Major

- **Confidence:** 0.99
- **Source:** Checkpoint continuity, Jin’s level-up bullet
- **Current:** - Jin leveled up after killing Black Hand, restoring some internal energy but not healing his existing injuries.
- **Defect:** Chapter 683 states that Jin’s internal energy was fully restored, not merely partially restored, while his existing injuries remained.
- **Replacement:** - Jin leveled up after killing Black Hand, fully restoring his internal energy but not healing his existing injuries.
- **Rationale:** The amount restored is an explicit System-related combat mechanic and materially affects the continuing duel.

## C03 — Minor

- **Confidence:** 1.00
- **Source:** Chapter 683, dialogue after the Great Snow Fiend rises (representative of a chapter-wide punctuation drift)
- **Current:** “I'm surprised. I thought you wouldn't be able to move after that.”
- **Defect:** Chapter 683 uses straight apostrophes throughout contractions and possessives, unlike the curly apostrophes consistently used in the surrounding reading copies.
- **Replacement:** “I’m surprised. I thought you wouldn’t be able to move after that.”
- **Rationale:** Normalizing the apostrophes throughout Chapter 683 restores the established reading-copy typography without changing wording or voice.
